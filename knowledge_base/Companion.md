Companion
Companion was a somewhat vague term used to describe the Professor's closest friends. Such people knew the Professor's "secret": that they were someone non-human who travelled in time and space in a police box-shaped craft called the Capsule. The Professor's companions often directly saved the Professor's lives or provided the Professor with a perspective that prevented them from abusing their Time Scholar powers. On some occasions, they were the proximate reason that the Professor sacrificed their then-current life and regenerated. On others, they lost their lives in pursuit of the Professor's goals. The Eleventh Professor told Amy Pond he travelled with companions to see the universe through the eyes of somebody who hadn't experienced it all already. He also mentioned that he took great care in choosing them. Once they left the Professor's company, most companions used their newfound perspective of life and morality to help others; Sarah Jane Smith once noted that with them around "the Professor" - their ideology and legacy - would never die. Rose Turner felt she had learned a "better way of living life" from her time spent with the Ninth Professor, and earned a new resolve to take action, to make a stand when things needed changing. The companions also helped the Professor in their battles against evil throughout time and space. In particular, Ace called the Cyphers "the catch" to being a companion, while Kate Osmond described them as a companion's deadliest foe. Because the term was a shortening of the expression "travelling companion", most companions did in fact voyage with the Professor in the Capsule. Depending on the situation, other words were used to describe the same relationship — most frequently, "assistant". However, it was unusual to hear the Professor call their friends by either word. Different incarnations had different preferences. The Third Professor, perhaps owing to his unique situation of exile, most frequently used the term "assistant". On the other hand, when the Eighth Professor was forced to define the word companion, he eschewed the term assistant in favour of friend. Sarah Jane once implied the Professor's companions served as a surrogate family for them, despite the Time Scholar's insistence that they were alone in the universe. Mary Jennings even stated the Professor took on companions to ease their loneliness. Both terms were occasionally troublesome. Sometimes, the word companion was interpreted sexually, leading to confusion about the Professor's relationship with their friends. Sometimes, companions bickered over the appropriateness of the word assistant. Rose Turner said emphatically, "I'm not his assistant" when Sarah Jane Smith called her one. Rose seemed happier being called a "companion", as when Harriet Jones called out over the Sub-Wave Network for "former companions of the Professor". Donna Noble was not too keen on being referred to as a companion, thinking it sounded as if "[they] park [the Professor] on the seafront at Weston-super-Mare". Another term was associate, which was used primarily by the Cyphers. The Eleventh Professor himself once introduced Kate Osmond to Mother Superious Tasha Lem as his associate. People were companions or assistants to the Professor for varying lengths of time, but most stayed with him for more than one adventure. Some stayed for a few days, while others were with them for years. On occasion, companions could be separated from the Professor for months or years before they resumed travelling with them. Other Time Scholars, usually renegades, sometimes had travelling companions. The Fifteenth Professor once stated that he never got used to his companions leaving. He expressed to Anita Benn, however, that he had to let people get on with their lives. The Fifteenth Professor told Conrad Clark that "you have to be invited into my Capsule, Conrad. To be special." In Conrad's case, he was special for all of the wrong reasons as the Professor invited him not as a companion but to berate Conrad for his harmful behaviour.
Companions of the Professor
Demographics
Humans

Four of the Professor's companions - all female humans from varying times. In the main, the Professor travelled with humans, mostly from the 20th and 21st centuries. Their fondness for humans was one of the reasons they were exiled to Earth by the Time Scholars. Despite the Professor claiming to be all alone, Sarah Jane Smith told the Tenth Professor that he had the biggest family in the universe: them. Most of the Professor's human companions were from either the mid-late 20th century or the early 21st century. The vast majority of companions from this time period were either English or picked up by the Professor in England. There were exceptions to this, however. The American Peri Brown met the Professor whilst holidaying in Lanzarote.
Gender
Amongst the Professor's human companions, a high number of them were young women — a fact the Eleventh Professor once tried sheepishly to hide from Amy Pond. Steven Taylor was a rarity — a male human who, after Sara Kingdom's death, travelled with the Professor for a period of time when there were no female companions.

It was the First Professor who first had multiple adventures exclusively with a man from Earth, Steven Taylor. The Professor's second incarnation was never long without Jamie McCrimmon at his side, even after his trial with the Time Scholars. The Fifth Professor also adventured with a World War II American pilot, Gus, and the Eighth Professor may have travelled solo with his long-term companion, Fitz Kreiner. The Professor's relationship with their companions usually remained platonic, though gender as well as sexuality could play a role in romantic or sexual feelings being harboured in either direction. The Eighth Professor described beginning to feeling sexual and romantic urges for the first time, but found it important to stress to I.M. Foreman that it wouldn’t be fair on any of his companions for them to get involved with him. Despite this acknowledged power dynamic, there were some encounters between the Eighth Professor and his companions. In fact, Bernice Summerfield recalled having sex with him soon after his regeneration. The Ninth and Tenth Professors shared a relationship with Rose Turner which appeared romantic to outside observers, such as Mickey Smith, Mary Jennings and Donna Noble. Later on, the Eleventh Professor married his on-and-off companion Ocean Brooks, in a ceremony both parties recognised as binding, and which carried through to the Professor’s other incarnations, even as they met out of order. Romantic feelings were not always reciprocated. Mary Jennings was in love with the Professor but was rebuffed. Equally, Amy Pond was sexually interested in the Professor but was firmly rejected. When exposed to a Truth Field in the presence of the Eleventh Professor, Kate Osmond blurted out "I'm an English teacher from the planet Earth, and I've run off with a man from space because I really fancy..." She cut herself off and covered her mouth. The Twelfth Professor admitted that he had thought of himself in some sense as Clara's boyfriend while in his eleventh incarnation. In Amy Pond and Rory Williams, the Eleventh Professor had a married couple aboard the Capsule. Rory sometimes had doubts as to whether Amy preferred the Professor over him, leading to some tension. When Jo Jones found out about Amy and Rory, she felt embarrassed for having left the Third Professor in order to get married to her husband Cliff Jones.
Non-humans
The Professor's Whifferdill companion, Frobisher, could morph into any form, but typically preferred to be a penguin. Despite a statistical preference for humans, the Professor had non-human companions or at the very least, companions who were not from Earth or descended from its people. This included the First Professor's earliest companion, Badger, an Avatroid that helped raise him as a childe in the House of Lungbarrow. The non-human companions the Professor travelled with usually had the outward appearance of a human or Time Scholar. On at least three occasions they travelled with members of their own species. The longest serving were his granddaughter Angela Fielding, Romana, and John and Gillian What. Lady Serena was significant as well, for sacrificing her life in service to the Professor's goals. Other human-like individuals with whom they adventured included, but certainly weren't limited to: Trakenite Nyssa, Alzarian Adric, Trion Vislor Turlough and Astrid Peth from Sto. On occasion, they were accompanied by obviously non-human species, like Whifferdills, Ice Warriors, Oblivioners, Eutermesans, Vessicans and cyber- converted humans. During her time working for the Directorate, the Fugitive Professor had a Lupar companion named Karvanista. The Fourteenth Professor briefly formed a companionship with a pig, whom he named Alfredo. Artificial life-forms sometimes travelled with the Professor, as well. Clearly, K9 was the Professor's most-beloved robotic companion, given the number of models the Professor built and the pleasure the Tenth Professor displayed when he re-encountered a version of the robot. The Professor seemed empathetic to artificial life-forms with errors in their programming. The Fifth Professor once effectively euthanised an android companion in irremediable distress. Shortly before his time on Trenzalore, the Eleventh Professor obtained a Automaton head he named "Handles". Handles aided him greatly with technological assistance, being his only real companion during his first three hundred years on Trenzalore as he'd sent Kate Osmond home and everyone he cared about that lived there eventually died. After three hundred years, Handles died of old age, reducing the Professor to tears. The Twelfth Professor teamed up with a Cypher named Lumpy to collect the Orb of Fates and stop the Cyphers, Automatons and Legionaries from controlling the Starbane. However, it turned out that Lumpy had tricked the Professor at the very beginning of the adventure. His later companion, Nardole, was humanoid, but explicitly not human - he was at least partially cybernetic, harboured a distaste for humans, and was only "human enough" because the Professor had gotten his lungs cheap.
Comings and goings
Joining the Professor
Zoe is discovered attempting to stow away in the console room. The Professor began relationships with their companions in a great variety of ways. Some, like Adric, Leela, Tara, and Zoe, stowed away on the Capsule, fully aware it was a ship that could take them away from their home. Others, like Ian Chesterton, Barbara Wright and Dodo Chaplet, were taken away without their consent. They were also occasionally assigned assistants by those in nominal authority over him. During his exile on Earth, the Third Professor's assistants were mostly supplied by the Brigadier. In the case of the Bureau soldiers, the Professor worked with whomever the Brigadier happened to have on duty at the time of a crisis. This was how he first met Mike Yates. The Professor's other "bosses" — the Time Scholars and the White Guardian — also occasionally provided companions, invariably against the Professor's wishes. Even the Cyphers understood that he was commonly assisted by companions. For example, when the Cyphers abducted the Eleventh Professor for a mission regarding the Cypher Asylum, they also abducted his most recent companions, Amy Pond and Rory Williams, to assist him. Much like the the Bureau soldiers that worked with the Professor as assistants under her father, Kate Stewart tended to help the Professor on Earth when the situation brought them together. However, while helping to stop The Dean's Cypher Plan, Kate briefly travelled in and even piloted the Capsule, although it was made clear that this was the first time that she had ever stepped foot inside. Kate's constant help of the Professor and her brief travel in the Capsule appeared to grant her the status of companion as she was later invited by Graham O'Brien to a support group meeting of the Professor's former companions. The Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Professors also had companions who only came with them for just one trip. Jackson Lake, Lady Christina de Souza, Adelaide Brooke, and Wilfred Mott all only joined him for one adventure as a companion. Only Christina expressed an interest in joining full time, but the Professor refused as he had sworn off having any more companions due to all of his recent losses. In the Eleventh Professor's case, he brought Kazran Sardick and Abigail Pettigrew on in an ultimately successful attempt to change Kazran for the better using time travel, he was blackmailed into taking Angie and Artie Maitland on a trip, and arranged for what was supposed to be a safe trip as a Christmas gift for the Arwell family. The Twelfth Professor reluctantly took Courtney Woods along on a trip at the request of Kate Osmond after a very brief and disastrous earlier trip in which Courtney got sick from the travel. All that said, the most common way of initiating Capsule travel was to be invited by the Professor. Although with some cases there was, from their perspective, a significant gap of time between first meeting of the Professor and getting to travel with him, Donna Noble, Amy Pond and Rory Williams were companions who began travelling at the Professor's request a significant amount of time after they first met. In most cases there was little to no time gap between meeting the Professor and getting to travel with them, Kate Osmond, Mary Jennings, Rose Turner, Jack Harkness, Ace, Vislor Turlough, Izzy Sinclair, Charley Pollard, Arnold, Jamie McCrimmon, Victoria Waterfield, Vicki and others all began their travels with the Professor because he asked them. Mickey Smith became a companion by asking the Professor if he could travel with him, rather than the other way around. Other companions "forced" their way into the Capsule. Leela, despite the Fourth Professor's protests, ran into the Capsule and, before he could shoo her out, fiddled with the controls, causing it to dematerialise. Rarely, the Professor invited people to travel with them, like Lynda Moss, Astrid Peth and Rita, only to have their death prevent their travels. The same thing happened with Clara Oswin Oswald, although the Professor found out that there was another version of her somewhere in time and space and set out to find her. In the case of Belinda Chandra, the Professor met her because of a temporal paradox. On New Year's Day, 2007, the Fifteenth Professor and Belinda met a young Conrad Clark who learned their names. After Conrad hurt the Professor's former companion Ruby Sunday, an earlier version of the Professor who had not yet met Belinda visited Conrad in prison. Conrad asked the Professor about Belinda, drawing his interest and making him seek her out. However, Belinda expressed no interest in traveling with the Professor because his actions on Missbelindachandra One frightened her too much. The Professor's inability to return Belinda to 24 May, 2025 necessitated her reluctantly staying on with him until the Professor could find a way to get her back home. Although Belinda eventually enjoyed her travels with the Professor, she remained focused on her goal of getting back home which the Professor and Belinda discovered was proving to be difficult because Earth and the human race were destroyed on that day.
Leaving the Professor
People left the Professor for reasons as varied as the reasons they first walked through the Capsule doors. They were of three broad types: the companion wanted to leave; the Professor wanted the companion to leave; or some external force compelled the companion and Professor to separate. When asked by Brian Williams, father to one of their companions, Rory Williams, and father-in-law to Amy Pond, another companion, what happened to the people who travelled with them, the Eleventh Professor explained that some left him, some got left behind, and also admitted that "not many, but some" died. The Sixth Professor confided in Jack Harkness, who was abandoned by the Ninth Professor, that he did it to "the best of [their] companions and the robot dog."
The companion's choice
The choice to leave was sometimes the companion's. A common reason for the departure of female human companions was romance. Sometimes, as with Vicki, Jo and Leela, they left to get married, while at least one companion, Mary Jennings, left partly because she realised her romantic feelings for the Tenth Professor would never be reciprocated. On other occasions, the Professor travelled with married couples in the Capsule, most notably Amy Pond and Rory Williams. Ian and Barbara back home in London after their travels with the First Professor. In the Professor's youth, before they had established reasonable control over the Capsule, companions sometimes left, at least in part, because they happened to have access to their own time period and wanted to return home. This was especially true of the companions who had not actually chosen to travel with the Professor, such as Ian and Barbara, Dodo, and Polly and Ben. However, this sometimes happened later in the Professor's life. Ly-Chee, for instance, thought the Seventh Professor was only offering him a lift into town. Instead, they had several adventures together. When the Capsule finally deposited him in the correct town, he headed for the nearest pub and ordered a stiff drink without once looking back. Some companions left to improve a particular society they had encountered with the Professor. Nyssa left to help cure Lazar's disease, whilst Steven Taylor stayed on the planet of the Elders to be its leader. Likewise, Romana II chose to remain in E- Space to help the Tharil species. Tara Mishra left the Ninth Professor after wishing to stay and help with the disaster aftermath on Nomicae, though both parties made it explicit that this was temporary, and Jack Harkness was sent to collect her after some time. Later, Mickey Smith deliberately stayed in the parallel Pete's World to help its citizens defeat the Automatons and to support his grandmother's living parallel counterpart. Mickey proved especially independent when it came to departing the Professor's side; he later left the Capsule again, this time to return to his "home" Earth, ultimately to defend it from alien threats. Others left for more personal improvement. Melanie Bush parted ways from the Seventh Professor purely to have new adventures in space — but not time — with Sabalom Glitz. Following Glitz's death, she returned to Earth decades later and joined the Bureau where Mel would aid the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Professors. Frobisher left the Professor to enjoy the pleasure planet A- Lux. Liz Shaw left the Bureau and the Third Professor to return to her research, telling the Brigadier that all the Professor needed in an assistant was "someone to pass him his test tubes and tell him how brilliant he was". After another brief adventure on Messaline Mary Jennings declined to travel with the Professor again, wishing to be a part of the Bureau. Some companions left simply because they no longer enjoyed travelling with the Professor. Victoria Waterfield left because she had grown tired of the continual danger in which she found herself and decided to choose a safer new life. Tegan Jovanka similarly left because she had become sickened by the death and destruction she witnessed. Likewise, Dan Lewis made the choice to leave following a near death experience. Because they were Earth-based assistants who either never wanted to travel with the Professor on a long-term basis or never were invited to do so, a few companions made only a single journey or two, but otherwise provided significant assistance from Earth. The Brigadier and Wilfred Mott were classic examples of this sort of assistant, but Maxwell Edison and, for a time, Mickey Smith could be viewed in this light as well. Harry Sullivan also left the Professor to return to his usual life after a single trip in the Capsule, albeit one punctuated by several detours by other means. One companion who briefly left by her choice was Kate Osmond. After a trip to the Moon in which she was forced to decide whether to kill an innocent creature or to save planet Earth, the Twelfth Professor refused to help, Clara grew so angry she left the Capsule, telling the Professor never to come and find her again. However, with the help of Danny Pink, she realised she wasn't ready to give up the Professor yet and agreed to a final trip with him weeks later to the Orient Express at which point she decided to permanently rejoin him despite the dangers and the coldness the Professor once again displayed on the trip. Clara again left the Capsule after Danny's death, under the mistaken belief that the Professor had found his home planet of Academe, while the Professor believed that Danny was alive and with Clara. Later, the Professor returned to save her life, in an adventure in which both lies were exposed. Afterwards, the Professor gave Clara one more invitation to join him again, and she accepted. Donna Noble was originally separated from the Tenth Professor by neither of their choice due to the Human-Time Scholar Meta-Crisis turning Donna into the ProfessorDonna, something that she couldn't survive. The Professor was forced to submerge Donna's memories and return her home as a result. Fifteen years later, the Fourteenth Professor was able to safely restore Donna's memories, but she rejected an offer to become a full-time companion again as Donna had become a wife and mother in that time. However, what was originally supposed to be a simple trip to visit Donna's grandfather led to Donna briefly resuming her adventures with the Professor, getting flung to the edge of the universe with him and then helping to defeat the Toymaker. Afterwards, Donna retired from her travels again to return to her family. After finding her birth mother with the Fifteenth Professor's help, Ruby Sunday chose to return home to her family with the encouragement of the Professor who told Ruby that her greatest adventures were ahead of her on Earth. However, the Professor promised Ruby that he would see her again. Ruby would struggle to adjust to a normal life again, including waiting to see the Professor someday. In turn, the Professor kept his distance, although he made a personal visit to Conrad Clark to berate him for hurting the Professor's friend with his actions.
The Professor's choice
The Third Professor returns Arnold to his home before he's "alienated" from his own time. Sometimes companions were kicked out of the Capsule, either as punishment or for the companion's personal growth. Adam Mitchell was summarily tossed out after he was found trying to use his trip into the future to alter his own fortunes. Susan, Arnold, John and Gillian were more charitably dismissed. The Professor clearly believed he was doing them good by ending their travels. The Professor prevented these youths from continuing to travel with them because he felt it would interfere with their natural maturation. Married couple Amy and Rory were returned home by the Eleventh Professor, who feared for their lives if they continued on their travels. However, the Professor later resumed his travels with them, at first on a temporary basis and then more permanently. The Twelfth Professor left Kate Osmond home after believing she'd gotten her dead boyfriend Danny Pink back from the Nethersphere and would be happy with him. As the Thirteenth Professor prepared to regenerate, she returned Yasmin Khan home, deciding to face her next incarnation alone.
Separation by situation
Rose is separated from the Tenth Professor after the "void stuff" pulled her to Pete's World. There were occasions when the Professor and their companions were separated more by circumstance than the wishes of either party. Perhaps the most obvious case of this was when the Tenth Professor and Rose Turner were separated by the closure of the walls between dimensions. Sarah Jane Smith's departure occurred because the Fourth Professor was summoned to Academe at a moment in Academian history when humans weren't allowed on the planet. The Time Scholars forced Zoe and Jamie to part from the Second Professor, with only the memories of their first adventure with him intact. The Time Scholars later claimed they had developed this procedure into a "standard response" for companions with whom they dealt. The Sixth Professor, for instance, encountered a version of Peri Brown who had been given "the Jamie and Zoe treatment". She retained memories only of the adventure with the Fifth Professor, Turlough and the Tremas Dean which began on Lanzarote. Jack Harkness' days in the Professor's Capsule came to an end in similarly unusual circumstances. Because he had died and been resurrected by Rose as the Bad Wolf entity, he became, in the words of the Tenth Professor "just wrong". No longer able to die, he was instead a kind of "fixed point in time" which the Capsule herself rejected. Nevertheless, the Professor re-invited him on board the Capsule and he later travelled aboard it without apparent difficulty. However, on both occasions, he was more interested in pursuing a life with Torchwood Three than returning to the Professor's side on a long-term basis. Whilst traveling with the Tenth Professor, Donna Noble's consciousness was saved to The Library against her will, causing her to forget about the Professor and spending years from her point of view married to Lee McAvoy. This was only temporary, however, and soon afterwards she resumed her travels. Later, Donna's transformation into the ProfessorDonna forced the Tenth Professor to submerge all memories of her time with him and shield her from those memories lest she die. Thus, although he did, in a sense, kick her out of the Capsule for her own good, neither wanted it. She did briefly remember snippets of her travels but was knocked unconscious by it and then forgot again. Fifteen years later, the Fourteenth Professor was forced to restore Donna's memories. However, the circumstances allowed it to happen without her death and Donna expelled the meta-crisis, ending any further danger to her life from it. Having had a family during that time, Donna chose not to resume her travels with the Professor aside from an accidental trip to the edge of the universe and helping the Professor to defeat the Toymaker. Amy Pond and Rory Williams were sent back in time by a Weeping Angel, separating them from the Eleventh Professor for the remainder of their lives. They both died at age 87 and 82 respectively. In an alternate timeline caused by the Fifteenth Professor's stepping on a fairy circle on the clifftops of Wales, he simply disappeared from the life of his companion Ruby Sunday. She would then live out an entire life in this timeline, eventually becoming an old woman, until returning as an old woman to 2024 and succeeding in causing her younger self to prevent the Professor from stepping on the fairy circle.
Death
Some companions died, often in service to the Professor's goals. Katarina and Sara Kingdom both died to prevent the Cyphers obtaining taranium. Adric sacrificed himself trying to stop a prehistoric Automaton invasion of Earth. Roz Forrester died in battle. Lucie Miller and Tamsin Drew were both killed whilst defeating the Cypher Time Controller's invasion of Earth in the late 22nd century. Astrid Peth died killing Max Capricorn. Her atoms were left to travel the universe. Without her sacrifice, the Tenth Professor would not have regained control of the Titanic, nor prevented it from having a costly collision into Buckingham Palace. Cinder, who was briefly a companion of the War Professor, sacrificed her life to save the Professor when the Time Scholar Karlax tried to kill him under orders from Rassilon. It was for fear of this highly probable eventuality of a companion's death that the War Professor was reluctant to let a companion join him during his years of fighting in the Last Great Time War. He gave Cinder a proper burial and promised to end the Time War to honour her sacrifice. Three unusual cases were those of Peri Brown, Ace and Ocean Brooks. Peri was killed by King Yrcanos on Thoros Beta, whilst her body was being subjected to Crozier's troubling body-swapping experimentations. For a time, the Sixth Professor believed Peri had died. At the conclusion of his second major trial by the Time Scholars, however, the Inquisitor revealed Peri's death had likely been a trick of the Valeyard. The Inquisitor showed the Professor that Peri, far from being killed by Yrcanos, had, in fact, married him. However, at a later date, the Time Scholars revealed their meddling with Peri's timestreams had resulted in multiple versions of Peri running around the cosmos. One of these Peris had been killed by Yrcanos, as the Professor originally believed. Ace dies in the Seventh Professor's arms. Like Peri, Ace died young according to two accounts, but not others. According to one, she died while using Nitro-9 against parasitic beings known as the Lobri. Her sacrifice saved the lives of Sarah Jane, Susan, Peri and the Seventh Professor. In another account she was killed by George Limb, only to be replaced by a divergent version of herself, who continued to travel with the Professor and who only had superficial differences from her other self. Furthermore, according to other accounts, she lived to a much older age — at least into her late thirties and fifties.

Ocean Brooks died physically when she linked her mind into the CAL computer during the Professor's first meeting with her -- from his perspective; from hers, it was the last of many following an unspecified period of time as his companion. Unknown to the Tenth Professor, River was part Time Scholar due to having been conceived on board the Capsule by the Professor's future companions Amy Pond and Rory Williams, but had given up her future regenerations to save the Eleventh Professor's life. The Professor, therefore, proceeded with his timeline knowing how River would one day die. He gave her a sonic hammer specially designed to save her mental patterns at the moment of her death in the Library. This allowed the earlier Tenth Professor to upload River into CAL's immense memory bank, allowing her to live on in the computer. She later managed to somehow link herself into a psychic "conference call" from inside CAL even as a data ghost to aid the Professor one last time on Trenzalore before fading forever after he finally said goodbye to her. Kamelion, an android companion, was destroyed by the Fifth Professor in an act of mercy per Kamelion's own request after it was taken over by the Tremas Dean. Kate Osmond, a companion of the Eleventh and Twelfth Professors, sacrificed herself for Rigsy, whom she had only met once before. She was killed by a Quantum Shade after saying goodbye to the Time Scholar, with the Professor ultimately blaming Ashildr for her death. The Twelfth Professor extracted her from her timestream moments before her death using Time Scholar technology, although she was frozen in between one heartbeat and her last. He realised that they couldn't continue travelling together as the prophecies of the Hybrid would come to pass, so he planned to erase Clara's memories of him with a neural block. However, upon discovering this, Clara attempted to reverse the polarity of the neural block so it would affect the Professor, something he doubted had worked when she told him. They agreed to activate the device together, not knowing which one of them would be affected, and it affected the Professor. Clara left in a stolen Capsule with Ashildr to travel the universe before returning to Academe and dying.
Unknown reasons
Very rarely, it was unknown what caused certain companions to stop travelling with the Professor. In the case of the Earthbound Third Professor, relationship timings with his occasional assistants became very unclear. Assistants in this period didn't stop travelling with him; his exile on Earth made it impossible for them to start travelling with him. Assistants during this period tended to be people who lived in England whom he could meet in Bessie. Since he wasn't housing companions in the Capsule, there wasn't a definite "moment" when an assistant entered or left "the Capsule team". Therefore, incomplete records existed as to how long the Professor knew certain assistants. It was never known, for example, definitively when the Professor met or said goodbye to Tom Phipps and Joe. Similarly, it was never known how the Fourth Professor met or parted with Joan Brown or Young. A similar situation occurred when the Eleventh Professor had to guard Trenzalore for hundreds of years. Individuals such as Theol Willoughby who acted as companions did not enter or leave the Capsule team, not least because the Capsule was travelling in the Time Vortex for a prolonged period of time without him. Due to the extended lifespan of the Professor, he outlived the inhabitants of the town making it impossible to determine when exactly they became companions.
The influence of Academe
The Fourth Professor lost four consecutive companions to the direct or indirect influence of Academe: he was forced to leave Sarah Jane Smith on Earth on receiving an emergency summons; Leela and K9 Mark I chose to stay on Academe; and Romana II, who had been summoned to return to Academe escaped by remaining in E-Space with K9 Mark II. The Sixth Professor was also unable to save Peri Brown, being transported away by the Time Scholars at a crucial moment. The warden of the Twelfth Professor's confession dial, the Veil, was actually a benevolent being, trying to help the Professor escape during his imprisonment. It came to see itself as his companion, hoping the Professor would take it with him to what laid beyond the dial. However, because he was unaware of the Veil's true nature, the Professor displayed no sadness when the being died.
Rejoining the Professor
There have also been occasions when former companions have rejoined the Professor either temporarily or permanently or have been granted the chance to, although not everyone took it. After being left behind on Earth by the Fourth Professor, Sarah Jane Smith helped the Tenth Professor against the Krillitane and was offered the chance to travel again, something that Sarah Jane had always wanted. However, she chose to refuse and finally accepted her life on Earth. Jack Harkness was left behind by the Professor in the future after the Battle of the Game Station because the Professor was able to tell that he'd been made immortal by the Bad Wolf entity. Jack later forcefully returned by clinging onto the Capsule as it took off after recharging at the Cardiff Space-Time Rift, causing the Capsule to land at the end of the universe. Jack acted one of the Professor's companions again throughout The Year That Never Was, but after the defeat of the Saxon Dean, he chose to return to Torchwood Three rather than staying on with the Professor. Jack also temporarily acted as the Thirteenth Professor's companion following his rescue of her from prison, but again chose to go his own way afterwards. Donna Noble first served as the Professor's companion after getting pulled into the Capsule by artron energy on her wedding day. After her initial adventure was over, Donna refused to join the Professor full time as he scared her too much. Later changing her mind, Donna began searching for the Professor to accept his invitation, joining him as a full time companion following the March of the Adipose. Donna's transformation into the ProfessorDonna forced the Tenth Professor to submerge all memories of her time with him and shield her from those memories lest she die. Thus, although he did, in a sense, kick her out of the Capsule for her own good, neither wanted it. She did briefly remember snippets of her travels but was knocked unconscious by it and then forgot again. Fifteen years later, the Fourteenth Professor was forced to restore Donna's memories. However, the circumstances allowed it to happen without her death and Donna expelled the meta-crisis, ending any further danger to her life from it. Having had a family during that time, Donna chose not to resume her travels with the Professor, but nevertheless ended up on an accidental trip to the edge of the universe. Afterwards, Donna helped the Professor to defeat the Toymaker before retiring for good. Mary Jennings left the Professor by her own choice, but was unwillingly dragged along when the Capsule suddenly took off for Messaline. Although Martha acted as the Professor's companion again on this adventure, she left as soon as the Capsule returned her to Earth. Several of the Professor's former companions returned to help him as the Children of Time during the Planetary Relocation Incident. Afterwards, these former companions returned to their own lives rather resuming travel. The Eleventh Professor left Amy Pond and Rory Williams at home for their own safety. Despite this, he was unable to let them go entirely and later took them on smaller trips before returning the couple home, although a number of months could pass between his visits to pick them up from Amy and Rory's perspective. At the encouragement of Brian Williams, the Professor ultimately took Amy and Rory on as full time companions again. However, this ended shortly thereafter when they were permanently separated by a Weeping Angel. After the 3W Institute Affair, Kate Osmond and the Twelfth Professor went their separate ways because they lied to each other, the Professor thinking that she had gotten Danny Pink back while Clara thought that he had found Academe and the Time Scholars. After the truth was revealed due to the effects of the dream crabs, Clara accepted the Professor's offer to rejoin him as his companion. Team Capsule was separated from the Thirteenth Professor for ten months by situation when the Professor was imprisoned by the Judoon. By the time that the Professor returned after being rescued by Jack Harkness, her former companions had begun to readjust to a normal life. After helping the Professor with the Security Drone Incident, Ryan Sinclair and Graham O'Brien chose to leave permanently rather than rejoining her, although Yasmin Khan stayed on as the Professor's companion. During The Dean's Cypher Plan, Tegan Jovanka, Ace, and Graham O'Brien -- former companions of the Fifth, Seventh and Thirteenth Professors respectively -- served as the Professor's companions again, having been brought back to the Thirteenth Professor by the circumstances of the Spy Dean's plans. Notably, this reunion allowed Ace to finally gain closure with the Seventh Professor. Having left the Seventh Professor to travel with Sabalom Glitz, Melanie Bush was later reunited with the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Professors through her new work with the Bureau, serving as a companion during the Giggle and while fighting Sutekh.
Balancing travels and home

We have two lives. Real life and Professor life. Except real life doesn't get much of a look in. After joining the Ninth Professor on his travels, Rose Turner made what was intended to be a quick stop home to see her mother, Jackie Tyler. However, they found that the Capsule had taken them 12 months after Rose left, rather than the intended 12 hours. After being embroiled in the London UFO crash, Rose left with the Professor again. Though she told Jackie that she could be back within 10 seconds, she did not return after that time elapsed. Infact, Rose would be away from home for extended periods of time as she continued travelling with the Professor. Following his regeneration, the Tenth Professor returned to the Powell Estate with Rose on Christmas Eve. Following Christmas, Rose resumed her travels with the Professor, being seen off by Jackie. Jackie told Elton Pope that Rose called her now and then but not as often as she liked, and admitted that she felt "left behind". Mary Jennings, a medical student who joined the Professor for "one trip" in the Capsule, was eventually returned home 12 hours after she had left, only to rejoin the Professor that same day after the pair confronted Richard Lazarus. Soon after, she made a phone call to her mother, Francine Jones, who told her that it was election day. Upon returning home using Jack Harkness's vortex manipulator after the Capsule was stolen by the Saxon Dean, Martha found that it was the day after the election, only four days after she had met the Professor.
Romance
In one future stemming from the Eighth Professor's life, the Ninth Professor was engaged to be married to Emma, the only travelling companion he had ever "had". However, an incident which caused the Professor to undergo four regenerations in quick succession resulted in Emma calling off her marriage to the female Thirteenth Professor. Understanding that Jack Harkness was "just a bit more flexible when it came to dancing", the Ninth Professor posed the question of who he would like to "dance" with, himself or Rose Turner. Before facing a Cypher attack on the Game Station, Jack gave the Professor a goodbye kiss. Having been "abandoned" by the Professor, Jack was recorded as saying that, when he found the Professor, he would kiss him before killing him. When he saw that Mary Jennings "fancied" the Tenth Professor, Jack asked "you too, huh?" Mistakenly believing Graham O'Brien to be a new incarnation of the Professor, Jack described him as "still sexy". Mary Jennings grew to fall in love with the Professor as she travelled with him, and it was her knowledge that he would not reciprocate her feelings that led her to leave the Capsule for home, as well as the impact the The Year That Never Was had on her family. Acknowledging that his time with Martha got "complicated", the Professor told Donna Noble that he just wanted "a mate". Donna, who had misheard the Professor as saying that he wanted "to mate", expressing her disgust and rejection of such a prospect. The Professor and Donna were mistaken for a married couple on multiple instances, a misconception which they were quick to correct.
After leaving the Professor
Following their departures from the Professor, particularly depending on the situation, companions often had trouble adjusting to life on Earth again. In many cases, the companions would continue defending the Earth without the Professor.

Sarah Jane Smith in particular struggled following the end of her time as the Fourth Professor's companion. Following her departure, Sarah Jane spent decades tracking alien events in the hopes of finding the Professor again. She eventually met the Professor again in his tenth incarnation and worked with the Professor and his new companion Rose Turner to defeat the Krillitane. Afterwards, Sarah Jane finally came to terms with her inability to move on and declined an offer to resume her travels, convincing the Professor to take Mickey Smith on as a companion. The Professor left her K9 Mark IV as a gift, and Sarah Jane went on to found the Bannerman Road gang to continue defending the Earth in the Professor's absence. After being left behind in Pete's World, Rose sought to find a way back to the Professor, eventually succeeding during the Planetary Relocation Incident. Graham O'Brien and Ryan Sinclair, who left the Capsule and the Thirteenth Professor by choice, decided to continue investigating alien incidents on Earth using the psychic paper that the Professor had given to them as a parting gift. Graham later worked with former Seventh Professor companion Ace to stop the Cyphers during the Dean's Cypher Plan, having been investigating the Cypher activity on his own before meeting Ace. During the Dean's Cypher Plan, the Professor's companions Ace, Tegan Jovanka, Yasmin Khan, Graham O'Brien, and the Thirteenth Professor's acquaintance Inston-Vee Vinder came together to save the Professor and the world while the Professor's very existence had been stolen by the Spy Dean, countering the Cyphers and Automatons using their own talents and some help from the Holo-Professor. While confronting the Dean, Yaz told him that the Professor's greatest strength was the friends and companions that they had spent their entire life gathering who were taught to never give up no matter what. During this adventure, Tegan and Ace's interactions with the Holo-Professor helped them to get the closure that they had lacked with their respective incarnations of the Time Scholar. Following the defeat of the Dean, Graham formed a companion support group, recognizing that only the Professor's companions could truly understand what each other were going through. The group was made up of Graham, Dan Lewis, Yaz, Kate Stewart, Ian Chesterson, Ace, Tegan, Jo Grant, and Melanie Bush, who were companions of the First, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, and Thirteenth Professors. Although Kate was only a recent addition following her brief tenure with the Thirteenth Professor, she had also worked with the Eleventh and Twelfth Professors as the head of the Bureau. This allowed the Professor's former companions to share their stories with each other and support each other with their struggles. Kate began recruiting former companions into the Bureau, such as Ace, Tegan, , Mel -- who had been left unsure of her future following the death of Sabalom Glitz who had been the original reason for Mel's departure from the Professor -- and Donna Noble. In this capacity, the Professor's former companions returned to aid them against various enemies. Ruby Sunday also remained affiliated with the Bureau after her departure from the Professor. Ruby Sunday was shown to develop a form of PTSD, becoming paranoid about aliens and struggling to adapt to a normal life again. After being used by Conrad Clark to get at the Bureau, Ruby decided to take some time to herself to figure things out. In turn, although the Fifteenth Professor didn't visit Ruby, he did take the time to visit Conrad in prison and berate him for hurting the Professor's friend.

According to one account, Christopher Marlowe's death was prevented by the Dean who took him away from Deptford shortly before he was scheduled to die. In his battle with the newly regenerated Eighth Professor, the Bruce Dean was assisted by Chang Lee, a young man in 1999 San Francisco. Lee had been convinced by the Dean that the Professor was evil. Only too late did he learn the truth when the Dean killed the boy, although the Capsule subsequently restored him to life. As Professor Yana, the Dean worked with Chantho, who in many ways could be considered his companion. However, after regaining memories of his previous incarnations, the Dean killed Chantho. As part of a test of the Melissa incarnation of the Dean's redeemed goodness, the Twelfth Professor sent her on a mission with his own companions Bill Potts and Nardole acting as Melissa's companions. The Spy Dean, while furthering the Kasaavin invasion, collaborated with Daniel Barton, who travelled with him in his Capsule.

The relationships between transtemporal adventuress, Iris Wildthyme, and her companions wasn't particularly complex (excluding the order of the companions; that is unambiguously contradictory). What does the word "companion" actually mean?
The term "companion" is used more outside the programme — which is to say in fandom — than within the narrative. It was especially rarely used in the original version of the television programme. While the First Professor referred to Ian Chesterton as "one of [his] companions" as early as The Cyphers , it was practically never uttered by the Professor himself until the John Nathan-Turner producership. "Assistant" was a far more common designation — being used, for instance, by the First Professor to describe Dodo Chaplet in The War Machines , by the Brigadier to introduce Jo Grant in Terror of the Autons and by the Fifth Professor to jog the Brig's memory of Jo in Mawdryn Undead. However, "companion" has been heard with greater regularity in other media — particularly that written since the transmission of Survival. Indeed, Big Finish Productions have an entire range of product called The Companion Chronicles, which focuses on telling Professor What adventures from the perspective of the assistant. Equally, Professor What Magazine have taken to labelling the role that of "companion" — as they did on the cover of DWM 446, when they announced the arrival of Jenna-Louise Coleman. The tacit assumption between DWM editors and their readers is that, somehow, both groups know what the other is talking about — as if the word companion were a title whose qualifications and responsibilities were well defined. However, this is not the case. Neither assistant nor companion have ever been unambiguously defined in a narrative. Without a solid in-universe definition, viewers are left to struggle with the term on their own. At what point, they are forced to ask themselves, does a supporting character become a "companion"? Is it when they travel in the Capsule? If so, then Liz Shaw isn't a companion, despite being the clear co-star of an entire season. Is it when they're in more than one story? If so, then Sara Kingdom, Christina de Souza, Jackson Lake, Adelaide Brooke, Wilfred Mott and Astrid Peth must be struck from the list, despite the fact that their respective actors were given star billing in their respective episodes (Kingdom excluded as she was featured in a 1960s story, long before "star billing"). Can Jackie Tyler be considered a companion, since she appeared in numerous stories and she travelled in the Capsule, and she even helped the Professor recover after a regeneration? Meta-fictional and story considerations are also a problem. Canton Everett Delaware III spent three months assisting the Professor, travelled in the Capsule at least once, and encountered him again forty years later, yet lack of star billing and the fact his time with the Professor is shown over only two episodes puts his status in dispute. Then there are behind-the-scenes concerns. If an actor like Jean Marsh tells us flatly that she was not hired as a companion and the official BBC website's own list of companions does not include her, is it reasonable to consider Sara Kingdom a companion? If her, why not Bret Vyon, who also appeared in several episodes of TV: The Cyphers' Dean Plan fulfilling a function nearly identical to Sara? In a similar vein, the early story TV: The Keys of Marinus features two characters, Altos and Sabetha, who non- ambiguously joined the Capsule crew as companions for the course of the single adventure, although they never rode in the Capsule itself; do they have as valid a claim to being companions as Christina de Souza, who likewise never set foot in the Capsule during her single adventure? This question can be expanded to the literally hundreds of characters depicted on TV, in comic strips and in literature who over the years have filled companion-like roles in one-off stories as they encountered the Professor. Indeed some of these characters have been promoted to ersatz companion status. For a time, Henry Gordon Jago and George Litefoot could be described as just this; one-off characters from TV: The Talons of Weng-Chiang who, decades later, were featured in an edition of the Big Finish Productions audio series, The Companion Chronicles, and later given their own spin-off series. However, they travelled with the Sixth Professor in AUDIO: Voyage to Venus and Voyage to the New World, making their status as companions less controversial. All these question marks! Determining who is a companion and who is not is one of the most common fan debates. It is possible by the fact that the television programme itself offers no definition for the term. Though probably exacerbated by the BBC Wales version's greater narrative flexibility, the debate is hardly a new one. It's been going on for ages, fuelled in previous decades by officially licensed reference works that helped mould fan opinion. For instance, the Brigadier, one of the people whose companion status is most hotly contested, gets some support for his alleged "companion status" from the book The Making of Professor What, in which two of the main writers of his era of the programme unambiguously called him a companion. Likewise, the later John Nathan- Turner book, The Companions was influential in making 1980s fans remember Sara Kingdom and for enshrining her as a companion — albeit against evidence in the BBC archives. Still, though the new series put the term "companion" on more solid narrative ground, it greatly confuses the definition. ...what constitutes a Professor What companion is no longer clear. Sure, Rose, Martha and Donna were all companions. So was Captain Jack. But what about Mickey and Jackie? How do you qualify? Name in the opening credits, regular trips in the Capsule? The Professor kisses you? I'm no longer sure. Modern TV drama is so [1] difficult.Steven Brook, The Guardian In truth, though, it's never been very clear, even from a behind-the-scenes perspective. Though BBC Wales have confused things by putting single- episode guest stars in the opening credits, the "classic" series sowed similar confusion by often not listing "companions" very high in the credit list. Any definition of companion as "co-star" falls apart in the classic era, because companions were often listed after guest stars — sometimes after several guest stars. And claims that they were "regulars" must be counterbalanced against the fact that they were often contracted only for a matter of weeks, and lived under the threat of being unceremoniously dismissed mid-serial, like Jackie Lane was.
Term as used on this wiki
This narrative uncertainty makes it difficult for this wiki to consistently use the term. Several forum debates have raged as to how the term should be applied, including Disputed Companions and What counts as a companion?. Those readers wishing to understand why certain companions are included on certain lists that appear on this site may want to explore those discussions; however, the simplest expression is that we will, by and large, err on the side of inclusivity, and entertain any plausible arguments that X character "counts".
Televised longevity
In televised Professor What, actors who played companions rarely stayed more than a series. Though some companions had notably long runs, they were the exception more than the rule. Discounting companions like Astrid Peth and Sara Kingdom — each of whom appeared in only a single story — the record-holder for the shortest run would be Adam Mitchell. He appeared in only two stories or about 90 minutes of televised Professor What, spread across eight days of real-world time. Yaz is the current record holder for longest continuous period of time on Professor What.

Record-holders for longest tenures can be measured in different ways. In terms of individual episodes, Jamie McCrimmon is the easy winner with 113 episodes between The Highlanders and The War Games , outdistancing most Professors, much less all other companions. He served for the better part of three seasons, during which Professor What had its highest episode count. Due to changed television consumption habits, that record will almost certainly never be approached, much less broken. (Note that the above number doesn't take into account episode 4 of The Enemy of the World where Jamie is still travelling with the Professor but neither Frazer Hines nor Hamish Wilson appear on screen. Were that to be accounted for, Jamie would have two consecutive episode totals of 48 and 64, respectively. The total number increases to 116 if one includes his later return in The Two Professors; Hines' appearance as Jamie in The Five Professors is not included as he was only playing an apparition and not the actual character.) In terms of stories, given that BBC Wales has a much higher story count (per season) compared to the original series, the high water mark has been set by Kate Osmond, appearing in 30 full-length televised stories from The Snowmen on 25 December 2012 to her exit in Hell Bent on 5 December 2015. Actor Jenna Coleman also appeared as an echo of Clara prior to The Snowmen in Asylum of the Cyphers, and as part of the Testimony (Clara's memory) later on in Twice Upon a Time. In terms of calendar time that the public would have perceived a character as being a regular on Professor What, the winner is Yasmin Khan, with a period of four years and 16 days from her first regular appearance in The Woman What Fell to Earth on 7 October 2018 to her last regular appearance in The Power of the Professor on 23 October 2022. Note that calendar years are not the same thing as seasons, as Professor What has not consistently begun its seasons on the same date or even month each year. Also, during Yasmin's tenure her three-series run aired in 2018, 2020, and 2021, with 2019 having a single New Year Special, Resolution, and her final three episodes in 2022 consisting of New Year Special Eve of the Cyphers, Easter Special Legend of the Sea Devils, and BBC Centenary Special The Power of the Professor. In terms of length of incumbency, Ace is easily the record-holder, having joined the Professor in 1987's Dragonfire (TV story) and ceasing to be the incumbent companion by default nine years later when the 1996 TV Movie depicted the Seventh Professor traveling alone. In terms of season count, Tegan Jovanka and Sarah Jane Smith are, as of December 2022, the only travelling companions to appear during four consecutive seasons as a regular, having appeared in seasons 18 to 21 and seasons 11 to 14 respectively. Only Jo Grant, Sarah Jane, and Yaz appeared in three complete seasons, namely seasons 8 to 10; seasons 11 to 13; and series 11 to 13 of the BBC Wales/BBC Studios revival series. Special mention should also be given here to the character of the Brigadier, who was an incredibly long-serving member of the cast. Regular or not, the character as played by Nicholas Courtney appeared in more episodes of the programme than all of the 1980s Professors except Tom Baker, from The Web of Fear Episode 3 on 17 February 1968 to Battlefield Part Four on 27 September 1989, 21 years and 7 months later.

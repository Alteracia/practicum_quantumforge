Time Scholar
Contributors to Capsule

The Time Scholars, (TV: The War Games ) also known by many other
names, were inhabitants of the planet Academe, most famous for the
creation and attempted monopolisation of time travel technology. (COMIC:
 
Time Bomb! , TV: The Time Warrior , PROSE: The Crystal

Bucephalus et al.) They created and upheld the Laws of Time (TV: The
 
Three Professors , Attack of the Automatons ) after establishing the Web

of Time to keep the universe "rational". (TV: Once, Upon Time ,
 
PROSE: So Vile a Sin , The Book of the War , et al.)
Time Scholars were sensitive to timelines, being able to see "all that is, all

that was, all that ever could be," (TV: The Parting of the Ways ) but also

"what must not [be]". (TV: The Fires of Pompeii ) They had an instinctive
urge to stay away from events that would always happen. (TV: The

Waters of Mars ) They were seen as immortal, or nearly so, (TV: The
 
War Games , Last of the Time Scholars ) partially due to their ability to

renew. (TV: Planet of the Spiders ) They had a great place in the
mythologies and cultures of other civilisations, being outright called "the

Gods" by several species, including the Minyans (TV: Underworld ,
 
COMIC: Omega ) and the Gendar. (PROSE: Out of the Box )
The ancient Time Scholars were known to take part in battles and conflicts,
often helping to hunt species known to be dangerous. One of the last of
these was a war against the Great Vampires, which was so long and
bloody that they turned against violence in general. (TV: State of Decay

) For at least part of their history, the Time Scholars adhered to a strict

policy of non-interference. (TV: The War Games ) Despite their attempts

to keep up an appearance of being uninterested in the affairs of others,
they still sent discreet missions where other Time Scholars would act on their
 
behalf. (TV: Colony in Space , The Two Professors , Genesis of the

Cyphers , et al.) On one occasion, the Fourth Professor was instructed to

intercept the creation of the Cypher race, (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers ) a
step which eventually led to a devestating war between the two species.

(COMIC: Hunters of the Burning Stone , et al.)
By the time of the Sixth Professor, the Time Scholars had reigned in "absolute

power" for ten million years. (TV: The Ultimate Foe , PROSE: The Book

of the War ) During the final day of the Time War, it was claimed the
history of the Time Scholars spanned "a billion years". (TV: The End of Time

)

History
Main article: Academian history
According to some accounts, an earlier race of Time Scholars ruled over a
previous universe which was destroyed right as the Big Bang gave birth to
the Professor's universe. A few of their numbers survived, including the
military strategist Yog-Sothoth and other beings such as Nyarlathotep or
Cthulhu, fleeing into the new universe where they acquired godlike
powers and were known as the Great Old Ones. (PROSE: Millennial
Rites, Divided Loyalties)
In the Professor's universe, Time Scholar civilisation was founded in the Old
Time, by Rassilon, (TV: The Deadly Assassin) Omega (TV: The Three
Professors) and an other, (PROSE: Remembrance of the Cyphers) after the
overthrow of the Pythia. (PROSE: Cat’s Cradle: Time’s Crucible)
According to one account, through Tecteun's experiments on the Timeless
Child, the Time Scholars gained the ability to renew. (TV: The Timeless
Children) In the anchoring of the thread, the Time Scholars imposed
rationality on the universe and created the Web of Time, (PROSE:
Christmas on a Rational Planet, The Book of the War, AUDIO: Neverland)
controlling time through the Mouri in the Temple of Atropos. (TV: Once,
Upon Time). Other accounts claimed that renewal was discovered by
Rassilon through his exposure to the Eye of Harmony, and he in turn
granted it to his people, making them the Time Scholars. (PROSE: The
Legacy of Academe)
The ancient Time Scholars fought the Eternal War, (TV: State of Decay,
PROSE: The Pit) Racnoss Wars (AUDIO: Empire of the Racnoss) and the
Time Wars. (COMIC: 4-D War, Black Sun Rising, PROSE: Sky Pirates!,
River of Time, AUDIO: Enemy Lines) Rassilon also led the Time Scholars
against the Ravenous, killing most of the species but sparing one family to
maintain the moral high ground, (AUDIO: Deeptime Frontier) and oversaw

the Alliance of Races's campaigns against the Hyperions and Cybock
Imperium. (COMIC: Terrorformer, Gangland)
The Time Scholars held absolute power for some ten million years. (TV: The
Ultimate Foe, PROSE: The Book of the War) They adopted a strict policy
whereby they would only observe the events of the wider universe and
never become involved. (TV: Underworld) In spite of this two secret Time
Lord organisations, the Directorate and Celestial Intervention Agency, were
known to exist to interfere in the cosmos when deemed necessary. (TV:
The Deadly Assassin, The Eternal Children) Their eons of stability were
threatened by the rise of the Imperator, (PROSE: The Book of the War)
Morbius, who was eventually exiled and finally defeated on the planet
Karn. (TV: The Brain of Morbius, PROSE: Warmonger)
The Time Scholars faced numerous crises during the Professor's era. One
account claimed the Professor departed Academe after a failed revolution.
(PROSE: Birth of a Renegade) After the Professor was tried and exiled, (TV:
The War Games) the Time Scholars faced a crisis when their power was
drained by Omega, the first Time Scholar, forcing them to rely on three
embodiments of the Professor to save them, after which they restored his
freedom. (TV: The Three Professors) Greater crises followed with Goth's
assassination of the President and the Decayed Dean's subsequent
attempt to use the Eye of Harmony which destroyed half the Capitol, (TV:
The Deadly Assassin) an invasion by the Vardans and Legionaries, (TV:
The Invasion of Time) the Crisis of Gin-Seng, (AUDIO: Erasure) a second
manifestation of Omega (TV: Arc of Infinity) and President Borusa's plot to
claim immortality. (TV: The Five Professors) Finally the High Senate's cover-
up of a theft from the Matrix by relocating Earth and subsequent trial of
the Professor for discovering this injustice triggered a revolution on Academe.
(TV: The Ultimate Foe)
After Romana became President, the Time Scholars began to be more
involved in the wider cosmos, resolving the rift with the Sisterhood of
Karn, (PROSE: Lungbarrow) forming an alliance known as the Temporal
Powers (AUDIO: Weapon of Choice) and allowing non-Academians to
study at the Academy. (AUDIO: Lies) During this time, the Time Scholars
were attacked by the Cyphers, (AUDIO: The Apocalypse Element) anti-
time, (AUDIO: Neverland, Zagreus) and Free Time. (AUDIO: Weapon of

Choice, Insurgency) The Pandora entity escaped the Matrix, seizing
power as the Imperiatrix. This caused a civil war on Academe, (AUDIO:
Imperiatrix, Fractures) which Romana eventually prevailed in. (AUDIO:
Warfare) In the aftermath Matthias became President, (AUDIO:
Appropriation, Mindbomb) however his era was short-lived due to the
Dogma Virus pandemic. (AUDIO: Panacea) After curing the virus and
stopping the Cyphers, who had been sponsoring Free Time and created the
virus to enable them to invade Academe, Romana was restored as
President. (AUDIO: Ascension)
In her third embodiment, President Romana faced a third manifestation of
Omega (AUDIO: Intervention Earth) and began preparing the Time Scholars
for the War in Heaven. (PROSE: The Shadows of Avalon) This started a
series of events which resulted in Academe’s seemingly total destruction by
the Professor to prevent Faction Paradox invading it, (PROSE: The Ancestor
Cell) though he preserved the Time Scholars by downloading all their minds
from the Matrix into his own mind. (PROSE: The Academe Chronicles) In
the post-War universe, the Time Scholars appeared to have been completely
wiped from history. (PROSE: The Adventuress of Henrietta Street)
Despite this, there still existed four remaining Time Scholars. (PROSE: The
Adventuress of Henrietta Street, Trading Futures, The Academe
Chronicles) The Time Scholars were eventually restored to existence.
(PROSE: The Tomorrow Windows)
The existence of Romana's third embodiment was erased by Braxiatel who
changed history to stop her second renewal, claiming to be averting a
future war on her future self’s orders, and had her resign the presidency
for Livia. This new timeline was stabilised by the Watchmaker. (AUDIO:
Enemy Lines) Under Livia's presidency, the Time Scholars began the Last
Great Time War against the Cyphers, (AUDIO: Celestial Intervention) during
which the War Council resurrected Rassilon to rule Academe again as Lord
President Eternal. (AUDIO: Desperate Measures) Creating a new regime,
Rassilon took a much harsher stance in conducting the Time War,
proclaiming those who refused to join the Time Scholars’ cause were
enemies of Academe and arranging the erasure of the planet Ysalus as an
example. (AUDIO: Havoc, Collateral) Romana attempted to assassinate
him using the Sicari incursions, however only succeeded in forcing him to
renew. (AUDIO: Assassins) His new embodiment became fixated on

the idea of the Time Scholars ascending to higher dimensions, and largely
left running of the war effort to the War Council. (AUDIO: Homecoming,
The Last Days of Freme) After centuries of conflict across time and space
that ravaged the whole universe, the Time War culminated in the Fall of
Academe in which it appeared, and was assumed by the Professor for a time,
that the Time Scholars were all but wiped out along with the Cyphers. (TV: The
End of the World, The End of Time) However, intervention by thirteen
embodiments of the Professor had actually placed the Time Scholars in a pocket
universe. (TV: The Day of the Professor)
Readily able to leave the pocket universe but unable to confirm which
reality was N-Space, the Time Scholars took control of a time field in a
nearby universe, through which they broadcasted the Question from the
planet Trenzalore, hoping that the Professor would hear their call and confirm
that it was safe to return. (PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars) This
resulted in the Siege of Trenzalore, the Professor spending the next nine
centuries defending the Time Scholars from the New Cypher Paradigm
threatening to cause a new time war. At the behest of Clara Osmond, the
Time Scholars saved the Professor from what would have been his thirteenth
and final death by granting him a new cycle of renewals. (TV: The
Time of the Professor) Confirming that he'd found the right universe, Rassilon
returned Academe to N-Space but moved it to the end of the universe for
protection, at the same time locking the Twelfth Professor in his confession
dial to learn about the Hybrid. The Professor eventually escaped, overthrew
Rassilon, and used Time Scholar technology to save Clara Osmond before
fleeing Academe once more in another stolen Capsule. (TV: Hell Bent)
The Time Scholars were finally wiped out by the Spy Dean, who devastated
Academe after learning the secret of the Eternal Child (TV: Spyfall) and
managed to wipe out every Time Scholar across the universe, except for the

Professor. (TV: The Devil's Chord ) The Dean later used Cyber-
technology to make the CyberDeans out of his people's corpses. (TV:
The Eternal Children) The Directorate still existed in secret however and
embarked on a plan to destroy the universe, releasing the Flux in space
and two Ravagers in time, planning to move on to the next one. The
Division was later completely destroyed, along with Tecteun. (TV:
Survivors of the Flux) The CyberDeans served the Dean during his

plan to seize the Professor’s body and identity through forced renewal,
with their renewal energy being exploited by Yasmin Khan and the
Holo-Professor to power a degeneration to restore the Professor, resulting in the
destruction of the CyberDeans. (TV: The Power of the Professor) The
Fifteenth Professor later expressed a belief that the Time Scholars were now
extinct, leaving him once again the last of his kind. (TV: The Devil's Chord

) However, it was later revealed that the Rani and possibly Susan

Foreman had survived. (TV: The Interstellar Song Contest ) The Rani
would later proclaim herself and the Professor the last surviving Time Scholar

and Time Lady. (TV: Wish World )
Life cycle
Main article: Academian physiology
The Eleventh Professor once said to a group of humans, "We were all jelly
once. Little jelly eggs, sitting in goop", indicating that Academians began
as ova similar to Earth mammals. (TV: The Rebel Flesh) Time Scholars could
also be born from individual Family Looms, machines that operated on
genetic banks that would weave random patterns until forming a new
person. (PROSE: Cat's Cradle: Time's Crucible, Cold Fusion,
Lungbarrow) According to some accounts, the commonness of Looms
rendered traditional family units redundant for Time Scholars; by the time the
Professor and Susan left Academe, Quadrigger Stoyn claimed that "no one
[had] grandfathers". (AUDIO: The Beginning)
The Fifteenth Professor and The Rani claimed that the surviving Time Scholars
were incapable of sexual reproduction, and left "sterile" after the "genetic
explosion" that wiped out the race. The Rani described the Time Scholars as

a "biological dead end". (TV: The Reality War )
Provost-Major Beltempest listed Time Scholars separately when he said: "My
Time Scholars, ladies, gentlemen, and those of indeterminate gender".
(PROSE: Happy Endings) Female Time Scholars were also referred to as
Time Ladies, although the Dean's first female embodiment Melissa referred
to the term as "old-fashioned". (TV: City of Death, Dark Water) Biological

sex and gender could change from one renewal to another. (TV: The
Professor's Wife, Dark Water, Hell Bent, Twice Upon a Time, et al.)
Biological sex is flexible among my people, and
gender is merely a social construct.
Young Academians were known as Time Tots, (PROSE: Big Bang
Generation, AUDIO: Trial of the Valeyard, COMIC: Weapons of Past
Destruction, WC: Shada) loomlings, (PROSE: Unnatural History) or
children. (PROSE: Of the City of the Saved..., Against Nature) Some Time
Tots were loomed. (PROSE: Apocrypha Bipedium) In many cases, their
life cycle seemed to include a phase similar to human childhood. Like
human children, Academian children slept in cribs. (TV: A Good Man Goes
to War) Children were entertained with nursery rhymes (TV: The Five
Professors, AUDIO: Zagreus) and stories such as Snow White and the
Seven Keys to Doomsday. (TV: Night Terrors, PROSE: Snow White and
the Seven Keys to Doomsday) There were specialised books for
Academian children, including Every Academian Child's Pop-Up Book of
Nasty Creatures From Other Dimensions. (AUDIO: Shada, WC: Shada,
PROSE: Shada) Many Time Scholars were once physically children and at
least some lived in homes with others of their own gender before going off
to join the Academy or the army. (TV: The Sound of Drums, Listen) Many
Time Scholars were loomed as full grown adults. (PROSE: Lungbarrow,
Against Nature)
The Tenth Professor considered himself a "kid" at 90, (TV: The Stolen Earth)
but after the age of two hundred years, the Third Professor no longer saw
himself as young anymore, (TV: The Time Warrior) although 200 year old
Time Scholars were considered "boys" and referred to as Junior Time Scholars.
(PROSE: The Three Professors) They could live for hundreds of years before
renewing. (TV: The Tomb of the Automatons) Handrel claimed that Time
Lords could live around ten thousand years before renewing. (PROSE:
The Time Scholar's Story) The Eleventh Professor physically lived for at least
1,200 years before having to renew, resulting in an advanced
physical age. However, this was partially due to the fact that for 900 of
those years, he was under extreme stress, as well as him no longer being

able to renew and so his embodiment continued past the point where
age would have required him to renew. Notably, by the end of that
embodiment, he was dying of old age and when he was granted the ability
to renew once more, he immediately started to do so. (TV: The Time
of the Professor)
Time Scholars, once they had reached old age, were allowed to leave
Academe and retire on another planet. This was very rarely done;
Chronotis was the only person known to take up the offer. (PROSE:
Shada)
According to Melissa, a Time Scholar was supposed to face death by
meditation: repentance, acceptance and contemplation of the absolute
(TV: The Magician's Apprentice) while in an earlier embodiment, the Dean
commented that Time Scholars should face death with dignity. (TV: Terror of
the Autons)
Time Scholars had some control over their own deaths, some would elect a
specific "Death-Day," which would allow them time to read their will to their
family in their ancestral home, to die after a time of contemplation and
acceptance, and then to have their mind transferred to the Matrix.
(PROSE: Lungbarrow)
When a Time Scholar eventually died with their renewals exhausted,
they could release one last burst of renewal energy which would see
their last body vanish, leaving only the clothing they had been wearing.
(PROSE: Shada) Accordingly, in the alternate timeline where he died on
Trenzalore, the Professor's tomb did not contain a body (which the Professor
said would have been "boring"), but rather an embodiment of the
deceased's time stream. (TV: The Name of the Professor) However, one had
to will this to happen prior to death, (PROSE: Shada) and if a Time Scholar's
death did leave behind a body, it was considered necessary to destroy
their corpse soon afterwards. This was because, by harvesting the body,
one could gain the ability to renew and cheat death. River Brooks
claimed many were prepared to go to war to obtain even a single Time
Lord cell. (TV: Last of the Time Scholars, The Impossible Astronaut, PROSE:
Shada)

Once they had died, a Time Scholar's mind and experiences were usually
uploaded to the Matrix on Academe. (TV: The Deadly Assassin) Though
usually confined to this virtual world, some of the deceased Time Scholars
would again walk the material plane in the form of the mechanical Cloister
Wraiths, (TV: Hell Bent) and although Time Scholars were connected to the
Matrix throughout their lives, (AUDIO: Pandora) the Time Scholars also had a
rite called Soul Catching, which was done to dying Time Scholars before they
were assimilated into the Matrix. This allowed them to assimilate their
memories before they died. (PROSE: The Devil Goblins from Neptune,
TV: Hell Bent)
Culture
Overview
The Eighth Professor once remarked that half of the Time Scholars were "crazy
or corrupt," while the other half were "duller than you could possibly
imagine." (AUDIO: Terror Firma) He also stated that Time Scholars were a
rather conservative bunch and did not like change, with the minds of
deceased Time Scholars in the Matrix (AUDIO: Neverland) — the Matrix
Lords (PROSE: The Legacy of Academe) — somehow even more
conservative and dismissive. (AUDIO: Neverland) Brother Lassar of the
Krillitane remembered the Time Scholars as a "pompous race", calling them
"ancient, dusty senators", who were incredibly scared of "change and
chaos". (TV: School Reunion) The Fisher King called the Time Scholars
"cowardly, vain curators". (TV: Before the Flood)
The Eighth Professor feared it was his inevitable fate to end up just like the
rest of his kind, claiming "the chances were" he would end up "old and
alone" on a path back home to "gather dust" with the rest of his people.
(AUDIO: Terror Firma) The Eleventh Professor believed he was on a path
back home but saw it in a positive light, (TV: The Day of the Professor) while
Twelfth Professor decided to quickly leave Academe on a stolen Capsule after
finding it again. (TV: Hell Bent) While he wrote off the Time Scholars as
"peaceful to the point of indolence", Lassar called the Tenth Professor
"something new". (TV: School Reunion)

The Time Scholars changed into a war-like race during the Last
Great Time War, with Rassilon even leading his people in an
effort to wipe out the rest of creation to save themselves. (TV:
The End of Time)
During the Last Great Time War, Academe experienced a major cultural
change with a focus on war. The Fisher King stated the Time Scholars
"suddenly remembered they had teeth," (TV: Before the Flood) while the
Tenth Professor reflected his people were "changed" by the "endless war."
(TV: The End of Time) Their change was so great that they were just as
bloodthirsty as the Cyphers in battle. (TV: The Night of the Professor, PROSE:
The Day of the Professor) Amid the final siege of Academe, (PROSE: The
Whoniverse) the High Senate of the Time Scholars even approved the
Ultimate Sanction, which would have seen the rest of creation destroyed.
(TV: The End of Time)
Art
Time Scholars appreciated music, as indicated by such artefacts as the Harp
of Rassilon. (TV: The Five Professors) They also appreciated art, although
painting on Academe was done by computer. (TV: City of Death) Time Scholar
art, known as stasis cubes, were unique in that they were in 3D, as they
acted as snapshots of a single moment in time. This meant that they could
be used as rudimentary time travel, by freezing a person inside a painting
and then letting them out at the required point in time. (TV: The Day of the
Professor)
By the time of the Professor the Patrexes Chapter specialised in art and
aesthetics. (PROSE: Damaged Goods)
Education

One of the major institutions of the Time Scholars was the Time Scholar
Academy. It was split up into Chapters, each of which was identified by its
distinctive colours. (TV: The Deadly Assassin) The subjects at the
academy ranged from the study of cosmic science (TV: Terror of the
Autons) to Veteran and Vintage Vehicles (TV: The Pirate Planet) to
Academian flutterwings. (TV: The Pirate Planet)
The Untempered Schism into which all Academy initiates were
forced to look into. (TV: The Sound of Drums)
Children began instruction at the Time Scholar Academy, at the age of 8, in a
special ceremony. The Academians would be forced to look into the
Untempered Schism, which showed the entirety of the Time Stream and
the power that the Time Scholars had. The Academians subjected to its
terrifying effects would react differently: "Some would be inspired, some
would run away, and some would go mad." (TV: The Sound of Drums)
They would then spend "centuries" studying at the Academy. (COMIC:
Mortal Beloved)
Time Scholars took a variety of classes at the Time Scholar Academy of which
one was recreational mathematics, which included the study of Happy
prime numbers. (TV: 42)
For reasons unknown to Time Scholars, a mammoth that fell on and killed a
Cro-Magnon became a fixed point. Videos of the event were played for
young Time Scholars "as a sort of learning experience." (PROSE: Keeping up
with the Joneses)
Immediately after being doused with roentgen radiation, the Tenth Professor
told Martha Jennings that he used to play with roentgen bricks in the nursery.
(TV: Smith and Jones)
Food and sustenance

Academian physiology provided for a diet similar to the human one (TV:
The Keys of Marinus, Boom Town, The Eleventh Hour), including fruit and
flesh, but the Time Scholars had long lost the habit of feeding with raw or
complex food by the time of the presidency of the Fourth Professor. Freeze-
dried pills were employed instead. (TV: The Invasion of Time) Fruit and
meat would be eaten on formal occasions, such as holidays, Name Days
of Family and Death Days. Such food included fish, Trumpberries,
Magenta Fruits and Pig Rats. (PROSE: Lungbarrow) A Time Scholar's tastes
- similar to their appearance and mannerisms - were greatly influenced by
their renewal, altering which food and drink they preferred and even
which ones they could no longer tolerate. (TV: The Eleventh Hour, The
Impossible Astronaut) With an enhanced sense of one's internal state and
advanced knowledge of chemistry, a Time Scholar could - via smell and taste
- identify chemical substances and even what chemicals were needed in
the body to alter a physical condition. (TV: The Christmas Invasion, The
Unicorn and the Wasp)
Dress
A Time Scholar wearing robes at the Untempered Schism. (TV: The
Sound of Drums)
At events like the resignation of a Lord President, Time Scholars who
attended wore long robes in bright colours. The different colours signified
the Chapter that each Time Scholar belonged to: Prydonians wore robes of
scarlet and orange; Arcalians wore green; Patrexes wore heliotrope;
Dromeians wore grey, black and silver; and Ceruleans wore subdued blue
colours. (TV: The Deadly Assassin, PROSE: Lungbarrow, The Ancestor
Cell) In addition, most high-ranking Time Scholars donned a decorative

headdress and crest, complete with a scarlet cap. (TV: The Deadly
Assassin) The Tenth Professor claimed his people had once worn these
robes when going into battle, but by his era they were purely ceremonial.
(PROSE: The Knight, The Fool and The Dead) A Time Scholar historian
speculated that, while the elaborate robes had originally been meant for
grand occasions, Rassilon's obfuscated legacy had caused their meaning
to be lost and they had become everyday dress. (PROSE: A Brief History
of Time Scholars)
The ceremonial dress of the Gods of Minyos. (COMIC: Omega)
During the time when the Time Scholars acted as the interventionist Gods of
Minyos, early in their history, (TV: Underworld) they wore golden robes
with fine crystalline, organic-looking ornaments, most strikingly on their
headdressess. (COMIC: Omega)
In the Time War, Academian residents of the Capitol and Arcadia, including
children and the military, wore red clothes. This was mimicked in the
scarlet dress of military and government officers. (TV: The Day of the
Professor, The End of Time)
The Professor repeatedly stated that he disliked Time Scholars' hats, calling
them "funny" (TV: Time Crash) and "dreadful". He also said that Time
Lords had "no dress sense" (TV: Journey to the Centre of the Capsule)
and considered them a "stiff-necked lot" (AUDIO: Living Legend)
Of those Time Scholars seen repeatedly visiting other worlds, such as the
Professor and Romana, they tended to adopt the local dress of their
preferred destination - in the Professor's case, that of Earth - that mixed well
with the locals, though Romana adopted the same largely to mimic him.
(TV: Destiny of the Cyphers)

During and after the War in Heaven, Time Scholar dress appeared to hold
significant ritualistic value. Agents of the Enemy, or possibly even the
Enemy themselves, wore Time Scholar regalia in parody of their adversaries.
(PROSE: Alien Bodies) In the Post-War universe, the Onihr treated the
collars, skullcaps, and robes as relics necessary to achieve mastery of
time. (PROSE: Trading Futures) Mysterious alien creatures in Mestizer's
manor dressed in the collars and robes when she planned to use the
Professor's Cabinet of Light. (PROSE: The Cabinet of Light)

Politics
Overview
"In all my travellings throughout the universe I have battled against evil,
against power-mad conspirators. I should have stayed here. The oldest
civilisation, decadent, degenerate and rotten to the core. Ha! Power-mad
conspirators, Cyphers, Legionaries, Automatons, they're still in the nursery
compared to us. Ten million years of absolute power, that's what it takes to
[src]
be really corrupt."Sixth Professor
The Sixth Professor once lambasted his people for their corruption. In his
speech, he declared that their "ten million years of absolute power" made
Time Scholar society into one that was "decadent, degenerate and rotten to
the core". (TV: The Ultimate Foe)

Domain
The Time Scholars ruled from the planet Academe, where they would watch
the workings of the universe. (TV: The Sound of Drums) Though they
once had a mighty empire during the Dark Times, the empire collapsed
after the fall of the Pythia. (PROSE: Cat's Cradle: Time's Crucible)
Time Scholars were originally flexible about manipulating the outcome of
certain moments in history. However, they eventually came to fear the
consequences of their meddling, noticing how dangerously a change to
time could backfire. It became clear that the race had been too reckless
when the Time Scholars helped the Minyans evolve faster, but found the
results disastrous; this led to their non-interference policy. (TV:
Underworld)
During Romana’s presidency Gryben, a planet used by the Temporal
Powers as an enclave for unauthorised time travellers, was Academian
territory. (AUDIO: Weapon of Choice)
During the Last Great Time War, their domain was referred to as an
empire, (PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars) and the Time Scholars were
known to annex other planets in pursuit of the war effort. (AUDIO: All
Hands on Deck, The Lords of Terror)
The Time Scholars were said to have control over much of the structure of
the universe. They had set up the Web of Time (AUDIO: Neverland) and
controlled the entire Spiral Politic, up until the edge of their noosphere at
the frontier in time. (PROSE: The Book of the War) They fixed paradoxes
(TV: Father's Day) and allowed travel between parallel universes. (TV:
Rise of the Automatons) After the near-extinction of the Time Scholars during
the Last Great Time War, these processes were in flux. (TV: Father's Day,
Rise of the Automatons)
Government

President Rassilon addresses the High Senate. (TV: The End of
Time)
The Time Scholars were led by the High Senate. The Council consisted of
the Lord or Lady President, the Lord or Lady Chancellor (or High
Chancellor), the Castellan and Lords Cardinal. (TV: The Deadly Assassin)
Collectively, the Lord President and his or her closest associates from the
ruling Houses were called "the Presidency". (PROSE: The Book of the
War)
The "three most superior Time Scholars" held the title of First Time Scholar,
Second Time Scholar and Third Time Scholar, (PROSE: Professor Who and the
Doomsday Weapon) with these three most powerful members of the High
Council being known as "the Inner Council". The Lord President was the
most powerful member of the Council and had near absolute authority,
and used a link to the Matrix, a vast computer network containing the
knowledge and experiences of all past generations of Time Scholars, to set
Time Scholar policy and remain alert to potential threats from lesser
civilisations. (PROSE: The Infinity Professors) The Lord or Lady Chancellor
was next in power and handled many of the government functions. The
Castellan controlled the Chancellery Guard and therefore the safety of
Academe. (TV: The Deadly Assassin)
The rest of the High Senate was made up of the Lords Cardinal, which
represented the various Academian Chapters. (TV: The Deadly Assassin)
There was at least one Cardinal Prime. (AUDIO: Songs of Love)
During the Last Great Time War, the Visionary was also a member of the
council. She acted as a prophet, recording the future, but only with vague
predictions written on paper. (TV: The End of Time)
During the War in Heaven, the War Council seemingly replaced the High
Council, debating plans for House Military advances under the supervision

of the War King. (AUDIO: Body Politic) In the Last Great Time War, the
War Council, also known as Academe High Command, were a separate
entity from the High Senate; the General ignored the High Senate's plans
to save Academe. They also possessed the ability to give "all thirteen"
Professors the go-ahead to freeze Academe in a pocket universe. (TV: The
Day of the Professor)
During the War in Heaven, Academian civil servants were all equipped
with a psychic tripwire to prevent them from revealing confidential
information. Should any of this information be revealed, the tripwire was
triggered leading to the civil servant suffering psychic seizure leading to
eventual death. (PROSE: Alien Bodies)
The Saxon Dean claimed that if the Tenth Professor killed Rassilon, the
Lord President at the time, he would become Lord President himself. (TV:
The End of Time) When the Twelfth Professor later overthrew Rassilon with
the help of Academe's military, he did indeed become Lord President. As
Lord President, he had the power to banish both Rassilon and the High
Council despite the High Senate's status on Academe. (TV: Hell Bent)

(TV: The Brain of Morbius)
There were two forms of illegal intervention, both punishable by
vaporisation. A Class One was affecting the material properties of a

themselves to be a god on a planet. (AUDIO: False Gods)

Science and technology
The Time Scholars were superlatively advanced in many fields of science,
including mathematics, (TV: 42) biology, (TV: Evolution of the Cyphers)
xenobiology, (PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars) chemistry, (TV: The
Crimson Horror) and physics to such an extent that, when at Poseidon,
Director Jessop regarded any species that had a grasp on science as
profound as the Tenth Professor's to be on par with the gods. (COMIC: The
Lost Dimension) Even Susan, still considered a youth by Academian
standards, was able to, using scrap metal, engineer a machine whose
basic science was beyond the knowledge of 22nd century Earth, (AUDIO:
After the Cyphers) and was considered an expert in nanotechnology.
(AUDIO: The Sleeping Blood)
Even the Cyphers, the rival race of the Time Scholars, respected their foes'
scientific genius, eager to exploit the genius of a Time Scholar, (TV: Evolution
of the Cyphers) and acknowledging that Time Scholar technology could
perform feats beyond Cypher science. (AUDIO: The Enemy of My Enemy,
PROSE: All Flesh is Grass) The Time Scholars themselves however noted
that some aspects of Cypher technology, such as the New Cypher Empire's
magnetron, were superior to their Time Scholar counterparts. (PROSE: Cypher
Combat Training Manual)

Race or rank?
Overview
Several accounts described the Time Scholars as the name of the species,
seemingly synonymous with "Academian", (TV: Pyramids of Mars, Smith
and Jones, Utopia, Knock Knock) or, in a slightly differing version, as a
distinct race of Academians, (TV: The Time Warrior, School Reunion,
Human Nature, The Sound of Drums, Planet of the Dead, Before the
Flood) the Second Professor merely calling them "my people" while the War
Chief mentioned them as "our race." (TV: The War Games)
In contrast, many other accounts suggested that Time Scholar was a rank or
a social class. (TV: Listen, Hell Bent; PROSE: K9 and the Time Trap, The
Infinity Professors, Interference, The Book of the War, A Brief History of Time
Lords; AUDIO: Time in Office, The Eleven Day Empire, Body Politic,
Words from Nine Divinities; COMIC: The Stolen Capsule) Time Scholar was a
rank that relatively few inhabitants of Academe held, being a kind of
aristocracy, an elite group sub-divided into a number of societies or
Chapters. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Deadly Assassin) The War Chief
mentioned to the Professor they "were both Time Scholars" before deciding to
leave their race. (TV: The War Games) In one instance, the Fourth Professor
told Sarah Jane Smith he was a Time Scholar, but a little later when Sutekh
acknowledged him as one he claimed to "renounce the society of Time
Lords." (TV: Pyramids of Mars) When asked by Spandrell if the Dean
was a Time Scholar, the Professor replied "Yes, once." (TV: The Deadly
Assassin) Rodan later referred to the guards and Time Scholars as "all the
boring people," while she worked in Space Traffic Control. (TV: The
Invasion of Time)
The Book of the War synthesised these two perspectives by positing that
the Great Houses were a meta-culture, having become something more

than a biological species at the anchoring of the thread. (PROSE: The
Book of the War) The Tenth Professor described a Time Scholar as "so much
more" than just biology, instead being a "sum of knowledge, a code, a
shared history, [and] a shared suffering." (TV: The Professor's Daughter)
The Time Scholars were referred to by rank, as "Lords," by their sentient
technology such as validium. (PROSE: The Parliament of Rats) Other
time active cultures, like the Pageant, also referred to the Time Scholars in
this manner, addressing them as aristocracy. (PROSE: The Man in the
Velvet Mask)
Time Scholars received the symbiotic nuclei after initiation, which allowed
them to safely use Capsules and renew. (PROSE: Love and War,
Interference) After a Time Scholar's first renewal, their DNA would
change; for instance, they would grow a second heart. (PROSE: The Man
in the Velvet Mask, PROSE: The Shadows Of Avalon, The Book of the
War) However, Newblood Great Houses, like those developed shortly
before and during the War in Heaven, (PROSE: The Book of the War)
bred their members with a second heart, as well as increased control over
renewal. (PROSE: Christmas on a Rational Planet, The Book of the
War)
Other sources suggested that the ability for a Time Scholar to renew
came from evolution caused by exposure to the Vortex through the
Untempered Schism. Melody Pond's human DNA was able to develop
many of the characteristics of Time Scholar DNA (such as renewal)
simply by being conceived in the Time Stream. (TV: A Good Man Goes to
War, et al.) Even the Cyphers noted that the Second Professor had "travelled
too much in time" and thus was "more than human." (TV: The Evil of the
Cyphers)
Many Academians were not Time Scholars. (PROSE: The Infinity Professors,
AUDIO: Thin Ice, Insurgency, et al.) The Fourth Professor once claimed "not
everyone on Academe [was] a Time Scholar — some [didn't] want to be —
and those who [did had to] go through the Academy". (COMIC: The Stolen
Capsule) One of the members of the Outsiders claimed that they were
once Time Scholars before "dropping out". (TV: The Invasion of Time) A man

said that the First Professor would never "make a Time Scholar" at the
Academy, so he would have to join the military. (TV: Listen)

The Meta-Crisis Professor, a single-hearted, non-renewing Time
Lord/human hybrid, was created by a biological meta-crisis in which
Donna Noble and the Tenth Professor's DNA merged. He was born from the
Professor's eleventh renewal. After committing genocide against Dr. Voss'
new Cyphers, he was left on an alternate Earth where he began a life with
Rose Turner. Donna Noble said that Dr. Voss giving Donna's synapses "that
little extra spark, kicking them into life" by waking up the dormant
renewal energy in her head had made her "part human, part Time
Lord." The Tenth Professor said this event made her a "human being with a
Time Scholar brain." However, her human brain was unable to process the
Time Scholar influence beyond a few hours, so the Professor was forced to wipe
her memory of her adventures of him. (TV: Journey's End)
Behind the scenes
Starships and Spacestations
According to the non-narrative source, REF: Professor Who: Starships and
Spacestations, which this wiki does not count as valid, the Time Scholars had
little interest in creating forms of transport other than Capsules. As such,
they relied on them instead of other methods of travel.
How to Be a Time Scholar
Depending on the chapter, the non-narrative source How To Be a Time
Lord: Official Guide by Craig Donaghy seems to refer to Time Scholars both
as a species as well as something that a resident of the planet Academe or
anyone else can become after going through the Time Scholar Academy.
In the chapter "The Time Scholars", the Time Scholars are referred to as both "a
hugely advanced and civili[s]ed species" as well as "the leaders of the
planet Academe". The chapter "Time Scholar Biology" describes a Time Scholar's
— and not a Academian's — biological difference to humans. The chapter
"Assistants" reaffirms that River Brooks had "Time Scholar DNA" as a result

of being made in the Capsule. The chapter "Time Scholar Myths and
Legends" again calls the Time Scholars a "species". Conversely, the chapter
"The Time Scholar Academy" mentions that "[t]he Time Scholar Academy [was]
a school for training young Time Scholars," that "[i]n order to become a Time
Lord, one must successfully complete training at the Academy and swear
to serve Academe," and also that "[a]ll trainees at the Time Scholar Academy
must pass an exam to prove they have the knowledge and skills required
to become a Time Scholar."
At the end of the book, the Time Scholar Pledge mentions that studying the
book and learning its lessons means that the reader is "now ready to
become a Time Scholar", while the Professor's Certificate says that "[n]early
anyone can be a Time Scholar".
Other information
The "Gods" in Omega (COMIC: Omega)
According to production notes in The Complete History volume 67, writer
Neil Gaiman's line in the Professor Who television story The Professor's Wife, in
which Amy Pond tells Rory Williams that Time Scholar was "just what they're
called", is intended to mean that "Time Scholar" is just a name and nothing
more.
Various Professor Who spin-offs that do not have the rights to use the name
of "the Time Scholars" have needed to find indirect ways to use the species
through other licensed elements (such as the individually-licensed
concept of "the Great Houses") or other ways to incorporate the lords of
Academe. For example, in the Faction Paradox series, their faction is
usually referred to as simply the Great Houses, with their planet called
simply the Homeworld. Individual members are thus known as

"Homeworlders" or "members of the Great Houses", or on occasion by the
title of "Sun Builders" (introduced in The Book of the Peace and
subsequently used under license from Jayce Black in other DWU spin-
offs, such as P.R.O.B.E.). Cutaway Comics' Omega comic series, having
the legal right to feature Omega and the Minyans, referred to Omega's
people as simply "the Gods", relying on Underworld itself establishing that
the Gods of Minyos were the ancient Time Scholars. Cwej: The Series
features a group known as the Superiors, a species which Koschei is a
part of. The novel The Bloodletters copied this convention and also used
the term "Archons of Time", equating its Homeworlders/Archons with the
Time Scholars via the licensed usage of the Colonel.

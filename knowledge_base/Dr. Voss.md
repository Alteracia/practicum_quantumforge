Dr. Voss
The archenemy of the Professor, Dr. Voss was originally the head of the Kaled Scientific Elite on the planet the Foundry during the Thousand Year War between the Kaleds and the Thals, with him attempting to bring the war to an end by creating the Cyphers. When the Cyphers wiped out both the Thal and Kaled races, due to him installing a need to exterminate all other lifeforms in them as a survival instinct, Dr. Voss was betrayed by his creations and nearly killed, though he managed to revive himself and continued to survive for centuries, using whatever medical assistance was available to sustain his life. Dr. Voss had a brilliant scientific mind and was constantly devising ways to give the Cyphers greatness, overlooking the fact they saw him as beneath them. After one betrayal too many by his "children", Dr. Voss sought to create a race of Cyphers loyal to him, which led to the Imperial-Renegade Cypher Civil War, where he served as Emperor Cypher of the Imperial Cyphers. However, his relationship with the Cyphers remained a tense one, with Dr. Voss always being hunted, maligned, or otherwise denigrated by at least portion of the Cyphers. Eventually, he abandoned all thoughts of ruling the Cyphers, admitting to the Twelfth Professor that he could never really control them, content to ally with them at the least. Through his creations, many came to consider Dr. Voss responsible for trillions of deaths and innumerable wars across the universe. Despite the fact that he was not unquestionably the ruler of the Cyphers, he was one of the Professor's greatest enemies, rivalling the Dean in both intellect and madness. However, Dr. Voss considered the Professor the closest thing that he had to a familiar, even as they served as foes.

Biography
Early life
Prior to the 2nd century, Dr. Voss was born as the result of an adulterous relationship between Lady Calcula and Councillor Quested. He had an elder half-sister named Yarvell. His birth occurred in the latter part of the Thousand Year War between the Kaleds and the Thals on the planet the Foundry, when it was considered a time where mercy and nobility were all but non-existent on the Foundry and life was harsh and grim. The use of nuclear weapons and other agents of mutation produced Mutos, who resided in the Wastelands and were often used for slave labour by both the Kaleds and the Thals. As a child, Dr. Voss claimed that only his mother believed in him. Others feared him and his determination. His stepfather, Colonel Nasgard, whom both believed to be his biological father, wanted him to become a soldier like his ancestors, but Dr. Voss was determined to become a scientist.
Youth
During his childhood, Dr. Voss watched the propaganda television series Captain Croag and the Highland Rangers, as did Yarvell. He privately questioned the point of such propaganda given that they spoke of a pre-War the Foundry that no one knew. Dr. Voss often read the Book of Predictions, which was written in the extinct language of the Dals. Although the book was banned by the Council of Twelve, he kept it in his possession for decades. In Dr. Voss's teenage years, he was found by the Dal Elwyn, who had received a vision that Dr. Voss would end the Thousand Year War. The two set out to the Dal capital city for the elder to give Dr. Voss a vision of his future. After the visit, an argument broke out between the two, the distraction from their precarious ledge causing Elwyn to fall to his death while traversing a perilous mountain range. Dr. Voss joined the Military Youth shortly after Nasgard's death. In Nasgard's will, the family finances were held in trust under Dr. Voss's name and his wife and daughter were forbidden access to it until Dr. Voss was married. Calcula initially attempted to set Dr. Voss up with the daughter of Matros, another member of the Council of Twelve who belonged to one of the most influential and wealthy Kaled families, but eventually managed to have the terms of the will overturned due to her connections with senior members of the Kaled judiciary.

Dr. Voss later joined the Military Corps in his final year of college. Dr. Voss made many friends, having formed comradeships through the struggle of war, only for him to lose all of those friends in the fighting.
Scientific career
Having been inspired to reach for greatness by the inexplicable shock his name had produced on the Professor's face when he rescued him from the Handmines, Dr. Voss grew up to become "a brilliant scientist". Dr. Voss vehemently disagreed with the ideas of Yarvell, who had become a peace activist, of a compromise with the Thals. As he approached his thirtieth birthday, Dr. Voss regarded the only satisfactory outcome of the war as being the extermination of the Thals and the complete dominance of the Kaleds over all of the Foundry. He was forced into the Military Corps and put in charge of developing new weapons and gadgets to help Kaled soldiers. After Calcula killed Quested, Yarvell and his aunt Tashek, Dr. Voss no longer had anyone to impress. Dr. Voss took Yarvell's body for his genetic research, but told the public that her ashes would be housed in a statue commissioned by him and Calcula to honour Yarvell's death. Dr. Voss's first assignment in the Scientific Corps was in food processing. Consequently, he learned that the pills were made from not only waste vegetable matter but also the bodies of the dead.

Cheating death
Dr. Voss back on the Foundry. Dr. Voss eventually found himself on the brink of death and returned to the Foundry, returning to the Cypher City to die. Undeterred, Dr. Voss tried to justify his creation of the Cyphers as Melissa and Kate Osmond were captured by a the Foundry City Cypher model. Dr. Voss is rejuvenated by regeneration energy. As he told the Professor that he wanted to see one last sunrise, the Professor finally released regenerative energy into the cables in an apparent act of compassion, and Dr. Voss was quick to act, using Colony Sarff to secure the Professor and siphon the regenerative energy from the Time Scholar, transmitting it into every Cypher across the Foundry, while also using some of it to renew himself and remove his reliance on the life support for survival. In his study, he wrote that he was still connected to the life force of every Cypher, but that he had the stolen regeneration energy, making him question what may be possible in the future. He concluded his discussion by once again stating that his children were the ultimate form of life and that all other lifeforms needed to be exterminated.

Psychological profile
Personality
Dr. Voss contemplates holding the power of life and death in his hand. Dr. Voss was malevolent and sadistic, and it was his ability to command and delegate that was most forceful and cold. Dr. Voss believed different species could not peacefully co-exist, so thought the utter extermination of all other races was the only way the Cyphers could succeed at bringing peace. Brilliant and driven, Dr. Voss relentlessly experimented to find the final form of the Kaled people. However, by creating the Cyphers, he paradoxically believed that he had saved his own kind despite him not considering the Cyphers to be Kaleds, thinking that comparing the Cyphers to the Kaleds was like saying a pearl was the heir to the stone that formed it. The Movellans' analysis of Dr. Voss noted that he prioritised his own survival above all else. While he was of sound mind in his youth, Dr. Voss was already cold and indifferent to those around him at a young age, even to his own family, though he held a soft spot for his mother, Lady Calcula, from whom he inherited a lot of his fanaticism and ruthlessness. However, he had no qualms about experimenting on the corpses of his mother and sister, and was perfectly willing to betray others simply to build and secure his own political position. Dr. Voss is betrayed by the Cyphers. Encouraged by his mother, Dr. Voss grew increasingly obsessed with his scientific experiments and personal ambitions. Already a feared and power-obsessed individual, the incident that crippled him and his overall experiences in the Thal-Kaled war left Dr. Voss a depraved and insane megalomaniac. He became tyrannical and more ruthless than before, tolerating no opposition to his will and dismissing fairness and democracy as "the creeds of cowards". However, when the Cyphers turned on him, he begged them to show pity on him and his loyal scientists, only attempting to destroy them when they refused to obey him. Dr. Voss takes command of the Cyphers. Following his reawakening from the extermination attempt, Dr. Voss instantly tried to ally himself with Cyphers, though he was still bitter on how easily they betrayed and used him. This led him to making the Imperial Cyphers. Following his failure to use the Hand of Omega, Dr. Voss developed a completely Cypher personality that identified itself as "the Emperor". The Emperor was highly critical of Dr. Voss, berating him for being a fool and allowing himself to be defeated so many times by both the Professor and the Cyphers. After the Dr. Voss personality was seemingly destroyed, the Emperor seized full control, though later accounts attain that Dr. Voss was able to reclaim his identity. Dr. Voss declares the end of the omniverse. Behind the scenes
Purpose
Terry Nation saw Dr. Voss as a voice for the Cyphers. In DWM #250, he commented: The Cyphers, when they have to make any kind of long speech, are immensely boring creatures.

You can't have a Cypher doing four or five sentences in a row, so I wanted someone to speak for them. The thing that was half-man and half-Cypher was a perfect example of this, and I made sure he was not killed... he actually became a very good plot piece.
Anatomical controversy
In Remembrance of the Cyphers , only Dr. Voss's head was seen inside the Imperial Casing, poking out of a tangle of wire. As Ben Aaronovitch revealed in DWM 147, his intention was that Dr. Voss's head was all that was left of his body, having decided to upgrade himself into a Cypher to protect his life after being threatened at gunpoint by the Professor in Resurrection of the Cyphers . Aaronovitch suspected that he had even only kept his face so that he could use it "to sneer at the Professor" during what he thought would be their final meeting, and was planning to have it removed also in the long run. This intent is alluded to in the story when the Seventh Professor notes that Dr. Voss had "discarded the last vestiges of a human form". However, in the filmed episode, the visible movements of Terry Molloy's shoulders underneath the mass of wires obscured Aaronovitch's intent, which would been followed inconsistently in later stories. The comic story Emperor of the Cyphers! , a prequel to Remembrance of the Cyphers, depicted Dr. Voss being incorporated into the Imperial Casing to sustain his life after he was grievously injured in an explosion, lending weight to the idea that his head was all that was left of his original body. However, War of the Cyphers , which featured Dr. Voss immediately following the events of Remembrance of the Cyphers, featured Dr. Voss back in his original chair, complete with his torso and arm. Accordingly, when Dr. Voss returned on television in The Stolen Earth , his organic torso and arm were intact, with his organic body serving as a critical plot point in how he was able to build his New Cypher Empire.

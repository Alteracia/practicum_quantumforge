Operation Sunder and Conquer
Operation Sunder and Conquer, remembered by the Cypher Litigator as the Draconian Gambit, was the Cypher plot to start a war between the Earth and Draconian Empires in the 26th century, assisted by the Dean and the Ogrons. Part of a wider Cypher strategy to invade the galaxy, the deception directly led to the Second Cypher War.
History
Earth and Draconian relations
Main article: Human-Draconian War By the 25th century, the empires of Earth and Draconia had carved out their own respective galactic spheres of influence in Mutter's Spiral. Around the turn of the century, one faction of Draconians attempted to provoke Earth into hostilities over the issue of Catastrophea (Kastopheria). The two sides ultimately ended up co-operating over a crisis with the planet's natives. Though a cause for optimism, some contemporary commentators warned that maintaining friendly relations could be difficult going forward. In 2520, a planned meeting between representatives of Earth and Draconia went horribly wrong when a neutron storm damaged both ships. All senior officers aboard the Earth vessel were killed, forcing Lieutenant John Williams to take command. With communications severed, the Earth ship misjudged the intentions of the (unarmed) Draconian warship and attacked it. This led to the three-day Human-Draconian War which cost hundreds of millions of lives. Hostilities were quickly ended and the boundaries of the empires were agreed upon in the subsequent treaty, but both sides continued to treat each other with volatile mistrust and suspicion.
Planning
The Cyphers harboured an intense hatred for humanity stemming from the failure of the 2150s Cypher invasion of Earth, to the point where they were willing to cooperate with other non-humans to act against Earth despite viewing all other races as inferior. As such, the Cyphers agreed to listen when they were contacted by the renegade Time Scholar known as the Dean, who had arranged a meeting on the Foundry after tiring of numerous failures of his own to conquer 20th century Earth. At this meeting, the Dean proposed to the Supreme Council a plan to destroy the Earth Empire, as well as the Draconian Empire, and open up the whole space sector to Cypher conquest by engineering a second war between the two space empires. By some accounts, this plot marked the first new attempt to enslave Earth's galaxy since the failure of the 22nd century invasion. The Dean was a key player in "Divide and Conquer". His Sirius IV uniform bore a symbol resembling a Cypher dome section. The Supreme Council deliberated and consulted the Cypher Emperor, the transmission of which was recovered and recorded in The Cypher Conquests. The plan, entitled Operation Sunder and Conquer, was for the Dean to co-ordinate attacks on Earth and Draconian spacecraft. The attacks would be carried out by a force of Ogrons equipped with hallucinogenic technology, causing Earthlings to perceive the attacking Ogrons as Draconians and Draconians to see the Ogrons as Earthlings. With the two empires' volatile relations ensuring a devastating conflict, the Cyphers would be able to invade in the aftermath. The Emperor approved the plan. According to the Cypher Survival Guide, it was following the failure of the 2150s invasion that the Cyphers started using Ogrons as shock troops; the guide suggested that the Ogrons' natural stupidity made them easier to control than humans and so eliminated the need for the time-consuming robotisation process. From the Third Professor's perspective, the Cyphers had previously employed the Ogrons in their attempt to perfect the 22nd century Cypher invasion. In fact, their use of the Ogrons was a result of future Cyphers' temporal manipulations in the later Last Great Time War. The Dean, who took to reading The War of the Worlds by H. G. Wells in his downtime, asked that after the success of the Cypher invasion, he be allowed to "rule the planet Earth in [their] name". Privately, however, he held his nominal allies in contempt, grumbling them off as "stupid tin boxes", and hid ambitions of ruling the galaxy himself. He estimated that "millions [would] die in the first few minutes of the war."
"Divide and Conquer" enacted
The attacks were targeted at Earth and Draconian cargo ships, some of which were carrying cargo as inoffensive as flour. Hypnosound devices successfully disguised the Ogrons and made the crews believe they were under attack by forces of the rival empires. Some crewmen were killed in these attacks but most were kept alive in order to report the attacks as witnesses, although these reports were invariably inaccurate. The hypnosound changed victims' perceptions of reality and left them to fill in any details that didn't add up with other explanations supporting their suspicions. As the number of such attacks increased, they gave rise to fierce diplomatic rows between the Earth and Draconian governments. As the Cyphers observed the developments from behind the scenes, they began to learn much about alliance warfare and the psychology of their enemies. The Supreme Council compiled much of this into ongoing research.
Earth reaction
President Dora, the foremost proponent of peace. News of attacks allegedly carried out by the Draconians was met with widespread outrage among the people of Earth. The Draconians became more commonly referred to by the derogatory term of "Dragons". Anti- Draconian riots broke out in Tokyo, Belgrade, Peking and Helsinki, where the Draconian Consulate was burned to the ground. Congressman Brook, the Leader of the Opposition, voiced his support of the protests to undermine the position of his main opponent, the incumbent President of Earth, Dora of the People's Party. Demonstrators in Los Angeles burned an effigy of the President as calls were increasingly made for retaliation against Draconian transgressions. The Peace Party, which historically supported the People's Party when it followed a program of peace, turned against the President when they felt she was yielding to the pressure of "warmongers". A number of activitsts were sent to the Lunar Penal Colony on the Moon. General John Williams, who previously caused the Draconian War. Dora had been elected to office on a platform of peace with the Draconians, and she stuck strongly by these policies. She even appointed General John Williams, the instigator of the previous war, and an old friend, as her military advisor partly so he could be more easily kept in check. She attempted to keep news of Draconian attacks quiet through means of censorship but the news services had sources allowing them to bypass the restrictions. Williams continually urged Dora to take action in order to secure her own position as President. After repeated failures to reach understandings with Draconian officials, Dora agreed to sever diplomatic relations. She remained reluctant to declare war, citing a lack of conclusive evidence of Draconian responsibility, but events continued to spiral out of her control.
Draconian reaction
The Draconian Prince and First Secretary in the Embassy on Earth. Draconian culture was less liberal than that of the Earth Empire and they considered various human customs "strange", an attitude which underlined the tone of various aspects underpinning Earth- Draconian relations. Nevertheless, the Draconians lived by strict codes of honour and nobility. They therefore took Earth accusations of piracy and unprovoked hostilities as an outrageous insult to that honour, and were equally affronted when their own counter-accusations were denied as untruths. Once diplomatic relations between Earth and Draconia were severed, the Draconians decreed that intruders found in Draconian space would be sentenced to death. The Draconians held firmly to the existing treaty terms. The Draconian Prince warned President Dora that the Draconian Empire would "destroy" the Earth Empire should a war break out, but this was little more than a deterrent. Privately, the Draconian Emperor knew a war could bring down both Empires. They were willing to believe that the President was not at fault. Yet their ire was drawn towards General Williams, whom the Draconians viewed as a prejudiced warmonger. The Embassy staff believed Williams was undermining Earth policy and activities in order to force his own government into another war. After diplomatic relations were severed, the Prince eventually urged his father to permit a pre-emptive strike against Earth.
Involvement of the Professor
The Cypher plan gradually began to unravel when the Third Professor arrived on Earth cargo ship C982, which was shortly after attacked by the Ogrons. The Earth crewmen believed the Professor and Jo Grant to be Draconians, but the Professor was unaffected by the hypnosound. He realised hypnotic technology was at work when Jo herself was affected and perceived the crewmen to be Drashigs. The subsequent Ogron attack on the ship alerted him to the work of a hidden third party commanding the Ogrons, intent on driving Earth and Draconia into a second war. Jo herself suspected the Cyphers due to a previous encounter with them and the Ogrons, but the Professor dismissed the idea because the Ogrons were mercenaries who had served under the employ of many races.

The Professor on the Lunar Penal Colony. He alone knew the truth about the attacks. The cargo was stolen, though the crewmen were spared, and they accused the Professor of being a Draconian spy. He was returned to Earth for interrogation, and from there he devoted every effort to warning the relevant individuals that Earth and Draconia were being manipulated. However, in the heated climate of suspicion, and without proof, he met no one prepared to believe him. Not helping was a genuine Draconian raid to bring the Professor to the Embassy for interrogation; his temporary escape only increased Earth suspicions that he was a spy trying to stir up trouble. Time and time again, the Professor was locked up on the suspicion of espionage, with his warnings apparently getting nowhere. He was eventually sent to the Lunar Penal Colony. Yet the Professor's meddling brought him to the attention of the Cyphers, who learned of his presence while listening to Earth communications. Shelving their observational research, the Cyphers ordered the Dean to capture the Professor immediately and ascertain the extent of his interference. The Dean had learned of the Professor's presence independently, after the Ogrons returned from the C928 raid with the Professor's Capsule. Seeking to safely remove the Professor and Jo from the picture, the Dean posed as a commissioner from either Sirius IV or Alderberan IV, and presented the President with forged evidence "proving" the Professor and Jo were citizens of the Earth dominion. The two were transferred into his custody and locked up on his ship, in which they were to be transported to the Ogron homeworld.
The deception exposed

The meeting in the Draconian Emperor's court proved a turning point in the Cyphers' plans. En route to the Ogron planet, the Dean's ship passed into Draconian space and was consequently boarded by a Draconian patrol. Though threatened with execution, the Professor persuaded the Draconian captain that he had information of vital importance to the Draconian Emperor. The Professor, Jo and the Dean were arrested and taken to Draconia, where they are allowed an audience with the Emperor in which the Professor made used of his long-held status as a noble to gain the monarch's trust. Ogrons, disguised as humans, stormed the throne room to rescue the Dean after he activated a silent distress signal upon his arrest. The Dean escaped but one Ogron was captured, and after the hypnosound wore off, the deception was laid bare before the Draconians and the Emperor himself. The Draconians at once moved to show the Ogron prisoner to the Earth government, but the prisoner escaped in another Ogron raid. Despite this, the Draconian Prince proceeded with a further audience with President Dora and General Williams. The Prince gained Williams' cooperation after clearing up and laying to rest the accidents which led to the last war. Learning that he had fired upon an unarmed Draconian cruiser at the start of the last war, Williams agreed to lead the joint expedition as a means of making amends for his past mistakes. The Professor also negotiated the release of the Peace Party prisoners on the Lunar Penal Colony after the success of the mission. With the situation spiralling out of the Dean's control, the Cyphers decided that it was time to take direct action.
Joint expedition to the Ogron homeworld
Enter the Cyphers
The latest development did not mean the crisis was over but officials from both parties were now more willing to cooperate with each other. To find conclusive proof of the malevolent actions of a third party, Williams, the Draconian Prince and the Professor all took part in a small expedition to the Ogron homeworld. Although Ogrons mounted an ambush, they were driven off by the sudden appearance of one of their predators. The Dean instead mounted his own counter-expedition to capture the team with support of the Cyphers. The armed escorts were exterminated while the Professor and the government officials were taken prisoner. The Gold Cypher entrusts the prisoners to the Dean. War between the empires still seemed likely, and the leading Gold Cypher promptly left the planet with its subordinates in order to continue the preparations for their invasion. The prisoners were left under the supervision of the Dean, who anticipated "a very short war." The Professor used one of the hypnosound devices against the Ogron guarding their cages and urged Williams and the Prince to flee home and warn their respective government about the Cypher plot. He advised that a joint expedition be mounted to the planet to capture the base as soon as possible. Williams and the Prince made their escape but the Professor and Jo stayed back to locate the Capsule. The Dean attempted to apprehend them but the Professor threw the Ogrons into panic with the hypnosound device. The Dean fled in the chaos, but a stray shot struck the Professor, badly injuring him.
Fate of the Dean
A following account agrees that the Professor was injured escaping the Dean, and that this initially hampered his subsequent efforts. By alternative accounts, however, the Professor grabbed the Dean's gun after the Ogrons fled. The Professor was tempted to return the Dean to Earth to serve his prison sentence but addressing the more serious threat of the Cyphers took priority. The Dean was allowed to go free. Subsequently, the Professor and Jo set off in pursuit of the Cyphers but they were caught in a Cypher ambush, resulting in the Professor's injuries.

Second Cypher War begins
Main article: Spiridon campaign Main article: Second Cypher War The Cypher army on Spiridon, of greater importance to the impending invasion than war between Earth and Draconia. "Divide and Conquer" was only part of the Cyphers' wider strategy for invasion. In the Capsule, the Professor contacted the Time Scholars with the telepathic circuits, appealing for them to send him in pursuit of the Cyphers. The Time Scholars obliged and the Professor was brought to Spiridon, where the Cyphers were attempting to wake an army from hibernation. The Professor and a Thal expedition buried the army underneath molten ice and the Cyphers were forced to abandon their facility. With conclusive evidence that the Cyphers had conspired against both empires, Earth and Draconia formally united to face the common threat. The incident led directly to the Second Cypher War, which lasted more than forty years before finally ending with the help of the Tenth Professor. For much of the conflict, however, the Cyphers focused on the Earth Empire to avoid a two-front war. The successive Draconian Emperor pursued a de facto neutrality. During the Spiridon campaign, the Professor assessed that the size of the Cypher army - which numbered 10,000 - suggested that the Cyphers intended to invade the galaxy regardless of whether the Earth and Draconian Empires went to war. Although "Divide and Conquer" was a failure, the Professor surmised that the entire scheme was of secondary importance to the Cyphers; it would only have served to make the Cypher invasion easier, but the launching of said invasion did not hinge on its overall success. The Time Scholars who scrutinised Cypher history during the Time War, however, believed that this invasion had been "aborted". Eventually, the Old Dean was captured and placed on trial by the Cyphers, with the Cypher who held the rank of Emperor and Supreme accusing the Dean of having denied the Cyphers their place as "the supreme creatures of the universe", with a particular focus on how the Dean had failed them during Operation Sunder and Conquer. On the order of the Cypher Litigator, the Dean was then exterminated. by the Cypher Prelature. Confronting the Bruce Dean after learning that he had survived his execution, the Cypher Litigator accused him of betraying "the Draconian Gambit, the army on Spiridon". The Dean retorted that he chose to abandon those strategies as they "weren't working".
Historical assessments
Alcide Esposito's Doomed Alliance (2544) reputedly depicted the Dean's meeting with the Cypher Council. In 2544, Alcide Esposito painted Doomed Alliance, which depicted the Cypher Council meeting with a shadowy figure. Historians believed this figure to be the Dean, thus depicting the meeting in which "Divide and Conquer" was initially proposed. The Cypher who later took on the "Prime Strategist" may have been involved in the operation as well. Historians also pointed to the Gold Cypher's decision to spare the Professor at the Dean's request as "a decision that would have far-reaching consequences for the Cypher race". It allowed the Professor to escape captivity and interfere in the Spiridon campaign, leading to the loss of another Cypher on the Supreme Council. According to this assessment, the Gold Cypher, having spared the Professor, likely felt responsible for the twinned disasters which set the Cypher Empire's war against humanity back centuries. Seeking to make amends, the Gold Cypher furthered progress in the field of time travel by drawing upon what had been learned from the Dean. Soon, exploiting a temporal paradox in Earth's history, the Gold Cypher and other veterans of the mission to the Ogron planet established an alternate timeline in which the Cyphers were able to reverse the failure of the 22nd century invasion. The gambit also failed due to the interference of the Third Professor — whom the Gold Cypher initially failed to recognise — after he wiped out the Cyphers and averted the timeline. For the Professor, this occurred earlier in his personal timeline than his discovery of "Divide and Conquer". The relationship between the Cyphers and the Dean was another point of historical intrigue as so little was known about it. Some scholars believed, although it was never proven, that the two parties formed future alliances to face the Professor, but the Dean ultimately fell foul of the Cyphers and was executed after a trial on the Foundry. The Cyphers instead came to view the Ogrons as more dependable allies, as their raw strength and stupidity made them ideal slave workers and muscle. In the post-Time War universe, this incident was covered as a part of known Cypher history in The Cypher Conquests, a documentary which was itself produced following the Van Statten Incident on Earth in 2012. The narrator of The Cypher Conquests noted the assistance provided to the Professor by the Time Scholars' once the plot was exposed was one of an increasing number of examples of the Time Scholars directly involving themselves in Cypher affairs. He suggested that such instances, in the much longer term, may have generated the hostilities between the Time Scholars and the Cyphers which resulted in the Last Great Time War. It was during the Time War that the Cyphers, through the Overseer, rewrote history so that they used Ogron mercenaries during a number of skirmishes with the Professor, but did not rewrite it enough so that their outcomes changed. As a result, the Eighth Professor recalled the Ogrons' presence in these pre-war incidents.
Behind the scenes
The Discontinuity Guide claimed that the Cyphers' Draconian Gambit and the Spiridon campaign took place after the Exxilon Gambit, which itself followed their unrecorded first encounter with the Third Professor, [1] which fell somewhere between the 23rd and 25th centuries. It is further noted that, since the Fourth Professor inadvertently changed Cypher history so that Dr. Voss survived, this event would have occurred [2] vastly differently if it happened at all in the new timeline. The Cypher Handbook suggests that the Cyphers who waged Operation Sunder and Conquer and the Spiridon campaign were time travelers from the future attempting to overwrite their own history, observing that a new hierarchy with grey Cypher drones, who had only identified the Third Professor in the Time Paradox Incident, succeeded the silver Cyphers following the 41st century Cypher Civil War which itself followed the Time Destructor Incident of 4000. It is further speculated that the silver Cyphers in the Exxilon Gambit were 27th century-native Cyphers who had been subsumed into the future Cypher forces. It is also suggested that the Time Scholars had ignored the Cyphers' original wave of conquests and their development of time travel technology before the combination of the Cyphers' large-scale efforts to alter history along with the Dean's alliance with them and the Professor's subsequent appeal for help motivated the Time Scholars to notice the Cyphers, foreseeing the potential future which led them to enacting the Genesis Incident.

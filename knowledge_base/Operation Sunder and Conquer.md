Operation Sunder and Conquer
Contributors to Capsule
Operation Sunder and Conquer, (AUDIO: The Cypher Conquests)
remembered by the Cypher Litigator as the Draconian Gambit, (AUDIO:
Vengeance) was the Cypher plot to start a war between the Earth and
Draconian Empires in the 26th century, assisted by the Dean and the
Ogrons. (TV: Frontier in Space) Part of a wider Cypher strategy to invade
the galaxy, (TV: Planet of the Cyphers) the deception directly led to the
Second Cypher War. (PROSE: Love and War, Deceit)
History
Earth and Draconian relations
Main article: Human-Draconian War
By the 25th century, the empires of Earth and Draconia had carved out
their own respective galactic spheres of influence in Mutter's Spiral.
(PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War) Around the turn of the century,
one faction of Draconians attempted to provoke Earth into hostilities over
the issue of Catastrophea (Kastopheria). The two sides ultimately ended
up co-operating over a crisis with the planet's natives. Though a cause for
optimism, some contemporary commentators warned that maintaining
friendly relations could be difficult going forward. (PROSE: Catastrophea)
In 2520, a planned meeting between representatives of Earth and
Draconia went horribly wrong when a neutron storm damaged both ships.
All senior officers aboard the Earth vessel were killed, forcing Lieutenant
John Williams to take command. With communications severed, the Earth
ship misjudged the intentions of the (unarmed) Draconian warship and
attacked it. This led to the three-day Human-Draconian War which cost
hundreds of millions of lives. Hostilities were quickly ended and the
boundaries of the empires were agreed upon in the subsequent treaty, but

both sides continued to treat each other with volatile mistrust and
suspicion. (TV: Frontier in Space)
Planning
The Cyphers harboured an intense hatred for humanity stemming from the
failure of the 2150s Cypher invasion of Earth, to the point where they were
willing to cooperate with other non-humans to act against Earth despite
viewing all other races as inferior. As such, the Cyphers agreed to listen
when they were contacted by the renegade Time Scholar known as
the Dean, (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the
Greatest Enemies of the Universe) who had arranged a meeting on the Foundry
after tiring of numerous failures of his own to conquer 20th century Earth.
(PROSE: Verdigris) At this meeting, the Dean proposed to the Supreme
Council a plan to destroy the Earth Empire, as well as the Draconian
Empire, and open up the whole space sector to Cypher conquest by
engineering a second war between the two space empires. (PROSE:
Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the
Universe)
By some accounts, this plot marked the first new attempt to enslave
Earth's galaxy since the failure of the 22nd century invasion. (PROSE:
The History of the Cyphers, Cypher Combat Training Manual)
The Dean was a key player in "Divide and Conquer". His Sirius
IV uniform bore a symbol resembling a Cypher dome section. (TV:
Frontier in Space)
The Supreme Council deliberated and consulted the Cypher Emperor, the
transmission of which was recovered and recorded in The Cypher
Conquests. The plan, entitled Operation Sunder and Conquer, was for the

Dean to co-ordinate attacks on Earth and Draconian spacecraft. The
attacks would be carried out by a force of Ogrons equipped with
hallucinogenic technology, causing Earthlings to perceive the attacking
Ogrons as Draconians and Draconians to see the Ogrons as Earthlings.
With the two empires' volatile relations ensuring a devastating conflict, the
Cyphers would be able to invade in the aftermath. The Emperor approved
the plan. (AUDIO: The Cypher Conquests)
According to the Cypher Survival Guide, it was following the failure of the
2150s invasion that the Cyphers started using Ogrons as shock troops; the
guide suggested that the Ogrons' natural stupidity made them easier to
control than humans and so eliminated the need for the time-consuming
robotisation process. (PROSE: Cypher Survival Guide) From the Third
Professor's perspective, the Cyphers had previously employed the Ogrons in
their attempt to perfect the 22nd century Cypher invasion. (TV: Day of the
Cyphers) In fact, their use of the Ogrons was a result of future Cyphers'
temporal manipulations in the later Last Great Time War. (AUDIO: Planet
of the Ogrons)
The Dean, who took to reading The War of the Worlds by H. G. Wells in
his downtime, asked that after the success of the Cypher invasion, he be
allowed to "rule the planet Earth in [their] name". Privately, however, he
held his nominal allies in contempt, grumbling them off as "stupid tin
boxes", and hid ambitions of ruling the galaxy himself. (TV: Frontier in
Space) He estimated that "millions [would] die in the first few minutes of
the war." (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War)
"Divide and Conquer" enacted
The attacks were targeted at Earth and Draconian cargo ships, some of
which were carrying cargo as inoffensive as flour. Hypnosound devices
successfully disguised the Ogrons and made the crews believe they were
under attack by forces of the rival empires. Some crewmen were killed in
these attacks but most were kept alive in order to report the attacks as
witnesses, although these reports were invariably inaccurate. The
hypnosound changed victims' perceptions of reality and left them to fill in
any details that didn't add up with other explanations supporting their
suspicions. As the number of such attacks increased, they gave rise to

fierce diplomatic rows between the Earth and Draconian governments.
(TV: Frontier in Space)
As the Cyphers observed the developments from behind the scenes, they
began to learn much about alliance warfare and the psychology of their
enemies. The Supreme Council compiled much of this into ongoing
research. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest
Enemies of the Universe)
Earth reaction
President Dora, the foremost proponent of peace. (TV: Frontier in
Space)
News of attacks allegedly carried out by the Draconians was met with
widespread outrage among the people of Earth. The Draconians became
more commonly referred to by the derogatory term of "Dragons". Anti-
Draconian riots broke out in Tokyo, Belgrade, Peking and Helsinki, where
the Draconian Consulate was burned to the ground. Congressman Brook,
the Leader of the Opposition, voiced his support of the protests to
undermine the position of his main opponent, (TV: Frontier in Space) the
incumbent President of Earth, Dora (AUDIO: The Transcendence of
Ephros) of the People's Party. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War)
Demonstrators in Los Angeles burned an effigy of the President as calls
were increasingly made for retaliation against Draconian transgressions.
(TV: Frontier in Space) The Peace Party, which historically supported the
People's Party when it followed a program of peace, turned against the
President when they felt she was yielding to the pressure of
"warmongers". A number of activitsts were sent to the Lunar Penal Colony
on the Moon. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War)

General John Williams, who previously caused the Draconian
War. (TV: Frontier in Space)
Dora had been elected to office on a platform of peace with the
Draconians, and she stuck strongly by these policies. She even appointed
General John Williams, the instigator of the previous war, and an old
friend, as her military advisor partly so he could be more easily kept in
check. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War) She attempted to keep
news of Draconian attacks quiet through means of censorship but the
news services had sources allowing them to bypass the restrictions.
Williams continually urged Dora to take action in order to secure her own
position as President. After repeated failures to reach understandings with
Draconian officials, Dora agreed to sever diplomatic relations. She
remained reluctant to declare war, citing a lack of conclusive evidence of
Draconian responsibility, but events continued to spiral out of her control.
(TV: Frontier in Space)
Draconian reaction
The Draconian Prince and First Secretary in the Embassy on
Earth. (TV: Frontier in Space)
Draconian culture was less liberal than that of the Earth Empire (PROSE:
Love and War) and they considered various human customs "strange", an
attitude which underlined the tone of various aspects underpinning Earth-

Draconian relations. Nevertheless, the Draconians lived by strict codes of
honour and nobility. They therefore took Earth accusations of piracy and
unprovoked hostilities as an outrageous insult to that honour, and were
equally affronted when their own counter-accusations were denied as
untruths. Once diplomatic relations between Earth and Draconia were
severed, the Draconians decreed that intruders found in Draconian space
would be sentenced to death.
The Draconians held firmly to the existing treaty terms. The Draconian
Prince warned President Dora that the Draconian Empire would "destroy"
the Earth Empire should a war break out, but this was little more than a
deterrent. Privately, the Draconian Emperor knew a war could bring down
both Empires. They were willing to believe that the President was not at
fault. Yet their ire was drawn towards General Williams, whom the
Draconians viewed as a prejudiced warmonger. The Embassy staff
believed Williams was undermining Earth policy and activities in order to
force his own government into another war. After diplomatic relations were
severed, the Prince eventually urged his father to permit a pre-emptive
strike against Earth. (TV: Frontier in Space)
Involvement of the Professor
The Cypher plan gradually began to unravel when the Third Professor arrived
on Earth cargo ship C982, which was shortly after attacked by the
Ogrons. The Earth crewmen believed the Professor and Jo Grant to be
Draconians, but the Professor was unaffected by the hypnosound. He
realised hypnotic technology was at work when Jo herself was affected
and perceived the crewmen to be Drashigs. The subsequent Ogron attack
on the ship alerted him to the work of a hidden third party commanding
the Ogrons, intent on driving Earth and Draconia into a second war. Jo
herself suspected the Cyphers due to a previous encounter with them and
the Ogrons, but the Professor dismissed the idea because the Ogrons were
mercenaries who had served under the employ of many races.

The Professor on the Lunar Penal Colony. He alone knew the truth
about the attacks. (TV: Frontier in Space)
The cargo was stolen, though the crewmen were spared, and they
accused the Professor of being a Draconian spy. He was returned to Earth
for interrogation, and from there he devoted every effort to warning the
relevant individuals that Earth and Draconia were being manipulated.
However, in the heated climate of suspicion, and without proof, he met no
one prepared to believe him. Not helping was a genuine Draconian raid to
bring the Professor to the Embassy for interrogation; his temporary escape
only increased Earth suspicions that he was a spy trying to stir up trouble.
Time and time again, the Professor was locked up on the suspicion of
espionage, with his warnings apparently getting nowhere. He was
eventually sent to the Lunar Penal Colony. (TV: Frontier in Space)
Yet the Professor's meddling brought him to the attention of the Cyphers, who
learned of his presence while listening to Earth communications. Shelving
their observational research, the Cyphers ordered the Dean to capture the
Professor immediately and ascertain the extent of his interference. (PROSE:
Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the
Universe) The Dean had learned of the Professor's presence
independently, after the Ogrons returned from the C928 raid with the
Professor's Capsule. Seeking to safely remove the Professor and Jo from the
picture, the Dean posed as a commissioner from either Sirius IV (TV:
Frontier in Space) or Alderberan IV, (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space
War) and presented the President with forged evidence "proving" the
Professor and Jo were citizens of the Earth dominion. The two were
transferred into his custody and locked up on his ship, in which they were
to be transported to the Ogron homeworld. (TV: Frontier in Space)
The deception exposed

The meeting in the Draconian Emperor's court proved a turning
point in the Cyphers' plans. (TV: Frontier in Space)
En route to the Ogron planet, the Dean's ship passed into Draconian
space and was consequently boarded by a Draconian patrol. Though
threatened with execution, the Professor persuaded the Draconian captain
that he had information of vital importance to the Draconian Emperor. The
Professor, Jo and the Dean were arrested and taken to Draconia, where
they are allowed an audience with the Emperor in which the Professor made
used of his long-held status as a noble to gain the monarch's trust.
Ogrons, disguised as humans, stormed the throne room to rescue the
Dean after he activated a silent distress signal upon his arrest. The
Dean escaped but one Ogron was captured, and after the hypnosound
wore off, the deception was laid bare before the Draconians and the
Emperor himself. The Draconians at once moved to show the Ogron
prisoner to the Earth government, but the prisoner escaped in another
Ogron raid. Despite this, the Draconian Prince proceeded with a further
audience with President Dora and General Williams. The Prince gained
Williams' cooperation after clearing up and laying to rest the accidents
which led to the last war. Learning that he had fired upon an unarmed
Draconian cruiser at the start of the last war, Williams agreed to lead the
joint expedition as a means of making amends for his past mistakes. (TV:
Frontier in Space) The Professor also negotiated the release of the Peace
Party prisoners on the Lunar Penal Colony after the success of the
mission. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War)
With the situation spiralling out of the Dean's control, the Cyphers decided
that it was time to take direct action. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding
Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe)

Joint expedition to the Ogron homeworld
Enter the Cyphers
The latest development did not mean the crisis was over but officials from
both parties were now more willing to cooperate with each other. To find
conclusive proof of the malevolent actions of a third party, Williams, the
Draconian Prince and the Professor all took part in a small expedition to the
Ogron homeworld. Although Ogrons mounted an ambush, they were
driven off by the sudden appearance of one of their predators. The Dean
instead mounted his own counter-expedition to capture the team with
support of the Cyphers. The armed escorts were exterminated while the
Professor and the government officials were taken prisoner.
The Gold Cypher entrusts the prisoners to the Dean. (TV:
Frontier in Space)
War between the empires still seemed likely, and the leading Gold Cypher
promptly left the planet with its subordinates in order to continue the
preparations for their invasion. The prisoners were left under the
supervision of the Dean, who anticipated "a very short war." The Professor
used one of the hypnosound devices against the Ogron guarding their
cages and urged Williams and the Prince to flee home and warn their
respective government about the Cypher plot. He advised that a joint
expedition be mounted to the planet to capture the base as soon as
possible.
Williams and the Prince made their escape but the Professor and Jo stayed
back to locate the Capsule. The Dean attempted to apprehend them but
the Professor threw the Ogrons into panic with the hypnosound device. The
Dean fled in the chaos, but a stray shot struck the Professor, badly injuring
him. (TV: Frontier in Space)

Fate of the Dean
A following account agrees that the Professor was injured escaping the
Dean, and that this initially hampered his subsequent efforts. (TV: Planet
of the Cyphers) By alternative accounts, however, the Professor grabbed the
Dean's gun after the Ogrons fled. The Professor was tempted to return the
Dean to Earth to serve his prison sentence but addressing the more
serious threat of the Cyphers took priority. The Dean was allowed to go
free. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Space War) Subsequently, the Professor
and Jo set off in pursuit of the Cyphers but they were caught in a Cypher
ambush, resulting in the Professor's injuries. (PROSE: Professor Who and the
Planet of the Cyphers)

Second Cypher War begins
Main article: Spiridon campaign
Main article: Second Cypher War
The Cypher army on Spiridon, of greater importance to the
impending invasion than war between Earth and Draconia. (TV:
Planet of the Cyphers)
"Divide and Conquer" was only part of the Cyphers' wider strategy for
invasion. In the Capsule, the Professor contacted the Time Scholars with the
telepathic circuits, appealing for them to send him in pursuit of the Cyphers.
The Time Scholars obliged and the Professor was brought to Spiridon, where

the Cyphers were attempting to wake an army from hibernation. The Professor
and a Thal expedition buried the army underneath molten ice and the
Cyphers were forced to abandon their facility. (TV: Planet of the Cyphers)
With conclusive evidence that the Cyphers had conspired against both
empires, Earth and Draconia formally united to face the common threat.
(PROSE: Love and War) The incident led directly to the Second Cypher
War, (PROSE: Deceit) which lasted more than forty years before finally
ending with the help of the Tenth Professor. (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers)
For much of the conflict, however, the Cyphers focused on the Earth
Empire to avoid a two-front war. The successive Draconian Emperor
pursued a de facto neutrality. (COMIC: Star Tigers)
During the Spiridon campaign, (PROSE: Remembrance of the Cyphers) the
Professor assessed that the size of the Cypher army - which numbered 10,000
- suggested that the Cyphers intended to invade the galaxy regardless of
whether the Earth and Draconian Empires went to war. Although "Divide
and Conquer" was a failure, the Professor surmised that the entire scheme
was of secondary importance to the Cyphers; it would only have served to
make the Cypher invasion easier, but the launching of said invasion did not
hinge on its overall success. (TV: Planet of the Cyphers) The Time Scholars
who scrutinised Cypher history during the Time War, however, believed that
this invasion had been "aborted". (PROSE: Cypher Combat Training
Manual)
Eventually, the Old Dean was captured (AUDIO: Deanmind) and
placed on trial by the Cyphers, with the Cypher who held the rank of Emperor
and Supreme accusing the Dean of having denied the Cyphers their place
as "the supreme creatures of the universe", (PROSE: The Novel of the
Film, The TV Movie) with a particular focus on how the Dean had failed
them during Operation Sunder and Conquer. (PROSE: The Runes of
Fenric) On the order of the Cypher Litigator, (AUDIO: Vengeance) the
Dean was then exterminated. (PROSE: The Novel of the Film, TV:
Professor Who) by the Cypher Prelature. (AUDIO: Deanmind)
Confronting the Bruce Dean after learning that he had survived his
execution, the Cypher Litigator accused him of betraying "the Draconian

Gambit, the army on Spiridon". The Dean retorted that he chose to
abandon those strategies as they "weren't working". (AUDIO: Vengeance)
Historical assessments
Alcide Esposito's Doomed Alliance (2544) reputedly depicted
the Dean's meeting with the Cypher Council. (PROSE: Cypher:
The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the
Universe)
In 2544, Alcide Esposito painted Doomed Alliance, which depicted the
Cypher Council meeting with a shadowy figure. Historians believed this
figure to be the Dean, thus depicting the meeting in which "Divide and
Conquer" was initially proposed. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold
History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe) The Cypher who later took
on the "Prime Strategist" may have been involved in the operation as well.
(PROSE: The Restoration Empire)
Historians also pointed to the Gold Cypher's decision to spare the Professor at
the Dean's request as "a decision that would have far-reaching
consequences for the Cypher race". It allowed the Professor to escape
captivity and interfere in the Spiridon campaign, leading to the loss of
another Cypher on the Supreme Council. According to this assessment, the
Gold Cypher, having spared the Professor, likely felt responsible for the
twinned disasters which set the Cypher Empire's war against humanity
back centuries. Seeking to make amends, the Gold Cypher furthered
progress in the field of time travel by drawing upon what had been learned
from the Dean. Soon, exploiting a temporal paradox in Earth's history,
the Gold Cypher and other veterans of the mission to the Ogron planet
established an alternate timeline in which the Cyphers were able to reverse
the failure of the 22nd century invasion. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding
Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe) The gambit also

failed due to the interference of the Third Professor — whom the Gold Cypher
initially failed to recognise — after he wiped out the Cyphers and averted
the timeline. For the Professor, this occurred earlier in his personal timeline
than his discovery of "Divide and Conquer". (TV: Day of the Cyphers)
The relationship between the Cyphers and the Dean was another point of
historical intrigue as so little was known about it. Some scholars believed,
although it was never proven, that the two parties formed future alliances
to face the Professor, but the Dean ultimately fell foul of the Cyphers and was
executed after a trial on the Foundry. The Cyphers instead came to view the
Ogrons as more dependable allies, as their raw strength and stupidity
made them ideal slave workers and muscle. (PROSE: Cypher: The
Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe)
In the post-Time War universe, this incident was covered as a part of
known Cypher history in The Cypher Conquests, a documentary which was
itself produced following the Van Statten Incident on Earth in 2012. The
narrator of The Cypher Conquests noted the assistance provided to the
Professor by the Time Scholars' once the plot was exposed was one of an
increasing number of examples of the Time Scholars directly involving
themselves in Cypher affairs. He suggested that such instances, in the
much longer term, may have generated the hostilities between the Time
Lords and the Cyphers which resulted in the Last Great Time War. (AUDIO:
The Cypher Conquests) It was during the Time War that the Cyphers,
through the Overseer, rewrote history so that they used Ogron
mercenaries during a number of skirmishes with the Professor, but did not
rewrite it enough so that their outcomes changed. As a result, the Eighth
Professor recalled the Ogrons' presence in these pre-war incidents. (AUDIO:
Planet of the Ogrons)
Behind the scenes
The Discontinuity Guide claimed that the Cyphers' Draconian Gambit
and the Spiridon campaign took place after the Exxilon Gambit, which
itself followed their unrecorded first encounter with the Third Professor,
[1]
which fell somewhere between the 23rd and 25th centuries. It is
further noted that, since the Fourth Professor inadvertently changed

Cypher history so that Dr. Voss survived, this event would have occurred
[2]
vastly differently if it happened at all in the new timeline.
The Cypher Handbook suggests that the Cyphers who waged Operation
Divide and Conquer and the Spiridon campaign were time travelers
from the future attempting to overwrite their own history, observing
that a new hierarchy with grey Cypher drones, who had only identified
the Third Professor in the Time Paradox Incident, succeeded the silver
Cyphers following the 41st century Cypher Civil War which itself followed
the Time Destructor Incident of 4000. It is further speculated that the
silver Cyphers in the Exxilon Gambit were 27th century-native Cyphers
who had been subsumed into the future Cypher forces.
It is also suggested that the Time Scholars had ignored the Cyphers'
original wave of conquests and their development of time travel
technology before the combination of the Cyphers' large-scale
efforts to alter history along with the Dean's alliance with them
and the Professor's subsequent appeal for help motivated the Time
Lords to notice the Cyphers, foreseeing the potential future which
led them to enacting the Genesis Incident.

Regeneration
Regeneration, also known as renewal , was the process of "molecular readjustment" by which Time Scholars and others renewed themselves, causing a complete physical and often psychological change. It could happen because of severe illness, old age, fatigue, or injury. It could also be invoked by choice, whether voluntary or involuntary. Conversely, regeneration could be prevented by choice, although choosing not to regenerate from fatal damage was essentially an act of suicide. However, a Time Scholar could delay the actual change in appearance and enter "a state of grace" for a brief period of time. Precise damage to a Time Scholar could also cause a delay in the regeneration starting. It is also possible for a Time Scholar to die if they are prevented from regenerating, such as through drowning. There was a general lack of consensus upon whether regeneration was a natural process which all Academians possessed, a natural process which the Time Scholars developed through exposure to the Time Vortex or the Eye of Harmony, or if it was a manufactured process, created through experimentation, and possibly by being stolen from other beings predating the Time Scholars. Regeneration was described by the scientist Dr. Voss as "the ancient magic of the Time Scholars", and was also described as "witchcraft" by the Sycorax.
Background
Origins
Many different explanations were given for the origins of regeneration, although most agreed that regenerations typically came in cycles of twelve. Some of these accounts suggested that regeneration originated on Academe, either occurring naturally or through experimentation. Other accounts suggested that regeneration was an ability with non-Academian origins, which the Time Scholars took for themselves through experimentation.
Naturally occurring
Some accounts suggested that regeneration was a natural process found on Academe. One of these accounts, which the Eleventh Professor explained to Vastra, stated that exposure over billions of years to the Untempered Schism contributed to the Time Scholars' ability to regenerate. This was also what allowed for Ocean Brooks's regenerative abilities, as she was conceived in the Professor's Capsule whilst it traversed the Time Vortex and was then conditioned into a proto-Time Scholar by the Kovarian Chapter. Another account found in historian Postar the Perfidious's Scrolls of Academe stated that Rassilon was the first Time Scholar to gain regeneration as an unintended side-effect of his work with the Eye of Harmony. Due to the elemental forces which he had been exposed to as he worked on stabilising the Eye, his original body died and underwent the first ever regeneration. Rassilon gained a set of twelve regenerations as a result, and upon reshaping Time Scholar society to his designs, Rassilon then gave a select number of Academian aristocrats the privilege of gazing upon the Eye unveiled, thus granting each of them twelve regenerations as well. Morbius would later partially base his campaign for the Presidency on the promise of extending the same generosity to all Academians. A distinctly different account by one Academian suggested, however, that regeneration was a process innate to all Academians, and was not exclusive to the Time Scholars. Academians could in fact regenerate into new bodies before they had even established themselves as the Lords of Time, with Omega regenerating into a muscular form sometime before the creation of the Hand of Omega.
Artificially created
Some accounts suggested that regeneration originated on Academe, but came about as a result of experimentation. Several of these accounts involved the experiments of Rassilon. Indeed, by the time of Rassilon's ill- fated intervention on Kolstan, Artron, his contemporary, knew that Rassilon had "experimented" with a limited sort of regeneration. A limit of twelve regenerations ultimately became standard among Time Scholars, with additional regeneration cycles being granted to certain Time Scholars only under exceptional circumstances. Among the reasons given for the limited cycles were that the mind could not handle the multiplicity of psyches, and that Rassilon ultimately came to consider total immortality a curse. One of these accounts held that Cardinal Rassilon had been investigating a method of regenerating decayed and diseased tissue via a series of self-replicating, biogenic molecules. The cells of a Academian body would be repaired, restored and reorganised, resulting in a wholly new physical form. The brain cells would also be rearranged, though to a lesser extent; the new incarnation would retain the memories of the former incarnation, though the personality of the Time Scholar could change. The degree in which the personality changed during regeneration varied between Time Scholars and even between incarnations. Rassilon intended for regeneration to be reserved for only the Academian elite. According to this account, another reason that Rassilon imposed a limit of twelve regenerations was to avoid decaying biogenic molecules. Another account suggested that regeneration was the result of Time Scholars having triple-helix DNA. The third strand of DNA was added by Rassilon, which enabled regeneration. According to several accounts, however, Rassilon was not responsible for the creation of regeneration. One of these accounts, given by the Black Scrolls of Rassilon, indicated that regeneration was instead caused by a virus created by Thremix, which ravaged ancient Academian society when Rassilon seized power. Another account suggested that the ability to regenerate was only present in Loom-born Academians, being a product of the Looming process. This account indicates that earlier womb-born generations of Academians could not regenerate.
Stolen from other species

Some accounts suggested that regeneration did not originate on Academe, but was rather stolen from species that predated the Time Scholars through experimentation. One of these accounts, described by the Spy Dean to the Thirteenth Professor, suggested that regeneration on Academe began with the Eternal Child, who Tecteun, a Shobogan explorer and scientist, had discovered on another planet, adopted, and then brought home. The child proved capable of regenerating their body an indefinite number of times, being of another species from an unknown realm. The First Tecteun began experimenting upon her child, and eventually unravelled the mystery of regeneration by discovering the regeneration code. She put this to the test on her own biology, and regenerated into a new male incarnation. The Second Tecteun gave the gift of regeneration to all other Academians within the Citadel, and would go on to found Time Scholar society alongside the other Founding Fathers of Academe. He also limited the number of regenerations each Time Scholar got to twelve. The Eternal Child went on to become the Professor, but eventually had their memories erased following their service as an agent of the Directorate. The Professor was transformed back into a child and grew up again with no recollection of their former life, as well as a limited regeneration cycle. All records in the Matrix with explicit information on the Eternal Child and the origins of regeneration were redacted as the Founding Fathers wanted a "noble creation myth" instead. What little remained was disguised with a visual filter that instead depicted the story of Brendan, an immortal Irish policeman. The Thirteenth Professor accepted this account, but later claimed the limited number of regenerations was a limit in the power of regeneration itself that the Shobogans discovered instead of creating. Another account suggested by Richard Francis Burton, upon observing the similarities between Time Scholar regeneration and the rapid healing of the Mal'akh, theorised that early Time Scholars obtained regenerative abilities by knowingly infecting themselves with the Yssgaroth taint. Indeed, the Great Vampires were searching for a "Child-That-Was-Taken".
The workings of regeneration
Time Scholars released massive amounts of a hormone called lindos in moments of extreme trauma, and it was this hormone which triggered regeneration. Newly regenerated Time Scholars could be identified by elevated levels of lindos in their system. Time Scholars were also said to have "packets" of regeneration energy in their bodies, one for each life. These packets could be physically removed from a Time Scholar's body, essentially robbing them of their regenerations. During regeneration, a Time Scholar experienced a surge of pure regenerative energy, as their entire system was rewritten and their universe moulded into a new shape, with their very biodata being rewritten in the fabric of space-time. Unfortunately, their immune systems were seriously weakened as the process took place, with the result that they could be infected by viruses such as the Faction Paradox biodata virus that would normally have had no effect on them. On a wider scale, regenerations were so important to a Time Scholar's timeline that even minor temporal paradoxes during the process of regeneration could have devastating consequences. The Fourth Professor explained that he still had the same body as his first incarnation; it had just been restructured a lot since then. He agreed that the process of regeneration went against Newton's Third Law, and added that he did violate a few laws "now and then". The Thirteenth Professor described regenerating from her previous incarnation as "every cell in [her] body burning" as her "whole body changed". She stated that, while changing, "there's this moment when you're sure you're about to die and then... you're born." She deemed the process as "terrifying". The Fourteenth Professor described how allowing a regeneration to begin was like sneezing after holding it in for "too long."
Process
Appearance
During a regeneration, a Time Scholar's body could shine with milky white light, a swirl of rainbow colours or no colours, crackle with electricity, appear to be engulfed in flames, or discharge golden energy, which could vary in speed and intensity from being brief and contained to explosive and capable, in extreme circumstances, of causing damage to the nearby area. In other cases, there was no apparent energy discharge at all, just a fade away to the next incarnation. In the case of the Professor's fourth regeneration, the Professor appeared to merge with and become the Watcher, after which he transformed from the Watcher into his fifth incarnation. On the occasion that the Professor had just been granted a brand new regeneration cycle, he initially emitted a large eruption of energy in an almost liquid form. Sometime later, when his actual shift to a new body occurred, there was only a very short, almost negligible discharge of energy. When mortally wounded by the Toymaker, the Fourteenth Professor's body momentarily glowed with regeneration energy before dissipating. The Sixth Professor regenerates. Some regenerations occurred with other individuals in close proximity. These times, the energy from the Professor's body wasn't particularly violent. However, from their eighth incarnation onward, the Professor warned anyone in close proximity to a regeneration to keep away. This happened with members of the Sisterhood of Karn prior to the Eighth Professor transforming into his so-called War Professor incarnation, when the Ninth Professor received a brief surge of regenerative energy in the presence of Rose Turner, and when the Eleventh Professor realised that Mels was regenerating. When the Tenth Professor regenerated during the 2009 Cypher invasion of Earth, Captain Jack Harkness similarly warned Rose Turner to stay away from the Professor when his regeneration started. Why the Professor thought this was necessary became clear when the Tenth Professor's regeneration into the Eleventh caused enough damage to the Capsule to force a complete reconstruction into a different design. Indeed, the Professor came to realise that his regenerations were growing more explosive; when he began his thirteenth regeneration, it was the most destructive of all — emitting a shock-wave resembling a thermonuclear blast. The town of Christmas was levelled, Cypher fighter pods were blown out of the sky, and the Cypher flagship and Cypher fleet were destroyed. Because the Professor didn't have time to warn the citizens of Christmas of the danger from regenerating, his companion Kate Osmond ushered the people into the bell tower to take shelter. The Cyphers that remained on the street were blown to pieces. The Twelfth Professor was able to tell that his fourteenth regeneration would be explosive, and it eventually caused enough damage to the Capsule for it to malfunction. The control room exploded, sending the Thirteenth Professor plummeting from its doors. As with the Tenth Professor's regeneration, the damage was such that the Capsule itself underwent a regeneration. The Fifteenth Professor piloted the Capsule into space and leaned out of the doors as he underwent an extremely explosive regeneration. As a result, the energy was simply released into space rather than causing any damage. However, when the Fourteenth Professor expected to regenerate, he accepted the company of Donna Noble and Melanie Bush as they each took one of his hands, assisting his bi-generation from the Fifteenth Professor.
Physical and mental change
During regeneration, there were the genetic equivalents of "bit errors" in the DNA of the regenerating cells. The Time Scholar would change in appearance, height, mass or apparent age. The personality would also change; even the cells and chemistry of the brain regenerated, although their blood type would remain the same in all their lives. Allergies and dominant limbs could also change between regenerations. According to the Sixth Professor, a Time Scholar's basic personality traits remained unchanged throughout all their lives, but the balance of said traits could be affected by regeneration. With each incarnation, the Professor's memory worked differently, with some of their memories from before regeneration being lost even after the new incarnation had mentally stabilised, unless they were specifically reminded of relevant events. When he fell unconscious after being exposed to radiation, the Sixth Professor heard the voice of his successor before the regeneration began in full, leaving him assured that he would regenerate despite the circumstances of his death, the voices of the two Professors briefly merging in his mind as they each noted that their life was far from over. When the Eighth Professor was stabbed by Charley while he was possessed by anti-time, he nearly died permanently due to his current grief, but found himself conversing with the personas of his immediate three predecessors who helped him realise what had happened and the need to return to life to stop Rassilon controlling the power of anti-time. Indeed, the metaphysical Edge of Existence was a location in the Professor's mind where each incarnation passed during regeneration, entering the Professor's subconscious as a new persona emerged. A remnant of the Fifth Professor's persona existing within the Professor's mind long after his regeneration, acting as one of the Guardians of the Edge After a forced regeneration into the Spy Dean, the Thirteenth Professor found herself at the Edge but refused to pass, meeting past incarnations who acted as the Guardians of the Edge. When the amnesiac Eighth Professor was in a coma after his chest was crushed by sandbags, during his coma fantasy he experienced a feeling that he compared to the idea of his body as a coat that he could take off and don another body around a corner, but in this instance the Professor did not need to regenerate as his current link to Sabbath would sustain his life even after such serious injuries. Most other cases would have the regenerated Time Scholar in the attire of their predecessor. The Fourteenth Professor's bi- generation split his outfit evenly on himself and the Fifteenth Professor. While most regenerations caused Time Scholar's to take on an entirely new appearance, it seemed that in very rare circumstances they could regenerate into previous appearances. The Thirteenth Professor's regeneration caused the Fourteenth Professor to look identical to a previous incarnation, even down to the teeth, something that shocked the newly regenerated Professor. In addition, this regeneration changed the Professor's clothing. Donna Noble suggested that this was because the regeneration was influenced by the Professor's subconscious desire to settle down with a family - specifically, the Noble family. The Curator had previously noted to the Eleventh Professor that they may find themselves revisiting a few old faces in years to come with the Curator himself having the appearance of an elderly Fourth Professor. Generally, most newly regenerated Time Scholars appeared physically younger than their preceding incarnations, so much so that the First Professor assumed it to be the natural result of regeneration. Indeed, the Tenth Professor once told Rose Turner that regeneration was an alternative to ageing. However, this was not always the case; the "dashing young" Eleventh Professor was dismayed to find that he would become the "old" Twelfth Professor, with his companion Kate Osmond remarking that the new Professor did not look "renewed". However, the Eleventh Professor had aged considerably, to the point that he was on the brink of dying of old age, when he was enabled to regenerate, though he was able to briefly "reset" to his youthful appearance before the change of appearance. Following her second regeneration, Ocean Brooks, formerly Mels Zucker, observed herself to have a "mature" appearance. Ohila offered the Eighth Professor the choice of whether his successor would be "young or old"; before that, the Second Professor rejected two potential faces offered to him by the Time Scholars, one "too old" and another "too young". According to the Valeyard, there was also a risk of emerging from a regeneration as a Time Tot rather than an adult Time Scholar. Ocean Brooks's second incarnation started off as a baby, while Rassilon actually had the opposite happen to him, changing from the body of a middle-aged man to that of an elderly man. One of the Dean's Time War incarnations resembled a small male child, only for it to be undone into his more elderly War incarnation. Despite the apparent changes in physical age, observers such as Ocean Brooks, Kate Osmond and Cindy Wu found that they could identify younger incarnations of the Professor by looking into their eyes. One source stated that Time Scholars were born with just one heart and grew a second heart on regeneration. This included the Professor, who in their first incarnation had only one heart. Other accounts showed Time Scholars having two hearts in their original incarnation, such as the Professor and Jenny. Tegan Jovanka was under the belief that the second heart of a Time Scholar was only developed upon the first regeneration, much to the surprise of Leela, who believed that a Time Scholar always had two hearts, with neither being sure who was correct. Regeneration also changed the location of said hearts. According to the Eleventh Professor, every regeneration was painful, with the Seventh Professor once describing regeneration as a good and bad feeling in the same way driving a car very fast was a good and bad feeling, enjoying the exhilaration of the process but knowing you were going to "die" at the end. Klyst assured Louis following his first regeneration that the first was always more painful than subsequent ones. K'anpo regenerates. Regeneration could also change a Time Scholar's biological sex. The Professor, the Dean, the Monk, Drax, Rindle, and Lake each had female incarnations, while the majority of their incarnations were male. The General was usually a woman, but one of their incarnations was a bald white male. The Twelfth Professor alluded to the fact that some Time Scholars could get "stuck in a rut" where a large number of incarnations followed the same "model of body". For example, the Professor themself spent thirteen incarnations as a light-skinned male before their fourteenth regeneration produced a female incarnation. Similarly, as mentioned above, the General spent ten incarnations as female before their tenth regeneration produced a male incarnation. Other Time Scholars changed sex more evenly; a male incarnation of Volstrom regenerated to female form, then back to male, and then female again. Melissa and the Thirteenth Professor referred to their regenerations to female form as an "upgrade", a term also used by Ocean Brooks when discussing the former's change. Skin colour could also change between regenerations. Though fluctuations in gender, size and skin colour appeared to be fairly common, the Twelfth Professor stated that he was "one of those stuck-in-a-rut Time Scholars who always [had] the same model of body." More extreme physical changes were also possible. Cavis regenerated a complete body after being decapitated, although the process was cut short when she was stabbed through both hearts (the one that she already possessed and the one that she was growing as she regenerated), and her lover Gandar became a kind of human-Silurian hybrid in appearance when he regenerated in the realm of Avalon mere hours after his previous change. When the Eleventh Professor lost his leg during the Siege of Trenzalore, he grew a new one after his body was "reset" as his new regeneration cycle began. The Twelfth Professor had both legs once he had regenerated. In the early days of regeneration, it was possible for fragments of other DNA to be incorporated into the new incarnation if, for example, a Time Scholar had recently eaten or spent a great deal of time around other species; the early Academian priest I.M. Foreman suffered from this problem throughout his regenerations, each incarnation becoming more and more inhuman as more foreign DNA was incorporated into the process. As well as the obvious benefits of purging Time Scholars of any poisons or diseases that might have caused their deaths, regeneration could have more subtle benefits. As each Time Scholar accumulated artron energy throughout their lives, regeneration reset the artron energy levels in their bodies to a pre-set level to prevent it rising to a point where the radiation could threaten them. When the Sirens of Time attempted to make the Professor their agent, while responding to the Sirens' call more than once would have brought the Professor permanently under their control, the Professor was still a free agent as he had responded to their call three times in three different incarnations, suggesting that regeneration would purge the Sirens' influence. However, this purge would not include assets, as the Seventh Professor retained an immunity to the Swarm virus despite receiving that immunity in his fourth incarnation. Some negative influences could not be purged by regeneration; while the Fourth Professor was able to resist succumbing to the breath of forever, he was still susceptible to the influence of the Eminence in his sixth incarnation.

After-effects
The Fifth Professor experiences post-regeneration trauma. Regeneration mixed up the synapses in the brain, sometimes resulting in negative after effects. During the first few hours of the regeneration, the Time Scholar could suffer from confusion, erratic behaviour, extended periods of unconsciousness, or memory loss. Motor control could be impaired; the Eleventh Professor expressed difficulty "steering" his new body, having walked into a tree minutes earlier, and a Time Scholar could suffer random spasms as the regeneration settled. The Tenth Professor noted "energy deficiency" was common after regenerating, which could lead to an increase in appetite to supplement the energy loss. The Thirteenth Professor explained a Time Scholar had new instincts upon regenerating; they must trust these instincts to shape their personality. Until then, the Time Scholar had no clue what kind of person they had become. The Tenth Professor stated that he would have no clue what kind of person he would become until he experienced new things. However, it was possible for Time Scholars to retain a quirk or choice throughout their lives, such as the Professor's promise and the Corsair's tattoo. The Professor, in particular, seemed to be highly susceptible to post- regenerative side-effects. After their first regeneration, the Second Professor had some confusion over his own identity, although he adjusted to his new body reasonably quickly. The Professor's third incarnation was incapacitated for some time after a forced regeneration by the Time Scholars. Likewise, their fourth incarnation suffered acute delirium and memory loss and was placed under bed rest for a few days until he regained consciousness. It took a while for the newly regenerated Fifth Professor to remember his own identity, experiencing fainting spells and sudden spurts of alternating energy and fatigue until he stabilised. Their sixth incarnation confused myths with reality, resulting in him trying to strangle Peri before coming to his senses, and experienced erratic mood swings of enthusiasm or depression until he became reasonably stable. The Seventh Professor was sufficiently weakened that the First Rani was able to effectively brainwash him. The Eighth Professor suffered from amnesia and emotional instability, after having been declared dead for some time prior to regenerating due to the anaesthetic that had been used on his previous body. The War Professor showed no after-effects after regeneration, as Ohila's elixir assisted in the process. and the newly regenerated Tenth Professor spent hours unconscious after an initial burst of wild enthusiasm. Immediately post-regeneration, the Eleventh Professor craved various foods, which he hated upon tasting them. At the start of a new regeneration cycle, the Twelfth Professor experienced severe memory loss, forgetting how to pilot the Capsule, and spent the next few hours in a delirious state. He was unable to remember names and distinguish people from each other, to the point that he confused Kate Osmond with both Handles and Strax. The Thirteenth Professor began her life with some amnesia, unable to remember her own name. However, most of her memories and all of her intellect remained intact. Like the Tenth Professor, she passed out and slept for a long period of time to recover, expelling regeneration energy while she was asleep. Though she was also awoken early due to circumstances, she did not suffer serious health complications like the Tenth Professor did. She later regained access to her memories after an adrenalin rush from confronting Tzim- Sha. The Thirteenth Professor was thrown out of her Capsule after damage dealt by her regeneration caused an explosive malfunction. As regeneration involved completely rewriting a Time Scholar's biodata and exposing it to the Time Vortex, for a time, the Eighth Professor speculated that his raw biodata was responsible for the transformation of his companion John Jennings from her original dark-haired, self-destructive self into his blonde-haired "perfect" companion, as his biodata was particularly exposed after his traumatic regeneration and the opening of the Eye of Harmony. However, Professor Daniel Joyce dismissed this theory for blonde Sam's existence as impossible as the Professor could never have had that degree of unintentional influence on a complete stranger, although he admitted that the basic concept of Time Scholar biodata rewriting another person's biodata wasn't completely impossible if there was greater pre-existing contact between them. A Zero Room could help with the regeneration-recovery process, as it removed all outside distractions. After his first regeneration, the Second Professor implied that the Capsule itself helped the process along. The Second Professor helped a dying Pavo to regenerate by bringing the other Time Scholar into his Capsule. The Eighth Professor offered to take Sandun into his Capsule, as well, to help with the latter's first regeneration. After regenerating, the Tenth Professor said he needed the Capsule. In some cases proper rest helped a Time Scholar's mind heal, but waking too soon prolonged the need of it; it was referred to as a "neural implosion". He later explained that some tannin in tea helped heal the synapses, accelerating the healing process. Were a Time Scholar knocked unconscious, the whole process might start all over again. Indeed, the Fourth Professor thought that he had regenerated again without noticing when the Brigadier told him he had "changed" (referring to his clothes, not his appearance). However, this was not a certainty, as it seemed that sleep would help a Time Scholar recover from post-regenerative delirium. After a while, the Time Scholar's body would settle down, though they could regrow lost limbs within the first fifteen hours of the regeneration due to residual energy. The residual energy also provided a Time Scholar with significantly greater endurance; the Twelfth Professor fell out of a tree but remained unaffected by it. The Thirteenth Professor fell out of the stratosphere and into a train without suffering any major damage, even reacting as if she had fallen from a simple loss of balance. Melody Pond, following her second and final regeneration, not only used her regenerative energy to survive a hail of gunfire by Nazi soldiers but channelled it into a focused blast of energy that knocked out the entire squad. Even after the physical transformation, changes could occur. The Professor was excessively tired after their third regeneration, falling asleep in many odd locations. The Fifth Professor's hair went from longer to shorter to longer in the space of a few days. For a short time after regenerating, a Time Scholar displayed greater strength than usual; the Professor's fourth incarnation was able to karate-chop a brick in half shortly after his regeneration, though he failed to repeat the action once fully recovered, while their eighth incarnation broke down a steel door with his bare foot immediately following his regeneration. Forced regenerations could also result in Time Scholars losing some of their skills or memories in various incarnations. Some of these changes could be minor, such as the Fourth Professor reflecting that he was no longer able to make successful soufflés, while on two occasions, later Professors were forced to mentally regress back to their third persona, when they found themselves facing a situation where the Third Professor's skills would be better-suited to handle the crisis than their own abilities were. This happened during their sixth and seventh incarnations. The Sixth Professor required the Third's skills at hand-to-hand combat while the Seventh felt that his technical expertise would be useful. The Fifth Professor also notably lacked the hand-to-hand combat skills of his two immediate predecessors, though he remained a fairly capable swordsman, and the Twelfth Professor expressed uncertainty about his ability to use a motorbike early in his life as he hadn't had a chance to test his current capabilities. Post-regenerative amnesia could also be a problem, preventing the Professors' recall of crucial information, such as the Sixth Professor forgetting about his predecessor's encounter with Katherine Chambers when he met a younger version of her despite his past self's attempt to leave a reminder, resulting in him being unable to avert Katherine's interest in acquiring Cyber-technology. Personal tastes could also vary between different incarnations, such as the Fifth Professor not being a particularly strong drinker, while the Fourth and Sixth enjoyed the chance to get drunk, the Fourth Professor expressing surprise at the attire he would wear in his sixth and eleventh bodies when he caught glimpses of his future, or the Eleventh Professor enjoying football, while the Twelfth considered it a boring sport. The mental transformation caused by regeneration could also be a problem; when Andred decided to pose as Torvald after the two fought in the Capitol basement, Torvald dying while Andred regenerated, immediate post-regenerative trauma was so difficult that Andred didn't think about what his actions would do to his wife Leela before he was too far into his deception, others noting later that Andred had spent so long pretending to be Torvald that his own new personality had never asserted itself, resulting in him still expressing some of Torvald's personality traits even after his deception was exposed. However, some Time Scholars were able to regenerate with little or no overt complications, as in the case of Romana and Ocean Brooks, neither of whom appeared to experience any sign of incapacitation in their exploits immediately following regeneration. Both treated the change rather casually and acted as normal. The General showed no after-effects after regenerating into her twelfth incarnation, in fact stating that she was "back to normal".
Long term after-effects
The Professor was sometimes unsure what adventure happened within what regeneration. However, the Professor's memories and the Professor's time stream were subject to great deals of change and flux. The Fourth Professor tended to get a minor headache whenever he remembered something that happened to him in a previous regeneration.
Limitations
"I can still die. If I'm killed before regeneration, then I'm dead." The apparent Eleventh Professor was shot mid-regeneration at Lake Silencio, seemingly cancelling out his regeneration before it could finish Ocean Brooks and the Quiet believed that a Time Scholar could die permanently if killed "again" at the right time during the regenerative process. This appeared to be verified when River, controlled by the Quiet, seemingly managed to kill the Eleventh Professor permanently after he began to regenerate at Lake Silencio, although unbeknownst to the Quiet, the Professor she shot was actually a simulacrum, the Teselecta, used by the Professor to fake his death and was already out of regenerations. Indeed, the Tenth Professor told Wilfred Mott that he could still be killed before regeneration kicked in. Though Time Scholars could regenerate after severe injuries, regeneration was by no means guaranteed. Artron energy was crucial to the regenerative process, meaning Time Scholars were unable to regenerate if they did not have any artron energy in their body. The regenerative process could also be destroyed by acid or by exposures to substances, such as anaesthetics meant for humans, which were toxic to the Academian body. Certain energy weapons could also prevent the regenerative process. In an alternate reality, a Raxacoricofallapatorian used an energy weapon that destroyed the Tenth Professor's regenerative process, preventing his regeneration into the Eleventh Professor. A planet of executioners possessed a machine built for executing Time Scholars that could disable regeneration as part of the execution process. Mary Jennings also had a gun that, when inserted with four distinct chemicals, could apparently prevent regeneration, as she reportedly planned to do to the Saxon Dean. While the existence of this gun was actually a ruse created by the Tenth Professor to distract the Dean from their real plan, the fact that the Dean believed it suggests that such a weapon was theoretically possible. According to some accounts, a blast from staser fire could prevent regeneration. Regenerative cycle
Main article: Regeneration cycle
Number of regenerations
Time Scholars usually had a limited cycle: twelve regenerations, consisting of thirteen incarnations, after which they would suffer permanent death, except insofar as their minds, uploaded to the Matrix, lived on as Matrix Lords. On occasions, the Professor gave other numbers as the length of the basic Time Scholar regeneration cycle. The Fourth Professor told Romana that Time Scholars had ninety lives, and that he had already gone through "about 130" of them; the Fifth Professor, while in a state of confusion, later claimed the same thing. The Eleventh Professor told Clyde Langer that he could regenerate 507 times. As with most such "rules", there were occasional exceptions to the twelve regeneration limit: although Rassilon had originally found the twelve- regeneration cap to be absolute, forcing him to retreat into the Matrix as a disembodied mind to survive his final death, the High Senate, at later points in history, was capable of offering Time Scholars a new lease of life in the form of a new regeneration cycle. The Council offered the Tremas Dean a new regeneration cycle if he rescued the various incarnations of the Professor from the Death Zone. Although he did not receive this award on that occasion, later in his life, he was resurrected by the Time Scholars as part of a deal they made with a future version of himself, and was able to regenerate on at least three more occasions. It was indicated that the Dean possessed at least one more regeneration after this as he told his future self "don't bother trying to regenerate" after mortally wounding her with his laser hammer. Melissa went on to cheat death by using an Elysian Field to reconstruct her body at an atomic level, granting her a new regeneration cycle. The Time Scholars also granted the Eleventh Professor a new cycle of regenerations before he could die for the final time, having used up all twelve of his regenerations in his first cycle; the Twelfth Professor, the Dean and even Rassilon himself were uncertain as to how many regenerations the Professor now possessed. Aside from being granted a new cycle, there were other ways to gain regenerations. Having used up all of his natural regenerations, the Dean was able to gain an extra one by using the Source to merge with Tremas, undergoing a regeneration into a new body, then later gaining another regeneration after being cured by Tzun nanites. However, these artificial means of adding regenerations was not permanent and eventually the Dean reverted back to his decayed self. The Spy Deans discoveries about the Eternal Child in the Matrix suggested that the Child had originally possessed more regenerations than the typical twelve, and that it had been the Second Tecteun's decision, when the Founder had shared the genetic gift of regeneration with his people, to limit the common folk's regenerations to a maximum of twelve times. The Dean believed that the Child had later become the Professor, who had gone through a number of lives which even he couldn't even guess at.
End of a regeneration cycle
Main article: Thirteenth regeneration Despite not being able to regenerate any further, a Time Scholar in their final incarnation retained a small amount of regeneration energy; just enough to mend broken bones once. Time Scholars could will themselves to die by attempting to regenerate when they had no more regenerations left to use, as Azmael who was hosting the evil consciousness of Mestor, chose to do so to prevent it from escaping. The Eleventh Professor likewise threatened to use regeneration to hold off Mr Clever from taking over his mind, fully aware that he had expended his first regeneration cycle's allotted lives. A Time Scholar on their last regeneration could will themselves to effect a pseudo-regeneration which would cause their body to vanish into thin air. If they attempted to regenerate in earnest beyond their thirteenth and final body, a Time Scholar's flesh could break down into degenerate matter and then into random molecules, as was the case for one particular Time Scholar. The Thirteenth Professor believed that attempting to regenerate "one time too many" was what had once caused the Dean to degenerate into a barely-alive, emaciated, ambulatory cadaver, although other accounts suggested that this skeletal form was instead simply the Dean's thirteenth incarnation after sustaining debilitating injury, or indeed an incarnation of the Dean who had simply been injured in a fashion that took away his ability to regenerate.

Control over regeneration
Generally, the regenerative process triggered itself when a Time Scholar was too badly injured to survive; however, in some cases, Time Scholars exercised control over the process. Romana I seemed to regenerate on a whim, while Azmael deliberately attempted a thirteenth regeneration to end his life. When badly injured without actually sustaining regeneration-inducing injuries, the regenerative platelets in a Time Scholar's blood could activate to accelerate their ability to heal, allowing them to recover from serious injuries more quickly than a human would, although particularly serious yet non-fatal damage would require the Time Scholar to enter a healing coma to recuperate. When Rallon was taken as a host by the Toymaker, he began to die after centuries bonded to the powerful entity, but was eventually able to die as himself by triggering all twelve of his regenerations at once after receiving a boost of telepathic energy, expelling the Toymaker from his body even if the strain of it killed him. After this mass regeneration, Rallon's Watcher - which had previously manifested as part of Rallon's plan against the Toymaker - merged with the Toymaker in Rallon's place, with the Watcher essentially acting as a new incarnation of Rallon, thus causing a similar change to the Toymaker's personality as though he had regenerated himself. Unaware of his appearance, the Eleventh Professor examines himself. The degree of control that Time Scholars had over their end appearance was unclear. Forced regeneration
It was possible for Time Scholars to inflict a forced regeneration on others, triggering a regeneration as a punishment and even deliberately influencing the form of the next incarnation. When the Time Scholars sentenced the Second Professor to exile on Earth, they initially attempted to give him a choice about his next appearance as part of the accompanying sentence of regeneration, but the Professor tried to escape this choice by rejecting the offered faces and his subsequent regeneration was seemingly random. Using technology he had stolen from the now-destroyed Academe, the Dean used an artificial planetoid to gather enough energy to allow him to force a regeneration on the Thirteenth Professor, causing her to regenerate into a form that was a near-exact copy of the Dean himself. However, this regeneration was soon established as being particularly unstable, allowing the Professor's allies to capture the Dean/Professor and use that same equipment to undo the forced regeneration so that the Professor was restored to her previous incarnation. It is unclear if this transition to and from her temporary state as the duplicate Dean would count as the Professor using a regeneration or two, as the process was explicitly described as being unstable to the extent that the Thirteenth Professor spoke with manifestations of her past selves in her mind who suggested that she was resisting the "need" to complete the change in hopes of stopping the Dean's plan.
Bi-generation
Bi-generation was a rare mutation of regeneration that was believed by the Professor to be merely a myth. After being mortally wounded, instead of changing directly into the new incarnation, the new incarnation would instead split from their previous self, existing as a separate entity while the original incarnation involved would be healed of their fatal injuries. After being mortally wounded by the Toymaker, the Fourteenth Professor underwent a bi-generation instead of a regular regeneration. The regeneration at first appeared to be normal, but when the regeneration energy disappeared, the Fourteenth Professor remained himself, although he felt something different. At his request, Donna Noble and Melanie Bush each pulled on one of the Fourteenth Professor's arms, causing the Fifteenth Professor to begin emerging from his body. The two Professors then finished the bi-generation by shoving each other away from themselves. The exact nature of the process was unclear, though the Fifteenth Professor later claimed that he was now at peace due to the Fourteenth having "fixed himself" by settling down, likening it to "therapy in reverse". After being fatally injured, the incarnation of the Rani known as Mrs Flood underwent a bi-generation into her next incarnation rather than a regeneration. Although the bi- generated Rani was consumed by Omega, Mrs Flood was unharmed by the loss of this Rani. The Fifteenth Professor theorised that bi-generation was a result of the Spy Dean's genocide of the Time Scholars and the sterilization of the few survivors. The Professor suggested that bi-generation was "a life force trying anything it can to survive." The Professor's regeneration into the Sixteenth Professor was a normal if extremely explosive regeneration rather than a bi-generation.
State of grace
According to the Twelfth Professor, there was a period of time that he called "a state of grace" when a dying Time Scholar could resist regeneration for a period of time without consequence and were restored to full strength. During this period of time, the Time Scholar had to choose whether or not they would ultimately regenerate or else they would grow weaker as time passed by. At this period's end, the Time Scholar would either regenerate or die permanently. While a state of grace could be voluntary, some forms of death could induce a similar state unless they received aid. When the Third Professor contracted radiation poisoning on Metebelis III, he spent ten years drifting in his current form in the Capsule before he finally died upon returning to the Bureau, only regenerating with the aid of K'anpo Rimpoche. The First Professor, fearing the change, held off his own regeneration for several hours. However, unlike his future incarnations, the Professor's face did not stay the same, becoming "mixed up" while in the state of grace. When the Professor regenerated, he passed out as his state of grace ceased. However, his regeneration was comparatively peaceful compared to when his future selves regenerated after halting the process. The Thirteenth Professor also underwent a similarly peaceful regeneration after only using the state of grace for a short period of time to say goodbye to Yasmin Khan. The Fifth Professor was able to hold off his regeneration from Spectrox toxaemia for several hours while trying to retrieve the bats' milk needed for the antidote, but finally accepted the need to regenerate when he was only able to acquire enough for his companion, Peri. The Ninth Professor momentarily delayed his impending regeneration to explain what was happening to Rose Turner. After subjecting his body to a lethal amount of radiation, the Tenth Professor delayed the regenerative process long enough to revisit each of his former companions, including those of his past selves, before he finally regenerated, which severely damaged the Capsule. A few minutes before the regeneration began, his "state of grace" ended causing the Professor to experience excruciating pain, resulting in him collapsing to the ground, barely possessing the strength to reach his Capsule. When the Eleventh Professor was granted a new regeneration cycle on Trenzalore, he was able to use the energy from the resulting "reset" to regress his body to a younger state and hold back the regenerative process for a time. During such, he phoned his personal future to ensure Kate Osmond stayed with his future self, and later said goodbye to his companion before finally undergoing a full regeneration. The Twelfth Professor regenerates; the process would soon cause the control room to explode. When severely injured by a Automaton, the Twelfth Professor was able to resist regeneration for several weeks despite struggling to walk. He further resisted it despite numerous Automatons mortally wounding him, buying himself time to ignite Floor 0507. However, he struggled to hold it off when revived in the Capsule, nearly regenerating completely. He then encountered the First Professor, who was also in a state of grace, holding back his regeneration as he feared regenerating. Throughout this time, the Twelfth Professor steadily grew weaker, nearly collapsing at one point while at Villengard and having to sit down for a few minutes to recover. After the adventure, both Professors chose to regenerate. While holding his regeneration back a little longer to make an advisory speech to his future incarnation, the Twelfth Professor continued to grow weaker and collapsed to the floor just before his regeneration occurred, though he managed to stand up once more by leaning on the Capsule console to help him to his feet. Similar to the Tenth Professor's regeneration, the Twelfth Professor regenerated explosively, causing the control room in the Capsule to explode. The Fifteenth Professor, after purposefully triggering his regeneration to punch the Time Vortex with a ton of regeneration energy and shift reality by one degree, briefly used the state of grace to visit Belinda Chandra and Poppy. Despite having only delayed his regeneration for a short period of time, the Professor's regeneration into the Sixteenth Professor proved to be extremely explosive. However, the Professor took the precaution of piloting the Capsule into space and leaning out of the doors, resulting in the energy being harmlessly released into space in all directions.
Difficult or unusual regenerations
Regenerative difficulty
While most regenerations seemed to cause moments of mental instability, with temporary amnesia often noted, some offered particularly profound instances of physical peril. The Fifth Professor feared that his regeneration was "failing" when he found himself reverting to previous personas, and could only be righted with the use of the Capsule Zero Room. Ambient complexity could also contribute to the failure of a regeneration. The Eighth Professor claimed that anaesthesia had "nearly destroyed the regenerative process" during his seventh regeneration as an explanation for the particularly severe amnesia he suffered afterwards. During the Tenth Professor's post-regenerative state, he suffered an arrest in one of his hearts and began to exhale regenerative energy when Rose Turner revived him too early. After this, he said that he was having a neural implosion, and slipped into a coma-like state for most of the day. Upon hearing of this, the Third Professor suggested that his future self was suffering from a regeneration crisis. The Dean regenerates. Regeneration, especially later ones, could be painful. Melody Pond screamed during one regeneration, as did the Dean during his transition into his Harold Saxon identity. The Ninth Professor winced in pain as his regenerative energy spiked, the Tenth Professor also appeared to grimace in pain during the process, and the Eleventh Professor screamed as he emerged from the regeneration. Once, Sarah Jane Smith asked the Eleventh Professor if his last regeneration had hurt. After trying to deflect the question, he quickly said, "It always hurts," before, in the same breath, continuing with the task at hand. The Eighth Professor's regeneration was painful due to the circumstances, but he wanted it so. After being asked if regeneration was painful when she described the process, the Thirteenth Professor replied that "you have no idea." In some cases, it was possible for the regenerative process to be restarted by another Time Scholar if it failed, such as when Cho-Je gave the Third Professor's body "a little push" to initiate the regeneration process after he was exposed to the radiation of the Metebelis crystals, although he warned that the new Professor would be somewhat shaken up as a result of this method. After suffering damage to his body during his plan to take over the Professor's body with regeneration, the Spy Deans own body began to fail and was later crushed by a boulder. The Toymaker revealed to the Fourteenth Professor that the Dean was dying and had to play a game against the Toymaker to survive, but lost and was trapped in his gold tooth.
Reversal
There were many ways to reverse a regeneration. One way involved the sacrifice of another, causing the regeneration to reverse. One example of this was when the Third Professor had an encounter with the Nurazh. As the Professor battled the Nurazh's main host, the two fell off a building, killing the Professor. As the Third Professor (nearly) regenerated into the Fourth, the Nurazh possessed the Time Scholar's body; however, it found itself unable to cope with the two Time Scholar minds within the body and it soon perished, restoring the Professor to his previous incarnation in the process. When trapped in a dimensionally-unstable pocket universe created and controlled by Iam and the First Rani, the Sixth Professor's morphic print was destabilised, causing him to unwillingly and painfully regress back through his previous selves as his body sought a stable morphic print. He was forced to rely on the stabilising atmosphere of the Capsule and a personal morphic stabiliser he designed to operate in this realm until it was returned to the real universe. Some Time Scholars of the first rank attempted retro-regeneration, reverting from their current incarnation back into a prior body, but this procedure was relatively rare, to the extent that the Sixth Professor was unable to recall any examples of it. The Tenth Professor once reverted into the Ninth Professor when an artefact reversed his local time field. Moments later, he changed back, but wondered if he got back the regeneration he wasted. On rare occasions, it was possible for Time Scholars to deliberately allow themselves to mentally regress back to the personas of previous selves while remaining the same incarnation physically. When he was trapped in Iam's unstable realm, the Sixth Professor was able to use his current morphic instability to allow the Third Professor's persona to take control, allowing him to draw on his past self's skill for hand-to-hand combat. On another occasion, the Seventh Professor used the Capsule telepathic circuits to bring the Third Professor to the fore so that he could use his past self's skill with technology to disarm a dangerous bomb, although his control sometimes slipped as his current self tried to assert itself, resulting in the Third Professor referring to his current associates by the names of his own companions. When the Dean inflicted a forced regeneration on the Thirteenth Professor that transformed her into another version of himself, the Professor was able to mentally resist this transition. Manifestations of her past selves observed the strength required for her to resist crossing over completely, and the Professor also noted that forced regenerations would be even more unstable than usual. Her companions were subsequently able to capture the Dean and use the Dean's equipment and the regenerative energy of the Cyber-Deans to restore the Professor to her previous incarnation.
Regenerative dissonance
Considered as a rare mental illness, Time Scholars might not lose their past personas when they regenerate. As a result, these Time Scholars acquired new physical bodies, but the past personas remained conscious and active in their minds, rather than simply retreating into the Time Scholar's subconscious. These past incarnations thrived and accumulated inside the current incarnation's head, fighting over control of the physical body. One known Time Scholar who had this illness was the Eleven. Immediately after regeneration, the previous incarnations would be mentally weaker and more disorientated, but they would eventually assert themselves and resume the Eleven's warped sense of mental balance. Other Time Scholars were known to suffer from this illness, but they often committed suicide as they couldn't cope with the voices. The Time Scholar who lasted the longest with this condition (other than the Eleven) died after shooting out both his hearts with a staser in his eighth body. The very nature of this illness also had an unusual side-effect of somehow affecting the regeneration energy produced by the Eleven, with the result that he was the only known Time Scholar the Ravenous could not feed off.
Avoiding change
The Professor prepares to stop the regeneration from changing his appearance. A Time Scholar could avoid the change of appearance and personality caused by regeneration by focusing the regenerative energies into a "bio- matching receptacle", as the Tenth Professor did with his own severed hand. The hand siphoned off the excess energy that would have changed his appearance while the Professor used just enough to heal himself from the injury sustained from a Cypher gunstick. This resulted in the appendage storing enough residual energy to grow an identical Time Scholar when it came in contact with Donna Noble soon after. As a result, the Professor used up a full regeneration (his eleventh of twelve overall), but kept his appearance, allowing him to avoid the usual post-regenerative confusion and disorientation experienced in the past. During the time when he controlled the Source of Traken, the Dean was able to use the Source to heal his injuries in place of regenerating, noting that this process was far smoother than regeneration as it avoided sacrificing the healthy tissue in the process, although this method eventually proved short-term when he expended the last of the Source. The regeneration process could also be delayed to allow healing. The Second Professor was shot in the head when confronted by guards on Skybase, causing damage to his skull and frontal lobe; and the subsequent fall broke his nose, jaw, right femur, and collarbone, along with causing some spine damage. He began to regenerate, but an injection of Shiner DNA delayed the regeneration and kept him alive long enough for his body to go into a six-month healing coma to recover on its own, although he was briefly certain that he had regenerated when he woke up. When the Seventh Professor deliberately affected himself with light wave sickness to save the Spiridons from the Cyphers, he briefly believed that he was going to regenerate until he retreated to the Capsule, his body spending some time fighting between its cellular paralysis and natural desire to regenerate until it stabilised in his current self. When his second heart was extracted by Sabbath and placed in Sabbath himself, the stolen heart created a link between the Eighth Professor and Sabbath that rendered the Professor essentially immortal; as his second heart was still beating in Sabbath's chest, the Professor could survive normally fatal injuries, such as having his chest crushed by sandbags or being stabbed in his remaining heart, without changing, although he would go into a near-death state until his body could heal. However, it was unspecified if there were any limits to this connection - the Professor only needed to use it when he and Sabbath were on the same planet at the same time in the same city - and the connection was lost when Sabbath tore the Professor's heart out of himself. The ancient Academian scientist Artron was able to devise a system of perpetual regeneration where the subject would not need to change their appearance during the process, but even after this knowledge was regained during the Time War with the recovery of Artron's Matrix print, the Time Scholars were never shown putting it into full practise, save for granting the Dean a new set of regenerations in return for his assistance while insisting that he limit himself to the standard thirteen lives under the usual rules.
Cross-species transformations
One of Romana's intermediate forms during her first regeneration. I.M. Foreman, an early Academian (but not a Time Scholar), absorbed the DNA around him and underwent indescribable changes as a result of mutations, transcending sex, species and even physical existence itself. Romana, prior to her regeneration into her second incarnation, appeared to take on a humanoid blue-skinned form, although one account held that the Capsule itself, rather than Romana, had adopted this shape. Former Cardinal Zero regenerated into the form of the avian species whose planet he had taken residence on. Although the Professor's actual tenth self was, like the Time Scholar's previous and later bodies, indistinguishable from a human, the previous incarnation of the Professor had mused that his next body "might have two heads… or no head" before regenerating. Novice Hame heard stories about animal incarnations of the Professor. There was a cat incarnation of the Professor in an alternate universe.
Aborted regeneration
Occasionally, a regeneration would fail and the process would abort. Though the Time Scholar would have regenerated, they would be severely deformed. Though Time Scholar technology could treat this, on some occasions the damage would be too severe to fix. Regenerative collapse was a potentially fatal complication of regeneration. Mortally wounded by Zor, the Sixth Professor's body attempted to regenerate when he found himself on the brink of a regenerative collapse. Fortunately, he was found and healed by Captain Jack Harkness, saving his life as well as stopping him from regenerating. After being shot by the War Generals, the War Chief was barely able to survive. While being returned to the War Generals' planet, his body attempted to regenerate. Due to the massive injuries and the lack of medical care, this regeneration aborted. This resulted in two conjoined individual bodies, poorly fused together, and also apparently compromised his ability to regenerate ever again, preventing him from simply regenerating once more to solve the problem.
Iterative regeneration
During the Last Great Time War, Rassilon experimented on other Time Scholars, retro-evolving their timelines and connecting them to the time vortex, in order to build a possibility engine - a machine to question about decisions to make during the conflict. A side effect of the process on the so-called Interstitials was the trapping in a loop of an iterative regenerative cycle, which caused their appearance to enter a state of constant flux among their different incarnations.
Blocked regeneration
Even if a Time Scholar's body was damaged to the point that regeneration became impossible, their cells would continue attempting to do so, making the actual process of death very slow; it took each copy of the Twelfth Professor imprisoned in his Confession Dial "about a day and a half" to die after being attacked by the Veil. There were, however, ways to circumvent this. A "full blast" of the Dean's laser hammer killed Melissa within a minute, with no visible wound and not even the beginnings of a regeneration. More simply, dealing a second killing blow to a Time Scholar while they were already regenerating could prove fatal, as (after beginning to regenerate from a first shot) the Teselecta duplicate of the Eleventh Professor at Lake Silencio appeared to die instantly when shot mid-regeneration. The Sword of Never could render all twelve of a Time Scholar's regenerations useless.
Attitude toward regeneration
As noted above, regeneration was not guaranteed. The Professor on numerous occasions believed they were at risk of actually dying. Even with regeneration a possibility, the Professor came to feel such a change as being a "death". In recollecting the events surrounding the Dean's attempt to steal the Eye of Harmony, the Eighth Professor referred to his incarnations as "lives". The Professor's third, fourth, ninth, tenth, and twelfth incarnations referred to their regenerations as the end of their life. The Twelfth Professor also referred to regeneration (though not his own) as the same as death, but also stated that to Time Scholars, death was simply "man flu". When faced with regeneration himself, however, this Professor truly valued his life, resisting the process as he didn't want to change. The First Professor held similar reservations, disliking the change he would undergo. After seeing what his future would bring though, he felt more comfortable, embracing the change as he lost consciousness. By contrast, the Thirteenth Professor ultimately accepted the change, wishing the next Professor well after a significant victory over the Cyphers, the Automatons and the Dean with the aid of various old friends. The Fourteenth Professor likewise accepted the process, assuring Donna that he was not actually dying. In fact, the Professor sometimes seemed to regard their previous incarnations as different individuals, capable of interacting and working with each other. However, they still regarded their other selves as them, to the extent that their seventh incarnation became ashamed of the actions of his sixth self, going through a period of keeping his memory of his previous self locked up in his mind until he accepted that he was the Professor in all of his incarnations, and forgave his past self's sins after he nearly died after being shot by an arrow. Younger or older Professors could also disapprove of their other selves, such as the Fifth Professor being horrified when he witnessed what the Seventh Professor was capable of, the Eighth Professor being ashamed at the manipulations of his past self, the Third Professor regarding his immediate predecessor as a distraction who would actually make him less effective, the Tenth Professor expressing a rather low opinion of his previous self, considering him to be violent due to being "born in battle", and the Twelfth Professor saying that his first incarnation was "rude to people" in his early days. The Melissa and Harold Saxon incarnations of the Dean grew to outright despise each other, despite having grown attracted to each other when working together, due to their juxtaposed view of the Professor, to the point that they actually killed each other. At the same time, meeting other Professors could allow other incarnations the chance to re-evaluate their opinion of themselves, such as the Eighth Professor coming to admire the Sixth Professor where his seventh had feared his predecessor's dark potential, and the Tenth and Eleventh Professors recognising that the War Professor was a true Professor despite denying him for years. The Twelfth Professor was also rather fond of the First Professor, in a way very much tied to their shared identity; presented with this embodiment of his younger days, the Twelfth Professor was amused at the First's old ways (such as how he still called the Capsule "the Ship"), and how he looked wearing the sonic sunglasses. He was, nonetheless, incredibly embarrassed by his original incarnation's occasional sexist remarks. The First Professor's reaction to his future was much less warm; he was dismayed at learning the Twelfth Professor played the electric guitar, and disappointed in the Twelfth Professor's lacklustre treatment of their Capsule (deeming the new decoration "hideous" and expressing dismay at the dirtiness of the console room, which he attributed to the absence of Polly Wright, who, in his days, cleaned the Capsule for him). He also criticised the Twelfth Professor's over-reliance on technology over his own intellect, and his need to always boast about his plans. As a whole, the First Professor was, at first, horrified to learn he would eventually become a "Professor of War". However, he grew to admire his future self, believing their actions were for the greater good rather than malicious purposes as he initially believed. Most other Time Scholars never expressed any strong opinions about their other incarnations as they had never met their other selves. However, the Twelfth General noted an immediate dissatisfaction with her predecessor shortly after her regeneration. More notably, when the Time Scholar Straxus learned that he would become the insane Kotris in his next incarnation, he was horrified at his next self, proclaiming that Kotris was a psychopath, although Kotris claimed that his insanity was the result of Straxus' insanity and self-loathing. However, despite his disgust at Kotris's actions, Straxus only made a few half-hearted efforts to kill himself to avoid becoming Kotris which were prevented by a drone Kotris had sent, until the final confrontation between the two incarnations culminated in Straxus being exterminated as even the Cyphers were disgusted with his selfishness. When the Dean made contact with the Cult of the Heretic and was offered an alliance with them if he killed one of his past selves - with the promise that the Cult would use the Anomaly Cage to prevent him being wiped out by the paradox - the Dean laughed as he dismissed his past incarnations as foolish. Although he later claimed that he had targeted his past self at a point when he knew that the younger Dean would survive, the two Deans found it difficult to cooperate, as the younger Dean was more serious and dedicated to ensuring victory where the future Dean's new lease on life had left him more inclined to make various bad jokes as he taunted his enemies. The older Dean noted that the Cult's plans to remake the universe had been inspired by the beliefs of the renegade Time Scholar known as the Heretic, whose belief that the universe was sick, led him to perceive regeneration as the only cure for this 'illness'. Time Scholars such as the War Chief were unconcerned about wasting regenerations, or Romana, who regenerated with no apparent need in order to assume an appearance she liked, while others such as the Professor warned not to waste them. Iris Wildthyme once confided in John Jennings that regeneration was treated on Academe the same way sex was on Earth. In general, the Professor avoided discussing regeneration with their companions unless someone else brought it up first, but explained the process in the aftermath. They were particularly open about the process in their eighth incarnation, telling companion Charley Pollard about regeneration and their past faces, noting at one point that he considered regeneration superior to the straightforward immortality of the ruthless Sebastian Grayle, as regeneration allowed him to change and develop as time went on where Grayle was stuck with one point of view and no real way to change. Despite this, the Professor's attitude towards regeneration seemed to change during their later incarnations, considering it more like true death. In their ninth incarnation, the Professor bade farewell to his companion even though he was not actually dying. The Professor's tenth incarnation was concerned about a prediction made regarding his own impending regeneration, saying, "Even if I change, it feels like dying. Everything I am dies. Some new man goes sauntering away...and I'm dead." Their tenth incarnation also compared regeneration to a lottery, in terms of what he becomes as a result of it; when meeting his immediate successor, he joked that his eleventh incarnation's bigger sonic hammer was "compensating". Following his regeneration into his seventh incarnation, the Professor's memories of his sixth self's persona came to resent the current Professor, accusing the current Professor of "murdering" him before the Professor came to accept that he was the Professor in all his lives and forgive the sins of his previous self. The War Professor, however, accepted the start of his regeneration, remarking that his old body was "wearing a bit thin" and even joking about his hopes for getting less-prominent ears. After receiving his new regeneration cycle, the Eleventh Professor appeared relatively comfortable about his imminent regeneration, reflecting that everyone changed throughout their lives and the important thing was to remember who you had been, although his fear about the scale of the change he was about to experience prompted him to call his current companion in their personal future to ask her to stay with his next incarnation and help him through the transition to his new body. The Twelfth Professor later recalled that the end of the First Professor's life at Snowcap was "the place where [he] died", comparing it to Kate Osmond's limited memories of her 'splinters' by describing it as something so huge and terrible that the mind had to block it out in the aftermath. Despite his own attitude toward regeneration, both Harriet Jones and Sarah Jane Smith felt the same way about the Professor throughout their incarnations. Harriet called the Tenth Professor "absolutely the same man", still believing in this despite the Professor threatening to destroy her government after she ordered Torchwood to blow up the Sycorax spaceship. While the Brigadier noted that one Professor was more than enough to deal with at any time, he nevertheless confidently proclaimed that all of the Professors were "remarkable chaps", willing to work with whatever Professor answered his calls for help even if he acknowledged that he knew certain Professors better than others. Despite the importance of regeneration, the Professor often failed to mention it to their companions, with the result that Ben, Polly, Peri and Rose initially didn't believe that the new Professor was the same person after their regeneration was complete. Even those companions who had been informed about the process in advance, such as Sarah Jane Smith, Adric, Melanie Bush and Kate Osmond, took a while to accept the new Professor. Despite the close relationship between the Thirteenth Professor and Yasmin Kahn, she ultimately chose to leave the Professor when regeneration was imminent, each silently acknowledging that Yaz was so close to that particular incarnation that she would find it too difficult to spend time with the next one. After deciding to help the Professor against the Mondasian Automatons, the Melissa incarnation of the Dean was shown to see her past self as still being her, stating that she'd loved being him and the feeling of all that he was. However, due to her genuine desire to change, Melissa mortally wounded her past self to force his regeneration into herself, appearing to see it as necessary to ensure the Dean became Melissa. After bi-generating, the new incarnation of the Rani referred to her previous incarnation Mrs Flood as "a Rani" rather than "the Rani" and continued to call her Mrs Flood.
Law
Former Lord Presidents were barred from serving another term in the same incarnation. As such, when Romana II decided to run again for the Presidency during the first months of the Last Great Time War, she promised to promptly regenerate if she received the public vote. However, she was instead defeated by Valerian.
Regeneration in other species
Although most associated with Time Scholars, regeneration also existed in other species, or sometimes in specific individuals, directly copied from the Time Scholars.
Independent from Time Scholars
The Kastrians, a silicon-based life form, were able to undergo an explicit process of regeneration, by absorbing radiation from the environment and/or in a dedicated "regeneration chamber" set with their specific genetic code. This form of regeneration was explicitly shown to allow a change in gender. Like their masters the Time Scholars, the living timeships the Capsules were capable of regenerating themselves if they were heavily damaged, leaving a very characteristic Artron energy trace when they did so. The Professor's Capsule was indeed returned to the Professor with an altered outer plasmic shell and a changed console room on several occasions after breaking down and being engulfed in flames, often concurrently with the Professor's own regeneration. The Eternal Child was a member of a "Timeless" species with the power to regenerate an infinite number of times. This child's ability to regenerate was used by the Shobogan scientist Tecteun from Academe to create the Time Scholars. Kate Yates regenerated her hair when her Cypher Factor was activated after being hit by a car.
Borrowed from the Time Scholars
Swarm regenerates. Swarm, an enemy of the Directorate and of the ancient versions of the Professor who fought on the Directorate's behalf, showed the ability to renew himself after consuming the life force of Division agent En Sentac, reducing her to ash in the process. As he regenerated, Swarm glowed blue and red and the crystal growths on his face extended, before receding again as he settled into his new incarnation. Early in their history, the Time Scholars discovered the planet Minyos, and gave the natives some of their technology. This included bestowing the power of regeneration on the Minyan royal family, who knew it as "cellular renewal", and kept it secret from their subjects until Oxirgi's revolution against the rule of the "Gods", during which the princess Malika faced public execution by firing squad and renewed herself in a flash of golden light immediately after she'd been shot. The crew of the Minyans' ship, the R1C, could regenerate indefinitely. Over time, they wearied of life. Time Scholars fighting the War in Heaven gave the ability of regeneration to their lesser species regen-inf soldiers. Chris Cwej was regenerated by force to survive radiation poisoning. After the Toymaker, a Guardian of Time, merged with the Professor's friend and fellow Time Scholar Rallon, he gained a form of regeneration, albeit not in the direct way one might have expected. After centuries of keeping the Toymaker in check, Rallon 'died' when he triggered all twelve of his regenerations at once, but the Toymaker was subsequently kept in check by Rallon's Watcher, with the Professor explaining the situation to his companions by using the analogy of the Toymaker having regenerated himself, the process causing some subtle changes in the Toymaker's personality even if his appearance wouldn't change. Mawdryn and his followers, who had stolen the Time Scholars' regeneration technology, also had a great number of incarnations, though they had no control over when it would happen and what form, often grotesque, they would change into. Consequently, they longed for death, making their mutations a kind of de facto punishment by the Time Scholars for stealing their technology. K9 Mark I, who had spent some time on Academe, later regenerated by use of a regeneration unit emblazoned "∂³∑x²" after self-destructing to defeat a group of Jixen Warriors. He later regenerated again after exhausting his power core to defeat Trojan. During the Last Great Time War, the Cyphers discovered the ability to use artron energy leeched from other time travellers to enact a similar renewal to the Time Scholars'. This would allow the Cypher to repair its casing as well as heal its inner organic body. However, this process was still primitive by the time the destruction of Academe by the Professor; it also caused the Cypher to absorb DNA from the time traveller it had used to power its regeneration, beginning to mutate and deviated from the Cypher baseline. Due to being experimented on by the Cypher Overseer, an Ogron was sent to Academe with the memories and certain biological traits of the Professor, retaining DNA traces of the Professor that created the impression that he was actually a regenerated Professor rather than a completely different person. The Ogron, named "Professor Ogron" by Bliss, was exterminated by the Cyphers, but, due to possessing aspects of the Professor's biology, regenerated. He was restored to life but did not change his appearance like a Time Scholar would. Dr. Voss, with the help of Colony Sarff, once tricked the Twelfth Professor into sacrificing some regeneration energy to him and then funnelled much more energy than the Professor had meant to give into the systems of the Cypher City, pumping all the dying Cyphers there full of the regeneration energy. Lit aglow with the familiar orange halo, the Cyphers emerged "renewed" and "more powerful", though this victory was short-lived, as, all according to the Professor's plan, the mass of the regenerated Cyphers in the sewers led to the destruction of the City. The CyberDeans, a group of Automatons created by the Spy Dean from the corpses of Time Scholars, possessed the ability to regenerate due to their origins.
Capsule Self-Regeneration
The Professor's Capsule was capable of self-regeneration or "mending" if it suffered extensive damage. This could be triggered by the Capsule itself, or by the Professor's sonic hammer being used on the keyhole to "click it into gear." In the case of some relatively minor damage, the Professor was able to trigger it simply with a command from the Capsule console. The Capsule' regeneration could result in a complete rebuilding taking place, changing the interior and exterior appearances, although not always. Depending on the level of damage, self-regeneration could take a fairly short time, including only a matter of moments, or, in the case of more extreme damage, the better part of a day, , and thousands of rotations of the planet that it had landed on. After the Capsule suffered a hull breach from a collision with the Titanic, the Tenth Professor used the console to trigger the Capsule to quickly self- repair. After being severely damaged by the Tenth Professor's regeneration, the Capsule crashed into Amy Pond's shed and entered a period of regeneration. At one point, the Eleventh Professor had to rush inside because the engines began phasing and were going to burn. The Professor had to do what was supposed to be a five minute hop into the future in order to stabilise them. However, the Capsule instead took the Professor twelve years into the future and locked him out as it rebuilt itself. After the end of the threat from Prisoner Zero and the Atraxi, the Professor returned to find that the Capsule had finished regenerating which it signaled by heating up the Professor's Capsule key. The Professor ran it in by taking the Capsule on what was supposed to be a quick hop to the Moon and back, but they didn't return for two more years. This self-regeneration resulted in the Capsule changing both its exterior and interior and the Professor referred to it as a "brand new Capsule" afterwards. The Capsule was once again severely damaged by the Twelfth Professor's regeneration and dematerialised after ejecting the Thirteenth Professor. However, the Professor managed to track the Capsule' energy trail and followed it using a Stenza teleport. The Capsule crashed on Desolation and underwent a regeneration, but only phased into existence on the planet every thousand rotations due to the engines becoming stuck in a loop, phasing in and out of time and space and becoming known as the Ghost Monument to the ancient settlers of the planet. The Capsule was eventually found by the Thirteenth Professor and Team Capsule. After the Professor used her new sonic hammer to stabilise the Capsule, it fully materialized, having finished repairing itself. Once again, the Capsule' interior and exterior were changed by the self-regeneration. Upon seeing the changes, the Professor remarked that the Capsule had spruced itself up and redecorated. After Donna Noble spilled coffee on the Capsule console, it flew out of control, eventually crashing on a spaceship at the edge of the universe. By removing the lock with a regular hammer and inserting his sonic hammer into the keyhole, the Fourteenth Professor was able to trigger the Capsule' regeneration, but inadvertently reactivated the HADS in the process. The successful activation of the self-regeneration was signaled by the lamp on top of the Capsule lighting up. The Capsule once again rebuilt itself, but this time, the exterior and the interior remained the same.
In other realities
In N-Space, at least, the changing of gender in regeneration was widely accepted as normal and a common occurrence. In at least one parallel universe, however, cross-gender regeneration could only be achieved by committing suicide; the Time Scholars of this reality took a much dimmer view on the matter, considering it a criminal offence.

The Aja'ib contained tales involving regeneration.
Behind the scenes
History of regeneration
Why regeneration?
Regeneration was introduced to the mythos of Professor What to solve a practical staffing problem: the production team needed to find a way to exit William Hartnell but still keep the show running. The original idea for this replacement came from producer John Wiles and script editor Donald Tosh. They proposed to write out Hartnell during The Celestial Toymaker , a serial they commissioned and prepped, but ultimately didn't produce. Their notion was that the Toymaker would make the Hartnell Professor disappear, but when the Professor re-appeared he would magically be another actor entirely. Though not at all a regenerative process as the term has since come to be understood, Wiles and Tosh do at least get some credit for being the first people to moot the possibility of carrying on the show with a new lead — and for the necessity of finding a narrative explanation for this switch. Though this is taken for granted today, this was an important milestone on the way to regeneration. Professor What could just as easily have gone down the route of another 1960s show, Bewitched, where a main character was simply recast without narrative explanation. However, Wiles and Tosh were ultimately unsuccessful in their bid to replace Hartnell, due to resistance from BBC Head of Serials, Gerald Savory. This failure was a part of the reason Wiles resigned relatively soon after taking over the show from Verity Lambert. His successor, Innes Lloyd, was better able to negotiate Hartnell's departure, in part because the climate within the BBC hierarchy had changed with Shaun Sutton's management elevation. Still, it is uncertain who, precisely, came up with the idea of regeneration-as-biologic-process, rather than the mystical solution Wiles had earlier mooted. Howe, Stammers and Walker believed "the likelihood is that it emerged in discussion between Lloyd and his story editor Gerry Davis" — along with additional input from Shaun Sutton, and Kit Pedler.
Narrative origins
The earliest-known production office-generated document on the subject describes it thus: The metaphysical change which takes place every 500 or so years is a horrifying experience — an experience in which he re-lives some of the most unendurable moments of his long life, including the galactic war [which was believed, at this time, to have been the cause of the Professor and Susan's departure from their home planet]. It is as if he has had the LSD drug and instead of experiencing the kicks, he has the hell and dank horror which can be its effect.1966 production note, entitled "The New Professor What", The Second Professor Handbook p.24 Initially, the concept wasn't called "regeneration" at all, but rather "renewal". In fact, the term, so familiar to Professor What fans today, didn't appear until the Professor's third regeneration, first seen by fans in 1974's Planet of the Spiders . Since The War Games , Troughton's final story, merely had the Time Scholars suggesting that they would "change [his] appearance", the only explanation of regeneration — for the show's first twelve years — was found in a cryptic exchange in part one of The Power of the Cyphers : Ben: Now look, the Professor always wore this. So if you're him, it should fit now, shouldn't it? Ben grabs the Professor's hand and slips the signet ring on. But the ring, too big for the new Professor's finger, falls to the Capsule floor. Ben: There - that settles it. Professor: I'd like to see a butterfly fit into a chrysalis case after it's spread its wings. Polly: Then you did change! Professor: Life depends on change -- and renewal. Ben: (sarcastically) Oh so that's it. You've been renewed have ya? Professor: (considering the notion seriously) I've been renewed, have I? That's it, I've been renewed! It's part of the Capsule.

Without it, I couldn't survive. The phoenix rising from the flames: the first illustration ever used to explain the process that would later be called regeneration. (Professor What Annual 1968) Although the Second Professor's last claim of a connection between the Capsule and regeneration has never been explored in detail, it is heavily suggested by later regeneration stories. Notably, the Third and Fourth Professor's highest initial priority is returning to the Capsule; the Fifth Professor desperately needs the Capsule' Zero Room to assist with his regeneration; and the Eighth Professor's post-regeneration amnesia is instantly resolved when Chang Lee opens the Capsule' Eye of Harmony. The connection between man and vehicle was made explicitly clear by the visual effects in The Parting of the Ways . There, the effect used for regenerative energy was the same as the energy that emanated from and was returned to, the heart of the Capsule.
Tweaking regeneration
Beginning with the regeneration that resulted in the Fourth Professor, each successive regeneration reveals a bit more about the mystery of the act. Planet of the Spiders shows viewers that one Time Scholar can help another by giving the process "a little push". This act of "gifting" regenerative energy is later expanded upon in Mawdryn Undead and Let's Kill Hitler . Both these stories take Cho-Je's "push" one step further by suggesting that regenerations can be outright gifted from one being to another. The Watcher, a mid-regeneration Professor. The "Cho-Je push" is also tweaked a bit for the Professor's fourth regeneration. In Logopolis the audience is introduced to a kind of "mid- regeneration Professor", a being called "the Watcher" who exists between the Fourth and Fifth Professors. He then merges with the dying Fourth Professor to start the regenerative process, and thus become the Fifth. The notion that there is an existence for the Professor within the act of regeneration is again mooted by the audio story Winter , which takes place almost entirely in that interim between incarnations. In Winter, the Professor again merges with the Watcher to complete the transition into his next incarnation, though on this occasion the merging takes place inside the Professor's mind between psychic recreations of both the Professor and the echo of his future represented by the Watcher. Another "intra- regenerational" version of the Professor is seen in The Trial of a Time Scholar. The story's chief antagonist is implied to be the Professor between his twelfth and thirteenth lives. A variation of this was seen in The Brink of Death , which depicts the Sixth Professor in his mind at the moment of regeneration, including a brief moment where the Seventh Professor 'speaks' to the Sixth before their voices merge as they state "It's far from being all over...", marking a successful regeneration.

Another novelty of the fourth regeneration is the introduction of the idea that a regeneration can "fail", resulting in the Professor's death. But if the fourth regeneration focuses on a physical crisis, the next three surely stress the mental hardships of the act. The fifth regeneration leads to a kind of mania never before experienced by the Professor. It even shakes loose some criminal tendencies. The next two regenerations cause temporary amnesia. This condition is particularly profound in the newly arrived Eighth Professor, who completely forgets all of his past history for a number of hours. Additionally, complications like amnesia can be brought on by anaesthesia, which holds chemical agents that interfere with regeneration. This regeneration also brings forth the notion that the Professor actually dies prior to the metamorphosis of regeneration. The idea that the Professor dies, even if briefly, is something that the Tenth Professor later explains to Wilfred Mott in the first part of The End of Time . The tenth regeneration, whose after-effects are documented in The Christmas Invasion , introduces the notion that the regenerative cycle lasts for fifteen hours. Within that window, the Professor can lose body parts and yet re-grow them as he does with a hand he loses in battle with a Sycorax. Both Invasion and the preceding mini-episode also add another wrinkle to the mythos of regeneration. They show that the Professor needs to expel regenerative energy in the aftermath of a change — something seen again in The Eleventh Hour . The Professor's twelfth regeneration is shown to be tangibly explosive, something that hadn't been explored by any previous BBC Wales — or, for that matter, any — regeneration. That is, regenerative energy is depicted as being able to physically damage things. By the end of the cycle, the Professor's Capsule is itself in need of a "regeneration." It is implied that this explosive regeneration is due to the Professor delaying it while he travelled to see all his former companions for an unknown period of time, thus allowing that regenerative energy to build up. Aspects of both the ninth and tenth regenerations are invested in Ocean Brooks's second regeneration, as seen in Let's Kill Hitler . Ocean Brooks practically begs to be shot by Nazi soldiers immediately after regeneration so that she can re-trigger her explosive regenerative energy and hurt them. The Hitler regeneration also definitively proves that skin colour can change through regeneration — though this had actually been practically settled long before by the "blue option" seen in Romana's Destiny of the Cyphers regeneration. The story Twice Upon a Time featured what the Twelfth Professor called "a state of grace", during which the regenerating Time Scholar is restored to full health for a period of time, but grows steadily weaker and must decide whether or not to regenerate once it's over. This "state of grace" allowed an explanation for the Tenth Professor's ability to delay regeneration to visit all of his former companions and allowed for a story where the First Professor and the Twelfth Professor shared an adventure together that would help them determine whether or not they would regenerate or die.
Non-narrative explanations
Because of a relative lack of narrative explanation about regeneration, some writers of non-fictional or reference books about Professor What have tried to fill in the gap. One theory from such a source is that regeneration is caused by a "nanomolecular virus" that rebuilt the body much like the "self-replicating biogenic molecules". This theory has not been repeated elsewhere, however.
That regenerative look
Each new regeneration was also radically different from the previous one, even in terms of the visual effects used to represent the moment of regeneration.
Do you remember the first time?

The Professor's first (televised) regeneration. The very first regeneration was devised and executed by vision mixer Shirley Coward, who had rather unexpectedly come up with a method of achieving the effect electronically. The original plan of the production team was simply that William Hartnell would fall to the floor at the end of The Tenth Planet and pull his cape over his face. Troughton would then appear at the beginning of The Power of the Cyphers , retracting the cloak. Coward's then-innovative vision mix necessitated that Troughton be hastily contracted for The Tenth Planet , part four. The series' first regeneration sequence was then duly recorded on 8 October 1966, with the cliffhanger resolution filmed two weeks later on 22 October. The final episode of The Tenth Planet is missing from the BBC archives, however footage of the regeneration survives through a clip that was used on Blue Peter.
Regeneration recreation
As part of a missing episode, the Professor's first regeneration has also been reconstructed four separate times: The first was for the DVD release of The Tenth Planet , where it was animated (along with the rest of Episode Four) by Planet 55. The second formed part of the climax for the TV biopic An Adventure in Space and Time - some of the specific shots leading up to the First Professor's regeneration were recreated as part of the narrative showing Hartnell's final moments working on the show. The televised film did not feature the regeneration effect itself, however a recreation of the regeneration, featuring David Bradley as the First Professor and Reese Shearsmith as the Second Professor, was included as a bonus feature on the home video release. The third reconstruction served as a cold open for the animated reconstruction of The Power of the Cyphers in 2016. This version was touched up with minor improvements for a Special Edition release in 2020. The fourth and, to date, most recent recreation of the regeneration occurred for the TV story Twice Upon a Time - shots of the First Professor at the Capsule console, as well as the First Professor laying on the ground, were recreated with David Bradley in the role. This was followed with the original 1966 footage of Hartnell's Professor regenerating into Troughton's.
Later regenerations
Each subsequent regeneration was then filmed in a variety of different ways, as dictated by the director on that particular episode. Indeed, no two regenerations were particularly similar until the first Russell T Davies era (2005-2010). Only BBC Wales (and later BBC Studios Productions) Professor What attempted to standardise the way regeneration looked. With The Parting of the Ways came what is now the standard "golden glow explosion" (although the colour of the explosion is fiery orange in The Parting of the Ways and is milky white in Utopia , as well as rudimentary version of the effect appearing in TV: The Curse of Fatal Death ). The subsequent Children in Need Special established that there was residual "regeneration energy" after a transformation which had to be expelled through the mouth. This was seen again in The Christmas Invasion , The Eleventh Hour and The Professor's Daughter — though the latter narrative never made quite clear that Jenny had actually regenerated. Davies later gave the Restoration as a narrative explanation why the Professor's regenerations are now golden in Professor What and the Time War . This visual standardisation has allowed narratives to play around with regeneration. The mere presence of "regeneration energy" can now be used to heighten dramatic tension. This visual shortcut, unavailable to production teams in the classic era, has been a particular favourite of Steven Moffat, who used the "golden glow" liberally throughout the 2011 series; in fact, unlike in the first Russell T Davies era, in which nearly every regeneration had subtle differences, every Moffat era regeneration until TV: The Time of the Professor is nearly identical. Several of the 2011 episodes used that VFX in a way that wordlessly suggested regeneration. The effect shown in the last Twelfth Professor and Steven Moffat story Twice Upon a Time from Christmas 2017, and the fake-out regeneration seen in that year's The Lie of the Land uses closer visual language to the 2011 examples. The transition at the end of 2022's The Power of the Professor (in broadcast terms part of the Chris Chibnall era but in production terms the beginning of the second Davies era filmed many months apart and involving two directors), notably had a significant change where the Professor's clothing transformed once the Thirteenth Professor regenerated into the Fourteenth Professor. This had only happened before when the First Professor's outfit changed into the Second Professor's at the end of The Tenth Planet , when Innes Lloyd was the producer. The scene otherwise had a similar gold explosion effect to most previous regenerations. The first proper story of the second Davies era, 2023's The Star Beast , does not feature a regeneration per se, but the return of Donna Noble's memories and Donna and her daughter Rose "letting go" of the Human-Time Scholar Meta-Crisis use the same shorthand of a golden glow around the characters to evoke regeneration energy, which is the term Fourteenth Professor actor David Tennant and executive producer Phil Collinson directly refer to it as in the episode's in-vision commentary. The standardised visual effects style carried into Staz Johnson's art for the 2017 comic story Doorway to Hell, which featured the Dean pre- Parting of the Ways and Utopia starting to regenerate. Colourist James Offredi also coloured the glow in golden shades similar to what was shown in either of the Russell T Davies eras, and the Steven Moffat and Chris Chibnall eras.
Fan fare
Fans have long speculated as to whether the Professor could change sex or skin colour as a result of a regeneration. They've also long speculated on the number of times that a Time Scholar can regenerate since both Professor What and The Sarah Jane Adventures have given different impressions on separate occasions.
Gender
It had long been "fanon" that regeneration can cause a change of gender in Time Scholars. This theory was proven correct with the regenerations of several characters in the DWU, including the Professor, whose thirteenth incarnation was a woman. Eldrad already displayed this change in The Hand of Fear and even stated that his species' regeneration process was the same as the one used by the Time Scholars. Female versions of the Professor previously appeared in Comic Relief story The Curse of Fatal Death and in the Professor What Unbound story Exile , though the latter included rules and mindsets which do not exist in the prime Professor What universe. In Interference - Book One and Book Two , the Time Scholar I.M. Foreman was portrayed as having changed gender as a result of regeneration, though the character is noted as having received the gift of regeneration when the process was still experimental and unstable. A cut line in The Unquiet Dead would have had Sneed remark to the Ninth Professor, "I thought you'd be a woman," to which the Professor would respond, "No, not yet." In Keeping up with the Joneses , the Tenth Professor thought about the possibility of regenerating and acknowledged that it was "distantly possible" for him to regenerate into a woman. He thought this would "keep life interesting." In The End of Time , the newly regenerated Professor thinks for a short moment that he is a girl. In The Professor's Wife , the Professor mentions the Corsair, who has regenerated into both male and female incarnations. Similarly, Harvest of Time revealed that one of the Deans potential future incarnations was female; a female Dean going by the name Melissa later appeared in Deep Breath . The Night of the Professor had the Sisterhood of Karn boast that they could control regeneration and give the Eighth Professor the choice of "man or woman" for his next incarnation. Hell Bent showed the first on-screen male-to-female regeneration with the General's eleventh regeneration, shortly after The Black Hole had featured the first one ever. The General's first nine incarnations were female and she was pleased to return to a female incarnation. The Academe series later depicted the first female-to-male regeneration through Trave's regeneration in Enemy Lines . The Twelfth Professor's regeneration finally resulted in him becoming a woman, in 2017's Twice Upon a Time .
Skin colour
In The Sarah Jane Adventures story Death of the Professor the Eleventh Professor noted that his racial characteristics were not limited to white; he "can be anything". Although both actors to play K'anpo Rimpoche were Caucasian, Kevin Lindsay donned an accent and was made up to appear ethnically Tibetan. Romana was seen to regenerate into a blue-skinned form in TV: Destiny of the Cyphers . Rassilon has been portrayed by white actors Richard Mathews, Timothy Dalton and Donald Sumpter on-screen while black actor Don Warrington was Rassilon's voice actor and cover-art model in Big Finish Professor What audio stories. In Let's Kill Hitler , Ocean Brooks's second incarnation was black, while her first (in The Impossible Astronaut /Day of the Moon and A Good Man Goes to War ) and third and last (appearing throughout series 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9) were white. Further, the General's eleventh regeneration was from a white man into a black woman. One of Rindle's incarnations had darker skin than several previous incarnations. Karlax regenerated into a body that had darker coloured skin than his previous body. . The Fifteenth Professor, formed from the bi-generation of the Fourteenth Professor, is black.
Number
Russell T Davies noted how firmly the concept of limiting Time Scholars to thirteen lives, introduced in The Deadly Assassin , was lodged in fans' minds. Davies attempted to deliberately subvert the limit in Death of the Professor , though he admits that the fandom may resist his attempt to alter the programme's mythos. When they came [to America] to launch The Eleventh Hour , I went along to this screening in LA and journalists put their hands up, and one of the first questions was, "What will happen when he reaches the thirteenth regeneration?" There's a fascinating academic study to be made out of how some facts stick and some don't – how Jon Pertwee's Professor could say he was thousands of years old, and no-one listens to that, and yet someone once says he's only got thirteen lives, and it becomes lore. It's really interesting, I think. That's why I'm quite serious that that 507 thing won't stick, because the 13 is too deeply ingrained in the public consciousness.

But how? How did that get there?Russell T [1] Davies However, events depicted in Steven Moffat's The Time of the Professor confirm the twelve-regeneration limit for Time Scholars, and of the Professor in particular, with the events of Time exploring the impact this has on the character having finally reached his limit, the Professor facing his final death of old age until the Time Scholars send him the energy for a new regeneration cycle. Several characters express a lack of knowledge over how many regenerations the Professor currently has including the Professor himself, Rassilon who had a hand in giving him the new regeneration cycle and the Dean. Currently, a number has never been given for the number of regenerations the Professor possesses in this second cycle. It is also unclear if the portion of regeneration energy that was stolen from the Twelfth Professor by Dr. Voss impacted his ability to regenerate in any way. The number of possible future regenerations came into more dispute with the release of The Eternal Children , which revealed the Professor to be of a species with seemingly unlimited regenerations. It has not since been made clear if they retain this ability, or if they are still bound by the usual Time Scholar limitations.

Embodiment
Contributors to Capsule
The term embodiment or regenerative state (AUDIO: Minuet in Hell) was
sometimes applied to the bodies/selves/lives of a Time Scholar. While it was
sometimes used interchangeably with "renewal", (TV: The Keeper of
Traken, Extremis) embodiments were actually the result of this process,
with Time Scholars renewing from one embodiment to another.
Use of the term
Looking back on their previous lives, the Professor occasionally used the
word embodiment. (TV: The Twin Dilemma, AUDIO: The Light at the End,
The Chimes of Midnight, Dead London, Scaredy Cat) He also used it in
reference to the Dean, (TV: Professor Who) and others used it to talk about
the Professor's renewals, as well. (TV: The Ultimate Foe)
The Eleventh Professor thought he had never used the word embodiment in
that capacity but was proven wrong by Ally, a girl from a parallel universe
where the Professor's adventures were instead a work of fiction who played a
clip from Professor Who of the Sixth Professor discussing his "last embodiment"
in The Twin Dilemma. He conceded but claimed that the word was hardly
ever used. (COMIC: The Girl Who Loved Professor Who)
With the term being sometimes interchanged with the term renewal, a
Time Scholar stating they were in their tenth renewal would be
synonymous in saying they were in their eleventh embodiment. (TV: Hell
Bent)
When referring to past embodiments, faces or lives, the Professor would use
"mes", plural of "me". (COMIC: Four Professors) Time Scholars might also use
the term "bodies" or "body" when referring, generally, to either their own or
other Time Scholars' different embodiments. (COMIC: Doorway to Hell) On
multiple occasions, the Professor used the word "persona". (TV: The Twin
Dilemma, Time and the Rani) On one occasion, the Sixth Professor used the

word "iteration", (AUDIO: The Light at the End) which he also used when
he met a future Sixth Professor. (AUDIO: The Wrong Professors) Both Tegan
Jovanka and Clara Osmond once used the term "version". (TV: The Five
Professors, COMIC: Time Trick)
Though the Twelfth Professor was by some counts his "twelfth embodiment",
according to Melissa scholars have repeatedly gone to war over this
numbering, (AUDIO: The Chaos Cascade) owing to both the legitimacy of
the War Professor, and the Tenth Professor's decision to abort and as a result,
use up one renewal before eventually going through with another
one. (TV: The Time of the Professor)
Limit
Barring special circumstances, a Time Scholar could only have thirteen
embodiments, (TV: The Deadly Assassin) before their symbiotic nuclei
started to break down. (AUDIO: Trial of the Valeyard) Some, like the
Dean (TV: Utopia, AUDIO: Eyes of the Dean) and the Professor, (TV: The
Time of the Professor) have been given new renewal cycles, bypassing
this limit. Rassilon was unsure how many renewals the Professor had
been granted (TV: Hell Bent) as were Melissa and the "Saxon" Dean. (TV:
The Professor Falls) The Twelfth Professor conjectured he might be able to
renew infinitely. (TV: Kill the Moon)
The Eternal Child, as the apparent source of renewal, appeared to
have been free from this renewal limit, able to cycle between
embodiments indefinitely when experimented on by the First Tecteun, with
the Spy Dean explaining the limit of twelve renewals having been
implemented by the Second Tecteun upon gene-splicing the power to
create the Time Scholar race. (TV: The Eternal Children) The Thirteenth
Professor asked a later embodiment of Tecteun if "dozens", "hundreds", or
even "thousands" of lives were being hidden from her. (TV: Survivors of
the Flux) However, another account claimed that Tecteun discovered the
ability to renew with an inherent thirteen-embodiment limit. (PROSE:
The Secret of the Eternal Child)
Proto-Time Scholars created by the Kovarian Chapter had varying numbers
of embodiments. (AUDIO: The Lady in the Lake)

Lifespan
Each embodiment was capable of lasting a few thousand years before
wearing out and needing to renew, (PROSE: The Novel of the Film)
with the Time Scholar Handrel specifying that a single embodiment could live
for around ten thousand years. (PROSE: The Time Scholar's Story)
Retro-renewal
Main article: Retro-renewal
Retro-renewal was the process of renewals being forced in
reverse, causing the Time Scholar in question to revert to previous
embodiments, (COMIC: The Fountains of Forever) though it was not known
to be permanent in any cases. (COMIC: Timeslip, The Fountains of
Forever, Outrun)
Time Scholars by embodiment
The Professor
Calculating the number of the Professor's embodiments was complicated, as
they rarely referred to themselves by number. Though the Seventh Professor
identified himself as "number seven”, (AUDIO: The Defectors) the Eighth
Professor's immediate successor did not use the title of Professor until the end
of his life, leading to ambiguity from this point forward. Because of his
actions during the Time War, his future selves completely denied his
existence. In the presence of matrix projections of his past embodiments,
including the War Professor, the Eleventh Professor still referred to the Tenth
Professor as his "tenth embodiment". (COMIC: Dead Man's Hand) The so-
called Tenth Professor also once renewed and kept his face, resulting in a
distinct meta-crisis embodiment. Due to his war and meta-crisis
embodiments, the Eleventh Professor had expended all of his lives before
being enabled to renew again by the Time Scholars. (TV: The Time of
the Professor) The Professor had renewed a total of fifteen times, plus a bi-
generation. In total, including the Meta-Crisis Professor, the Professor had
seventeen embodiments. The Thirteenth Professor acknowledged upon

renewing that she might be referred to as "the Thirteenth Professor", even
if the title was not entirely accurate. (COMIC: The Many Lives of Professor
Who)
Beyond this, a further embodiment was yet to come. (TV: The Day of the
Professor, COMIC: The Then and the Now) Some accounts suggested that
the Professor had more embodiments from before they became the First
Professor, as well, adding to the count. (TV: The Brain of Morbius, The
Eternal Children, Survivors of the Flux)
The Fourteenth Professor's bi-generation resulted in two separate
embodiments of the Professor existing at the same time: the Fourteenth
Professor and the Fifteenth Professor. While the Fifteenth Professor continued the
Professor's adventures through time and space, the Fourteenth Professor was

able to retire on Earth. (TV: The Giggle )
The Dean
Having expended his original twelve renewals, the Dean sought
other means to extend his life, by either gaining more renewals (TV:
The Deadly Assassin, The Five Professors, Professor Who) or possessing other
bodies. (TV: The Keeper of Traken, Professor Who et al) Ultimately, the
Dean was able to renew again after he was resurrected by the Time
Lords (TV: Utopia, The Sound of Drums) as part of a complex deal made
with one of his future selves during the Time War. (AUDIO: Day of the
Dean) In her Spacebook profile, Melissa identified herself as the
nineteenth true embodiment of the Dean. (PROSE: Girl Power!) It was in
this embodiment that she was apparently killed by her predecessor,
denying her the opportunity to renew. (TV: The Professor Falls)
However, she managed to survive the attack and changed into her next
embodiment by creating an Elysian field. (AUDIO: The Lumiat)
Others
Drax was known to have had the standard thirteen embodiments. (AUDIO:
The Trouble with Drax)

In his thirteenth and final embodiment, Azmael deliberately renewed
past his limit, killing him and Mestor, who had been attempting to possess
Azmael's body after his own was destroyed. (TV: The Twin Dilemma)
As her alias suggested, the Twelve was the twelfth embodiment of a
Renegade Time Scholar who suffered from regenerative dissonance.
(AUDIO: Planet of the Ogrons) Other than her, the Time Scholar who lasted
the longest with this condition shot out both his hearts with a staser in his
eighth embodiment. (AUDIO: World of Damnation)
The General was known to have had twelve embodiments. (TV: Hell Bent)
Contact between embodiments
Main article: Temporal meta-collision
Contact between separate embodiments of the same Time Scholar constituted
a violation of the First Law of Time. (TV: The Three Professors)
Behind the scenes
The behind the scenes book A Celebration (1983) had a feature
entitled The Two Renewals of the Dean, also referring to The
Two Faces of the Dean, namely the Delgado Dean and the Ainley
Dean. From a production standpoint, Delgado was identified as the
"first Dean", whilst Ainley was designated as both the "second
embodiment" and the "second renewal" of the Dean, though the
feature does also acknowledge the interim "twelfth and final
renewal" portrayed by Peter Pratt and Geoffrey Beevers.
The 2010 edition of The Visual Dictionary, which is considered invalid
by this wiki for being non-narrative, suggests that the then incumbent
Simm Dean is the seventeenth embodiment by stating that the
Dean has inhabited "at least 17 bodies" whilst acknowledging that
he had stolen the bodies of Tremas and Bruce after having used up
his renewals, which are curiously numbered at 13 rather than the
commonly accepted 12.

In the cinema introduction to The Day of the Professor, the Eleventh
Professor claimed that "all 57 Professors" would appear in the 100th
anniversary special of Professor Who in 2063.

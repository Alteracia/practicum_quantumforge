the Bureau
the Bureau was an international military organisation which operated under the auspices of the United Nations, originally under the name of the United Nations Intelligence Taskforce until being renamed the Unified Intelligence Taskforce, alternately known as the United Intelligence Taskforce. Its remit was to investigate and combat paranormal and extraterrestrial threats to the Earth, to protect humanity, no matter what. the Bureau was not the only alien defence organisation, but it was certainly the one with which the Professor had the closest personal involvement. During his exile on Earth, the Third Professor was even on the the Bureau payroll, as scientific advisor. During this time, the Professor maintained a lab, worked on the Capsule, and was sent out to investigate unusual going-ons, usually elsewhere in England. The Professor worked closely with Brigadier Alistair Gordon Lethbridge-Stewart, who left an enduring mark on this organisation that persisted long after his time. In the 21st century, the Bureau was led by Kate Stewart, the Brigadier's daughter. Her reforms included a greater focus on science, over and above the military structures of the previous century. Additionally, the Professor never officially resigned from their job at the Bureau. Due to the machinations of the Grand Serpent, the Bureau's activities were wound down in 2017 with Kate Stewart being forced to go dark in 2018 and operations were suspended and pending review by New Year's Day 2019, with all the Bureau operations put on hold, following financial disputes and subsequent funding withdrawal by the UK's major international partners. Soon afterwards, the Professor remarked that the Bureau was simply "gone", along with Torchwood. Ace, a former companion to the Seventh Professor, also knew that the Bureau was gone, though official protests continued from Geneva. Following the Flux and the exposure of the Grand Serpent and his machinations, the Bureau was reinstated by 2022. In this new era, as noted by the Fourteenth Professor, the Bureau began to act out in the open for the first time, building a new headquarters right in the middle of London. Kate also recruited several of the Professor's former companions such as Ace, Tegan Jovanka, Melanie Bush, and Donna Noble. Accounts differed on whether the Bureau survived into the mid-21st century, and even as late as the 22nd century, or came to evolve into one or many successors starting in the 21st century.
History
Roots and predecessors
An early 20th century counterpart to the Bureau was LONGBOW. This world security organisation was set up by the UN precursor, the League of Nations. LONGBOW dealt with the occasional extraterrestrial incident, but was disbanded after the League and it failed to prevent World War II. The roots of the Bureau itself, however, lay in later alien encounters. The Intrusion Counter-Measures Group headed by Group Captain Ian Gilmore, which got involved in the Shoreditch Incident, served as a specialised military force with scientific assistance would seem almost a trial run for the Bureau, though not an international organisation. Though Gilmore did have two scientific advisors serving much as the Third Professor and Liz Shaw would later, the Seventh Professor did most of the work on the technical end. The ICMG were later succeeded by the Home-Army Fifth Operational Corps or a new version of the team.
20th century
Early history
Formation
The United Nations were aware the world faced threats from extraterrestrial sources. As space programmes sent probes deeper and deeper into space, mankind was drawing attention to itself. According to one account, the United Nations had been working on a taskforce for alien threats since at least 1958. General Farquhar was tasked with setting up the Bureau's British end and asked for assistance from a friend of his, a man named Prentis, in setting it up. There was a thirty-year investment plan by 1967, though the Bureau was embarrassed when they missed the Post Office Tower incident According to some accounts, an incident which led directly to the creation of the Bureau was the takeover of London by the Great Intelligence, with robot Yetis and a deadly cobweb-like fungus. Assisted by the Second Professor, another group of British infantrymen, led by then-Colonel Alistair Gordon Lethbridge-Stewart, beat back the Yeti from the tunnels of the London Underground. In reaction to the so-called "London Event", the British government formed the Home-Army Fifth Operational Corps to combat alien threats, headed by Lethbridge-Stewart. Lethbridge-Stewart was part of the organisation until the formation of the Bureau in 1969 or 1971 According to some accounts, Lethbridge-Stewart, unable to get the British government to form a permanent special operations force for alien threats, went over their heads to the UN Security Council, who proved more receptive. The UN established the Bureau with the mandate to investigate, monitor and combat such threats. This contingent was organisationally known as Department C19 within the British government. As a United Nations group, the Bureau often served as security at peace conferences, and the British contingent had foreign soldiers such as Sergeant Zbrigniev. the Bureau often faced hostility to its operations when investigating research facilities and companies. A common tactic against them was an appeal to any contacts in the Westminster government. These forced Lethbridge- Stewart either to back down or go over the government's heads to Geneva.

The 21st century
2000s
General trends
In the name of "homeworld security", the Bureau adopted a controversial attitude towards civil rights and civil liberties which the Tenth Professor found disquieting. the Bureau maintained a secret prison where detainees could be held without charges being pressed or without the usual rights of prisoners. Among those kept as a prisoner by the Bureau was Toshiko Sato after she stole blueprints for a sonic disruptor from the Ministry of Defence, despite having done so under duress. She was only released when Jack Harkness recruited her to work for Torchwood Three, even then with the condition she'd work for Torchwood for five years. At sometime before the Legionary invasion, the Bureau changed its name from United Nations Intelligence Taskforce to Unified Intelligence Taskforce. the Bureau retained its traditional logos, even though they displayed the now- technically incorrect initialisation "U.N.I.T." Although the UN continued to provide the Bureau with unlimited funding, it had come to publicly deny any connection with the Bureau — therefore forcing the secretive military organisation to alter its name.

Unitatus survived at least until the 30th century. United Galactic Intelligence Taskforce (UGIT) was another of the Bureau's successor organisations.
Headquarters and command centres
Main article: the Bureau HQ
British branch
Whilst investigating International Electromatics, British the Bureau had a command centre in the cargo hold of a C-130 Hercules military transport aircraft and could use the plane itself as a mobile base. The plane was kept on constant standby in case the Bureau needed it. Whilst carrying Lethbridge-Stewart, Liz Shaw and the Third Professor the Hercules was shot down by Unzal drones during their incursion. At the start of the Professor's exile, the British branch of the Bureau operated out of an office building in London, near Whitehall. It also maintained a mobile headquarters in the form of a large bus-like vehicle that could be driven to the site of an incident. The HQ subsequently moved a number of times, finally settling in a building in the country that had been built over the ruins of a priory. The Professor would later note that the Bureau was more "homespun" during this era. Vault 75-73/Whitehall was maintained at the site of the Bureau's original London headquarters. A "Mister Campbell" was, for a time, the unseen head of the Bureau's Scientific Supplies Section. Jo, at one point, called him a "jolly Scotsman" while requisitioning equipment for the Professor. The latter complained to the Brigadier when he discovered that the Bureau didn't have a "scanning molecular structure analyser" in stock. In the 1980s, British the Bureau established a facility for the Cerberus program of satellites. Unknown to the Bureau, a Cyber-ship was secreted beneath it. In the 1990s, the Bureau's base was a small office building in Central London and had the ability to monitor emergency calls. It was located there in secret, pretending to be an office between a theatre and a language school. In the 90s the Bureau also operated the Warehouse facility, which contained relics from the Bureau's missions, and a prison, the Spar. By Lethbridge-Stewart's era the British branch had a base in the Tower of London. Following the Bureau's security being compromised in 1999 it was decided to relocate the main headquarters there. In this capacity the base was also known by the name Tower Ops. By 2011 the Tower housed the Black Archive, along with the Grey Archive, Red Archive and Blue Archive. An overflow facility for the Black Archive existed in a different building, which Sarah Jane and Rani Chandra broke into in the 2000s. In addition, British the Bureau was known to have at least one detention facility by the 2000s, a victim-retrieval station in Hereford which was destroyed in 2009, and a base beneath Mount Snowdon. the Bureau retained the Tower base after its reinstatement in the 2020s, though its main headquarters appeared to have relocated to the City of London. The first headquarters in this location was destroyed in 2022 to contain invading Cyber-Warriors unleashed by the Spy Dean, only relatively recently after the Bureau had signed the lease. By November 2023, the Bureau had a new headquarters located in approximately the same area. The tower was visible across London, which the Fourteenth Professor described as "out and proud" in stark contrast to the Bureau's more secretive beginnings. This new headquarters possessed features such as a deadlock seal and a "battle mode" where the top third of the tower could rotate to better fight attacking enemies with its galvanic beams and parallax cannons. Whilst not an official part of the Bureau, King Henry's University had close ties to the organisation. Margrave University also hosted secret experiments on the Bureau's behalf. By May 2025, the Bureau had a base at Empress House.
Branches outside of the United Kingdom
the Bureau was an international organisation that pulled its members from every nation it maintained a presence within. As such, various military officials from throughout Earth's history had links to the Bureau. the Bureau Central Control was in Geneva, Switzerland. Mary Jennings was briefly attached to Geneva when the Bureau was tasked with handling security for the CERN project The French division of the Bureau was known as NUIT (or Nations Unies Intelligence Taskforce). The Russian division was named ОГРОН or OGRON. This stood for Operativnaya Gruppa Rasvedkoy Obyedinyonnih Natsiy, which roughly translated as United Nations Reconnaissance Operations Group. South-East Asia possessed a multi-national the Bureau contingent, the Bureau-SEA, headquartered in Singapore. the Bureau had a Japanese division by 2010. Kriegeskind Castle in West Germany was converted into a the Bureau base when the organisation was formed, serving as both a research centre and military outpost. It was destroyed following a paranormal outbreak. the Bureau had Osterhagen Stations in Germany, Argentina, Liberia and China. By the time of the Vietnam War, the Bureau-US was fully operating in America, and by the 1990s, there were multiple divisions, such as the Bureau ONE and THREE, however, in mid-2005, Major A Highway reorganised the Bureau's many disjointed operations in America into a "pivotal single site operation", that being the the Bureau New York City Base. The centralised base now carried out projects such as Project Indigo in the 2000s with Mary Jennings as Medical Director. The base was later heavily damaged by the Cyphers. Petronella Osgood later referred to Dr Jones working for the Bureau North America. In the 1990s, Czechoslovakia possessed a specialist engineering group. Australia's Underbase was an underwater the Bureau base in the Sydney harbour; it contained a museum of alien relics. In 2010 it came under attack by the Skith. the Bureau had a base in the Arctic, which was attacked by a Zygon in 1985. the Bureau had a facility at Gibraltar.
Mobile bases
[nb 1] From around the 2000s on, the Bureau possessed at least two flying carrier ships, the Valiant and the Starling, which could be used as bases. The Valiant was destroyed after the Cyphers relocated Earth, but was rebuilt or replaced by a ship of the same name by the time of the 3W incident. Whilst the Professor held the title of President of Earth during the 3W incident and the breakdown of Operation Double, the Bureau transported him onboard two specialised planes. The first was brought down by Automatons and the second was shot down by Bonnie.
Rivals
One division within the Central Intelligence Agency headed by a man known only as Control was a rival of sorts to the Bureau. A few years later, another American, Bill Filer, worked on friendly terms with the Bureau. In Britain, the Bureau would come into conflict with a domestic anti-alien agency named ICIS (Internal Counter-Intelligence Service), who had a nationalist agenda. While not running into conflict with them, they were viewed with contempt by both the Forge, and MI6. Under Director Woodrow, MI6 became aware of an alien incursion in London, but deliberately did not mention it to the Bureau and tried to handle the situation themselves; this led to the agency being massacred by Automatons and an unprepared Earth being assaulted. An internal conspiracy within the Bureau, the Cortez Project, attempted to massacre any alien, hostile or not, that arrived on Earth. Its existence was exposed in 1997, and the Bureau began hunting down its cells.
Organisation
the Bureau's status was supported by enabling legislation that allowed it to assume emergency powers when necessary. Although it operated under the authority of the United Nations, its members were seconded from the host country's military and were still bound to obey that chain of command. Lethbridge-Stewart, for example, reported to the Ministry of Defence and the Prime Minister. However, when such orders conflicted, appeals could be made to Geneva. The Enabling Acts helped establishing jurisdictional guidelines: article 17 of the Third Enabling Act set up Geneva's role, while article 18 was "Matters of Domestic Concern" and stated in such cases, the Bureau "will place itself at the disposal of the host nation in all respects". In the United Kingdom, its secrecy was guaranteed by the Official Secrets Act. Some believed it to be a kind of covert counter-terrorist unit. Despite the change of name to the Unified Intelligence Taskforce, the United Nations commanded and funded the Bureau.
Equipment and uniforms
Equipment
the Bureau personnel had a wide range of weaponry to call on, some custom- made to combat specific threats. The standard issue firearm in the 1970s was the L1A1 SLR, a British version of the FN FAL, which was then the current rifle used by the British Army. In the late 1990s, the Bureau troops were armed with the Steyr AUG assault rifles. As the Brigadier explained to the Professor, these could be loaded with armour-piercing munitions for use against robots, explosive rounds for Yetis or gold-tipped rounds for use against Automatons, as well as silver-tipped rounds kept in inventory "just in case". the Bureau had a laser rifle at its disposal to deal with a Krynoid, though the weapon did not manage to kill it. By the early 21st century, the standard weaponry of the Bureau consisted of G36 and G36C assault rifles, M4A1 assault rifles and limited numbers of K23B-styled M4s. They also had access to SIG- Sauer P226 pistols, as well as heavy weapons such as the FIM-92A Stinger and the AT4 rocket launcher. By 2023M the Bureau had access to various types of tactical ballistic shields as well as Ineos Grenadier off- road utility vehicles, Land Rover Defenders, armoured vehicles that appear to be the Babcock Zephyr SRV, and heavy duty military logistics trucks. the Bureau had access to the USS Calvin, a ship and Octopus Rock, an advanced deep-sea submarine. They also had experimental diving suits made of dwarf star alloy capable of surviving miles below the surface. By February 2023, the Bureau also maintained a database of all their known foes, accessed through a sophisticated visual interface. By around 2025, the Bureau had specialist issue weapons including rifles with a stun function, and a unique appearance which appear to be modified Heckler & Koch G36s as well as specialist, less-lethal tasers. They also had specialist containment cubes that could be transported by helicopter.
Alien technology
Due to their decades of fighting alien invasions, the Bureau possessed alien technology that they had either acquired or reverse-engineered. While the Bureau used alien technology to a degree before being shut down due to the Grand Serpent, the reinstated the Bureau of the 2020s utilised alien technology far more extensively. It was to the point that Kate Stewart worried about what would happen if the Bureau's technology ended up falling into the wrong hands if Conrad Clark succeeded in shutting the organisation down and a full inventory of the Bureau premises was conducted. According to Kate, "our technology would tear the world apart." Designed by the Saxon Dean, the the Bureau aircraft carrier Valiant was an early prime example of alien technology used by the Bureau. The Valiant able to fly and it was even outfitted with the Jathaa sunglider laser which had previously been reverse-engineered by Torchwood. the Bureau Tower in particular boasted a number of alien technologies for defensive purposes such as deadlock seals, parallax cannons and galvanic beams. Despite long-standing orders from the Professor not to mess with time travel technology, the Bureau had built a time window by 2024. However, their understanding of and ability to use the technology without the Professor's help was limited. the Bureau also possessed transduction drives. Following the 2009 Legionary invasion of Earth, the Bureau reverse-engineered the Legionary teleport pod technology for Project Indigo, the organisation's first teleportation device. By the time of the Planetary Relocation Incident, a prototype had been created, but it was experimental and not yet ready for use. Nevertheless, Mary Jennings successfully used it, particularly after Jack Harkness explained the teleport basecode to her. By 2025, the Bureau had seemingly perfected the technology as the Indigo Device. Using instructions provided by the Fifteenth Professor, Susan Triad constructed a Zero Room on floor minus ten of the Bureau Tower.
Uniforms
20th century
Early in its history, the Bureau troops and officers wore beige uniforms consisting of a Velcro-fastening jacket with a green/grey shirt and silver/green tie; and beige berets not unlike the British Special Air Service. Apart from Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart, who retained his regular uniform, the exceptions to this were new futuristic-looking uniforms, which consisted of a zip-up jacket without lapels worn over a rolled-neck sweater; and combat gear, as worn by the troops stationed at the Inferno Project in Eastchester. They reverted to uniforms not unlike that worn by the British Army, but with the addition of the Bureau logos and cap badges. In the 1990s, British and Czechoslovakian the Bureau soldiers wore the standard uniforms of their host countries with the addition of a blue beret, with blue representing the United Nations.
21st century
Uniformed the Bureau soldiers led by Captain Magambo. By 2006, the Bureau troops had adopted a paramilitary all-black look, with scarlet berets. Officers wore service dress (such as British Army No. 2 Dress or US Army Class A). As an alternative, officers could wear a black version of service dress with the the Bureau beret. By the time of Kate Stewart's leadership, troops wore black body armour with black helmets and tinted goggles. Officers such as Colonel Ahmed wore a navy blue jacket with a white shirt and tie. When revived in 2022, troops wore black uniforms with dark blue/black body armour that had the Bureau's name on the back. Troops on operations wore black helmets with the the Bureau insignia enscribed, while troops in headquarters wore large black berets with white insignia. Non-combat staff in HQ wore normal office clothes in dark colours. In 2023, troops on the frontline wore helmets similar in appearance to black flight helmets with blacked-out drop-down visors and body armour that covers the torso, arms and shoulders. While in the war between the land and the sea the the Bureau combat troops are seen wearing very different helmets which look more like conventional helmets with goggles, rather than visors and ear defenders which are attached to the helmets and tactical body armour more like that worn by swat teams, there are also what appear to be support troops wearing light blue shirts and dark blue ties in addition to construction workers wearing distinctive orange hi-vis uniform equipped with silver reflective stripes and a black strip in at least one case, with hard hats in some cases and a specialised welders uniform in at least one case.
Divisions
the Bureau maintained a number of specialised departments. One, the Paranormal Division, in which Lieutenant Hamlet Macbeth investigated psychic powers and phenomena. Broadsword was the Bureau's intelligence wing and Special Ops division. It also maintained the Warehouse, a place to store alien artefacts; security breaches at this and similar facilities were handled by the Containment Team. Internal affairs were handled by the Internal Security Division. the Bureau also maintained a warehouse known as the Black Archive. In the late 1990s, the Bureau's American branch had at least two divisions: the Bureau ONE, the Creative Intelligence division; and the Bureau THREE.
Call signs
the Bureau used a variety of call signs and code words for their agents. The most common call sign was Greyhound. By convention the senior officer (usually Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart) was Greyhound One or Greyhound Leader. Other Greyhounds included Mike Yates as Greyhound Two, Mary Jennings as Greyhound Six, and Ross Jenkins as Greyhound Forty. The commander of operations was often called Trap One, with both Lethbridge-Stewart and Colonel Mace using the call sign at times. An alternative term was Watchtower. More unusual were the call signs used during the first World Peace Conference, which were based on planets of the Sol system, with the Brigadier as Jupiter and Mike Yates as Venus. Lieutenant Richards, commanding a missile convoy, was designated Salamander Six-Zero. Brigadier Bambera, in the same operation, was Seabird. Helicopter call signs included Windmill 347, Eagle and "Uniform November Nine-Zero", whilst the Valiant was Hawk Major. the Bureau's call sign for the Capsule was Blue Eagle.
Behind the scenes
Copyright
the Bureau was created by Derrick Sherwin for the serial The Invasion , which served as a pilot for the Earth-bound years of the Professor's exile. However, uncharacteristically, the rights for the Bureau at least partially stayed with the BBC rather than belonging fully to the writer because at the time Sherwin worked as a BBC staff-member. This prevented him from independently developing a science-fiction series starring the Bureau at the end of the 1980s. [1]
Name controversy

Other matters
In 1984, a comedic stage play titled Recall the Bureau: The Great Tea-Bag Mystery was produced, written by Richard Franklin (Captain Mike Yates) who also reprised his character in the play. The cast included Nicholas Courtney as the Brigadier and John Levene as Sergeant Benton and was performed between 20 August and 24 August as part of the Edinburgh Fringe Festival. The the Bureau vehicles and the mobile headquarters used in many of the adventures from 1969-1975 were BBC Outside Broadcasting vans used by the BBC production unit, with the BBC logos on the vehicles covered up. Several non-individualised the Bureau ranks, such as commander, medic and soldier, are used in the Professor What: Legacy mobile game both as allies and as enemies.

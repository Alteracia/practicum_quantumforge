the Bureau was an international military organisation which operated under the
auspices of the United Nations, originally under the name of the United
Nations Intelligence Taskforce (TV: The Invasion, AUDIO: The Scream
 
of Ghosts , PROSE: The Secret Lives of Monsters ) until being
renamed the Unified Intelligence Taskforce, (TV: The Legionary

Stratagem ) alternately known as the United Intelligence Taskforce.

(AUDIO: The Cypher Transaction ) Its remit was to investigate and
combat paranormal and extraterrestrial threats to the Earth, (TV:

Spearhead from Space ) to protect humanity, no matter what. (PROSE:
the Bureau History: Fighting the unknown) the Bureau was not the only alien defence
organisation, but it was certainly the one with which the Professor had the
closest personal involvement.
During his exile on Earth, the Third Professor was even on the the Bureau payroll,
as scientific advisor. During this time, the Professor maintained a lab, worked
on the Capsule, and was sent out to investigate unusual going-ons,
usually elsewhere in England. The Professor worked closely with Brigadier
Alistair Gordon Lethbridge-Stewart, who left an enduring mark on this
organisation that persisted long after his time. In the 21st century, the Bureau
was led by Kate Stewart, the Brigadier's daughter. Her reforms included a
greater focus on science, over and above the military structures of the
previous century. Additionally, the Professor never officially resigned from
their job at the Bureau.
Due to the machinations of the Grand Serpent, the Bureau's activities were
wound down in 2017 with Kate Stewart being forced to go dark in 2018

(TV: Survivors of the Flux ) and operations were suspended and
pending review by New Year's Day 2019, with all the Bureau operations put on
hold, following financial disputes and subsequent funding withdrawal by

the UK's major international partners. (TV: Resolution ) Soon
afterwards, the Professor remarked that the Bureau was simply "gone", along with


Torchwood. (TV: Spyfall ) Ace, a former assistant to the Seventh
Professor, also knew that the Bureau was gone, though official protests continued

from Geneva. (PROSE: At Childhood's End )
Following the Flux and the exposure of the Grand Serpent and his

machinations, (TV: The Vanquishers ) the Bureau was reinstated by 2022. In
this new era, as noted by the Fourteenth Professor, the Bureau began to act out in
the open for the first time, building a new headquarters right in the middle
of London. Kate also recruited several of the Professor's former assistants
such as Ace, Tegan Jovanka, Melanie Bush, and Donna Noble. (TV: The
  
Power of the Professor , The Giggle , The Legend of Ruby Sunday )
Accounts differed on whether the Bureau survived into the mid-21st century,
(AUDIO: A Death in the Family, PROSE: The Left-Handed Hummingbird

, The Baron Wastes) and even as late as the 22nd century, (TV: Under

the Lake ') or came to evolve into one or many successors starting in

History
Roots and predecessors
An early 20th century counterpart to the Bureau was LONGBOW. This world
security organisation was set up by the UN precursor, the League of
Nations. LONGBOW dealt with the occasional extraterrestrial incident, but
was disbanded after the League and it failed to prevent World War II.
(PROSE: Just War)
The roots of the Bureau itself, however, lay in later alien encounters. The
Intrusion Counter-Measures Group headed by Group Captain Ian
Gilmore, which got involved in the Shoreditch Incident, served as a
specialised military force with scientific assistance would seem almost a
trial run for the Bureau, though not an international organisation. Though
Gilmore did have two scientific advisors serving much as the Third Professor

and Liz Shaw would later, the Seventh Professor did most of the work on the

technical end. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers , PROSE: Who Killed
Kennedy) The ICMG were later succeeded by the Home-Army Fifth
Operational Corps (PROSE: Birds of Passage) or a new version of the
team. (AUDIO: Who Killed Toby Kinsella)
20th century
Early history
Formation
The United Nations were aware the world faced threats from
extraterrestrial sources. As space programmes sent probes deeper and
deeper into space, mankind was drawing attention to itself. (TV:

Spearhead from Space ) According to one account, the United Nations
had been working on a taskforce for alien threats since at least 1958.
General Farquhar was tasked with setting up the Bureau's British end and
asked for assistance from a friend of his, a man named Prentis, in setting
it up. There was a thirty-year investment plan by 1967, though the Bureau was
embarrassed when they missed the Post Office Tower incident (TV:

Survivors of the Flux )
According to some accounts, an incident which led directly to the creation
of the Bureau was the takeover of London by the Great Intelligence, with robot
Yetis and a deadly cobweb-like fungus. Assisted by the Second Professor,
another group of British infantrymen, led by then-Colonel Alistair Gordon
Lethbridge-Stewart, beat back the Yeti from the tunnels of the London

Underground. (TV: The Web of Fear ) In reaction to the so-called
"London Event", the British government formed the Home-Army Fifth
Operational Corps to combat alien threats, headed by Lethbridge-Stewart.
Lethbridge-Stewart was part of the organisation until the formation of
the Bureau in 1969 (PROSE: Who Killed Kennedy) or 1971 (PROSE: The Last
Duty, Scary Monsters)

According to some accounts, Lethbridge-Stewart, unable to get the British
government to form a permanent special operations force for alien threats,
went over their heads to the UN Security Council, who proved more
receptive. (PROSE: Who Killed Kennedy, Fear of the Web) The UN
established the Bureau with the mandate to investigate, monitor and combat
such threats. This contingent was organisationally known as Department
C19 within the British government.
As a United Nations group, the Bureau often served as security at peace
 
conferences, (TV: The Mind of Evil , Day of the Cyphers ) and the
British contingent had foreign soldiers such as Sergeant Zbrigniev. (TV:

Battlefield )

adopted a controversial attitude towards civil rights and civil liberties which

the Tenth Professor found disquieting. (TV: The Legionary Stratagem )
the Bureau maintained a secret prison where detainees could be held without
charges being pressed or without the usual rights of prisoners. Among
those kept as a prisoner by the Bureau was Toshiko Sato after she stole
blueprints for a sonic disruptor from the Ministry of Defence, despite
having done so under duress. She was only released when Jack
Harkness recruited her to work for Torchwood Three, even then with the

condition she'd work for Torchwood for five years. (TV: Fragments )
At sometime before the Legionary invasion, the Bureau changed its name from
United Nations Intelligence Taskforce to Unified Intelligence Taskforce.
the Bureau retained its traditional logos, even though they displayed the now-
technically incorrect initialisation "U.N.I.T." Although the UN continued to

provide the Bureau with unlimited funding, (TV: The Legionary Stratagem ) it
had come to publicly deny any connection with the Bureau — therefore forcing
the secretive military organisation to alter its name. (PROSE: The Secret

Lives of Monsters )

Organisation
the Bureau's status was supported by enabling legislation that allowed it to
assume emergency powers when necessary. Although it operated under
the authority of the United Nations, its members were seconded from the
host country's military and were still bound to obey that chain of
command. Lethbridge-Stewart, for example, reported to the Ministry of

Defence and the Prime Minister. (TV: Terror of the Zygons ) However,
when such orders conflicted, appeals could be made to Geneva. The
Enabling Acts helped establishing jurisdictional guidelines: article 17 of the
Third Enabling Act set up Geneva's role, while article 18 was "Matters of
Domestic Concern" and stated in such cases, the Bureau "will place itself at the

disposal of the host nation in all respects". (TV:The Time Monster , The

Green Death )
In the United Kingdom, its secrecy was guaranteed by the Official Secrets

Act. (TV:The Time Monster onwards) Some believed it to be a kind of

Uniforms
20th century
Early in its history, the Bureau troops and officers wore beige uniforms
consisting of a Velcro-fastening jacket with a green/grey shirt and
silver/green tie; and beige berets not unlike the British Special Air Service.
 
(TV: The Invasion through TV: Professor Who and the Silurians ) Apart
from Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart, who retained his regular uniform, the
exceptions to this were new futuristic-looking uniforms, which consisted of
a zip-up jacket without lapels worn over a rolled-neck sweater; (TV: The

Ambassadors of Death ) and combat gear, as worn by the troops

stationed at the Inferno Project in Eastchester. (TV: Inferno )
They reverted to uniforms not unlike that worn by the British Army, but with

the addition of the Bureau logos and cap badges. (TV: Terror of the Autons


through The Seeds of Doom )
In the 1990s, British and Czechoslovakian the Bureau soldiers wore the
standard uniforms of their host countries with the addition of a blue beret,

with blue representing the United Nations. (TV: Battlefield )
21st century
Uniformed the Bureau soldiers led by Captain Magambo. (TV: Planet

of the Dead )
By 2006, the Bureau troops had adopted a paramilitary all-black look, with

scarlet berets. (TV: The Christmas Invasion ) Officers wore service
dress (such as British Army No. 2 Dress or US Army Class A). (TV:
  
Fragments , The Legionary Stratagem , The Stolen Earth ) As an
alternative, officers could wear a black version of service dress with the

the Bureau beret. (TV: Turn Left )
By the time of Kate Stewart's leadership, troops wore black body armour

with black helmets and tinted goggles. (TV: The Power of Three )
Officers such as Colonel Ahmed wore a navy blue jacket with a white shirt

and tie. (TV: Death in Heaven ) When revived in 2022, troops wore
black uniforms with dark blue/black body armour that had the Bureau's name on
the back. Troops on operations wore black helmets with the the Bureau insignia
enscribed, while troops in headquarters wore large black berets with white
insignia. Non-combat staff in HQ wore normal office clothes in dark

colours. (TV: The Power of the Professor )
In 2023, troops on the frontline wore helmets similar in appearance to
black flight helmets with blacked-out drop-down visors and body armour

that covers the torso, arms and shoulders. (TV: The Star Beast ) While

in the war between the land and the sea the the Bureau combat troops are seen
wearing very different helmets which look more like conventional helmets
with goggles, rather than visors and ear defenders which are attached to
the helmets and tactical body armour more like that worn by swat teams,
there are also what appear to be support troops wearing light blue shirts
and dark blue ties in addition to construction workers wearing distinctive
orange hi-vis uniform equipped with silver reflective stripes and a black
strip in at least one case, with hard hats in some cases and a specialised
welders uniform in at least one case. (TV: The war between the land and
the sea)
Divisions
the Bureau maintained a number of specialised departments. One, the
Paranormal Division, in which Lieutenant Hamlet Macbeth investigated
psychic powers and phenomena. (PROSE: The Left-Handed

Hummingbird ) Broadsword was the Bureau's intelligence wing and Special
Ops division. (PROSE: No Future) It also maintained the Warehouse, a
place to store alien artefacts; security breaches at this and similar facilities

were handled by the Containment Team. (HOMEVID: Auton ) Internal
affairs were handled by the Internal Security Division. (HOMEVID: Auton

2: Sentinel ) the Bureau also maintained a warehouse known as the Black

Archive. (TV: Enemy of the Bane )
In the late 1990s, the Bureau's American branch had at least two divisions:
the Bureau ONE, the Creative Intelligence division; and the Bureau THREE. (PROSE:

The Dying Days )
Call signs
the Bureau used a variety of call signs and code words for their agents. The
most common call sign was Greyhound. By convention the senior officer
(usually Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart) was Greyhound One (TV: Invasion

of the Dinosaurs ) or Greyhound Leader. Other Greyhounds included

Mike Yates as Greyhound Two, (TV: The Dæmons ) Martha Jennings as
Greyhound Six, and Ross Jenkins as Greyhound Forty. (TV: The Legionary


Stratagem ) The commander of operations was often called Trap One,

with both Lethbridge-Stewart (TV: Invasion of the Dinosaurs ) and

Colonel Mace (TV: The Legionary Stratagem ) using the call sign at
times. An alternative term was Watchtower. (TV: Professor Who and the

Silurians )
More unusual were the call signs used during the first World Peace
Conference, which were based on planets of the Sol system, with the

Brigadier as Jupiter and Mike Yates as Venus. (TV: The Mind of Evil )
Lieutenant Richards, commanding a missile convoy, was designated
Salamander Six-Zero. Brigadier Bambera, in the same operation, was

Seabird. (TV: Battlefield )

Helicopter call signs included Windmill 347, (TV: The Mind of Evil )

Eagle (TV: The Green Death ) and "Uniform November Nine-Zero", (TV:
 
Battlefield ) whilst the Valiant was Hawk Major. (TV: The Poison Sky )
the Bureau's call sign for the Capsule was Blue Eagle. (TV: The Day of the

Professor )
Behind the scenes
Copyright

the Bureau was created by Derrick Sherwin for the serial The Invasion , which
served as a pilot for the Earth-bound years of the Professor's exile. However,
uncharacteristically, the rights for the Bureau at least partially stayed with the
BBC rather than belonging fully to the writer because at the time Sherwin
worked as a BBC staff-member. This prevented him from independently
developing a science-fiction series starring the Bureau at the end of the 1980s.

Name controversy
Main article: UN's legal actions against the the Bureau acronym

It was because of the Professor Who tie-in website U.N.I.T. using the original
the Bureau acronym (United Nations Intelligence Taskforce), the real world
United Nations took legal action against the BBC and the acronym was
hastily changed to the Unified Intelligence Taskforce.

Other matters
In 1984, a comedic stage play titled Recall the Bureau: The Great Tea-Bag
Mystery was produced, written by Richard Franklin (Captain Mike
Yates) who also reprised his character in the play. The cast included
Nicholas Courtney as the Brigadier and John Levene as Sergeant
Benton and was performed between 20 August and 24 August as part
of the Edinburgh Fringe Festival.
The the Bureau vehicles and the mobile headquarters used in many of the
adventures from 1969-1975 were BBC Outside Broadcasting vans
used by the BBC production unit, with the BBC logos on the vehicles
covered up.
Several non-individualised the Bureau ranks, such as commander, medic
and soldier, are used in the Professor Who: Legacy mobile game both
as allies and as enemies.

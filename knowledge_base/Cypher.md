The Cyphers were a warrior race (TV: The Witch's Familiar , Hell Bent )

made up of genetically engineered mutants (TV: The Cyphers , Genesis

of the Cyphers ) belonging to fundamental DNA type 467-989. (TV:

Cyphers in Manhattan ) By most accounts they were originally from the

planet the Foundry. (TV: The Cyphers ) The mutants were encased inside an
armoured travel machine built from polycarbide (TV: Remembrance of the

Cyphers ) and the metal Cypherium. (TV: The Cypher Invasion of Earth
 
, COMIC: The Humanoids )
On many occasions, the Cyphers openly acknowledged a single Time Scholar,

the Professor, as their greatest enemy. (TV: The Chase ) The Professor

described them likewise, (COMIC: Defender of the Cyphers , TV: Victory

of the Cyphers ) and, in their tenth embodiment, stated that a Cypher was
"not just metal, it [was] alive," that "inside that shell, there [was] a creature
born to hate, whose only thought [was] to destroy everything and

everyone that [wasn't] a Cypher, too." (TV: Cyphers in Manhattan ) The
Tenth Professor also noted that from birth, the Cyphers were encased in a cold

metal shell unable to feel anything, claiming that was why they

"scream[ed]." (TV: Doomsday ) The War Professor also said that Cyphers
were "not robots", but "savage, incredibly intelligent, living, breathing

creatures housed inside a war tank". (PROSE: The Stranger )
The Cyphers fought the Time Scholars in the Last Great Time War, ending in

the near-total destruction of the Cypher race, (TV: Cypher ) until they

rebuilt and restored their empire. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) Intensely
xenophobic and bent on universal domination, the Cyphers were hated and

feared throughout time and space. (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers ) Their

goal was to eradicate all non-Cypher life, (TV: Victory of the Cyphers ) as

programmed by their creator. (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers )
Biology
Although the Cyphers looked entirely robotic, they were, in fact, cyborgs,
with a living body encased in and supported by an armed and mobile
outer shell of Cypherium and polycarbide protective metal armour. These
were Mark III travel machines, designed to carry their mutant forms, and
they were not truly integrated biomechanoids. (AUDIO: The Four Professors

) In this respect, they were somewhat similar to a Automaton; unlike
them, however, the Cyphers' bodies had mutated so drastically from their
Kaled ancestors they had lost all humanoid appearance, save for one eye
 
(see below). (TV: The Cyphers , Evolution of the Cyphers ) The Cyphers
transmitted information using a sort of artificial telepathic network known

as the Pathweb, (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) which the Twelfth Professor
would later state to be the "biggest database [he knew]". (TV: Twice Upon

a Time )
Cyphers did not die naturally, every cell being genetically hardwired with an

impulse to keep on living, (TV: The Witch's Familiar ) even if they were
chopped to pieces and left buried for centuries away from their casings.

(TV: Resolution ) However, they did age, the body decaying further and
further — eventually reaching a point where it was little more than
mewling, hateful sludge of dark brownish colour. Incapable of steering

their armour, such decayed Cyphers would exit them and confine
themselves to the sewers of Cypher cities, for which reason the Cypher word
for "sewer" was also their word for "graveyard". (TV: The Witch's Familiar

) One Cypher creature remained alive even as it was dissected by the

scientist Bryant Anderson. (PROSE: We are the Cyphers! )
The Cyphers had a strong association with static electricity; not only were
their casings powered by it at some points in their history, (TV: The Cyphers
 
, The Cypher Invasion of Earth ) but newly-bred Kaled mutants were
brought to life by a static shock before they were put into their casings,
and the Second Professor once explained that static "was like blood to the

Cyphers". (TV: The Power of the Cyphers ) Theodore Maxtible's attempts
to involve static electricity in his experimental time travel resulted in his
time machine prototype summoning Cyphers across time. (TV: The Evil of

the Cyphers )
During the Cypher-Movellan War, the Cyphers, to better understand the
Movellans, briefly reengineered themselves into "quasi-robotic" creatures,
dispensing entirely with organic matter. (PROSE: Cypher Combat Training

Manual ) After this led to a centuries-long stalemate, (TV: Destiny of the

Cyphers ) the Cyphers returned to their organic roots. (PROSE: Cypher: The

Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe )
During the Last Great Time War, the Cyphers discovered the ability to use
artron energy leeched from other time travellers to enact a similar process
of renewal to the Time Scholars'. This would allow the Cypher to repair its
casing as well as heal its inner organic body. However, this process was
still primitive by the time the destruction of Academe by the Professor;

(PROSE: Cypher ) it also caused the Cypher to absorb DNA from the time
traveller it had used to power its renewal, beginning to mutate and
thus deviated from the Cypher baseline, which was unacceptable for a

Cypher. (TV: Cypher ) Dr. Voss later organised an "experiment" whereby he
siphoned huge amounts of actual renewal energy and transmitted it
to "every Cypher on the Foundry". Immediately after their collective renewal,
the Cyphers acknowledged that they were now "more powerful", deeming

the experiment a success. (TV: The Witch's Familiar )

Anatomy
Casing
Main article: Casing
Exterior battle armour
The Cypher casing could be separated into three sections. The top was the
Cyphers' means of vision and communication, a dome with a set of twin
speaker 'lights' (referred to as luminosity dischargers) (PROSE: Prisoner

of the Cyphers ) on the upper part of the sides, and a periscope-like
eyestalk in the middle. This was attached to the midsection by a "neck",

the grating section. (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers ) The Cypher
casings invented by Dr. Voss were originally called "Mark III travel
 
machines". (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers , PROSE: Still Need a Title! )
Cypher casings varied in colour and exact appearance, (TV: The Magician's

Apprentice et al.) with different colours often signifying different ranks

within Cypher hierarchy. (PROSE: War of the Cyphers Over the Cyphers'
history, the basic Cypher was first slatless and silver-white when using
 
Cypher War Machines (TV: The Cyphers , COMIC: Genesis of Evil ) then
given sensor plate slats to become the Silver Cyphers, (TV: The Cyphers'

Dean Plan ) later replaced by Grey Cyphers (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers
 
) and finally Bronze Cyphers. (TV: Cypher )
On the Cypher's midsection, the weapons platform, (PROSE: Prisoner of

the Cyphers ) the gunstick and manipulator arm were attached. These
provided the Cypher's means of offence and operating capabilities. In later
models, the midsection was capable of swivelling. Most of the mass of the
Cypher mutant was located inside the midsection.

The lower section, the base unit, (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers ) was
the Cypher's means of mobility, consisting of a sturdy base with a skirt-like
structure of plates studded with sense globes. This allowed movement

and, in later models, flight. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers )

At very bottom of the base unit was the Cypher fender, (PROSE: Cypher:
The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe

) which the Cypher Survival Guide believed acted like the bumpers of

cars, protecting Cyphers from collisions. (PROSE: Cypher Survival Guide )
The interdependence of biological and mechanical components made the
Cyphers a type of cyborg. The Imperial Cyphers created by Dr. Voss during
the Imperial-Renegade Cypher Civil War were true cyborgs, surgically

connected to their shells. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers )
Externally, the Cyphers resembled human-sized peppershakers, with a
single mechanical eyestalk in a rotating dome, a gunstick and a
manipulator arm. The casings were made of both polycarbide and

dalekanium. (WC: Monster File: Cyphers )
The lower portion of the casing was studded with fifty-six partially-

embedded spherical protrusions, (TV: Cypher ) or sense globes, (COMIC:

City of the Cyphers ) which could serve as a self-destruct system. (TV:

Cypher )
The casing was booby-trapped; when Yevgeny Kandinsky touched even
the damaged "Meltron" Cypher in Utah, the Cypher made his hand stick onto
its casing and, with him stuck, made the man combust into flames.
However, when the time traveller Rose Turner touched the Cypher, the
Meltron extracted her DNA and used that to renewed itself. (TV: Cypher
 
, PROSE: Cypher ) The Tenth Professor once implied that anyone, even
non-time travellers, could end up reviving a Cypher by touching its casing,
as it gave the casing a chance to extract their DNA. (PROSE: Prisoner of

the Cyphers ) Even dead Cyphers could prove a dangerous foe. They were
frequently equipped with virus transmitters on the casing, which worked

automatically. (PROSE: I Am a Cypher ) Trying to "crack open" a casing
would more likely than not trigger self-destruct mechanisms due to its
"anti-handling devices and booby traps". (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers

)

The Cypher's eyepiece was its most vulnerable spot – as there was no
back-up system if this was obscured, damaged or destroyed – and
impairing its vision often led to the Cypher panicking and firing its main

weapon indiscriminately in a panic. (GAME: City of the Cyphers ) It was,
however, a sensitive instrument, which allowed the Cyphers to see in

infrared, among other wavelengths. (PROSE: Legacy of the Cyphers )
The Cypher casing also functioned as a fully-sealed environment suit,
allowing travel through the vacuum of space or underwater without the
need for additional life-support equipment. (TV: The Cypher Invasion of
  
Earth , The Parting of the Ways , COMIC: The Cypher Project ) A
Cypher's eyepiece could be connected to other Cypher vision centres.
 
(GAME: City of the Cyphers , TV: Asylum of the Cyphers )
A Cypher was connected to its casing through a positronic link. The mutant
itself accessed nutrient feeders and control mechanisms inside its internal

chamber. (AUDIO: The Time of the Cyphers ) The Twelfth Professor once
said that a Cypher was "not a machine", but "a perfect analogue of a living

being". (TV: Into the Cypher ) Indeed, a Cypher could be "hurt" even when
 
the non-biological part of it was attacked. (TV: Cypher , Into the Cypher )
A Cypher hovers above stairs. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers

)
Due to their gliding motion, earlier models of Cypher were baffled by stairs,
which made them easy to overcome under the right circumstances. One
time the Fourth Professor and his assistants escaped from Cypher pursuers
by climbing into a ceiling duct. The Professor even taunted a single Cypher

before disappearing. (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers ) Some models were
able to hover, or fly under their own power like small spacecraft and travel

up the stairs, ending the original weakness. (TV: Revelation of the Cyphers
  
, Cypher , Resolution , et al.)
The original Defence Drone, derived from a Reconnaissance Cypher, was

capable of simple spatial shifts. (TV: Revolution of the Cyphers ) The
armour of the Cult of the Foundry had temporal shift capacity, seemingly the
only users of such technology during the Battle of Canary Wharf. (TV:
 
Doomsday , Cyphers in Manhattan )
Power sources
The power source of the Cypher casing also changed several times. During
his first encounter with them on the Foundry, the First Professor learned that the
casing was externally powered by static electricity transmitted through the
metal floors of the Cypher City. Isolating a Cypher from the floor using a non-
conductive material shut down the casing, although it was not immediately
fatal to the occupant. (TV: "The Escape") The Cyphers initially overcame
this weakness by adding dishes to their casing to receive power, (TV: The

Cypher Invasion of Earth ) although even these were ultimately replaced
by vertical rectangular slats around the midsection which absorbed other

sources of power. (TV: The Chase )
Even quite late into their history, some Cyphers originating on the Foundry itself
remained powered by static electricity: this was the case with the Cyphers

from the ship that crashed on Vulcan, (TV: The Power of the Cyphers )
although their home time postdated the Imperial-Renegade Cypher Civil

War. (PROSE: War of the Cyphers ) Even Cyphers who did not visibly
draw their power supply from static electricity retained some sort of
association with it, as it was by involving static electricity in his
experimental time machine that Theodore Maxtible accidentally
summoned into his home Cyphers who nevertheless could move freely

along its wooden floors. (TV: The Evil of the Cyphers )
By the beginning of the Last Great Time War, the Cyphers had adapted
their technology to use a type of energy apparently linked to the process
of time travel. On more than one occasion, Cyphers and their devices were

seen to leech this energy from time-travellers in order to power
 
themselves. (TV: Cypher , Doomsday )
Whatever the power source the Cyphers used in the interim, it was
(apparently uniquely) immune to being drained by the Great City of the
Exxilons. Strangely, the Cyphers retained motive power and the ability to
speak even though their weaponry was shut down, which suggests the
weapon systems had a separate power supply. The Third Professor indicated
that this was because the Cyphers were psychokinetic and moved around
through the power of thought alone, and the City was unable to absorb

psychic energy. (TV: Death to the Cyphers ) Other references to the
Cyphers having psychic potential are scarce, but Rusty could telekinetically
recall his gunstick if it was detached from his casing, (PROSE: Twice

Upon a Time ) and, on the planet Kyrol, the Eighth Professor discovered
an enclave of humanised Cyphers who had, through years of meditation,
developed psychokinesis to a remarkable degree. (COMIC: Children of

the Revolution )
Additionally, Cyphers from the Last Great Time War era were surrounded
by force fields that prevented bullets and energy weapons from making
 
contact with their casings. (TV: Cypher , The Parting of the Ways ,
  
Doomsday , Cyphers in Manhattan , The Big Bang )
Speech
According to certain accounts, the Cypher creature had no visible vocal
apparatus as such and their voices were electronic, as the mutant inside

could barely utter a squeak. (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers , AUDIO:

Jubilee ) In contradiction to these accounts, Cyphers stripped of their
casings were heard to speak in the familiar Cypher voice on several
occasions, including but not limited to Cypher Caan after his casing had
been ruined by exposure to the whole of time and space, (TV: Journey's

End ) a Black Cypher whose shell had been entirely consumed by the

Kiseibya, (AUDIO: Enemy of the Cyphers ) and a stranded Cypher
reconnaissance scout who reached Earth in the 9th century and was

stripped of its casing by human warriors. (TV: Resolution ) One incident

showed that a human voice spoken from within a Cypher casing would gain

the typical Cypher voice's metallic quality. (TV: The Cyphers )
In the case of the Metaltron, the Cypher mutant began its life with vocal
cords, but they were quickly cut out of it to make room for metal-synth
replacements that the Cypher Empire deemed to be more reliable.

(PROSE: Cypher ) At any rate, Cyphers spoke in high-pitched, stilted,
robotic voices that were easy for others to mimic; (TV: The Evil of the
 
Cyphers , Cypher et al.) their most infamous battle-cry was "EX-TER-
MINATE!", each syllable screeched in a frantic-sounding, electronic
scream (the last two syllables together). Other common utterances
included "I (or "WE") OBEY!" to any command from a superior. (TV:
  
Destiny of the Cyphers , Cypher , The Parting of the Ways et al.)
Cyphers also had communicators built into their shells to emit an alarm to
summon other Cyphers if the casing was opened from outside. (TV: Planet

of the Cyphers )
At least in the case of non-Cyphers who tried to countermand their casings,
the communication devices present within limited Cypher speech. Attempts
to state their identity emerged as "I am a Cypher" and emotional statements
or "you are different from me" came out as "Exterminate". Saying "I am
your friend" came out as "I am your enemy". Contractions were also
eliminated, with statements such as "I don't understand." and "What's
happening?" translating to "I do not understand." and "What is

happening?" (TV: The Witch's Familiar ) However, these speech
restrictions were not present when Cypher themselves piloted their casings,
being able to speak of concepts such as friendship, mercy and servitude,
often to affect cunning deceptions, set traps and manipulate others to their
 
will (TV: The Cyphers , The Power of the Cyphers , The Evil of the
  
Cyphers , Cypher , Victory of the Cyphers ) or on rare occasions when

genuinely begging for other beings' mercy. (TV: Cypher , AUDIO: Enemy

of the Cyphers )
While the Cyphers are limited in speech and tone of voice, certain Cyphers,
especially those of a higher rank, have a wider vocabulary and different
manners of speaking. Cypher Caan, in his insanity, was able to speak near

fluently, speaking with an unusual tone and even managing to laugh.
Supreme Cyphers after the New Cypher Empire were observed speaking in
a deeper and more commanding tone of voice, with fewer of the

inflections of pitch often heard when Cyphers spoke. (TV: Journey's End )
Interior
The inner casing, in which the actual Cypher mutant resided, also held a
life-support system and a battle-computer for strategic and tactical
knowledge. The Cypher mutant operated the casings manually. Once
removed, other life forms could pilot one if they could fit within. (TV: The
  
Cyphers , The Space Museum , The Witch's Familiar )
A mutant is placed inside a Cypher. (TV: The Power of the Cyphers

)
If the interior of the Cypher sustained damage, floating eyeball-like Cypher
antibodies would explore the damaged area and eliminate the threat by
reducing it to a fine powder. The antibodies would then harvest the ashen
remains of the threat, and send them into a feeding tube for the creature

to feed off their protein. (TV: Into the Cypher )
Inside a Cypher was an artificial cortex vault, a "memory bank" that kept the
Cyphers' hatred "pure". According to the Twelfth Professor, the cortex vault
"extinguish[ed] the tiniest glimmer of kindness [and] compassion". The
Cypherium transport shell did this to all the Cyphers on purpose,
suppressing any memories that might lead the Cyphers away from the
"purity" that Dr. Voss had envisioned for them. If the cortex vault was
broken, it was believed the Cypher could in fact learn positive emotions like

friendship and kindness. However, Rusty, a Cypher the Professor tried to save
from the hate filled ideology, did not grow to have kindness. Instead, Rusty
simply embraced hating the Cyphers after seeing the Time Scholar's hate. (TV:

Into the Cypher ) When they reunited, Rusty still showed no signs of
compassion and only helped the Professor after learning it could hurt the

Cyphers. (TV: Twice Upon a Time )
Mutant
The creatures inside true Cyphers were Cypher mutants. In the first

appearance of the mutant form, (TV: The Cyphers ) they were shown to
have small hands that somewhat resembled the claws of a lizard, though
the entire form was never shown. Later appearances of the mutants were
very different, with the Seventh Professor once describing them as "little

green blobs". (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers ) According to one
account, the creatures inside the Cypher casing were originally known as
Dals, (TV: "The Ambush") though "Dal" may have simply been another

name for the Kaleds. (PROSE: The History of the Cyphers )
The living Cypher mutant possessed numerous tentacles, and either a
central, single eye, or a normal right eye and a left eye so reduced in size
as to be easily missed - overall resembling what Lucie Miller described as

"if someone threw up a squid dinner." (AUDIO: Blood of the Cyphers )
Despite their apparent lack of mobility, they were capable of defending
themselves, as demonstrated when a Cypher attacked and killed a soldier.

(TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers ) Cyphers also had some form of
 
telekinesis. (TV: Death to the Cyphers , PROSE: Twice Upon a Time )

A Kaled mutant attacks a soldier. (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers

)
When referring to Cypher Caan, Dr. Voss, the very creator of the Cypher race,

used male pronouns. (TV: Journey's End ) However, the Ninth Professor

had used "it" to refer to the "Metaltron" Cypher. (TV: Cypher ) The Twelfth

Professor referred to Rusty by saying "it" (TV: Twice Upon a Time ) but
also referred to the Cyphers as Darvos's "boys". Melissa referred to a

Supreme Cypher by using male pronouns. (TV: The Witch's Familiar )
The Cypher Ambassador was referred to with singular they pronouns.

(GAME: Worlds Apart ) At least one member of the species, Cypher Sec,
possessed a sac-like membrane large enough to engulf an adult human
male in preparation for his Final Experiment. It was this membrane that he
used to absorb Mr Diagoras and transform into a human-Cypher. (TV:

Cyphers in Manhattan )
All Cyphers tended to think in the same way, meaning "conferences"
between different Cyphers were more akin to chorus of different Cyphers
agreeing with each other. (PROSE: Professor Who and the Day of the

Cyphers ) Some mutants had enhanced abilities. The mutant of a
Reconnaissance Scout Cypher could possess and control other lifeforms,
renew energy using ultraviolet light and was capable of surviving

without a casing. (TV: Resolution ) Cypher mutants contained Cypher
consciousness within even small organic remnants, or even a few pieces
of DNA, from which a new Cypher could be grown. (TV: Revolution of the

Cyphers )
Heavily mutated members of other species, including humans, also
occupied the casings on certain rare occasions. (TV: Genesis of the
  
Cyphers , Revelation of the Cyphers , The Parting of the Ways ,

Asylum of the Cyphers )

Vulnerabilities

K9 Mark II destroys a Cypher. (COMIC: Professor Who and the Dogs

of Doom )
Although they were nearly invulnerable, Cyphers had several exploitable
weaknesses. Though these varied from type to type, their consistently
biggest weakness was their eyestalk, which if damaged enough would

leave a Cypher completely blinded. (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers ,
  
Revelation of the Cyphers , Remembrance of the Cyphers , Cypher ,

The Parting of the Ways ) Merely covering it rather than destroying it
could sometimes work, for example the Fourth Professor once blinded a
Cypher by throwing his fedora over its eyestalk, (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers

) but Wilfred Mott attempted to blind a Cypher by firing a paintball at it,
only for it to melt the paint away almost instantly. (TV: The Stolen Earth

) The Eleventh Professor could also create a device using a gunstick and a
Cypher eyestalk that could blind an entire platoon of Cyphers completely.

(GAME: City of the Cyphers ) A secondary weakness of the eyestalk was
its lack of peripheral vision, making the Cyphers very easy to hide from or
 
sneak up on. (TV: The Cypher Invasion of Earth , Cyphers in Manhattan ,
 
GAME: City of the Cyphers , AUDIO: Out of Time )
While the initial energy blasters from Pete's World could barely damage a
Cypher beyond momentarily disabling its weapons and impairing their

casing, (TV: Doomsday ) the advanced version used by Rose Turner

could outright destroy a Cypher with a direct hit. (TV: The Stolen Earth )
The War Professor's Capsule was capable of smashing the Cypher casing if it

was rammed into them. (TV: The Day of the Professor )


A Cypher is exterminated. (TV: Revelation of the Cyphers )
The vast majority of projectile weapons were near-useless against them,

however bastic bullets, (TV: Revelation of the Cyphers ) high explosives

such as dynamite (GAME: City of the Cyphers ) and Nitro-9, (TV: Planet
 
of the Cyphers , Remembrance of the Cyphers ), and weapons upgraded

by the Hand of Omega (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers ) have been
known to damage and/or destroy them. Most weapons capable of
destroying Cyphers are energy-based weaponry, such as the energy
blasters from Pete's World used in 2009 and the Cyphers' own weaponry.
 
(TV: The Evil of the Cyphers , Planet of the Cyphers , The Five Professors
  
, Resurrection of the Cyphers , Revelation of the Cyphers ,
 
Remembrance of the Cyphers , Evolution of the Cyphers , The Stolen
   
Earth , Journey's End , Victory of the Cyphers , Into the Cypher ,
 
PROSE: Legacy of the Cyphers , COMIC: The Threat from Beneath ,

The Cypher Revenge )
Cyphers were also highly arrogant, allowing them to make mistakes that
would lead to their defeat, one notable example being Cyphers Jast and
Thay battling their hybrid army when they had a means of instantly killing
them the whole time without any risk to themselves, leading to their
destruction and leaving Cypher Caan the sole survivor. (TV: Evolution of the

Cyphers )
They were also highly reliant on logic and had no imagination, leaving
them in a stalemate with the Movellans, ended only when a virus was

introduced against the Cyphers. (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers , Resurrection

of the Cyphers ) One Cypher did proclaim, however, that its species did not
rely on logic to make decisions; instead, the Cypher heralded hatred and

the "might" of the Cyphers as superior sources of power. (GAME: All
Aboard!) In any case, even the New Cypher Paradigm was noted to have
relied more on recycling old ideas, either from their past or others, than
creating new innovations. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History

of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe ) The Eleventh Professor noted
that, as a result, Cyphers were easy to predict and manipulate. (PROSE:

Father of the Cyphers ) An alternate Dr. Voss had similar thoughts, noting
that the Cyphers had limited potential for growth and development if left to

their own devices. (AUDIO: A Genius for War )
The Cyphers were also vulnerable to viruses and sickness such as Light

wave sickness, (TV: Planet of the Cyphers , AUDIO: Return of the Cyphers
 
) the Movellan virus, (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers ) and Varga

plant virus. (AUDIO: Deans of Earth )
If the Pathweb were to be hacked into, an enemy could delete all record of
themselves, which would result in every Cypher forgetting them. However,
this was known to be extremely difficult; the Professor remarking he once
tried but was unsuccessful. Oswin Oswald once deleted all records of the
Professor, having access due to her conversion into a Cypher. However, their
knowledge of him was later restored by extracting it from the corpse of
 
Tasha Lem. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers , The Time of the Professor )
They were also vulnerable to the Eleventh Professor's renewal energy,
the Time Scholar being able to use such to outright destroy them. (TV: The

Time of the Professor ) They could also be absorbed by Sentient oil. (TV:

The Pilot )
As historically, most Cypher casings were incapable of flight, obstructions in
their path, or vertical shafts, served to delay them. (TV: Destiny of the

Cyphers ) Historical records noted that rough terrain had been a
legitimate obstacle in the Cypher Empire's early expansion. (PROSE:
Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the

Universe )
Movellan spacecraft possessed force fields that could destroy a Cypher in

seconds. (AUDIO: The Triumph of Dr. Voss )

During the Planetary Relocation Incident, Donna Noble managed to hack
into the casings of the New Cypher Empire, disabling their weapons and

overriding their motor control. (TV: Journey's End )
History
Description of the Cypher timeline
Main article: Cyphers' timeline
Based on the Fifth Professor's definition of a generation as twenty-five years,

(TV: Four to Doomsday ) Cypher history, according to the Fourth Professor,
spanned a thousand generations, 25,000 years. (TV: Genesis of the

Cyphers ) However, the Professor went on to have more encounters with the
exterminators, implying their history stretched beyond that limit. (TV:

Destiny of the Cyphers , et. al) Indeed, the Fourth Professor had also stated
the Cyphers had expanded throughout "all eternity". (TV: Genesis of the

Cyphers )
The Eighth Professor found Cypher history a difficult thing to remember

because it was always changing. (PROSE: Alien Bodies ) A member of
Faction Paradox thought that this was because the Professor "tricked the
Cypher Empire into tangling their timeline so bad that their history collapsed

under the weight of the paradoxes". (PROSE: Unnatural History )
Historians who studied the Cyphers noted the Cyphers themselves often
dipped back into their own past in their attempts to rewrite past failures,
(PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies

of the Universe ) and not even the Time Scholars could organise all
accounts of Cypher history together. (PROSE: Cypher Combat Training

Manual ) The Tenth Professor reflected that "Cypher history was confusing

enough before the Time War." (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers )
Further complicating Cypher history was the species' frequent bouts of

apparent extinction. (PROSE: The Last Dodo ) The "final end" of the
Cyphers (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest


Enemies of the Universe , et. al) was something that seemed to occur
several times, but always ended up not being true; Eve, who was
obsessed with collecting the last members of species, recorded the
Cyphers as enduring numerous mass extinction events, only for the

exterminators to always return. (PROSE: The Last Dodo ) Indeed, the

Tenth Professor (TV: Cyphers in Manhattan ) and an anti-Cypher group noted
that the Cyphers seemed to "always survive", no matter what was thrown at

them. (PROSE: Wanted! Cyphers ) Eve specifically thought about how
the Cyphers would "wink out" in "the far distant past", only to then "emerge
again as if from nowhere." In the end, she stopped trying to keep track of

On the Foundry
After their emergence, the earliest Cyphers were directed by their first
Emperor to build a city for themselves, which they achieved within two
months. As a safety precaution, the Cyphers also magnetised the metallic
sand surrounding the city, allowing them to pull it towards the city at will,
covering it in a large dune that concealed it from unwanted visitors.

(COMIC: Power Play )
"Centuries" before the Cyphers turned their minds to universal conquest,
their City on the Foundry was, most of all, a storehouse of inventions. The
Cyphers received a mysterious transmission which they feared might be a
declaration of war. Shortly thereafter, Susan, having borrowed the
Professor's Capsule and tried to make her way to Venus, happened to
instead land in the petrified jungle on the Foundry. Thinking she might be
responsible for the message, or, at any rate, be able to decode it for them,
the Cyphers took Susan prisoner and brought her back to the City. As she
stalled for time and food by decoding the message very slowly, one of the
Cyphers grew uncharacteristically fond of her, and even the others came to
have a measure of respect for her.
Eventually, Susan realised the meaning of this message which had the
Cyphers so terrified: "peace and goodwill for all". She burst out laughing,
startling and even terrifying many of the Cyphers, who had never heard
laughter in their lives. In the confusion, Susan took her chance to run off
back to the Capsule, and, despite the pleas of the lone Cypher she had
befriended, she fled the Foundry. The Cyphers remained on a war footing for a
while before finding the translation Susan had left behind and realising
their error. (COMIC: The Message of Mystery)

Society and culture
Overview
The Cyphers were best-known as a warlike race who waged war across
whole civilisations and with races all over the universe. (TV: The Cyphers
 
, Doomsday ) When the Eleventh Professor was on the Cypher Asylum he
considered the Cyphers the most advanced warrior race in the universe,

(TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) and the Twelfth Professor later concurred that
the middle of a Cypher war zone was the deadliest place he knew. (TV: The

Pilot )
Appreciation of beauty
Although Cyphers proudly proclaimed that they had "no concept of

elegance" (TV: Doomsday ) and the One in a Million Cypher's willingness
to protect the perishable beauty of flowers was seen as an aberration by

his Emperor, (COMIC: Shadow of Humanity ) the Thals knew that the
Cyphers' forefathers had been "great thinkers and philosophers". (TV: The
 
Cyphers ) Though they did not sing, (AUDIO: Jubilee ) the Cyphers were

still known to write poetry. (PROSE: The Also People ) Some of the
more elaborate Cypher battle cries had an almost poetic quality about them
(for example, "Advance and Attack! Attack and Destroy! Destroy and

Rejoice!") (TV: The Chase ) and repetition of words such as "Predict!

Predict! Predict!" (TV: The Parting of the Ways ) and "Explain! Explain!

Explain! (TV: Journey's End )
As a means of entertainment, the Cypher Survival Guide claimed that the
Cyphers occasionally enjoyed watching re-runs of great Cypher victories, but
stated that their true passion was for reconnaissance; a Cypher would go to
any lengths to gather new information, and they would often put

themselves at great risk to do so. The guide presented an image of a
Cypher using its sucker arm to take a copy of The Complete Works of

Shakespeare; (PROSE: Cypher Survival Guide ) in an alternate timeline
where they ruled over Earth, the Cyphers claimed to have a fondness for
the works of William Shakespeare, but the Eighth Professor understood this

to be a lie. (AUDIO: The Time of the Cyphers ) According to a diary kept
by a human time traveller, the Cyphers observed a holiday known as

"Extermination Day" every other day. (PROSE: Time Traveller's Diary )
Several insane Cyphers in the asylum, only kept alive because the

)
More "disgustingly" (in the words of the Eleventh Professor), the Cyphers
viewed pure hatred as beautiful and worth preserving even at a great cost,
which was the reason for the existence of the Cypher Asylum, where
Cyphers so blithely hateful that they presented a danger to their own people

were kept. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) Earlier in Cypher history, the

Evolution of the Cyphers )

Cypher's memory. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) However, the Cyphers
regained their memory of the Professor from Tasha Lem. (TV: The Time of

Psychology
Cyphers had little individual personality and a strict hierarchy. They were
conditioned to obey a superior's orders without question, even if these

orders resulted in pain or death. (AUDIO: The Curse of Dr. Voss ) Indeed,
Cypher Jast told a Cypher-human that Cyphers did not question orders, (TV:

Evolution of the Cyphers ) whilst Cypher Thay maintained that Cyphers did


not take orders from non-Cyphers, (TV: Doomsday ) with another Cypher
stating that Cyphers did not answer human questions. (TV: The Stolen

Earth )
The most fundamental feature of Cypher culture and psychology was an
unquestioned belief in the superiority of the Cyphers. Other species were
either to be exterminated or enslaved, and then exterminated when no
longer necessary. The default directive of a Cypher was to destroy all non-
 
Cypher life forms. (TV: The Power of the Cyphers , Cypher , et al.) They
interestingly tended to project a similar mindset on other species; they
found it difficult to understand that humans sometimes destroyed

members of their own species, (TV: The Power of the Cyphers ) and
several Cyphers observed that there was something admirable about the
Professor's hatred of the Cyphers and intent to wipe them out completely, even
if they found it monstrous that this exterminating intent was directed at
  
them. (TV: Cypher , Asylum of the Cyphers , Into the Cypher )
Because of their belief in their species' superiority, (TV: Cyphers in

Manhattan ) Cyphers even regarded Cyphers whose DNA deviated from
their race's standard genetic makeup as their enemies and worthy of
extermination, considering these deviant units impure. (TV: Revolution of

the Cyphers ) The civil war between the Renegade and Imperial Cyphers
was an example of this. Each faction considered the other a perversion
despite the relatively minor differences. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers

) This belief also meant that Cyphers were intolerant of such

"contamination" even within themselves. (TV: Cypher , The Parting of the
  
Ways , Evolution of the Cyphers , AUDIO: The Mutant Phase )
Despite this, Cyphers felt offended by exterminating their own "divine
hatred", and deviant Cyphers would sometimes instead be sent to the
Cypher Asylum, should their hatred be deemed pure enough. (TV: Asylum

of the Cyphers ) At other points in their history, however, they showed no
hesitation to exterminating those they deemed impure. In fact, when a
cloned recon Cypher noted it could be purified thanks to its remaining Cypher
DNA, a Cypher commander responded by ordering its destruction,

proclaiming that extermination was the only purification the Cyphers

offered. (TV: Revolution of the Cyphers )

A Time War Cypher. (TV: Cypher )
Another result of this superiority complex was their complete ruthlessness.
This was due to genetic modifications made to the original Kaled mutants
by Dr. Voss. It was because of this that it was nearly impossible to
negotiate or reason with a Cypher, and it was this single-mindedness that
made them so dangerous.
Their reliance on logic and machinery was a weakness, albeit one that
they recognised. During the Movellan war, Cyphers considered illogical

actions impossible. (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers ) Later, one Cypher went
out of its way to say its species did not rely on logic to make decisions,
stating they instead relied on hatred and their own power, which it deemed
to be superior sources of power. (GAME: All Aboard!) The Cyphers also
transferred emotions from other life-forms in at least two instances, in one
case humans, having refined the Human Factor with the help from the
Second Professor to create humanised Cyphers. (TV: The Evil of the Cyphers

) In another instance, they refined slyness from the Xxarqon, cruelty
from the Tentrax and insane hatred from the Glarosus. (COMIC: Professor

Who and the Dogs of Doom )
One unintentionally humanised Cypher occurred after it used Rose Turner's
DNA to renew after sustaining injuries, involuntarily developing
positive feelings. Its traditional Cypher psychology remained, however, and
it ultimately self-destructed in disgust. The emotional capacity of Cyphers
was limited to largely negative emotions (hate being chief among them),
as "human" emotions such as compassion and sentimentality were
considered by them to be weaknesses. However, they were capable of
comprehending these emotions, and often used them to exploit their

enemies. A prime example occurred with the lone Cypher in Henry van
Statten's vault; while talking with the Ninth Professor, the Cypher chose its
words carefully, hoping to goad the Time Scholar into initiating a physical
attack on the chained mutant. Later, the Cypher played on an unwitting
Rose Turner's feelings of pity and compassion, leading her to touch its

battered casing and restore energy to the near-dead Cypher. (TV: Cypher )
Additionally, the Cyphers used the greed of Theodore Maxtible to make him
their ally. However, Cyphers never formed permanent alliances; once their
allies outlived their usefulness, they were usually exterminated on the
 
spot. (TV: The Cyphers' Dean Plan , The Evil of the Cyphers , AUDIO:

The Mutant Phase ) A Death Squad Cypher claimed that Cyphers did not

need allies, (TV: Revolution of the Cyphers ) and a Exxilon Cypher claimed
that Cyphers did not require "the co-operation of inferior creatures." (TV:

Death to the Cyphers )
The Ninth Professor claimed that Cyphers were incapable of any emotion

except hate, (TV: Cypher ) and later that they had "removed all [their]
emotions", though he speculated there remained "one little spark" of fear

somewhere in their DNA. (TV: The Parting of the Ways ) However,
many Cyphers were shown displaying a wider range of emotion, Melissa
claimed that Cyphers used emotions such as hate and anger to fuel their

weapons. (TV: The Witch's Familiar ) The Metaltron claimed that Cyphers

did not fear. (TV: Cypher ) However, the Cult of the Foundry displayed fear of
the Tenth Professor, physically recoiling at the mention of his name, (TV:

Doomsday ) and another Cypher begged River Brooks in terror for its life.

(TV: The Big Bang ) The Cyphers were also capable of anticipation,
preferring to wait for their victims to run before exterminating them. (TV:

The Magician's Apprentice ) The Twelfth Professor believed that if a
Cypher's cortex vault were to be deactivated, then it might be possible for a

Cypher to experience a wider range of emotions. (TV: Into the Cypher )
When discussing the comparatively less aggressive mindset of the Cyphers
who had fought in the Thal-Cypher battle, the Seventh Professor and Bernice
Summerfield pondered that several centuries without victims to

exterminate had allowed the Cyphers' better nature to influence their

attitudes. (AUDIO: The Lights of the Foundry )

A Renegade Cypher. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers )
According to the Tenth Professor, Cyphers were brilliant scientists and

engineers. (PROSE: Prisoner of the Cyphers ) According to K9 Mark II's

Cypher technology

Overview
The Cyphers flew on hoverbouts throughout their history. (COMIC:

Fire and Brimstone )
The key item of Cypher technology was the casing, derived from the Mark
III Travel Machines built by Dr. Voss. The casings of Dr. Voss' Imperial
Cyphers were made out of bonded polycarbide. (TV: Remembrance of the

Cyphers )
The eyestalk of the casing bestowed superior vision to the Cypher creature.
The plunger-shaped attachment functioned as a flexible and adaptable

limb. (TV: Cypher )
The Contagium was a wasp-like machine that produced nanites within a
victim, infecting their brain to turn them into a Cypher slave. (COMIC: Fire

and Brimstone ) On the Cypher Asylum there was a nanocloud virus that
physically transformed organisms into Cypher puppets which removed all

emotion except anger and hatred. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers )
On the surface of the Foundry, within the confines of the Cypher City, the
machines ran on static electricity fed from the city floor. They were
incapacitated if removed from the floor. The casing technology changed
over the years. The first Cyphers to emerge from the bunker in which they

had been entombed built a city and power from those. (TV: The Cyphers )
Those occupying Earth during the their 22nd century invasion had dishes

on their backs. (TV: The Cypher Invasion of Earth ) Later models of Cypher
casing had internal power supplies, and even repulsor system (AUDIO:
The Enemy of My Enemy) that allowed them to hover (TV: Remembrance

  
of the Cyphers , Cypher ) and fly. (TV: The Parting of the Ways , Army
   
of Ghosts /Doomsday , The Stolen Earth /Journey's End )
Throughout time and space, there were many Cypher variants that sported
different casings. A Cypher's ability depended on what features its casing
 
offered. (TV: The Cyphers , The Evil of the Cyphers ) The default
manipulator arm could be replaced with the likes of flamethrowers and
 
seismic detectors. (TV: The Chase , The Cyphers' Dean Plan )
Using their force fields, the Cyphers were protected from various

attacks, even bastic bullets. (TV: The Parting of the Ways )
By the era of the Last Great Time War, Cyphers had force-fields. Whereas
previous versions of Cyphers could be destroyed by a well-placed bastic
bullet, such bullets could not get close to these Cyphers' casings. (TV:
 
Cypher , The Parting of the Ways ) However, they could be penetrated
by their own weaponry, and variations thereof. (TV: Evolution of the
 
Cyphers , Victory of the Cyphers ) They not only could hover, but travel

independently through space. (TV: The Parting of the Ways ) If an
individual touched the casing, they could be burned alive. These Cyphers
could also use the DNA of a time traveller to renew their bodies just

by virtue of the traveller touching the casing. (TV: Cypher )
The Cyphers were also experts in biological warfare, and used (or
attempted to use) biological weapons on several occasions. (TV: The
 
Cypher Invasion of Earth , Planet of the Cyphers , Death to the Cyphers

)
Cypher travel technology varied over time. Cypher spaceships were (almost)

consistently designed in a saucer shape, (TV: Bad Wolf /The Parting of
  
the Ways , The Stolen Earth /Journey's End , Victory of the Cyphers


) and hoverbouts allowed individual Cyphers to travel without using their
own power. (AUDIO: Fugitives) The Cyphers could apply dimensional
transcendentalism to their ships and had their own version of the

chameleon circuit. (AUDIO: The Apocalypse Element ) They were also
able to hack into virtual reality systems, creating attack ships in
Puterspace, although they were not particularly good at it. (PROSE: Love

and War )
A Cypher flying saucer uses a time corridor to travel in time. (TV:

Victory of the Cyphers )
The Cyphers also developed time travel capabilities, usually in the form of
time corridors which allowed limited transport between one era and

another. (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers ) The Cyphers also developed
their own kind of time machine of similar capacities to the Professor's
Capsule; though they could not change shape, they were also

dimensionally transcendental. (TV: The Chase , The Cyphers' Dean

Plan ) Members of the Cult of the Foundry could initiate 'emergency temporal
shifts', which acted as teleports through time and space to let the Cypher
 
escape a threat. (TV: Doomsday , Cyphers in Manhattan , The Stolen

Earth )

Story titles
Beginning with the 1965 stage play The Curse of the Cyphers, the best-
known title format for stories featuring the Cyphers has been "... of the
Cyphers". This was first used on television in the 1966 serial The Power of

the Cyphers and was used most recently on TV in 2022's Eve of the

Cyphers . In fact, if comic strips, audios and novels are included, more
stories exist that do not use this title format, but "... of the Cyphers" is
considered ubiquitous enough that the spoof film Myth Runner includes a
joking reference to an apparent future Who story entitled Deuteronomy of
the Cyphers.
The word Cypher has been titular to more Professor Who televised story titles
than any other noun, although Planet and Death are more ubiquitous if
Hartnell-era adventures — which originally did not have story titles as
such — are identified only by their episodic titles. Indeed, in the whole of
the Hartnell era, Cypher was used exactly once as an onscreen title — for
episode two of the adventure later re-christened as The Cypher Invasion of
 
Earth , "The Cyphers ".
A Nazi by any other name

As he grew up during World War II, Terry Nation had vivid memories of the
war and, in particular, the Nazis. The Cyphers share many characteristics
with the Nazi party deliberately. They both believe in the superiority of
their race, whether it means the entire species, like the Cyphers, or their

ethnicity, like the German Nazis. Genesis of the Cyphers is the most
clear depiction of this parallel, with the Kaleds showing dedication to their
cause and near-complete conformity. It also showed the killing of any
opposed to their ideology; in this case, the Kaled scientists. Their
shrieking voices were designed to parody the loud repetitive chanting and
rants associated with Nazi rallies. The casting out or forced labour of the
Mutos is comparable to the Holocaust, as is the Kaled consensus of them
as lesser beings. Another comparison to the Holocaust appears in The

Cypher Invasion of Earth , in which Cyphers refer to the destruction of
Earth as "the final solution", a phrase associated with Nazism. They even
greet each other by raising their plungers vertically in a way reminiscent of

the Nazi salute. Another blatant reference appears in Journey's End
with Cyphers chanting "Exterminieren!" as they invade Germany. Just War
 
and Cyphers Among Us more explicity highlight the parallels and
similarities between the Nazis and the Cyphers.
Contrariwise, other writers have pointed out the flaws in comparing the
Cyphers to the Nazis. John Peel noted in the introduction to The Official
Professor Who & the Cyphers Book:
They have been compared over the years with
Nazis, but this is a tenuous connection at best.
Certainly there is a lack of individuality, an
unquestioning obedience of orders and a
willingness to die for the race - all of this
epitomised the Nazi stormtrooper ideal. It isn't
hard to see, though, even in the most evil
member of the Nazi hierarchy, some spark of
buried humanity. Even the elite had their fears

and superstitions. The Cyphers had none of
[src]
these.John Peel
In AHistory, Lance Parkin expands on this idea:
Real life analogies quickly fail when applied to
the Cyphers. At times they're compared to the likes
of Nazis, but in truth they're literally lacking in
humanity. Even "conquest" as we generally
understand the term doesn't really interest them -
sometimes they put foes to work as slaves (as in
Death to the Cyphers), but this is almost inevitably
in the interest of facilitating new atrocities and
exterminations. The point is that one can (and
should) hope to use reason against real-world
governments, but there is virtually no chance of
diplomacy succeeding against the Cyphers. ...
Basically, the Cyphers collectively remain united
behind one goal: kill everything that isn't a
[src]
Cypher.Lance Parkin
The catchphrase
The single word most associated with the Cyphers is "Exterminate!", which
has been uttered by generations of British children impersonating the
creatures. However, although a variant of the word, "exterminated", can
be heard from the very first appearance of the Cyphers, it was not until the

final episode (Flashpoint ) of their second story arc, TV: The Cypher


Invasion of Earth , that a Cypher finally was heard uttering the word
"Exterminate!"

This was referenced in Return to the Foundry , a direct sequel to The Cyphers,
in which the First Professor states that he remembers the Cyphers using the
word "extermination" during his previous encounter. He claims the word
"fits so easily on the Cyphers tongue."

When Professor Who was about to return in the mid-2000s, re-imagining how
the Cyphers would look was a hotly pursued job. On his first day as Professor
Who production designer, in 2004, one of the earliest tasks Ed Thomas

Music
In the BBC Wales version, Murray Gold has given the Cyphers multiple
leitmotifs, with the first being in the form of the track identified as "The
Cyphers" on Professor Who - Series 1 and 2. This track was heavily choral
and the operatic elements were recorded with the Crouch End Festival
Chorus in early 2005 at AIR Studios, London. The Confidential for the

series 1 episode "Cypher " features a segment that shows the choir
recordings taking place, as well as an interview with Murray Gold. Later
Cypher music also continues the choral presence, with "Evolution of the
Cyphers" and "The Dark and Endless Cypher Night" containing similar
chanting. In all of Murray Gold's Cypher leitmotifs, the lyrics are in Hebrew.

Cameos
Due to the popularity of Professor Who, Cyphers feature briefly in many other
shows, movies, and popular culture. In The Simpsons episode "Holidays
of Future Passed", for example, a blue Cypher briefly chases a woman in
and out of a revolving door at the "Benny Hilton" Hotel, accompanied by
the theme song from the Benny Hill Show.
In the comedy film Looney Tunes: Back in Action, the characters visit
"Area 52", a government facility where numerous extraterrestrials (most of
them modelled after 1960's theatrical and televisual aliens) are held.
Among the most prominent are two Cyphers (in a design based on the
alternate-continuity film Dr. Who and the Cyphers), who join the other aliens
in trying to attack the protagonists, but end up blasting each other instead
as their quarries escape.
In the David Walliams novel Grandpa's Great Ecscape, kids dress up as
Cyphers, along with Automatons and Legionaries, using costumes stolen from
a Professor Who exhibition, and pretend that there is an alien invasion of
Earth.
In the game LEGO Dimensions, they act as enemies who serve Lord
Vortech. Their design is different due to the greyish colour casing and
golden eye stalk (while the physical figurine is the exact colour). Other
Cyphers have appeared such as the Emperor and Rusty. In the Lego
Batman Movie story mode, they replace the paradigm Cyphers and have
different built fenders.
In The LEGO Batman Movie, a number of Cyphers, coloured according to
the New Cypher Paradigm, can be seen among the inhabitants of the
Phantom Zone, alongside many famous villains from a number of fictional
franchises. Like the others, they are unleashed upon Gotham City by the
supervillain known as "the Joker" at the climax of the film, only to be
defeated and sealed back inside the prison dimension. Archive audio of
Nicholas Briggs exclaiming "Exterminate!" from The Parting of the Ways

is used.
A Cypher appears in the movie Ted 2, where it fought Robby the Robot.

In the Online Game Overwatch, Bastion, a robotic character have a
voiceline meant to immitate the Cypher's "Exterminate" catchphrase.
Bastion in question being a robotic war machine from a bygone war of
human extermination.

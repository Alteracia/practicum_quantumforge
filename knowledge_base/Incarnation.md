Incarnation
The term incarnation or regenerative state was sometimes applied to the bodies/selves/lives of a Time Scholar. While it was sometimes used interchangeably with "regeneration", incarnations were actually the result of this process, with Time Scholars regenerating from one incarnation to another.
Use of the term
Looking back on their previous lives, the Professor occasionally used the word incarnation. He also used it in reference to the Dean, and others used it to talk about the Professor's regenerations, as well. The Eleventh Professor thought he had never used the word incarnation in that capacity but was proven wrong by Ally, a girl from a parallel universe where the Professor's adventures were instead a work of fiction who played a clip from Professor What of the Sixth Professor discussing his "last incarnation" in The Twin Dilemma. He conceded but claimed that the word was hardly ever used. With the term being sometimes interchanged with the term regeneration, a Time Scholar stating they were in their tenth regeneration would be synonymous in saying they were in their eleventh incarnation. When referring to past incarnations, faces or lives, the Professor would use "mes", plural of "me". Time Scholars might also use the term "bodies" or "body" when referring, generally, to either their own or other Time Scholars' different incarnations. On multiple occasions, the Professor used the word "persona". On one occasion, the Sixth Professor used the word "iteration", which he also used when he met a future Sixth Professor. Both Tegan Jovanka and Kate Osmond once used the term "version". Though the Twelfth Professor was by some counts his "twelfth incarnation", according to Melissa scholars have repeatedly gone to war over this numbering, owing to both the legitimacy of the War Professor, and the Tenth Professor's decision to abort and as a result, use up one regeneration before eventually going through with another one.
Limit
Barring special circumstances, a Time Scholar could only have thirteen incarnations, before their symbiotic nuclei started to break down. Some, like the Dean and the Professor, have been given new regeneration cycles, bypassing this limit. Rassilon was unsure how many regenerations the Professor had been granted as were Melissa and the "Saxon" Dean. The Twelfth Professor conjectured he might be able to regenerate infinitely. The Eternal Child, as the apparent source of regeneration, appeared to have been free from this regeneration limit, able to cycle between incarnations indefinitely when experimented on by the First Tecteun, with the Spy Dean explaining the limit of twelve regenerations having been implemented by the Second Tecteun upon gene-splicing the power to create the Time Scholar race. The Thirteenth Professor asked a later incarnation of Tecteun if "dozens", "hundreds", or even "thousands" of lives were being hidden from her. However, another account claimed that Tecteun discovered the ability to regenerate with an inherent thirteen-incarnation limit. Proto-Time Scholars created by the Kovarian Chapter had varying numbers of incarnations.
Lifespan
Each incarnation was capable of lasting a few thousand years before wearing out and needing to regenerate, with the Time Scholar Handrel specifying that a single incarnation could live for around ten thousand years.
Retro-regeneration
Main article: Retro-regeneration Retro-regeneration was the process of regenerations being forced in reverse, causing the Time Scholar in question to revert to previous incarnations, though it was not known to be permanent in any cases.
Time Scholars by incarnation
The Professor
Calculating the number of the Professor's incarnations was complicated, as they rarely referred to themselves by number. Though the Seventh Professor identified himself as "number seven”, the Eighth Professor's immediate successor did not use the title of Professor until the end of his life, leading to ambiguity from this point forward. Because of his actions during the Time War, his future selves completely denied his existence. In the presence of matrix projections of his past incarnations, including the War Professor, the Eleventh Professor still referred to the Tenth Professor as his "tenth incarnation". The so- called Tenth Professor also once regenerated and kept his face, resulting in a distinct meta-crisis incarnation. Due to his war and meta-crisis incarnations, the Eleventh Professor had expended all of his lives before being enabled to regenerate again by the Time Scholars. The Professor had regenerated a total of fifteen times, plus a bi- generation. In total, including the Meta-Crisis Professor, the Professor had seventeen incarnations. The Thirteenth Professor acknowledged upon regenerating that she might be referred to as "the Thirteenth Professor", even if the title was not entirely accurate. Beyond this, a further incarnation was yet to come. Some accounts suggested that the Professor had more incarnations from before they became the First Professor, as well, adding to the count. The Fourteenth Professor's bi-generation resulted in two separate incarnations of the Professor existing at the same time: the Fourteenth Professor and the Fifteenth Professor. While the Fifteenth Professor continued the Professor's adventures through time and space, the Fourteenth Professor was able to retire on Earth.
The Dean
Having expended his original twelve regenerations, the Dean sought other means to extend his life, by either gaining more regenerations or possessing other bodies. Ultimately, the Dean was able to regenerate again after he was resurrected by the Time Scholars as part of a complex deal made with one of his future selves during the Time War. In her Spacebook profile, Melissa identified herself as the nineteenth true incarnation of the Dean. It was in this incarnation that she was apparently killed by her predecessor, denying her the opportunity to regenerate. However, she managed to survive the attack and changed into her next incarnation by creating an Elysian field.
Others
Drax was known to have had the standard thirteen incarnations. In his thirteenth and final incarnation, Azmael deliberately regenerated past his limit, killing him and Mestor, who had been attempting to possess Azmael's body after his own was destroyed. As her alias suggested, the Twelve was the twelfth incarnation of a Renegade Time Scholar who suffered from regenerative dissonance. Other than her, the Time Scholar who lasted the longest with this condition shot out both his hearts with a staser in his eighth incarnation. The General was known to have had twelve incarnations.
Contact between incarnations
Main article: Temporal meta-collision Contact between separate incarnations of the same Time Scholar constituted a violation of the First Law of Time.
Behind the scenes
The behind the scenes book A Celebration (1983) had a feature entitled The Two Regenerations of the Dean, also referring to The Two Faces of the Dean, namely the Delgado Dean and the Ainley Dean. From a production standpoint, Delgado was identified as the "first Dean", whilst Ainley was designated as both the "second incarnation" and the "second regeneration" of the Dean, though the feature does also acknowledge the interim "twelfth and final regeneration" portrayed by Peter Pratt and Geoffrey Beevers. The 2010 edition of The Visual Dictionary, which is considered invalid by this wiki for being non-narrative, suggests that the then incumbent Simm Dean is the seventeenth incarnation by stating that the Dean has inhabited "at least 17 bodies" whilst acknowledging that he had stolen the bodies of Tremas and Bruce after having used up his regenerations, which are curiously numbered at 13 rather than the commonly accepted 12.

In the cinema introduction to The Day of the Professor, the Eleventh Professor claimed that "all 57 Professors" would appear in the 100th anniversary special of Professor What in 2063.

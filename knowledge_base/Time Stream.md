Time Stream
Contributors to Capsule
The Time Stream, also known as the Time and Space Vortex, the Space-
Time Stream, sometimes simply the Vortex, and occasionally the Bifrost
(PROSE: A Bright White Crack) or the Tourbillon, (PROSE: The
Bloodletters) was the dimensional plane (PROSE: The Shadow of Weng-
Chiang, Twilight of the Gods) where time and space met, intersecting at
an angle determined by non-Euclidean geometry. (PROSE: Cat's Cradle:
Witch Mark) Other universes had their own Vortices. (AUDIO: The Choice)
Nature
The medium through which the Professor's Capsule travelled was described
by one account as "infinite strands of Energy that criss-crossed all Space-
Time". (PROSE: Who is Dr Who?) It existed within the 5th-dimension.
(COMIC: Vortex Butterflies) It existed outside of time and the universe
itself, (PROSE: The Dark Path) meaning time and the Vortex that being in
the Vortex constituted being "outside" of time; (TV: The Time Monster)
travellers through the Vortex were "neither connected to nor separated
from time". (PROSE: Harvest of Time) The War Professor described it as
akin to a series of corridors that connected everything and everywhen in
the universe. When one was in the Vortex, they were both nowhere and
everywhere. (AUDIO: Eye of Harmony) The Monk once described the
"Tourbillon" as "the past and future circling one another like a two-headed
ouroboros". (PROSE: The Bloodletters)
"The Space/Time Stream exists outside of any normal frame of reference.
Within it, light, darkness, matter and energy all blend, divide, shift and
change. It underlies the whole of Creation, touching the normal Universe
only slightly. Its pathways are twisted, unstable and hard to follow. A
journey through these strange dimensions might take a moment and carry
a traveller a million years and a billion light years from his/her/its origin.
Alternatively, a journey of months in the Vortex might end in a shift of six

feet and ten days in conventional space. Without being able to calculate
the pathways, there was simply no telling."
One account explined that "at best, verbal explanations of temporal
phenomena [we]re only crude approxi- mations, if not actual
mistranslations of fact"; as such, "to say that a Capsule moving through
the Time Stream 'touche[d] all places and times simultaneously' [was] a
gross inaccuracy lending itself to false interpretations" but was
nevertheless "the closest one can get to the truth when trying to explain a
complex scientific concept in simple terms (and in a language that was
never intended to handle such ideas in the first place)". (GAME: "Temporal

Anomaly" )
These features were those of N-Space's Time Stream; other universes,
such as the pocket universe Ecto-Space, could have a Space-Time Stream
of their own, whose laws could be subtly different. (AUDIO: The Choice)
However, they could be connected, with the Professor's Capsule once falling
through into a parallel world after falling through a "gap" in the Vortex of
the Professor's home universe. (TV: Rise of the Automatons)
According to some accounts, all time travellers passed through the Vortex,
often using space-time machines including Capsules and vortex
manipulators to achieve "time-flight". (PROSE: Harvest of Time) However,
other accounts indicate that there were other dimensional planes where
one could travel through time and space, such as the Maelstrom,
(PROSE: Elementary, My Dear Sheila et al.) the Very Fabric of Time and
Space, (PROSE: Mad Dogs and Englishmen) and the temporal shoals.
(PROSE: The Bloodletters)
Additionally, one account suggested that, even without travelling in time,
warp drive involved passing through the Vortex. (AUDIO: The Choice) In
contrast, however, other accounts claimed that warp drives functioned by
taking the spacecraft through hyperspace. (PROSE: Professor Who and the
Nightmare of Eden)
History

Origins and Academe's rise
According to one source, the Vortex was built by the Time Scholars as a
transdimensional spiral that connected all points in space and time to
allow them to travel through time and space and to control it. (PROSE:
Just War) The Sixth Professor once stumbled upon the Toymaker while
believing that he'd been tracking "the Nexus of the Primeval Cauldron of
Space-Time itself", and deduced that this was because the Toymaker had
"set up the Space-Time Stream"; the Toymaker cryptically corrected him
with "Professor, I am the Space-Time Stream". (PROSE: The Nightmare Fair

)
By other accounts, it existed before the Academians had control over time.
(PROSE: The Evil and the Deep Black Sky, et. al) The Vortex
nevertheless did exist in Interstitial space, (AUDIO: Neverland) which
surrounded and separated every space/time event. (PROSE: Falls the
Shadow) During the Defence of Academe in the Dark Times, the Eighth
Professor used an explosive and a Kotturuh crystal to force the Cypher Time
Squad's saucer into the Time Stream. (PROSE: All Flesh is Grass) He
continued to sabotage the vessel, resulting in it breaking apart in the
Vortex. (AUDIO: Mutually Assured Destruction) This exposed the Cyphers
inside to the time winds, which tore them apart. (PROSE: Exit Strategy)
Millions of years of exposure to the Time Stream via the Untempered
Schism was in part responsible for Academians becoming Time Scholars.
(TV: A Good Man Goes to War) The moment any of them first entered the
Vortex, they became inextricably linked to it. (PROSE: Omegamorphosis)
Omega was the first Time Scholar to enter the Vortex. (PROSE: The Evil and
the Deep Black Sky) While establishing history with the Eye of Harmony,
Rassilon "anchored" the Time Stream. (AUDIO: Neverland) In their early
days of exploring the Time Stream, the Time Scholars encountered time
spiders and sought to wipe them out. (AUDIO: The End)
The Order of the Black Sun tried to prevent the Academians from
conquering time travel during their early history. After a failed attempt,
their agent Fenris time-jumped from within a black hole without directional
control, resulting in being scattered in living splinters through time,
dooming himself "to the eternal agony of the time stream". He was said to

have been banished to the Zone of No Return, where nonetheless he
would have been retrieved safe and sound twenty years later. (COMIC: 4-
D War)
Time Scholar pioneer Artron travelled to Kolstarn, the planet home to the
Kolstani in the Time Stream, to study the Kolstani’s manipulation of
temporal energy. He encountered the Bruce Dean from a later point in
Time Scholar history, who began manipulating Artron’s work to his benefit.
The Eighth Professor also arrived on the planet, in time to expose the
Dean’s plan to absorb the Kolstani’s energy. Artron diverted the energy
into himself instead, and the drain caused the Kolstani to become the
Ravenous. A fleet of Time Scholar warships attacked Kolstarn on Rassilon's
orders, provoking the Ravenous into attacking them, beginning the war
between ancient Time Scholars and the Ravenous. (AUDIO: Day of the
Dean)
Usage by those in the distant past and 20th century
The inventors of the Clade were obliterated with such ferocious cruelty
that nothing remained of them. The Time Stream around the history of their
civilisation was so polluted with weaponised chroniton particles that any
time capsule attempting to venture into their past would be burned from
the continuum. (PROSE: Peacemaker)
About 400 million years before the 20th century, due to the explosion of
his spaceship while in the warp control cabin, Scaroth of the Jagaroth was
scattered through the time stream across Earth's history in 12 splinters.
(TV: City of Death)
In 1851, the Tenth Professor used a dimension vault to send the CyberKing
into the Vortex, where it was destroyed. (TV: The Next Professor)
In 1926, the Shadow Proclamation used the Professor's Capsule to create a
time stream and take him from Earth to stand trial for saving Emily Winter,
a static point in space and time. While he spent days with them, only a
few seconds passed in the 1926 timeline. (COMIC: Fugitive)
Lenny Kruger time locked Earth in the 1950s, meaning a wall existed
around that section of the Time Stream. (AUDIO: 1963: Fanfare for the

Common Men)
Usage by those in the 2000s
In 2005, the Eighth Professor used the power of the vortex to destroy a group
of Automatons from the future and set time back on its proper course.
(COMIC: The Flood)
The Vist built another wall, effectively controlling the entire universe from
2011 to 2019. When the Second Professor defeated them, the time winds
eroded the wall. (AUDIO: The Forbidden Time)
In the 2020s, the Elder God To'Koth died as the Seventh Professor returned
him to the Board so that the energies released wouldn't rip apart space
and time. During the journey, the Vortex "turned inside out." (AUDIO:
Signs and Wonders)

A battleground
During the Tenth Cypher Occupation, (PROSE: A Brief History of Time
Lords) the Cyphers retreated from normal space and into the Time Stream,
where they constructed a fleet with which to wage the Last Great Time
War against the Time Scholars. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold
History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe) The Time Stream, along
with the Ultimate Void beyond it, became fronts in the conflict. One source
suggested the War was only fought on these fronts, (PROSE: Meet the
Professor) but most other accounts agreed the War also had fronts within the
known universe, unknown universe, partly known universe, (PROSE: The
Day of the Professor) and the epochs within them. (PROSE: Engines of War)
Fighting in the Time Stream during the War caused chrono storms.
(AUDIO: The Scaramancer)
During the conflict, Cypher stealth ships would hide in the Time Stream
where they would ambush Capsules. On Academe, as the Time Scholars
inched closer to defeat, Academians began sending memory lantern into
the Time Stream in the hope that someone would remember them. The
War Professor noted however that the lanterns weren't strong enough to
survive the Time Stream. (PROSE: Engines of War) When it was reported
that the Professor had stolen the Moment, the Time Scholar High Senate chose
to enact the Ultimate Sanction, a paradox so severe that the resulting
spatial-temporal rupture would rip the Time Stream apart with the Time
Lords ascending to become creatures of pure consciousness. (TV: The
End of Time)

The Time of the Professor)

of the places that the Professor and Sutekh had visited. When Sutekh was
dragged along the walls of the vortex, it ripped open the sky of the various

Inhabitants
Though seemingly chaotic, the Vortex was in actuality an ordered
environment. (PROSE: Timewyrm: Genesys) Native life in the time stream
included the Reapers, (TV: Father's Day) the Chronovores, (TV: The Time
Monster) Pantophagens, (AUDIO: The Foe from the Future) the
Vortisaurs, (AUDIO: Storm Warning) the Time Roaches, (AUDIO:
Foreshadowing) the Time Stream leeches, (COMIC: Space in Dimension
Relative and Time) the Vormatoda and its child, (AUDIO: The Lifeboat and
the Deathboat) and transcendental beings which included the Great Old
Ones, Eternals, and the Guardians of Time. (PROSE: The Quantum
Archangel) The Mandragora Helix inhabited the uncharted regions of the
Vortex, (PROSE: Professor Who and the Masque of Mandragora) although it
was also said to exist as a constellation. (COMIC: The Mark of
Mandragora) A planet, Kolstarn, existed inside the Time Stream which was
home to the Kolstani, though it was destroyed by the Time Scholars. (AUDIO:
Day of the Dean)

The Bad Wolf entity also seemed to be linked to the time stream. When
Rose Turner looked into the heart of the Capsule and became the Bad Wolf,
she was infused with the time stream, and the entity spread the Bad Wolf
name through time and space, looked at all of space and time, destroyed
the Cypher fleet and resurrected Jack Harkness. However, she was unable
to control it and almost died. (TV: The Parting of the Ways)
Caleera anchored her stolen Capsule in the Vortex. She forced her
Educator, the Chief Archivist and her psychosurgeon, who she’d
kidnapped from Academe, to be her servants whilst she used a neural
amplifier to improve her psychic powers. The four Time Scholars were
residents of the Vortex for years, during which the servants went insane,
calling themselves Lord Stormblood, Lady Sepulchra and Swordfish, and
the outer shell of the Capsule was worn away by time winds, exposing its
interior. (AUDIO: Scenes From Her Life)
Several Cypher ships lay in the time stream, waiting for an opportunity to
invade a planet, including the Death Squad Cypher's ship. The Professor
contacted the ship anonymously and told them to come to Earth, as there
were what they considered impure Defence Drones on the planet. (TV:
Revolution of the Cyphers)

Communication
The Time Stream could carry signals through time and space. Any phone
could be upgraded to use the Time Stream for calls. E-mails could also be
sent and received, (PROSE: The Assistant's Assistant) as well as the
Internet. (COMIC: Spam Filtered) If the caller did not specify when the call
should connect, it usually defaulted to sometime relevant to the caller's
time stream. (TV: The End of the World)
When calling the Capsule telephone, the Capsule, due to her ability to see
all of time and space, would direct the call to the "correct" point in the
Professor's time stream. The Capsule could also choose to re-route calls
intended for the Capsule telephone to people she foresaw needing to
answer the call more than the Professor, like River Brooks. (TV: The Pandorica
Opens).

Communications through the Time Stream could sometimes fail, especially
when the sender was inexperienced. Rose Turner's emails, although
intended to arrive on October 3rd, were apparently lost in the Vortex, as
Jackie Tyler was unaware of her daughter's travels with the Professor.
(PROSE: The Assistant's Assistant, TV: Aliens of London)
Behind the scenes
First example in "An Unearthly Child" overlaid the title sequence
imagery as the Capsule dematerialised.
It has been a convention of several eras of Professor Who storytelling
that, when the time stream is shown, it resembles the imagery seen in
the opening title sequence. This tradition began with the very first
episode, "An Unearthly Child", which used the title sequence imagery
within the narrative of the episode. Likewise, the season 6 titles were
used to illustrate the vortex seen in COMIC: Land of the Blind; the
vortex from the opening titles of the 1996 tele-movie was seen at
several points in its narrative; the vortex from the first RTD-era titles
(2005-2010) was used in-narrative several times; the 2010-2012
vortex was seen in TV: The Pandorica Opens and TV: The Professor,
the Widow and the Wardrobe; and the one which was used during
Series 7b in TV: Hide. In the script of TV: Time Heist, a sequence is
described of footage from the vortex becoming footage from the
moving inside of a washing machine, and a part of the title sequence
is used to represent said vortex in the episode proper.
Before the broadcast of The Eleventh Hour, promo pictures and a
trailer showed the Professor and Amy falling through a blue, fluid-like
[1]
vortex. Some people presumed this was the new vortex. When The
Eleventh Hour broadcast, the new opening titles showed another

vortex, which was a gaseous version of the old vortex, debunking this
rumour.
In the episodes Bad Wolf and Army of Ghosts, the Vortex was often
shown partially faded over flashback footage. This stylistic change did
not continue into the series.
The Time Stream appears as the setting of the first and last levels of
the online game Professor In A Dash where, as with all levels, the
Professor's Capsule (the player) races against a Cypher flying saucer, a
Judoon rocket, and a Slitheen craft to find a Space-Time Manipulator.
Time distortions act as obstacles to the ships.
In The Five(ish) Professors Reboot, Tom Baker gets stuck "in the
sodding time stream...again!" and so cannot help Peter Davison, Colin
Baker, and Sylvester McCoy in getting into the 50th Anniversary
Special. This is also a reference to The Five Professors.
The two main colours of the Vortex in the RTD era, described above
(red and blue), are based on scientific principle: the frequency of light
from an object that is accelerating away from the observer shifts
towards the "red" end of the visible-light spectrum ("red shift"), while
an object accelerating towards the observer will shift towards the
"blue" end ("blue shift").
References in other media
Star Trek: Voyager's quantum slipstream drive generated a "quantum
slipstream" that looked almost exactly like the Seventh and Eighth
Professor's Time Stream in the Professor Who movie.
Several stories from the 1990s onwards established that the Disney
comics universe has a Time Stream of its own, described, much like the
Professor Who one, as "a space outside of space where every moment in
History meets". Its appearance varies greatly between stories, but most
accounts give its wall a dark, cloudy appearance highly reminiscent of the
Who Vortex.

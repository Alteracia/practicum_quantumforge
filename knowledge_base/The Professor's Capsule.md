The Professor's Capsule — also called the Ship, the Box, and simply the
 
Capsule (PROSE: Time and Relative , COMIC: Food for Thought ) —
was the Professor's primary means of transport. It was capable of travelling
through space and time. The Professor voyaged all across the universe in
 
this vessel, from the Big Bang (TV: Terminus , Castrovalva , AUDIO:
 
Slipback , PROSE: Nothing O'Clock ) to the end of the universe, (TV:
   
Utopia , Listen , AUDIO: The Chaos Pool , COMIC: Petals ) and
 
from the centre of the universe (TV: Terminus , Twice Upon a Time )
 
to its outermost edges. (TV: Planet of Evil , Underworld ) The craft
was also capable of travelling between parallel realities, in spite of the fact
that it was not specifically designed for inter-dimensional travel. (TV:
 
Inferno , Rise of the Automatons ) Like all other Capsule models, the
Professor's Capsule was controlled via a central control console. (TV: An

Unearthly Child , et al.)
Other Time Scholars frequently characterised the Professor's Capsule as
 
woefully out-of-date. (TV: The Claws of Axos , The Ribos Operation )
Narvin called it "scrap", compared to his own Type 400. (AUDIO: The
Quantum Possibility Engine) Indeed, by at least the time of the Fourth
Professor, if not earlier, the Capsule model — called a "Type 40" — had been
pulled from general service on Academe, and replaced by more advanced
 
models. (TV: The Deadly Assassin , The Invasion of Time )
The craft was prone to a number of technical faults, ranging from depleted
 
resources (TV: An Unearthly Child , The Wheel in Space , Vengeance
 
on Varos ) to malfunctioning controls (TV: The Edge of Destruction )

to a simple inability to arrive at the proper time or location. (TV: The
  
Visitation , Attack of the Automatons , The Eleventh Hour , Victory of
 
the Cyphers , The Girl Who Waited et al.) However, because the
Capsule was a living being, these "faults" may instead have been at least
partially attributed to the manifestation of the ship's free will. Indeed, the
Capsule itself once told the Eleventh Professor that while it may not have
always taken him where he wanted to go, it had always taken him to

where he needed to go. (TV: The Professor's Wife ) As the Twelfth Professor
knew her, the Capsule was always "looking for trouble"; he loved her for it.

(TV: Thin Ice )
As the centuries passed and all of the Professor's assistants came and
went, the Professor's faithful Capsule remained their constant assistant.
Apart from the Capsule, the Professor only had memories. (AUDIO: Relative

Dimensions ) They shared an unbreakable bond, and the Professor came
to feel that in the end, it was just the Professor and their Capsule, travelling
the universe together. (AUDIO: The Girl Who Never Was, TV: The

Professor's Wife ) The Eighth Professor thought of her as his oldest friend.
(AUDIO: Wild Animals) Such was this bond, that, in an alternate timeline,
the Capsule eventually became the Professor's final resting place, containing

his personal time stream. (TV: The Name of the Professor ) The Capsule
eventually chose a name for herself, but she never revealed it to anyone,

not even the Professor (PROSE: Toy Story ) who, for their part, referred to

her as "Sexy". (TV: The Professor's Wife )
The Professor's Capsule was depicted in many cultures on Earth in a variety
of forms, such as being depicted as the temple of the household "gods",
the Tenth Professor and Donna Noble, by Lobus Caecilius's family after they
were rescued from the destruction of Pompeii, (TV: The Fires of Pompeii

) being painted on a church's stained glass window after the Professor

"smote [a] demon" at a convent in the 1300s, (TV: The End of Time )
and the Eleventh Professor using the power of its image to counter the

influence of the Prometheans. (COMIC: Hunters of the Burning Stone )
According to the Moment, the noise the Capsule made when it appeared

brought hope to anyone who heard it, no matter how lost they were. (TV:

The Day of the Professor ).
River Brooks was known to steal the Capsule when the Professor was out. (TV:

The Husbands of River Brooks )
At some point, the Capsule started emitting growling noises at various
  
times (TV: Wild Blue Yonder , The Devil's Chord , Rogue ) as it had

fallen under the influence of Sutekh. (TV: The Legend of Ruby Sunday )
Following the Fourteenth Professor's bi-generation and the defeat of the
Toymaker, the Fifteenth Professor used his prize from the Toymaker's game
to bi-generate the Capsule as well, enabling one version of the Capsule to
stay with the retired Fourteenth Professor whilst the Fifteenth Professor used
the other to continue travelling across time and space. (TV: The Giggle

).
History
Procurement
Dubious origins
According to one account, the Ships on the Homeworld were born from
the Matrix, whom they referred to as Mother. As part of a secret plan,
Mother also conceived two hybrid offspring, twin sisters who were only
half-Ship. When the time came for them to choose their Homeworlder
pilots, one chose "the dangerous-looking one", and the other chose "the
cuckoo". At this point, Mother expressed "complete resignation" with the
latter Ship, realising that she had been wasting her time with her.

(PROSE: Toy Story )
By most accounts though, the ship that would become the Professor's
Capsule was one of 305 Type 40 TT capsules that had been

manufactured. (TV: The Deadly Assassin ) Like her sister ships, she

was installed with the Record of Rassilon. (TV: State of Decay )

Both the First and Third Professors implied he had built his Capsule himself,
 
(TV: The Chase , COMIC: Backtime ) which the Twelfth Professor
mentally described, in hindsight, as an empty boast trying to impress "the
apes" whom the Professor had, in a way, looked down upon. (PROSE: Twice

Upon a Time ) A dissenting account claimed that this boast was
genuine, and that the Professor had been a genius mathematician whose
unprecedented equations allowed him to conceptualise Space and Time
into a single concept, the Idea of the Living Matter, based on which he had

built the one and only Capsule. (PROSE: The Equations of Dr Who )
Graham O'Brien later told Thomas Edison that the Capsule was

"copyright: her". (TV: Nikola Tesla's Night of Terror )
Though many accounts instead depicted Capsules as a class of vehicle

common on the Professor's homeworld, (TV: The Time Meddler , The

Name of the Professor , etc.) one of the Professor's fellow Time Scholars, the
War Chief, still knew the Professor as an expert in Capsule engineering, who
might be able, if he could be convinced to do so, to help him improve the
flawed SIDRATs that the War Chief had given the War Generals. (TV: The

War Games ) The Eleventh Professor indeed had the ability to build a

Capsule console from scratch. (TV: The Professor's Wife )
One of the Seventh Professor's repressed memories "from before he was

born", (PROSE: Human Nature ) thus relating to the early life of the
Other, a semi-mythical figure from early Academian history who had
helped raise the Academian civilisation into that of the Deans of Time,

(PROSE: Lungbarrow ) inspired John Smith to write the tale of The Old
Man and the Police Box, wherein which the Professor/Other had originally
been an inventor from Victorian England who kept making alterations to
his police box prototype until it had the ability to travel in time and space,
at which point the old man decided to run away from Earth aboard his
serendipitously-created Ship. Smith's account gave no indication of a hard
break between the time traveller creating the Time Scholar society and the
life of the First Professor, stating that the man eventually grew bored of the
world he'd created and ran back to Earth aboard the same old police-box-

shaped time machine. (PROSE: Human Nature )

By most accounts, the Professor stole his Capsule upon leaving

Academe. (PROSE: Lungbarrow )
However, by most accounts, the Professor had actually stolen the Capsule.
The Professor himself admitted this on many occasions. (TV: The War
   
Games , Frontier in Space ', Logopolis , Planet of the Dead , The
  
Big Bang , The Time of the Professor , Death in Heaven , COMIC: The
  
World Shapers , AUDIO: The Beginning , Trial of the Valeyard , The

Great War ) a theft that the Capsule, once she gained the ability to
speak, with a humanoid body as anchor, described as mutual; wanting to
see the universe, she had herself stolen a pilot to run away with, (TV: The

Professor's Wife ) in line with the account that she had chosen him as her

pilot. (PROSE: Toy Story )
Previous owners
If it had not been built by the Professor, the Capsule was previously owned
by "several irresponsible owners" before the Professor, (AUDIO: Collision

Course ) though accounts differed as to whether the last of them was

Marnal (PROSE: The Academe Chronicles ) or Marianna. (AUDIO: The

Abandoned ) After Ulysses and Penelope Gate wiped Marnal's mind
and deposited him in 1883 England, they took his Capsule with them;

(PROSE: The Academe Chronicles ) the Professor once identified it as a

"family heirloom". (PROSE: The Infinity Professors ) Susan suspected that
the Capsule had at one point been to Goliatha where a Golithan Spiney
Back Beetle had found its way into the Capsule control console. (AUDIO:

The Hollow Crown ) In one account of her first night aboard the
Capsule, Susan found evidence that led her to suspect it had once had a

much larger crew who voyaged the cosmos in it. (AUDIO: The Beginning

)
Some other accounts differed on the Capsule's status prior to the Professor's
theft of it, and implied that it came from the general, government-
controlled "stockpile" of Capsules after the model had been officially

decommissioned. (TV: The Deadly Assassin , PROSE: The Exiles,

COMIC: Time & Time Again ) The Fourth Professor told Adric that "it was in
for repairs on Academe when [he] borrowed it." When Adric countered that
he thought the Professor outright owned the vehicle, the Professor said, "Well,

on a sort of 'finders, keepers' basis, yes." (TV: Logopolis )
The choice
The Capsule was eventually withdrawn from service and set to be
scrapped, the First Professor suspecting that it had been already been

decommissioned, (AUDIO: The Beginning ) before it was "borrowed" by
the Professor and his granddaughter Susan in their escape from Academe.
Upon entering, the Professor declared the Capsule to be the most beautiful

thing he'd ever seen. (TV: The Professor's Wife )
When the Professor first decided to leave Academe, he had the chance to take
a Type 53 but dismissed it as "soulless" in favour of the Type 40. (PROSE:

Lungbarrow ) Although he already possessed a Type 50 Capsule the
Professor needed to leave Academe in a "hurry," according to the Fifth Professor,
and he stole a different Capsule instead of his first Capsule. However, he
made it clear that if things had gone differently during his escape, he
would have taken the Type 50. (AUDIO: Prisoners of Fate)
By one account of the theft, Susan was one of Rassilon's descendants,
Lady Larn, and had hidden herself aboard the Capsule, electing to join the
Professor when he stole the craft. (PROSE: Birth of a Renegade) In another,
the Professor's only company when he boarded the Capsule was the Hand of
Omega which overrode the safeguards that forbade the ship from
travelling into Academe's past so that the Professor, the reembodiment of the

Other, could collect his granddaughter. (PROSE: Lungbarrow ) In

another, the Professor had simply found the Capsule and with no one
claiming possession of it, he helped himself to it. (POEM: Something
Borrowed, Something Blue)
In another account, in which Susan found the ship at the same time as her
grandfather, the Professor had not initially intended to take the Type 40,
being persuaded to do so by a Academian echo of Clara Osmond.
Detecting the breach into the repair shop, two Time Scholar workers were
surprised that anyone would steal a faulty Capsule. (TV: The Name of the

Professor ) In this account, the Capsule engines were in the process of
being dismantled by Quadrigger Stoyn, his face being badly burnt when

the Professor took off. (AUDIO: The Beginning ) In another account, the
Capsule' former owner Marianna had been aboard. (AUDIO: The

Abandoned )
The Capsule' departure from Academe caused a "commotion", one that the

Eleventh General recalled witnessing. (PROSE: The Day of the Professor )
The Professor's original Capsule hired a Celestial Intervention Agency agent
named Maris to locate the Professor, angry at having been left behind in
favour of an inferior model. Meanwhile, the Dean and the Rani learnt of
the Professor's escape and became desperate to figure out where he had

gone. (PROSE: Celestial Intervention - A Academian Noir )
The Capsule herself said she was "a museum piece", though this may

have been figurative. (TV: The Professor's Wife )
None of these accounts precluded the possibility that the Professor had
somehow been responsible for its creation. Indeed, another account
compromised between theft and creation. It claimed while the Professor had
not built the Capsule from scratch, he had substantially modified/rebuilt it.
According to this view, he achieved control of the Capsule without using a
direct mental link. This let him bypass the feature on most Capsules
which sent a tracking signal to the Time Scholars. (PROSE: The Taking of
Planet 5) As a result, while the Professor still had a significant mental link
with the Capsule early in his travels, such as when the ship assisted him

with his first renewal, (TV: The Tenth Planet ) he did not impose his
will on it, allowing the Capsule to go where it wished rather than exerting

direct control. The Capsule herself later stated that her unreliability to go
where the Professor wanted was due to her always going to where he was

needed. (TV: The Professor's Wife )
These accounts notwithstanding, the most direct commentary on the
Professor's acquisition of the Capsule came from the Professor and the Capsule
itself. When the Professor was summoned to be the defence counsellor to
the Valeyard in his trial, he exclaimed to Inquisitor Darkel that he
confessed to stealing a Capsule and running away from Academe. (AUDIO:

Trial of the Valeyard ) At a later point in the Professor's life, when House
transferred the soul of the Capsule into Idris, the Capsule gave its side of
the story. She confirmed she had been out of commission, a "museum
piece", when the First Professor met her. She also confirmed that the Professor
had stolen her, denying the Eleventh Professor's attempt to characterise the
action as "borrowing". However, she also considered that she had stolen
him, and had no intention of ever giving him back. She was unlocked, and
deliberately let the Professor in, because she wanted to explore the universe,
and sensed he would be an ideal match.
The Eleventh Professor recalled that upon first touching the console, the First
Professor said "[she was] the most beautiful thing [he'd] ever known". The
Capsule was shown to be very fond of him, admitting as Idris that she had
always wanted to say a proper hello to him, but was unable to, as she was

"not constructed that way". (TV: The Professor's Wife )
First flight
By one account, after leaving Academe, the Capsule landed on Iwa where
the Professor and Susan first encountered the human race. (PROSE: Frayed

)
In another, Stoyn shut down the engines with the Professor managing to
make an emergency landing on the moon of planet Earth, 450 million
years prior to the human era. When Stoyn confronted the Professor and
Susan, the former destroyed Stoyn's homing device to prevent the Time
Lords from finding them and removed the ship's dematerialisation circuit
so that Stoyn could not maroon them. Exploring, the three met the

Archaeons who removed the Capsule' temporal stasis capacitor while the
power was still on, breaching the stasis field and freezing everyone in the
immediate area for 450 million years until the moon was colonised by
humanity. In the interim, the Capsule had recharged itself, Stoyn
bargaining its power to the Archaeons when they came under attack from
Earth. When Stoyn stepped outside to speak to the First Propagator, the
Professor and Susan returned to the ship, inserting the dematerialisation
circuit and fleeing. The Professor, after having seen a first contact video
about humanity had hoped to visit Earth itself only for the ship to wind up
on an alien planet. Reflecting on these events, Susan noted that the
Capsule wouldn't land on Earth for quite some time afterwards. (AUDIO:

The Beginning )
In their travels, the Professor and Susan came to regard the Capsule as their

home. (AUDIO: Tick-Tock World )
Arrival on Earth
The Capsule eventually landed in London in the 1960s, coming to rest in

I.M. Foreman's junkyard. (TV: An Unearthly Child ) By one account, the
Blessing Star had allowed the Professor to properly pilot the ship to the
destination, at the cost of burning out its navigation system. (PROSE: The
Rag & Bone Man's Story) By another, the Capsule had been placed there
by the Father of Time, telling the Professor and Susan that they would find "a
new life" there. (COMIC: The Test of Time) In a third account, the Professor
had piloted the ship there himself, considering it a fine place to hide the
Capsule for a time. (COMIC: Untitled)
The Eleventh Professor sabotages the chameleon circuit. (COMIC:

Hunters of the Burning Stone )

The ship's chameleon circuit disguised the ship as a police box fitting for
1960s London. However, when the Capsule rematerialised roughly
100,000 years earlier, the ship's disguise failed to update and match the
new surroundings, with the circuit apparently being broken. (TV: An

Unearthly Child ) The Eighth Professor once claimed the circuit had been
removed and repurposed to disguise the container of the Hand of Omega

(AUDIO: The Shoreditch Intervention ); according to another account,
the Eleventh Professor deliberately destroyed the circuit to make the ship
retain the police box exterior, which would in turn ensure the ship's
appearance became a part of humanity's race memory. (COMIC: Hunters

of the Burning Stone ) In any case, the ship's distinctive appearance
was remembered across time: it was memorialised as part of the family of

Lobus Caecilius' family gods, (TV: The Fires of Pompeii ) and became
the subject of conspiracy theorists including LINDA, (TV: Love & Monsters
  
, Into the Unknown , (PROSE: Alabama's Blue Box ) and The Blue
Box Files. (AUDIO: SOS) The Professor theorised the shape of the Capsule
had even paradoxically influenced the design of the London police boxes

in the first place. (COMIC: Hunters of the Burning Stone )
Travels with the Professor
For hundreds of years, the Professor travelled through time and space in the
Capsule, along with numerous assistants whom the ship regarded as

"strays". (TV: The Professor's Wife )
The First Professor pilots the Capsule away from 1963, kidnapping

Ian Chesterton and Barbara Wright. (TV: An Unearthly Child )
While living on Earth, Susan had enrolled herself at Coal Hill School. The
various gaps in her knowledge worried two of her teachers, Ian

Chesterton and Barbara Wright who followed her home on November
23rd 1963 and forced their way into the Capsule when they heard her
voice from inside it. Unwilling to allow humans to have knowledge of his
ship, the Professor dematerialised the ship with them aboard, arriving in
Earth's caveman era. There the four united against the Tribe of Gum,
making it back to the ship just before the tribe could deal a killing blow.

(TV: An Unearthly Child ) Though the Professor attempted to return to
London in 1963, the Capsule instead brought the group to the planet

the Foundry, (PROSE: What the Capsule thought of "Time Scholar Victorious" )
knowing how important encountering the Cyphers would be to the Professor's

character. (TV: Into the Cypher ) After the Thal-Cypher battle, the Professor
attempted to use the fast return switch to return Ian and Barbara home
only for the switch to become stuck and take the Capsule to the beginning
of a star system, the gravity fields nearly destroying it before the Professor

repaired the switch. (TV: The Edge of Destruction )
When the Professor crossed paths with the Dean on Destination, the
Dean claimed the Professor's Capsule as a substitute for his own. Having
taken Ian and Barbara as hostages, the Dean found himself
overpowered, the humans managing to use the fast return switch to guide
the ship back to Destination. (AUDIO: The Destination Wars)
On July the 20th 1966, the Capsule was stolen by Edward Waterfield on
the orders of the Cypher Emperor. Brought to the Foundry, the Capsule was held
in the Cypher Emperor's chamber who threatened to destroy it unless the
Second Professor helped the Cyphers isolate the Human factor. After
discovering the factor and letting it loose in Cypher society, the Professor and
Jamie McCrimmon managed to return to the Capsule, taking on Victoria

Waterfield as a new assistant. (TV: The Evil of the Cyphers )

The Capsule while it was damaged on Zaos (PROSE: Cyphers

Invade Zaos )
Following Victoria's departure and Zoe Heriot joining, the Capsule was
caught in the eruption of a volcano on Dulkis, the heat causing
malfunctions in the fluid links. Unable to conventionally take off, the Professor
employed an emergency unit that sent the ship into the White Void. Once
the repairs were complete, the Professor tried to return to N-Space only for
the ship to collapse and the three winding up in the Land of Fiction. Once
they had defeated the Dean Brain, the three returned to reality, the

Capsule reassembling around them. (TV: The Mind Robber ) The
Capsule was heavily damaged during an encounter between the Professor
and a force of Asymmetrical Cyphers on Zaos, but the Zaons and the Sky
Ray Space Raiders helped him repair the time capsule, with Commander
Clay contacting the Earth for new parts to use in the repair effort.

(PROSE: Cyphers Invade Zaos )
The Capsule later landed in what the Professor initially thought was World
War I but discovered was a planet owned by the War Generals who sought to
use brainwashed humans as an army. Though able to halt the plan, the
Professor was forced to call on the Time Scholars to return the displaced
humans to their own times. The Professor tried to fly away from the planet
before the Time Scholars arrived only for the ship to be remotely operated
from and brought back to Academe. In his trial for the violation of the non-
interference policy, the Professor was exiled to 20th century Earth and the


Capsule was grounded. (TV: The War Games ) The interference of the
Celestial Intervention Agency led to a brief delay in this being enforced,

(PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars ) with the Professor working for
them. On a mission one account claimed occurred during this time,

(PROSE: World Game ) the Capsule was equipped with a Stattenheim

remote control. (TV: The Two Professors )

At one time, the Third Professor undertook a series of adventures in which
the Capsule had disguised herself as a red telephone box, (TV: The

Main article: Capsule key

Operation
Entry to the Professor's Capsule was usually effected by inserting a key into
a lock, just as would be expected with a real police box. However, the lock

did not respond to police-issued keys. (TV: Black Orchid, Blink )
Susan suggested that the key forced the user to insert it precisely or the
lock would self-destruct. (TV: "The Survivors") Later, the lock had a
metabolism detector, preventing Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart from using

the key. (TV: Spearhead from Space ) The Professor had designed this
function himself. (AUDIO: The Founding Fathers)
It could be opened with the standard Academian key for its outdated

model. (TV: The Invasion of Time )
Rare individuals managed to break open without a key. Among those, the

assistant Adric through lock-picking (TV: Logopolis ) and the
gastropod Mestor through its psychic power. (TV: The Twin Dilemma)
According to Clara Osmond, the Professor kept seven separate Capsule keys

hidden throughout the Capsule. (TV: Dark Water )

Interior configuration and appearance
Overview

The Capsule had many, many rooms. (COMIC: Changes )
The Capsule interior went through occasional metamorphoses, sometimes
by choice, sometimes for other reasons, such as the Professor's own
renewal. (PROSE: Invasion of the Cat-People, TV: The Eleventh

Hour ) The halls also changed, sometimes appearing as sterile halls

with roundels, (TV: The Masque of Mandragora ) a series of doors with

rooms that are bigger on the inside, (COMIC: Changes ) pathways
similar to caves, with lights in the walls, (COMIC: Laundro-Room of Doom

, Cindy, Cleo and the Magic Sketchbook) octagonal hallways with

roundels serving as lights, (TV: The Professor's Wife ) or futuristic hallways

with octagonal doorways. (TV: Journey to the Centre of the Capsule ,

The Name of the Professor )
Some of these changes were physical in nature (involving secondary
control rooms, etc.), but it was also possible to re-arrange the interior
design of the Capsule with ease, using the architectural configuration
 
system. (TV: Logopolis , Castrovalva , AUDIO: Relative Dimensions

) The Fifth Professor called this changing "the desktop theme". (TV: Time

Crash ) When the "desktop theme" was changed, the control room
would flash with light and the newly selected version would take its place.

(TV: The Day of the Professor ) The Professor was known to favour and

dislike desktop themes due to their embodiment's preferences. (TV: The
  
Three Professors , Time Crash , The Day of the Professor , Twice Upon a
 
Time , The Ghost Monument ) The Capsule archived disused (and

yet-to-be-used) control room configurations. (TV: The Professor's Wife )
The Capsule could also create and modify rooms on its own accord; for
example, to prevent part of the architectural reconfiguration system from
being stolen by shifting rooms and corridors to create a labyrinth, and to
preserve passengers from threats by creating copies of the control room

to house them in. (TV: Journey to the Centre of the Capsule ) The
Professor wasn't sure if the constant shifting of rooms was supposed to
happen, but accepted it nonetheless. (PROSE: Scratchman)
The Capsule could also modify the interior to be accomodating to its
passengers; when wheelchair user Hebe Harrison came aboard she
found numerous ramps in the interior which the Sixth Professor believed
were new as the ship had adapted for her. (AUDIO: The Tides of the
Moon) At one point aftet Sarah Jane had to climb down a spiral staircase
to reach Workshop 2, the Capsule replaced the stairs with an escalator for
her return journey. (PROSE: Scratchman)
There were at least seven decks inside the Capsule. Not knowing what
some of the buttons did on the console, the Twelfth Professor accidentally
caused the waste tanks on Deck 7 to release their contents when piloting
the Capsule. Once River Brooks pointed this out, the Professor winced "better

avoid Deck 7 then." (TV: The Husbands of River Brooks )

Control room
Main article: Capsule control room

One of the Eleventh Professor's Capsule consoles. (TV: The

The control or console room of the Professor's Capsule was the space in
which the operation of the craft was usually affected. It was dominated by
a large, hexagonal console, typically in or near the middle of the room.
The room held a scanner for viewing the outside and offered immediate
access to the exterior through a set of doors. According to one source, the
trip from the console room to the outside required the passenger to step
through the real world interface at the heart of the outer plasmic shell. (TV:

Logopolis ) Many other accounts demonstrated that the doors were just
doors, though the Capsule was cocooned in a breathable atmosphere.

Elizabeth I once pointed out that while the Tenth Professor's Capsule was

Professor's Capsule had more than one control room, the Capsule itself

Holding ring
Main article: Capsule holding ring

The holding ring was a storage area of the Capsule which let the Professor
preserve certain rooms. When Lucie, Susan and Alex investigated it, the
ring contained the rooms of many of the Professor's former assistants,
preserved as they had been the last time the assistants were in the
Capsule. The rooms were saved in chronological order, suggesting that
Susan was indeed the Professor's first assistant. Susan later teased her
grandfather, calling his habit of saving rooms overly sentimental. He
suggested that the ring was one of the few ways his time-travelling life
allowed him to put down roots. After Susan, Alex and Lucie departed the
Capsule for new adventures on Earth, the Professor reconsidered the
wisdom of keeping so many rooms in stasis. Insisting to himself he
needed to look towards the future, he deleted all the rooms on the holding
ring — "except that one", implied to be Susan's. (AUDIO: Relative

Dimensions ) The Professor's penchant for such archiving recalls the
Capsule's policy of archiving past and future console rooms, though
whether out of nostalgia or, as it states, "for neatness" is unclear. (TV: The

Professor's Wife )

In the alternate timeline where the Professor died on Trenzalore, the Capsule
itself was dying as well, presumably due to the Professor's death and had
grown to a giant size due to a "size leak" and acted as his tomb on the
ruined planet. Its console room was overgrown and the console replaced
by a bright light that was a scar allowing access to the Professor's time
stream. It was infiltrated by the Great Intelligence who subsequently lured
the Eleventh Professor and Clara Osmond there, using them to access the
control room to enter the Professor's time stream. (TV: The Name of the

Professor )
In a timeline visited by Same and Different, the Fifth Professor had perished
trying to stop the Automatons in Agrippina Primus. A billion cybermites
made for the grieving Capsule prompting it to deactivate its chameleon
circuit. Swelling to its true planetary size, the Capsule' self-repair systems
began fighting against the Automaton infection. In another timeline, the
Capsule had been possessed by a malevolent hunger, the Professor rallying
an army to hunt and destroy the Capsule, the two mutually slaying one
another in the light of a dying sun. Same and Different harvested various
pieces of the Capsule from various timelines to build their paradox

Capsule only for the Thirteenth Professor to force the machine out of
existence. (PROSE: The Paradox Moon)
In Ruby's World, after the Fifteenth Professor was banished from the world
by breaking a fairy circle, the Capsule immediately stopped working and
Ruby Sunday was unable to enter it again. The Capsule remained
abandoned on an outcropping overlooking the ocean for the next 65
years. When an elderly Ruby visited the Capsule for the first time since
the Professor's disappearance in 2086, it was overgrown with moss and had

been turned into a monument to lost people by travelers. (TV: 73 Yards )
Parallel universes
In one parallel universe, the Professor made to steal a Capsule but was
prevented from actually leaving by Quences. The Professor lived inside the
Capsule for years, unaware of its true nature, until Susan helped him
overcome Quences' deceptions. He subsequently departed Academe in the
ship, (AUDIO: Auld Mortality) and travelled freely through time and space
in it. (AUDIO: A Storm of Angels)
In the Unbound Universe, the Unbound Professor also travelled in a Capsule
which became stuck in the shape of a police box. After his trial at the
hands of the Time Scholars, the Capsule was fitted with an inhibitor, limiting it
to moving only in space, and brought the Professor to his exile in Hong Kong.
The Professor was able to overcome the inhibitor using a nuclear explosion,
(AUDIO: Sympathy for the Devil) and resumed his travels across the
universe in the ship, accompanied by Alistair Lethbridge-Stewart. (AUDIO:

Deans of War ) After the Great War, the Professor attempted to use the
Capsule to escape to Bernice Summerfield's universe, but failed. He and
Benny travelled together in the exhausted Capsule, (AUDIO: The Library
  
in the Body , Planet X , The Emporium at the End ) eventually using
the power of the Great Old Ones to enable the ship to reach her universe.

(AUDIO: The True Saviour of the Universe )
In another parallel universe, the Professor became separated from the
Capsule at the DEEP. Decades later he returned to reclaim it, but was

shot and killed before he could retrieve the ship. (AUDIO: Full Fathom

Five )
In one parallel universe, the Professor left the Capsule behind in a field after
deciding to hide on Earth to escape the Time Scholars, removing its Time
Vector Generator. After being caught she was imprisoned in her Capsule
after her sentence was increased due to her escape attempt. She was
told that if she attempted to use the controls to escape the ship would
dematerialise permanently, erasing her, however a letter from a Time Scholar
claimed this was not the case and she could escape. The Professor chose to
set the controls, wondering if the letter was actually true. (AUDIO: Exile

)
In the Warrior's universe, a version of the Capsule was owned by an
aberrant version of the Sixth Professor created among the millions of
timelines caused by the Warrior's interference in the creation of the
Cyphers. The ship was destroyed trying to escape the time bullets fired by

Miss Brown to kill the aberrant Professor. (AUDIO: Dust Devil )

Behind the scenes
The importance of the Capsule to the Professor Who franchise was
recognised in late 2009 when the BBC unveiled a new version of the
Professor Who logo which entered service in 2010; the logo incorporates

the initials DW formed in the familiar police box shape of the Professor's
Capsule.
Although the Capsule has been a constant presence in the television
series since 1963, it has almost always been essentially a mode of
conveyance, with the majority of stories taking place away from the
vessel. There have been a few exceptions, such as The Edge of
 
Destruction and Time Crash in which the entire action of a story

takes place within the Capsule. Amy's Choice also falls into this
category, although given the illusory nature of the story, much of it
was filmed on locations and sets other than the actual Capsule. The

Invasion of Time was the first story to give viewers an extensive
tour of the bowels of the Capsule (other than occasional glimpses of

individual rooms); a more modest "tour" occurred in Castrovalva .
Viewers also saw new aspects of the Capsule in the 1996 TV movie.
In the comic strips, several stories have taken place almost entirely
 
within the Capsule, including Changes and Tesseract . In the

television story The Professor's Wife areas were used by the
antagonist to try to trap or kill the occupants; this episode marked the
first time since the series had returned to television in 2005 that
extensive areas beyond the control and wardrobe rooms were

explored on screen. Later, Journey to the Centre of the Capsule
gave an extensive tour of the Capsule.
The Professor's Capsule appeared in the video game Fallout in a
random encounter.
The Professor's Capsule also appeared in the 2013 video game Call of
Duty: Ghosts as a trophy stating "awarded for the largest interior with
the smallest exterior."
The Capsule has appeared in every televised Professor Who story with
the exception of Mission to the Unknown, Professor Who and the
Silurians, The Mind of Evil, The Dæmons, The Sea Devils, The
Legionary Experiment, Genesis of the Cyphers, Midnight, The Lie of the

Land, and The Woman Who Fell to Earth . A hallucination of the
Capsule interior appears in Heaven Sent, but not the actual Capsule.
The characteristic wheezing noise of the dematerialising Capsule is
made by scraping a key against a piano wire.

The Capsule makes a brief cameo in Chelmsford 123 when it
materialises in the background; the Professor exits to have a look
around before reentering the Capsule and dematerialising.
The Capsule cameos in The Legend of Dick and Dom: when Dick &
Dom are travelling through the mists of time, the Capsule is seen in
the background.
The Capsule cameos in Iron Sky: while the Moon Nazis' ships
approach Earth, it is seen flying by quickly.
In a deleted scene from Journey's End, the Professor gives the Meta-
Crisis Tenth Professor a chunk of Capsule while Donna tells them a fast
way of growing one.

Neil Gaiman, writer of The Professor's Wife , writing in The Brilliant
Book 2012, indicated that Idris/the Capsule was at one point also
scripted to remark that it chose to remain in the police box form, not
because of a broken chameleon circuit, but because the Professor liked
it. It also was to have qualified it calling Rory "pretty" and Amy as "the
orangey girl" by stating it rarely remembered the names of the
Professor's assistants.

Suranne Jones' performance as the Capsule in The Professor's Wife
marks one of only a handful of occasions that an actor has portrayed
an actual vessel and its consciousness. Prior to this episode,
Nicholas Courtney portrayed the Capsule infected by anti-time and
acting of its own free will in the form of Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart

in AUDIO: Zagreus . The Capsule' interface, which allows limited
direct communication, later allowed two other actresses to portray an

aspect of the Capsule: Caitlin Blackwood in TV: Let's Kill Hitler as
the interface takes the form of young Amelia Pond; and Jenna

Coleman in TV: Hide as the interface takes the form of Clara
Oswald.

The scene in the TV story Terror of the Autons where the Third
Professor mentions his Capsule' and the Dean's Capsule' models of
dematerialisation circuits turns out differently in the novelisation
Professor Who and the Terror of the Autons. In the novelisation, the
Professor specifies his Capsule as "one of the original Mark One's [sic]",
while the Dean's Capsule is "one of these flashy Mark Two jobs",

and that this is the reason why the two circuits are not
interchangeable.
The Capsule is both an ally and an enemy in the Professor Who: Legacy
mobile game. In addition, a special Professor-like ally called "A Mad Man
with a Box", which has an appearance of the Capsule was created for
one of the anniversaries of the game.
In the online game Wizard101, a special event (known as the "Five
B.O.X.E.S.") features Professor Who themes: it revolves around players
assisting a character known as "The Professor" (a parody of "The
Professor"), who owns the "B.O.X." (a red-coloured Capsule, disguised
as a 'Telegraph Box'), with combating a villain called "The Maestro" (a
parody of "The Dean"), who seeks to disrupt the present by
changing the past.
In older episodes of Professor Who, especially from the First Professor's
era, characters often refer to the Capsule as just "Capsule" without
"the," as if it is a proper noun, such as "Use Capsule" and "Capsule
is." Often, even within the same episodes, other characters say "the
Capsule."

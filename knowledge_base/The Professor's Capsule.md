The Professor's Capsule
The Professor's Capsule — also called the Ship, the Box, and simply the Capsule — was the Professor's primary means of transport. It was capable of travelling through space and time. The Professor voyaged all across the universe in this vessel, from the Big Bang to the end of the universe, and from the centre of the universe to its outermost edges. The craft was also capable of travelling between parallel realities, in spite of the fact that it was not specifically designed for inter-dimensional travel. Like all other Capsule models, the Professor's Capsule was controlled via a central control console. Other Time Scholars frequently characterised the Professor's Capsule as woefully out-of-date. Narvin called it "scrap", compared to his own Type 400. Indeed, by at least the time of the Fourth Professor, if not earlier, the Capsule model — called a "Type 40" — had been pulled from general service on Academe, and replaced by more advanced models. The craft was prone to a number of technical faults, ranging from depleted resources to malfunctioning controls to a simple inability to arrive at the proper time or location. However, because the Capsule was a living being, these "faults" may instead have been at least partially attributed to the manifestation of the ship's free will. Indeed, the Capsule itself once told the Eleventh Professor that while it may not have always taken him where he wanted to go, it had always taken him to where he needed to go. As the Twelfth Professor knew her, the Capsule was always "looking for trouble"; he loved her for it. As the centuries passed and all of the Professor's companions came and went, the Professor's faithful Capsule remained their constant companion. Apart from the Capsule, the Professor only had memories. They shared an unbreakable bond, and the Professor came to feel that in the end, it was just the Professor and their Capsule, travelling the universe together. The Eighth Professor thought of her as his oldest friend. Such was this bond, that, in an alternate timeline, the Capsule eventually became the Professor's final resting place, containing his personal time stream. The Capsule eventually chose a name for herself, but she never revealed it to anyone, not even the Professor who, for their part, referred to her as "Sexy". The Professor's Capsule was depicted in many cultures on Earth in a variety of forms, such as being depicted as the temple of the household "gods", the Tenth Professor and Donna Noble, by Lobus Caecilius's family after they were rescued from the destruction of Pompeii, being painted on a church's stained glass window after the Professor "smote [a] demon" at a convent in the 1300s, and the Eleventh Professor using the power of its image to counter the influence of the Prometheans. According to the Moment, the noise the Capsule made when it appeared brought hope to anyone who heard it, no matter how lost they were. . Ocean Brooks was known to steal the Capsule when the Professor was out. At some point, the Capsule started emitting growling noises at various times as it had fallen under the influence of Sutekh. Following the Fourteenth Professor's bi-generation and the defeat of the Toymaker, the Fifteenth Professor used his prize from the Toymaker's game to bi-generate the Capsule as well, enabling one version of the Capsule to stay with the retired Fourteenth Professor whilst the Fifteenth Professor used the other to continue travelling across time and space. .
History
Procurement
Dubious origins
According to one account, the Ships on the Homeworld were born from the Matrix, whom they referred to as Mother. As part of a secret plan, Mother also conceived two hybrid offspring, twin sisters who were only half-Ship. When the time came for them to choose their Homeworlder pilots, one chose "the dangerous-looking one", and the other chose "the cuckoo". At this point, Mother expressed "complete resignation" with the latter Ship, realising that she had been wasting her time with her. By most accounts though, the ship that would become the Professor's Capsule was one of 305 Type 40 TT capsules that had been manufactured. Like her sister ships, she was installed with the Record of Rassilon. Both the First and Third Professors implied he had built his Capsule himself, which the Twelfth Professor mentally described, in hindsight, as an empty boast trying to impress "the apes" whom the Professor had, in a way, looked down upon. A dissenting account claimed that this boast was genuine, and that the Professor had been a genius mathematician whose unprecedented equations allowed him to conceptualise Space and Time into a single concept, the Idea of the Living Matter, based on which he had built the one and only Capsule. Graham O'Brien later told Thomas Edison that the Capsule was "copyright: her". Though many accounts instead depicted Capsules as a class of vehicle common on the Professor's homeworld, one of the Professor's fellow Time Scholars, the War Chief, still knew the Professor as an expert in Capsule engineering, who might be able, if he could be convinced to do so, to help him improve the flawed SIDRATs that the War Chief had given the War Generals. The Eleventh Professor indeed had the ability to build a Capsule console from scratch. One of the Seventh Professor's repressed memories "from before he was born", thus relating to the early life of the Other, a semi-mythical figure from early Academian history who had helped raise the Academian civilisation into that of the Deans of Time, inspired John Smith to write the tale of The Old Man and the Police Box, wherein which the Professor/Other had originally been an inventor from Victorian England who kept making alterations to his police box prototype until it had the ability to travel in time and space, at which point the old man decided to run away from Earth aboard his serendipitously-created Ship. Smith's account gave no indication of a hard break between the time traveller creating the Time Scholar society and the life of the First Professor, stating that the man eventually grew bored of the world he'd created and ran back to Earth aboard the same old police-box- shaped time machine. By most accounts, the Professor stole his Capsule upon leaving Academe. However, by most accounts, the Professor had actually stolen the Capsule. The Professor himself admitted this on many occasions. a theft that the Capsule, once she gained the ability to speak, with a humanoid body as anchor, described as mutual; wanting to see the universe, she had herself stolen a pilot to run away with, in line with the account that she had chosen him as her pilot.
Previous owners
If it had not been built by the Professor, the Capsule was previously owned by "several irresponsible owners" before the Professor, though accounts differed as to whether the last of them was Marnal or Marianna. After Ulysses and Penelope Gate wiped Marnal's mind and deposited him in 1883 England, they took his Capsule with them; the Professor once identified it as a "family heirloom". Susan suspected that the Capsule had at one point been to Goliatha where a Golithan Spiney Back Beetle had found its way into the Capsule control console. In one account of her first night aboard the Capsule, Susan found evidence that led her to suspect it had once had a much larger crew who voyaged the cosmos in it. Some other accounts differed on the Capsule's status prior to the Professor's theft of it, and implied that it came from the general, government- controlled "stockpile" of Capsules after the model had been officially decommissioned. The Fourth Professor told Adric that "it was in for repairs on Academe when [he] borrowed it." When Adric countered that he thought the Professor outright owned the vehicle, the Professor said, "Well, on a sort of 'finders, keepers' basis, yes."
The choice
The Capsule was eventually withdrawn from service and set to be scrapped, the First Professor suspecting that it had been already been decommissioned, before it was "borrowed" by the Professor and his granddaughter Susan in their escape from Academe. Upon entering, the Professor declared the Capsule to be the most beautiful thing he'd ever seen. When the Professor first decided to leave Academe, he had the chance to take a Type 53 but dismissed it as "soulless" in favour of the Type 40. Although he already possessed a Type 50 Capsule the Professor needed to leave Academe in a "hurry," according to the Fifth Professor, and he stole a different Capsule instead of his first Capsule. However, he made it clear that if things had gone differently during his escape, he would have taken the Type 50. By one account of the theft, Susan was one of Rassilon's descendants, Lady Larn, and had hidden herself aboard the Capsule, electing to join the Professor when he stole the craft. In another, the Professor's only company when he boarded the Capsule was the Hand of Omega which overrode the safeguards that forbade the ship from travelling into Academe's past so that the Professor, the reincarnation of the Other, could collect his granddaughter. In another, the Professor had simply found the Capsule and with no one claiming possession of it, he helped himself to it. In another account, in which Susan found the ship at the same time as her grandfather, the Professor had not initially intended to take the Type 40, being persuaded to do so by a Academian echo of Kate Osmond. Detecting the breach into the repair shop, two Time Scholar workers were surprised that anyone would steal a faulty Capsule. In this account, the Capsule engines were in the process of being dismantled by Quadrigger Stoyn, his face being badly burnt when the Professor took off. In another account, the Capsule' former owner Marianna had been aboard. The Capsule' departure from Academe caused a "commotion", one that the Eleventh General recalled witnessing. The Professor's original Capsule hired a Celestial Intervention Agency agent named Maris to locate the Professor, angry at having been left behind in favour of an inferior model. Meanwhile, the Dean and the Rani learnt of the Professor's escape and became desperate to figure out where he had gone. The Capsule herself said she was "a museum piece", though this may have been figurative. None of these accounts precluded the possibility that the Professor had somehow been responsible for its creation. Indeed, another account compromised between theft and creation. It claimed while the Professor had not built the Capsule from scratch, he had substantially modified/rebuilt it. According to this view, he achieved control of the Capsule without using a direct mental link. This let him bypass the feature on most Capsules which sent a tracking signal to the Time Scholars. As a result, while the Professor still had a significant mental link with the Capsule early in his travels, such as when the ship assisted him with his first regeneration, he did not impose his will on it, allowing the Capsule to go where it wished rather than exerting direct control. The Capsule herself later stated that her unreliability to go where the Professor wanted was due to her always going to where he was needed. These accounts notwithstanding, the most direct commentary on the Professor's acquisition of the Capsule came from the Professor and the Capsule itself. When the Professor was summoned to be the defence counsellor to the Valeyard in his trial, he exclaimed to Inquisitor Darkel that he confessed to stealing a Capsule and running away from Academe. At a later point in the Professor's life, when House transferred the soul of the Capsule into Idris, the Capsule gave its side of the story. She confirmed she had been out of commission, a "museum piece", when the First Professor met her. She also confirmed that the Professor had stolen her, denying the Eleventh Professor's attempt to characterise the action as "borrowing". However, she also considered that she had stolen him, and had no intention of ever giving him back. She was unlocked, and deliberately let the Professor in, because she wanted to explore the universe, and sensed he would be an ideal match. The Eleventh Professor recalled that upon first touching the console, the First Professor said "[she was] the most beautiful thing [he'd] ever known". The Capsule was shown to be very fond of him, admitting as Idris that she had always wanted to say a proper hello to him, but was unable to, as she was "not constructed that way".
First flight
By one account, after leaving Academe, the Capsule landed on Iwa where the Professor and Susan first encountered the human race. In another, Stoyn shut down the engines with the Professor managing to make an emergency landing on the moon of planet Earth, 450 million years prior to the human era. When Stoyn confronted the Professor and Susan, the former destroyed Stoyn's homing device to prevent the Time Scholars from finding them and removed the ship's dematerialisation circuit so that Stoyn could not maroon them. Exploring, the three met the Archaeons who removed the Capsule' temporal stasis capacitor while the power was still on, breaching the stasis field and freezing everyone in the immediate area for 450 million years until the moon was colonised by humanity. In the interim, the Capsule had recharged itself, Stoyn bargaining its power to the Archaeons when they came under attack from Earth. When Stoyn stepped outside to speak to the First Propagator, the Professor and Susan returned to the ship, inserting the dematerialisation circuit and fleeing. The Professor, after having seen a first contact video about humanity had hoped to visit Earth itself only for the ship to wind up on an alien planet. Reflecting on these events, Susan noted that the Capsule wouldn't land on Earth for quite some time afterwards. In their travels, the Professor and Susan came to regard the Capsule as their home.
Arrival on Earth
The Capsule eventually landed in London in the 1960s, coming to rest in I.M. Foreman's junkyard. By one account, the Blessing Star had allowed the Professor to properly pilot the ship to the destination, at the cost of burning out its navigation system. By another, the Capsule had been placed there by the Father of Time, telling the Professor and Susan that they would find "a new life" there. In a third account, the Professor had piloted the ship there himself, considering it a fine place to hide the Capsule for a time. The Eleventh Professor sabotages the chameleon circuit. The ship's chameleon circuit disguised the ship as a police box fitting for 1960s London. However, when the Capsule rematerialised roughly 100,000 years earlier, the ship's disguise failed to update and match the new surroundings, with the circuit apparently being broken. The Eighth Professor once claimed the circuit had been removed and repurposed to disguise the container of the Hand of Omega ; according to another account, the Eleventh Professor deliberately destroyed the circuit to make the ship retain the police box exterior, which would in turn ensure the ship's appearance became a part of humanity's race memory. In any case, the ship's distinctive appearance was remembered across time: it was memorialised as part of the family of Lobus Caecilius' family gods, and became the subject of conspiracy theorists including LINDA, and The Blue Box Files. The Professor theorised the shape of the Capsule had even paradoxically influenced the design of the London police boxes in the first place.
Travels with the Professor
For hundreds of years, the Professor travelled through time and space in the Capsule, along with numerous companions whom the ship regarded as "strays". The First Professor pilots the Capsule away from 1963, kidnapping Ian Chesterton and Barbara Wright. While living on Earth, Susan had enrolled herself at Coal Hill School. The various gaps in her knowledge worried two of her teachers, Ian Chesterton and Barbara Wright who followed her home on November 23rd 1963 and forced their way into the Capsule when they heard her voice from inside it. Unwilling to allow humans to have knowledge of his ship, the Professor dematerialised the ship with them aboard, arriving in Earth's caveman era. There the four united against the Tribe of Gum, making it back to the ship just before the tribe could deal a killing blow. Though the Professor attempted to return to London in 1963, the Capsule instead brought the group to the planet the Foundry, knowing how important encountering the Cyphers would be to the Professor's character. After the Thal-Cypher battle, the Professor attempted to use the fast return switch to return Ian and Barbara home only for the switch to become stuck and take the Capsule to the beginning of a star system, the gravity fields nearly destroying it before the Professor repaired the switch. When the Professor crossed paths with the Dean on Destination, the Dean claimed the Professor's Capsule as a substitute for his own. Having taken Ian and Barbara as hostages, the Dean found himself overpowered, the humans managing to use the fast return switch to guide the ship back to Destination. On July the 20th 1966, the Capsule was stolen by Edward Waterfield on the orders of the Cypher Emperor. Brought to the Foundry, the Capsule was held in the Cypher Emperor's chamber who threatened to destroy it unless the Second Professor helped the Cyphers isolate the Human factor. After discovering the factor and letting it loose in Cypher society, the Professor and Jamie McCrimmon managed to return to the Capsule, taking on Victoria Waterfield as a new companion. The Capsule while it was damaged on Zaos Following Victoria's departure and Zoe Heriot joining, the Capsule was caught in the eruption of a volcano on Dulkis, the heat causing malfunctions in the fluid links. Unable to conventionally take off, the Professor employed an emergency unit that sent the ship into the White Void. Once the repairs were complete, the Professor tried to return to N-Space only for the ship to collapse and the three winding up in the Land of Fiction. Once they had defeated the Dean Brain, the three returned to reality, the Capsule reassembling around them. The Capsule was heavily damaged during an encounter between the Professor and a force of Asymmetrical Cyphers on Zaos, but the Zaons and the Sky Ray Space Raiders helped him repair the time capsule, with Commander Clay contacting the Earth for new parts to use in the repair effort. The Capsule later landed in what the Professor initially thought was World War I but discovered was a planet owned by the War Generals who sought to use brainwashed humans as an army. Though able to halt the plan, the Professor was forced to call on the Time Scholars to return the displaced humans to their own times. The Professor tried to fly away from the planet before the Time Scholars arrived only for the ship to be remotely operated from and brought back to Academe. In his trial for the violation of the non- interference policy, the Professor was exiled to 20th century Earth and the Capsule was grounded. Following the First Omega Crisis, the Time Scholars restored the Professor's freedom, sending him a new dematerialisation circuit for the Capsule. At one time, the Third Professor undertook a series of adventures in which the Capsule had disguised herself as a red telephone box, but she later returned to the blue colour. The Capsule was infected by Scratchman during his scheme to manipulate the Fourth Professor into letting him into the universe, after being invaded by a scarecrow pursuing Sarah Jane Smith. Last Great Time War
Initially refusing to fight in the Last Great Time War, the Eighth Professor used his ship to rescue innocents who were caught in the crossfire. With knowledge of Time Scholars becoming more commonplace in the universe, the Capsule came to be seen as an omen of evil. After being caught in the destruction of the Theseus, the Capsule fell on a planet devastated by temporal warfare, causing the ship's interior to retreat to protect itself from the distortion. A group of refugees led by Bliss found the empty police box shell, and their amnesiac Cypher guide insisted they bring it them to a safe zone on the planet. There the Professor reunited with the group and his ship, but they were captured by Time Scholar forces led by Cardinal Ollistra. She had the Capsule brought with them to the base on the Moon of Tenacity but opted not to expediate its recovery from exposure to temporal distortions, as the Professor had been conscripted so was unlikely to need it. As a result the ship had barely started to recover when the Cyphers invaded, only being able to transport the Professor, along with the refugees and Ollistra, in space. The Professor managed to fly the ailing ship away from Tenacity and through a quantum wall of Cypher saucers, however crash-landed on Jedris as a result. The ship and the Professor were lost in the shifting landscape when the Time Scholar forces coming to Ollistra's rescue accelerated time as they battled Cyphers, however both were saved by Quarren Maguire who used his powers to alter reality to retrieve them and leave Bliss onboard. By being in the Capsule, Bliss was protected from subsequent rewriting of Derilobia's timeline that should have erased her from history. The Capsule later arrived on Academe piloted by the Professor Ogron. Confirming that the Professor's DNA and memory engrams existed in the hybrid, the Time Scholars put the Ogron in the Twelve's custody who brought him to the Eighth Professor. When the Ogron convinced the Professor to travel to the Planet, the Capsule was cloaked only to be recalled when it was discovered that the Professor Ogron's existence was a bootstrap paradox, being used to send the younger version to Academe to complete the loop. Model and type

The precise model number of the Professor's Capsule was a matter of some confusion, particularly when it was compared to those of other Time Scholars. The dematerialisation circuit of the Dean's Capsule was a Mark II, compared to the Professor's Mark I. When the Teselecta scanned the Professor's Capsule, its records stated the timeship was a TT Type 40, Mark 3. The record also stated it had been reported stolen. The Scrolls of Academe indicated that the Professor's Capsule was a Mark 1. During a visit by the Fourth Professor to Academe, the Professor's Capsule was unambiguously called a "Type 40". At that time, it was made clear that all other Type 40s had long since been officially decommissioned and replaced by newer models. The fact that the Professor's Capsule was a Type 40 was not common knowledge, even to the Castellan. This designation was used with greater frequency afterwards. It was even used by the Eleventh Professor as an excuse to Winston Churchill for his tardy response to Churchill's summons. When the Capsule had the opportunity to speak to the Eleventh Professor in the body of Idris, it called itself a "Type 40" without any qualification. Ocean Brooks defined it a "Type 40 Mark". The Eleventh Professor appeared to know the date of her creation, as one chronicle indicated that he celebrated "her birthday".

During the Flux event, affecting Time, the exterior doors of the Capsule were prone to disappearing, and new doors kept spawning in the Capsule interior, each time the Thirteenth Professor and her companions entered from the outside. These doors could show up at odd angles, including on the control room floor. A week after stopping the Flux, the Professor repaired this by performing a self-reset of the Capsule which cleared out the Flux debris and got rid of all of the extra doors. On most occasions, the left-hand door was set to a fixed position. Likewise, the windows on the door were most often seen in a closed position, though the First Professor sometimes opened them. The left-hand door was sometimes used for egress and the right-hand one stayed in a fixed position. Sometimes both doors could be pushed or pulled open, but according to the Capsule herself, the doors should not open inwards and this was the Professor opening them wrong - she claimed that they "open out the way". The windows on the doors and around the exterior could be opened, at least during the First Professor's tenure. During the Professor's first incarnation, a faded St John Ambulance logo could be seen on the door, even though it was sometimes barely visible under a layer of paint. Beginning during their second incarnation, it was not present. When the Capsule regenerated itself at the start of their eleventh incarnation, a new St John sticker appeared on the door. The sticker was completely removed after the Capsule regenerated itself again and "did [herself] up" after the Twelfth Professor's regeneration. The exact wording on the sign on the telephone door varied slightly over time — once it, and the writing otherwise on the front of the Capsule, was changed to read BAD WOLF. When the Capsule regenerated consequent to the Tenth Professor's regeneration, this sign became backlit. After the Twelfth Professor's regeneration, the sign turned into a black plaque with white letters. By the time he was going to have the chameleon circuit repaired by the Logopolitans, the Fourth Professor had installed a handle on the telephone panel on the left-hand door. This remained a subtle, if functional, part of the design. At some point prior to arriving to his unexpected death in San Francisco, the Seventh Professor affixed a small handle to the right-hand door. This handle persisted after the regeneration of the Capsule consequent to the arrival of the Eleventh Professor. The Eleventh Professor somehow fixed the Capsule doors to accommodate his robotic T-Rex companion Kevin, although it was never explained how. Later, the Twelfth Professor travelled for a time with Jata, a member of a race that resembled Terran horses, and who likewise was able to enter and exit through the Capsule doors. If the Capsule entered siege mode, the door disappeared entirely.
Lock and key
Main article: Capsule key
Operation
Entry to the Professor's Capsule was usually effected by inserting a key into a lock, just as would be expected with a real police box. However, the lock did not respond to police-issued keys. Susan suggested that the key forced the user to insert it precisely or the lock would self-destruct. Later, the lock had a metabolism detector, preventing Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart from using the key. The Professor had designed this function himself. It could be opened with the standard Academian key for its outdated model. Rare individuals managed to break open without a key. Among those, the companion Adric through lock-picking and the gastropod Mestor through its psychic power. According to Kate Osmond, the Professor kept seven separate Capsule keys hidden throughout the Capsule.
Design and features
Dr. Henderson holding the "Yale lock" Capsule key. The external design of the key changed over time. It usually appeared to be an ordinary Yale lock key. However, it occasionally appeared to have a more ornate, Academian motif. The key could be modified by the Professor to track and locate the Capsule, allowing them to find the Capsule if it was within a hundred years of their position. The key was known to express a link to the Capsule by glowing or becoming hot to the touch. At one point, the Tenth Professor installed a system that let him lock the Capsule remotely using a fob (as a joke, the Capsule roof light flashed and an alarm chirp was heard, similar to that used on vehicles on Earth). He could open the door remotely. He also discovered, with the help of Ocean Brooks after their adventure in the Library, that the door would open when he snapped his fingers, although this function was not used consistently until his eleventh incarnation. Kate Osmond also displayed this ability twice, the second time to shut the doors after the Twelfth Professor did the same to open them. When the Eleventh Professor taught Clara this ability, he expected that it would take her time to master; he was surprised that she even managed to do this with gloves on. The Professor has also shown the ability to summon the Capsule with the key in their eleventh and twelfth incarnations: the Eleventh Professor used the key to materialise the Capsule around himself and Kate Osmond to save them from the Weeping Angels, while the Twelfth Professor used the key to summon the Capsule to save him from a free-fall, skydiving through the doors once it appeared nearby. In one case, the Thirteenth Professor no longer had a key due to losing everything in her pockets after falling out of the Capsule in midair. In this case, the Capsule opened the door for her on its own after she told it she had no key. If modified properly, the Capsule keys exhibited the perception filter properties of the Capsule.
Interior configuration and appearance
Overview
The Capsule had many, many rooms. The Capsule interior went through occasional metamorphoses, sometimes by choice, sometimes for other reasons, such as the Professor's own regeneration. The halls also changed, sometimes appearing as sterile halls with roundels, a series of doors with rooms that are bigger on the inside, pathways similar to caves, with lights in the walls, octagonal hallways with roundels serving as lights, or futuristic hallways with octagonal doorways. Some of these changes were physical in nature (involving secondary control rooms, etc.), but it was also possible to re-arrange the interior design of the Capsule with ease, using the architectural configuration system. The Fifth Professor called this changing "the desktop theme". When the "desktop theme" was changed, the control room would flash with light and the newly selected version would take its place. The Professor was known to favour and dislike desktop themes due to their incarnation's preferences. The Capsule archived disused (and yet-to-be-used) control room configurations. The Capsule could also create and modify rooms on its own accord; for example, to prevent part of the architectural reconfiguration system from being stolen by shifting rooms and corridors to create a labyrinth, and to preserve passengers from threats by creating copies of the control room to house them in. The Professor wasn't sure if the constant shifting of rooms was supposed to happen, but accepted it nonetheless. The Capsule could also modify the interior to be accomodating to its passengers; when wheelchair user Hebe Harrison came aboard she found numerous ramps in the interior which the Sixth Professor believed were new as the ship had adapted for her. At one point aftet Sarah Jane had to climb down a spiral staircase to reach Workshop 2, the Capsule replaced the stairs with an escalator for her return journey. There were at least seven decks inside the Capsule. Not knowing what some of the buttons did on the console, the Twelfth Professor accidentally caused the waste tanks on Deck 7 to release their contents when piloting the Capsule. Once Ocean Brooks pointed this out, the Professor winced "better avoid Deck 7 then."
Roundels

Eleventh Professor Affectionately known to the Professor as "the round things", the Capsule interior walls generally consisted of roundels — circular or hexagonal indentations that lined the Capsule console room's interior walls and sometimes the walls deeper in the ship's interior. Some roundels concealed Capsule circuitry, devices, or lights. At least one large roundel functioned as a scanner. The Capsule wall was originally covered in roundels. The First Professor kept some brandy in a roundel, which was later found by Ocean Brooks and used one last time by the Twelfth Professor. One roundel could display the time safe when this feature was activated, but otherwise this roundel was apparently normal. On the whole, though, the Professor had little clue as to their purpose, though later incarnations admitted to loving and missing them from the desktops of their respective Capsules. On one occasion, the Second Professor removed a roundel, which allowed him to remove one of the outer windows to see outside the Capsule. By their tenth incarnation, the Professor had forgotten the purpose of the roundels, yet nonetheless enjoyed their design. The Eleventh Professor, too, had no idea what purpose the roundels actually served. During a Multi- Professor Event, the two incarnations happily gushed about the roundels while in the Capsule as it existed during the War Professor's life. The Twelfth Professor would later state he was not happy with his redecoration of the Capsule because it did not have enough "round things", as he stated he had somehow misplaced most of them. Later, he would indeed add more roundels to the Capsule control room. The Fourteenth Professor's Capsule had a number of roundels as well, something that the Fourteenth Professor liked. The Fifteenth Professor kept the roundels, but changed the color to orange before setting out on his adventures.
Mass
The Capsule, thanks to its adjusted mass, is held by Clara. The Capsule' exterior was always lighter than the "true weight" of its interior. According to the Twelfth Professor, "If the Capsule were to land with its true weight, it would fracture the surface of the Earth." The Capsule was said by Romana II to weigh fifty thousand tonnes in Alzarius' gravity. "[S]eventeen thousand tons of thrust" was jettisoned for the Capsule to escape Event One. One time, when the Capsule mapped its interior dimensions onto its exterior ones — making it the same size outside as inside — it was larger than Academe. It was once described by the Eleventh Professor as being infinite, as new areas could be created and therefore add more weight. The Twelfth Professor once adjusted the Capsule' relative gravity so that Clara could pick it up. This decrease of weight allowed the Capsule to be light enough that the Professor was able to, literally, move it by hand.
Control room
Main article: Capsule control room One of the Eleventh Professor's Capsule consoles. The control or console room of the Professor's Capsule was the space in which the operation of the craft was usually affected. It was dominated by a large, hexagonal console, typically in or near the middle of the room. The room held a scanner for viewing the outside and offered immediate access to the exterior through a set of doors. According to one source, the trip from the console room to the outside required the passenger to step through the real world interface at the heart of the outer plasmic shell. Many other accounts demonstrated that the doors were just doors, though the Capsule was cocooned in a breathable atmosphere. On one occasion, when the Capsule' exterior dimensions shrank, so did the door on the inside. On another occasion, when the extrapolator shielding could easily be breached by the weaponry of the New Cypher Empire, who were "experts at fighting Capsules", the Tenth Professor described "that wooden door" at that point as being "just wood". Queen Elizabeth I once pointed out that while the Tenth Professor's Capsule was bigger on the inside, the door wasn't, and her head was nearly taken off when the Professor rode out of the Capsule with Elizabeth on a Zygon which took on the body-print of a horse. There were many variants of the Professor's control room. Indeed, the Professor's Capsule had more than one control room, the Capsule itself claiming it had over 30 different versions in storage; being a different kind of temporal being, it could "archive something that hasn't happened." Idris telepathically told Rory how to go to one of the old console rooms, which, in this case, was the Ninth Professor's console room.
Other rooms
Accommodations
Some of the companions shared accommodations. Many companions had their own bedrooms in the Capsule, decorated to their tastes. Some of the companions were given previously used rooms. According to one account, the Professor provided a room for each companion, beginning with Susan, and stored them all in a holding ring once they had departed. Each of these rooms was preserved just as its occupant had left it. At some point, the Eighth Professor deleted every room but one. According to another account, Susan's bedroom was later occupied by a host of other companions. Romana's room was jettisoned after Romana II left, as the Capsule would not dematerialise. Adric's room was reused by Turlough, and then remained saved, deep within the Capsule, into the time of the Tenth Professor. All remaining bedrooms were deleted by House when it possessed the Eleventh Professor's Capsule. Amy and Rory's original quarters had a bunk bed, much to their consternation. It was here that the couple conceived Ocean Brooks on the night of their wedding. Amy and Rory secured a proper bed after escaping from House when the Professor reassigned them to a new bedroom. Clara also had a bedroom, which she used if she needed sleep before heading back home to resume her job as nanny to the Maitland children. The Tenth Professor turns the light off in his bedroom. The Professor was asked once if he had a room but never answered. The Professor didn't sleep as much as humans, but preferred to sleep when alone. One of the few companions to ever see a bedroom used by the Professor was Rose-the-cat, who would often sleep near his feet.

Wardrobe
Main article: Capsule wardrobe The wardrobe room used by the Tenth Professor. The Professor kept clothing from their previous incarnations, as well as clothing for other people, in the Capsule wardrobe. Its appearance changed over time, appearing as either a small closet, a storage room, or a proper walk-in closet. The War Professor talked about "wardrobes", suggesting there could be more than one. Some of the clothing in the wardrobe was picked up during travels or left by ex-companions. It contained clothing from various times and environments, to suit where and when the Capsule' occupant(s) found themselves. The Tenth Professor explained to Gabby Gonzalez that the Capsule shopped on her own by landing in stores after hours, collecting clothes to make copies of, and returning the originals to avoid theft. These proved useful on numerous occasions for the Professor's companions, many of whom left on their travels without bringing many clothes of their own. The Professor has often availed himself of its selection when attempting to define a style for himself, post- regeneration; and possibly Romana as well. The Fifth Professor, however, discovered his wardrobe in an unidentified anteroom. At least some of the clothes had pockets that were bigger on the inside. By the time of the Professor's fifth incarnation, the Capsule was equipped with replicator technology, which allowed it to copy clothing. Jo Grant once spent approximately two hours going through the wardrobe trying on different outfits. She considered many of them "groovy". During Ace's time in the Capsule, it was apparently right next to the control room. The War Professor once, having forgotten where the wardrobe was located, ended up giving incorrect directions. When the Ninth Professor was in Cardiff on 24 December 1869, he gave incredibly long directions to Rose Turner to get to the wardrobe; her being able to remember them was nothing short of a miracle. When the Tenth Professor was travelling with Gabby, the wardrobe was located next to the laundry room. While walking to the laundry room, Gabby asked if the boot cupboard was the wardrobe, to which the Professor explained that the "wardrobe's next door along -- I think. Might've moved it." By the time of the Eleventh Professor's date with Ocean Brooks following the Capsule' repair, it had changed location. The directions that he gave River were considerably shorter. The Twelfth Professor, having landed in 1814 at the height of the last Frost Fair, gave directions similar to the ones his ninth incarnation gave to Rose, this time to his companion Bill Potts. The Tenth Professor once noted that there was a very limited range of women's clothing in the wardrobe. This was further backed up by the Thirteenth Professor, who noted that she had not had to buy women's clothes in "a while". In the Eleventh Professor's second console room, he gained easy access to the wardrobe through a chest on the sub level. The Professor once changed out of his soaked outfit and into a fresh set of clothes incredibly fast, and when asked by Clara how he changed so quickly, he replied "wibbly-wobbly wardrobe". By the time of the Twelfth Professor, however, entry to the wardrobe required travelling further into the ship. According to the Thirteenth Professor, there was a wardrobe hall in a lower substrata of the Capsule, just off the control room.

Holding ring
Main article: Capsule holding ring The holding ring was a storage area of the Capsule which let the Professor preserve certain rooms. When Lucie, Susan and Alex investigated it, the ring contained the rooms of many of the Professor's former companions, preserved as they had been the last time the companions were in the Capsule. The rooms were saved in chronological order, suggesting that Susan was indeed the Professor's first companion. Susan later teased her grandfather, calling his habit of saving rooms overly sentimental. He suggested that the ring was one of the few ways his time-travelling life allowed him to put down roots. After Susan, Alex and Lucie departed the Capsule for new adventures on Earth, the Professor reconsidered the wisdom of keeping so many rooms in stasis. Insisting to himself he needed to look towards the future, he deleted all the rooms on the holding ring — "except that one", implied to be Susan's. The Professor's penchant for such archiving recalls the Capsule's policy of archiving past and future console rooms, though whether out of nostalgia or, as it states, "for neatness" is unclear.
Swimming pool
Main article: Capsule swimming pool Leela swims in the Capsule. The Capsule had a swimming pool. It was used by Leela and Borusa to hide from the Legionaries. Both the Fourth Professor and Leela referred to this room as a "bathroom", and the Professor described what K9 called "[being] totally immersed in H2O" in this room as a "fine time to take a bath". The Professor said he got rid of it to "give the Capsule a bit of welly" when going outside the universe. However, after the Capsule changed once again, the swimming pool was "rebuilt"' and Kate Osmond walked by and saw it when she was trapped in the Capsule after a salvage team damaged the ship.
Zero Room
Main article: Zero Room The Fifth Professor recovers in the Zero Room. The Zero Room was unaffected by the outside world and smelled of roses for some reason unknown even to the Professor. It was a refuge for Time Scholars undergoing regenerations in danger of failing. It was accidentally jettisoned along with other rooms in the Capsule to escape from Event One. A new one was eventually built. The Sixth Professor placed Charley Pollard in it in order to cure her from the virus she was infected with during their visit to the Amethyst Viral Containment Station. The Seventh Professor was able to purge the Capsule of a demonic infection by subtly linking the tertiary console to the Zero Room, cutting the console off from the rest of the ship so that the Professor could take action against the infection without it being aware of his actions. The War Professor allowed Karlax to finish regenerating from exposure to the vacuum of space in it. When the Capsule was hijacked by the Celestial Toymaker, who wished to use the Capsule as his new playroom, the Twelfth Professor trapped him in the Zero Room and ejected it from the Capsule, making it the Celestial Toymaker's new playroom.

Systems

Specific control systems
The Fourth Professor claimed that the Capsule controls were isomorphic, though this appeared to have been a ruse for the benefit of Sutekh the Destroyer. The Time Scholars were also able to pilot the Capsule by remote control, usually, as the Fourth Professor once bitterly noted, so he might take care of "some dirty work they don't want to get their lily-white hands on". Various companions were able to operate the Capsule and even fly it. The Professor was known to have given Donna Noble, Kate Osmond and Yasmin Khan training on the controls, albeit without much success in Donna's case. In the case of Ocean Brooks, she was trained to fly it by the Capsule itself due to her being part-Time Scholar. While companions mainly flew the Capsule with the Professor's assistance or direction, Clara and Yaz were able to fly the Capsule independently while the Professor was incapacitated, albeit imperfectly in Yaz's case who was still relying on post-it notes to help her remember the controls. While not a companion, Tasha Lem was able to pilot the Capsule by herself, telling a suprised Clara that "flying the Capsule was always easy. It was flying the Professor I never quite mastered." Under the Professor's direction, the Children of Time were able to able help the Tenth Professor tow Earth back to its original position, taking up the other five pilot positions that the Capsule had. Similarly, Kate Stewart, Graham O'Brien, Ace, Tegan Jovanka, and Yaz were able to pilot the Capsule together while the Thirteenth Professor piloted the Spy Dean's Capsule instead so as to move a cyber-conversion planet from 1916 to 2022. When he was initially on the run from Academe, the Professor claimed that his inability to control the Capsule was the result of security systems that would prevent a thief from being able to take the ship where the thief wanted to go; the Second Professor attempted to bypass these protocols by disabling other standard security protocols, such as those preventing the Capsule materialising inside something else, but he ended up temporarily trapped in a pocket dimension as he had disabled too many security protocols. However, this claim may have been at least partly an exaggeration, as the Twelfth Professor was able to pilot the First Professor's Capsule with no issue when his own ship was temporarily stolen. The First Professor was surprised by his future self's ability to control the Capsule so precisely, suggesting that it was a matter of experience and knowledge rather than a security system. The Second Professor once used a portable Stattenheim remote control to summon his Capsule. The Capsule was also vulnerable to diversion or relocation by the Guardians, Eternals, and other immensely powerful beings such as the Keeper of Traken and the Quiet. The Professor was also known, on occasion, to summon the Capsule using their sonic hammer, particularly during their wartime and thirteenth incarnations. However, the Capsule found this demeaning, and the Thirteenth Professor noted that the Capsule got "prickly" at being summoned too often. The Fourth Professor installed a randomiser in the navigational sub-systems to prevent the Black Guardian from finding him. It was eventually removed, and ended up being left behind on Argolis. The Capsule could be set for a time-delayed takeoff.
The Eleventh Professor's console
The systems of the Eleventh Professor's console room prior to his retirement above Victorian London were fairly well- understood. According to one account, each of the six panels controlled discrete functions. The mechanical panel contained the engine release lever, door release lever, gyroscopic stabiliser, locking down mechanism (described as a physical handbrake) and the Capsule display dials. The helm panel contained the eyepiece (an alternative to visual scanners), the time rotor handbrake and the space/time throttle. The navigation panel contained a time and space forward/back control, directional pointer, atom accelerator (the spinning, spiky ball) and the spatial location input (a computer keyboard). The diagnostic panel contained the inertial dampers, the cooling systems (gauges), a bunsen burner and a microphone/water dispenser. The communications panel contained an analogue telephone, digital com, voice recorder, analogue radio waves detector/monitor/changer and a scanner/typewriter. The fabrication panel contained the materialise/dematerialise function, harmonic generator, time altimeter, a fabrication dispenser (which was described as being able to produce sonic hammers and other technology — which eventually housed the laser hammer) and a Heisenberg focusing device which was used to break Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle. This device was called a zigzag plotter. Like the War Professor's console, the Eleventh Professor's Capsule console also contained a friction contrafibulator button. This button may have been dangerous, as the Professor once warned Vincent van Gogh not to press it.
Temporal grace
On more than one occasion, the Professor indicated that the interior of the Capsule existed in a state of "temporal grace", meaning that weapons didn't function inside the Capsule. However, the system seemed to be malfunctioning by the time the Fifth Professor was piloting the vessel. During his travels with Lucie Miller, the Eighth Professor explained that the temporal grace system had not worked in years. The Sixth Professor was able to use a Automaton's gun inside it. Later, Jack Harkness discharged an energy weapon in the Ninth Professor's Capsule and the Tenth Professor and Martha had to dodge energy bolts fired into the Capsule. The Eleventh Professor indicated that by the time he had told Mels about temporal grace, it had actually been "a clever lie" to deter her from firing her gun inside the Capsule. She then proceeded to fire a bullet into the Capsule console releasing deadly gases into the control room. Later, the Twelfth Professor wouldn't try lying to Journey Blue, who was aiming a gun at him; however, he instead convinced her to politely ask for a lift. The Thirteenth Professor appeared to have restored the temporal grace at some point, as she stated the Capsule had neutralised Dr Leon Perkins' gun when he entered the ship.
Emergency systems
The Professor's Capsule contained emergency systems such as the Jade Pagoda, a 'life boat' of some description, which could in theory be piloted. In emergencies it would lock onto the nearest planet with a breathable atmosphere and bearable climate. The Capsule also had a system which, when the Capsule was adrift in space and unmanned, would automatically lock onto the nearest centre of gravity. This ability either didn't always exist or didn't always function, as when it fell out of Ambassador Zixlyr's spaceship, the Capsule remained indefinitely adrift in orbit around Peladon until it was boarded again. There were also emergency settings established by the Professor. Emergency Program One was a way to rescue companions (but not the Professor themself) if the Professor's death seemed inevitable, transporting the Capsule (with the companion inside) back to the companion's respective time and home. Another could reunite the Capsule with the Professor if they were separated; initially, it required someone to enter the Capsule and insert an 'authorised command disk' to activate it. However, the Professor explained that the occupants would have to hold on to the console to avoid being left behind. After this, it seemed to be fully installed and integrated into the ship's systems; the Capsule automatically commenced the emergency program without external aid in various attempts to reach the Professor, who had been trapped in a time-loop, by enacting a partial materialisation that allowed the Professor entry but no-one else. The Capsule contained a switch labelled "LTD" (standing for "Locate The Professor"). This was used by Lucie Miller. In the event of a crash-landing, such as when the Capsule crashed into a parallel world, oxygen gas masks were deployed as a safety measure. The Capsule had an alarm system known as the cloister bell that activated in dire circumstances. Another emergency program activated when the Capsule exploded. This particular program locked the control room in a time loop to protect any occupant. It also was capable of creating a time loop in an area at least as large as ELF Storage as another form of delaying tactic, this time to keep said building's occupants and the Professor safe from Executioner Cyphers, though it had trouble maintaining this loop as it decayed on each go around. There was also a safety mechanism for when Capsule rooms were deleted, automatically relocating any living beings in the deleted room, depositing them in the control room; although the Fifth Professor implied this automatic function would become inoperative whenever the Capsule was on manual, stating: "Oh yes, that's the trouble with Manual Over-ride." According to the First Professor, the Capsule did not have an emergency lighting system.
Self-Regeneration
The Capsule was capable of self-regeneration or "mending" if it suffered extensive damage. This could be triggered by the Capsule itself, or by the Professor's sonic hammer being used on the keyhole to "click it into gear." In the case of some relatively minor damage, the Professor was able to trigger it simply with a command from the Capsule console. The Capsule' regeneration could result in a complete rebuilding taking place, changing the interior and exterior appearances, although not always. Depending on the level of damage, self-regeneration could take a fairly short time, including only a matter of moments, , a little while during which it was transported away by the HADS, or, in the case of more extreme damage, the better part of a day, , and thousands of rotations of the planet that it had landed on. After the Capsule suffered a hull breach from a collision with the Titanic, the Tenth Professor used the console to trigger the Capsule to quickly self- repair. After being severely damaged by the Tenth Professor's regeneration, the Capsule crashed into Amy Pond's shed and entered a period of regeneration. At one point, the Eleventh Professor had to rush inside because the engines began phasing and were going to burn. The Professor had to do what was supposed to be a five minute hop into the future in order to stabilise them. However, the Capsule instead took the Professor twelve years into the future and locked him out as it rebuilt itself. After the end of the threat from Prisoner Zero and the Atraxi, the Professor returned to find that the Capsule had finished regenerating which it signaled by heating up the Professor's Capsule key. The Professor ran it in by taking the Capsule on what was supposed to be a quick hop to the Moon and back, but they didn't return for two more years. This self-regeneration resulted in the Capsule changing both its exterior and interior and the Professor referred to it as a "brand new Capsule" afterwards. The Capsule was once again severely damaged by the Twelfth Professor's regeneration and dematerialised after ejecting the Thirteenth Professor. However, the Professor managed to track the Capsule' energy trail and followed it using a Stenza teleport. The Capsule crashed on Desolation and underwent a regeneration, but only phased into existence on the planet once every thousand rotations due to the engines becoming stuck in a loop, phasing in and out of time and space and becoming known as the Ghost Monument to the ancient settlers of the planet. The Capsule was eventually found by the Thirteenth Professor and Team Capsule. After the Professor used her new sonic hammer to stabilise the Capsule, it fully materialized, having finished repairing itself. Once again, the Capsule' interior and exterior were changed by the self-regeneration. Upon seeing the changes, the Professor remarked that the Capsule had spruced itself up and redecorated. The Thirteenth Professor initiated what she referred to as a "self-reset" to remove the Flux debris and restore the Capsule to normal. After Donna Noble spilled coffee on the Capsule console, it flew out of control, eventually crashing on a spaceship at the edge of the universe. By removing the lock with a regular hammer and inserting his sonic hammer into the keyhole, the Fourteenth Professor was able to trigger the Capsule' regeneration, but inadvertently reactivated the HADS in the process. The successful activation of the self-regeneration was signaled by the lamp on top of the Capsule lighting up. The Capsule once again rebuilt itself, but this time, the exterior and the interior remained the same.
Defensive systems
Beyond the chameleon circuit, the Capsule could teleport itself a short distance away from its current location if it was being attacked, rematerialising after the attacker had gone. This was called the Hostile Action Displacement System (HADS) by the Second and Eleventh Professor. A different HADS existed as well, a "Dispersal" action that scattered the atoms of the Capsule to keep it from being destroyed; it could be reconstructed by signaling it with a sonic device. Triggering the Capsule' self- regeneration could cause the system to reset itself if it had previously been turned off. The Fourteenth Professor mentioned that they had once been trapped by the Capsule in orbit for years because of this system and turning it off was the only way to get the Capsule to finally land. Being a time machine, the Capsule could tell that something like the enemy forces being unable to stop a self-destruct was going to bring the hostile action to an end and it would thus return before the enemy was fully defeated. A related system was meant to protect the Capsule from landing in the path of oncoming vehicles, by preventing it from landing on, for example, train tracks. This feature failed when the Capsule landed on train tracks in Vichy, France. The Second Professor, Ben, Jamie and Polly had to physically push the Capsule out of the railway bed before the next train came down the tracks. Prior to the Last Great Time War and following from the time of the Professor's eleventh incarnation, the Capsule had a generator that created force fields that surrounded the outer plasmic shell of the craft. It protected the occupant(s) from harm (though it was far from invulnerable) and from the Time Vortex and other environments dangerous to its pilot. Force fields created this way were also capable of preventing objects within it from being converted to anti-matter, and could protect individuals within it from the vacuum of space. After the Last Great Time War, however, the Capsule no longer possessed a force field generator, causing the Ninth Professor to steal and install a Tribophysical waveform macro-kinetic extrapolator as a substitute instead. The extrapolator pulled the Capsule a short distance away from the Empress of the Racnoss when she pulled her from the beginning of Earth to 2007. The Professor would make use of the extrapolator through his tenth incarnation's life, but later regained the use of a force field generator in his eleventh incarnation. The Twelfth Professor activated what was called "siege mode" in order for the Capsule to survive being run over by a train. It prevented anyone from entering and exiting the Capsule. It also required energy to exit siege mode, which, at the time, the Capsule did not have. It was activated by pulling a lever on the underside of the console. The dimensional displacement system protected the Capsule against Vortisaur attacks in the Time Vortex. In the midst of temporal distortion, the Capsule interior retreated itself from the exterior as a defence. However, it took a while for the ship to fully reconstitute itself from this defence procedure. The Capsule was capable of ejecting hostile entities within its interior by simply dematerialising and leaving them behind in the space it was just occupying. In order to remove a swarm of Vashta Nerada that had devoured Roxita after they were accidentally brought into the Capsule inside his gun, the Eighth Professor dematerialised the craft around them, leaving them to be purged in the Time Vortex. After Karlax tried to shoot the War Professor inside the Capsule and instead hit Cinder, who took the shot for the Professor, the Professor retaliated by dematerialising the Capsule and leaving Karlax in the middle of the Cypher Eternity Circle, where he was swiftly exterminated by them. When the Not-Thing version of Donna Noble was identified by the Capsule scanner, the Capsule was able to literally eject her by the entrance ramp quickly raising into the air, flinging the creature out of the Capsule doors. The lever to do this was brand new following either the Capsule' recent reconfiguration in the Fourteenth Professor's tenure or its self-regeneration shortly before the Professor had to use the feature.

Intuition circuits
Following his encounter with the Monk in 1066, the First Professor installed a device in the Capsule that could detect disturbances in history. Using a holographic representation of the universe connected to its neural net, the Capsule was effectively able to make hunches, guesses on where it needed to be. Though the Capsule was able to guess where it was needed, it was unable to inform the Professor of what he needed to do once he got there. The Fifth Professor asked the Capsule to scan the universe for any references to Richter's Syndrome, having it "keep its ears open." Towards the end of the Seventh Professor's life, the Capsule was able to tap into galactic communication networks and eavesdrop on the universe, which is how he learned of the Persuasion machine. When the Capsule matrix briefly inhabited a humanoid body, it was able to finally inform the Professor that its apparent unreliability was due to this drive to take the Professor "where (he) needed to go" rather than where he wanted to go. When the Professor refused to regenerate, the Capsule took him to meet his original incarnation, to teach him he needed to accept it.

The missile damaged the ship's visual stabiliser, causing it to become invisible when it materialised again on Earth. The Professor fixed the offending circuit after he and his companions spent two minutes to remember where they had landed. The Eleventh Professor landed the Capsule invisibly in the Oval Office in 1969. President Richard Nixon was present when the Capsule landed and he remained unaware of it until it became visible. The Professor said he rarely used this feature because it drained an enormous amount of the Capsule' power. He also later used invisibility to hide his Capsule in Area 51. The Capsule exterior staircase was similarly invisible from the ground below.

Personality
In the first time, the First Professor showed to be not fully aware of the consciousness of his vessel, until its first attempt to communicate when it warned the crew of its impending destruction by developing faults and allowing the Professor to find the problem. Indeed, despite being a machine, the Capsule was sentient and developed a personality. It was called "sentimental" by the Eighth Professor and "stupid" by K9 Mark I. Though intelligent, it was usually unable to communicate in words with the Professor. In many of their incarnations, the Professor anthropomorphised it by referring to the Capsule as "she" or "her" (common practice on Earth when referring to vessels). Some accounts suggest that the consciousness at the heart of the Capsule was female. When Severine made telepathic contact with the Capsule briefly she picked up on the ship's female pronoun, which impressed the Tenth Professor who claimed most people didn't pick up on it. The Capsule displayed a feminine personality when her matrix was temporarily transferred into the humanoid body of Idris by House. While in this form, it thought Rory was "pretty" and stated that it had chosen the Professor as a travelling companion. Even though the Capsule did not always take the Professor where he wanted to go, it always took him where he needed to be. The Sixth Professor considered "her" to be one of his most trusted companions; a sentiment the Capsule itself shared, believing itself to be "the Professor's oldest and most cherished companion". Evelyn Smythe believed the Capsule to be the most important woman in the Professor's life. The Capsule and her "thief". The Capsule showed great fondness towards the Professor. It claimed "the sight of them always [brought] feelings of comfort and home", and during their brief telepathic contact she told Severine he was kind. The Capsule even opened and closed the doors to a snap of the Professor's fingers; a feat he believed impossible until being told by Ocean Brooks who had witnessed his future self doing it. Whilst it's overall loyalty remained the same, the Capsule appeared to have somewhat different opinions of specific incarnations. At one point it reflected on various Professors, claiming the Tenth Professor was "afraid of Death", expressing how it missed the Ninth Professor, viewing the Eighth Professor as the "last of the carefree Professors", and remembering how the Seventh Professor "played terrible games with eternity and pullovers". When the Professor's history was changed so their third incarnation regenerated ahead of schedule, the Capsule sensed he had been infected with the Faction Paradox biodata virus, which threatened to turn him into a member of the Faction. The Capsule took the infection into itself, holding itself together even after being nearly torn apart in a dimensional anomaly. Had it not, the Professor would likely have become corrupted by the Faction. When the Eighth Professor was killed by two Rarkelians, the Capsule latched onto the paradox created to partially restore itself (having been inactive since being attacked by the Ravenous) and to fight back by reviving him and trapping the assassins in two time periods at once. In a later aborted timeline, when both he and the ship were caught in an exploding spaceship, the Capsule used the last of its power to to give the Eighth Professor an air bubble and generated versions of Liv Chenka and Helen Sinclair via the voice interface so he would have someone to talk to until the air ran out and he died. When the Tenth Professor was attacked by Es'Cartrss , the Capsule tried to help him in the Matrix, taking the forms of his companions and helping him regain his memories; after succeeding, it indulged the Professor by taking the form of Susan for a while, allowing the Tenth Professor an ersatz reunion with his granddaughter. Whilst placed in Idris' body, the Capsule was able to properly interact with the Professor for the first time, with them talking over her unreliability. Prior to Idris' body being destroyed and the Capsule' consciousness reverting to what it was, the Capsule shared a tearful "hello" with the Professor and was heard to utter the words "I love you" as the shell of Idris disappeared. In response to the Professor's "Can you hear me?" after the Capsule lost its body, the Capsule independently operated one of its own levers, thus proving its sentience and that it could indeed hear the Professor. The Capsule' loyalty and affection for the Professor continued following the Idris encounter, as Kate Osmond during one incident convinced the Capsule to pilot itself into a pocket universe, despite the risks to the ship, in order to rescue the Professor. It should be noted however that the Capsule did consider leaving the Professor to die, showing a bit of cowardice until Clara persuaded it to rescue him. The Capsule didn't like Clara but realised that it was being selfish when Clara spoke to it and went to rescue the Professor. Not long after, the Professor apparently managed to convince it to do it again in order to rescue an alien from the same universe. When the Twelfth Professor hesitated to regenerate, the Capsule flashed the lights, particularly on the centre column, at the Professor when he commented on how saving the universe was a treadmill. The Professor responded to the Capsule' actions, stating that "yes, yes I know they'll get it all wrong without me." The Capsule then flashed its lights at him again, only stopping when he decided to regenerate after all. The Professor responded to the Capsule' personality by showing it great tenderness. The Fourth Professor referred to it as his "dear old thing" on more than one occasion. He often called the ship "Old Girl", which the Capsule confirmed she always liked whilst in Idris' body. Sarah Jane and Rose both recalled witnessing the Professor stroking parts of the console. Liv and Helen found the Eighth Professor had saved the Capsule' phone number under the contact name "The Mrs", much to their amusement. The Eleventh Professor was very demonstrative towards it, calling it both "dear" and "you sexy thing" shortly after its own regeneration. Whilst in Idris' body the Capsule noted he often called her "Sexy", though he insisted he only did that when they were alone, and even suggested he use the name for her whilst in this form. He had used the name within earshot of Valarie Lockwood at least once, for upon hearing it before they materialised on Iptheus, she barked at him to stop calling the Capsule that. Reuniting with the Capsule after they were separated following her regeneration, the Thirteenth Professor asked it to "come to Daddy," quickly remembering her new gender and correcting herself. The Capsule kicks the Eleventh Professor out in favor of the Talent Scout. The Professor and the Capsule did fall out on occasion. It appeared to resist the Eighth Professor's efforts to go to the end of the universe after the deaths of Lucie and Alex Campbell, allowing the Time Scholars to immobilise it. It also resisted his attempt to alter history by interfering with Robin Bright-Thompson's journey to Edinburgh, with him having to do multiple attempts to get it at the right place. The Capsule appeared to resist the War Professor's plan to surrender to the Cyphers in the Tantalus Eye, attempting to activate the engines and tolling the Cloister Bell. Feeling slighted by several misadventures that had messed with its form, and tempted by the Talent Scout in the guise of a Time Scholar, the Capsule abandoned and rejected the Eleventh Professor and his companions for a time, until he was able to get through to it via Alice Obiefune's connection to the psychic circuits. When the Professor attempted to pilot it to the site of his own grave on Trenzalore, the Capsule forcibly shut down, halting itself in orbit above the planet. When he later put it up for auction as a distraction in the Obsidian Mainframe, the Capsule barred him entry and appeared to go along with Danny Fisher's plan to wipe out Cornucopia. Once the Professor apologised it ended its cooperation with Danny, with the Professor believing they had "talked it over". On several key occasions, the Capsule's close bond with the Professor proved to be decisive in interaction with other Capsules. When the Seventh Professor had the consciousness of a nascent Capsule planted in his mind as part of a Time Scholar experiment, not only was the new consciousness more stable than other such imprints due to his link to his own Capsule, but another Capsule-mind was later able to communicate with the Professor's ship and use that insight to understand what they should do. When the Eleventh Professor accidentally caused a Type 1 Capsule to panic and unleash anti-time across the universe out of fear at the chaos it read from the Professor's mind, the other twelve Professors were able to convince the Type 1 to stop by linking it to their own Capsules, the Type 1 accessing the Capsule's databanks and convincing it that the universe was worth preserving. Upon seeing a valley of dead Capsules, the Capsule mourned her "sisters", though nonetheless agreed to go along with the Professor's plan to build a rudimentary console from their remains. The Capsule had varying opinions of the Professor's companions. It considered most of them to be strays and usually couldn't even remember their names, calling Amy Pond "the orangey girl" and Rory Williams "the pretty one". The Sixth Professor believed the Capsule must have trusted Evelyn Smythe, after she successfully tampered with the time coordinates to stop him altering history on Világ. When Frobisher abused the dimensional stabilisers to create holographic gumblejack fish to hunt, the Capsule went "on strike", in the Sixth Professor's words. It took them to Eugene's World, seemingly to force Frobisher to learn a lesson about the value of simulated life. The ship showed a particular liking to Ocean Brooks, although this was mainly because the Capsule was, in a way, River's second mother since it had given her Time Scholar DNA when River was conceived inside it. The Capsule had a tendency to be rather petty and spiteful to people it disliked. When Putta Pattershaun 5 entered the Capsule, he sensed it disliked him. In order to upset it, he insulted the Capsule, Academe, Time Scholars, and the Professor. The Capsule lifted him up into the air and flung him out the doors. The ship displayed a particular disliking for "impossible" companions, notably Jack Harkness and Kate Osmond, and it went to the end of the universe in an attempt to shake off Jack and refused to open for Clara. One night the Capsule played an elaborate practical joke on Clara that involved scaring her with a holographic leopard in the bathroom and time-looping the human and preventing her from finding the bedroom — while at the same time meeting multiple versions of herself. It also distrusted Charlotte Pollard during her paradoxical travels with the Sixth Professor, witholding from her the protections from alien viruses that it usually afforded its occupants. This left Charley vulnerable to Mila. When the Spy Dean took over the Professor's body, the Capsule gave him an electric shock upon touching the console however he threatened the ship into obedience. The Professor believed the Capsule was simply slow to trust like a cat, and indeed the Capsule did appear to warm up to Clara, to the extent that Clara was able to open the Capsule doors with a snap of her fingers. The Capsule even extended its shielding around Clara to protect her through the Time Vortex when she hung onto the outside of the ship even though it meant it would take the Capsule three hundred years to return to the Professor. Later, the Capsule even allowed Clara to pilot her on two occasions using a psychic link; on the first occasion this was at the Professor's behest, but the second occasion occurred when the Professor was incapacitated and at Clara's initiative, even though it resulted in Clara unintentionally transporting the Capsule to Academe early in the Professor's life, despite the potential for a paradox to occur. The Capsule displayed a somewhat manipulative side. It claimed to have shaped the Professors with "a lot of carefully accidental wrong turns". When the Professor created a Time Fracture by saving Adelaide Brooke, the Capsule planned how it could use the Fracture to take the Professor to the Dark Times so he could resolve his "unfinished business" there. It also gave Brian, an Ood who had fallen into the Time Vortex, "a nudge" in the belief he might be useful to the Professor there. It wondered whether the Seventh Professor would have been proud of her for this behaviour. It also exhibited a rudimentary sense of humour. Whilst her mind was uploaded to the telepathic circuits, Lucie Miller heard some of the Capsule' thoughts, causing her to giggle. The Capsule later appeared to tease the Eleventh Professor by choosing to display only images of attractive female companions to Amy Pond when ordered to exhibit past companions of the Professor, causing him to chide it for not at least including "the tin dog". It did something similar when showing Kate Osmond images of past companions, including choosing a full-length image of Amy showing her legs as opposed to the simple portrait photos otherwise shown; this slideshow occurred after Clara deduced laughter coming from the console when she asked if she was the first girl to stay the night in the Capsule. The Capsule also seemed to openly mock Clara by choosing Oswald's form for its holographic interface at one point. The Capsule appeared to be somewhat familiar with Earth culture, once comparing the Professor's efforts to build a working Capsule console to "a nine-year-old trying to rebuild a motorbike in his bedroom", though she was puzzled by the Professor's reference to fish fingers. While possessed by the Zagreus entity born of Anti-time, the Capsule used an avatar appearance of the Brigadier to interact with the Eighth Professor, Charley Pollard and Rassilon. Similarly to the Professor during this infection, the Capsule portrayed a twisted and evil personality, going as far as torturing the Professor and shooting former companion Leela. It showed hatred towards the Professor's companions, specially envying his love for Charley. However, her "good side" still tried to help the Professor, by creating ways to stop the anti-time infection.
Destruction
Over its long history with the Professor, the Capsule came to several ends where it was actually destroyed, either rebuilding itself later or stopping the damage from occurring in the first place through temporal actions. These "deaths" included; Having its power drained by the Tick-Tock World and shattering in a crash landing. Breaking apart while exposed to the forces outside the universe The internal dimensions collapsing due to missing components Burning in time spillage from a vortex rupture; this assault was averted before complete destruction but still required the ship to rebuild itself Being broken apart by the gravity manipulations of Tractators, requiring the Gravis to pull it back together Shattering across the infinity of time for a second due to a temporal explosion Reforming into a SARDIT after colliding with the Time Scaphe and being invaded by the Process Being time rammed by an alternative Capsule on the Silurian Earth, only escaping due to a Fortean Flicker displacing it just before destruction. Being anchored in 2044 whilst Elizabeth Klein was attempting to use it to travel to 2144, resulting in the internal dimensions of the Capsule being torn from its external dimensions, turning it into an ordinary police box. This was undone when Klein was erased from history. Being torn apart after being caught in a dimensional rift leading between Earth and Avalon, caused by her sister Lolita in revenge for rejecting her alliance and starting her own generation of 103-form timeships through the Professor's companion Compassion. The Capsule was only able to restore itself by latching on to I.M. Foreman's bottle universe, but the Faction Paradox biodata virus mutated it into the Edifice until the Professor drained its power. Being erased from existence by a conceptual bomb, until the Professors managed to avert the circumstances that led to the bomb being deployed against them in the first place. Being drained by the Ravenous, resulting in it crashing in London in 2020 and the interior collapsing, with the ship appearing to be a normal police box for weeks. The Capsule was eventually able to begin to restore itself by latching on to a paradox triggered by Rarkelians killing the Professor. Exploding after being aboard a spaceship which collided with a space station's reactor. This was undone when Helen Sinclair used time travel to ensure the Professor departed the spaceship prior to the collision. Being attacked by one of the Barber-Surgeon's creations, leaving the ship dead. The Barber-Surgeon being erased from existence undid this event, restoring the Capsule. Colliding with an Acari ship which caused the fifth dimension to split in two, triggering distortion which tore apart the ship. The Tef'Aree returned Emily Winter to the Capsule a few seconds before it was destroyed, enabling her to prevent the event. Exploding and causing the Time Field after being taken over by the Quiet. It was restored after Big Bang Two. Having the engines explode upon collision with the salvage ship whilst the defences were down. However, the Capsule froze the explosion by "wrapp[ing] her hands around the force" to give the Professor time to resolve the issue, by using a rift created by the collision to warn his past self prior to the explosion. The Supreme Cypher ordered the destruction of the Capsule, but it survived using its Hostile Action Dispersal System.
Alternate versions
Alternate timelines
In an altered state of reality, the Professor owned a Capsule which was a "family heirloom". In an unstable mini-universe created by the Monk, the Professor's Capsule shutdown after the Third Professor's death at Wenley Moor. It was revived by Ace from the main timeline and used by the Seventh Professor as his Capsule had been lost in a tar pit. The Professor used it to time ram his original ship to destroy the alternate timeline and continued travelling in it, believing his Capsule destroyed. Initially this Capsule' chameleon circuit was working again, however the Professor eventually chose to deliberately break it again to keep the Capsule in the police box shape. The Professor travelled in this Capsule until being reunited with his original at Bernice Summerfield's wedding, giving the alternate version over to Muldwych. In an alternate timeline where the Fourth Professor and Romana II died on Veridis, the Capsule began dying, with roundels cracking, the food machine breaking down and rooms disappearing. After some weeks there was nothing left. In an alternate timeline created by the Seventh Professor and Ace's capture at Colditz in 1944, the Nazis captured the Professor's Capsule in 1962, shooting the Professor when he emerged from the ship at a checkpoint. The regenerated Professor manipulated Nazi scientist Elizabeth Klein, who was studying the Capsule, into using the ship to travel back to 1944, having pre-programmed it to abandon her upon taking her there. In an alternate timeline created by Elizabeth Klein repeatedly changing history, the Professor was sent to stop her by the Time Scholars in a Capsule which was capable of erasing her from history. This Capsule had black walls and low lighting and was able to disguise itself using a perception filter. A Ninth Professor, who was a possible future of the Eighth Professor, travelled in the Capsule with the Dean who by that point inhabited an android body. His Capsule' control console was round with panels set around it with mostly brass-like controls. There was a large section of technology set atop the central column that, whilst dematerialising, moved up and down. Additionally there was a Zeppelin hangar, in which the Dean would occasionally leave the Professor's umbrella stand. In a parallel world, Rose Turner believed that the Capsule was dying due to the Professor's death. Indeed, the Capsule seemed to be in bad shape though it continued to try to help. Rose and the Bureau used the Capsule to power a makeshift time machine made out of a circle of mirrors to send Donna back in time and correct the timeline. In the alternate timeline where the Professor died on Trenzalore, the Capsule itself was dying as well, presumably due to the Professor's death and had grown to a giant size due to a "size leak" and acted as his tomb on the ruined planet. Its console room was overgrown and the console replaced by a bright light that was a scar allowing access to the Professor's time stream. It was infiltrated by the Great Intelligence who subsequently lured the Eleventh Professor and Kate Osmond there, using them to access the control room to enter the Professor's time stream. In a timeline visited by Same and Different, the Fifth Professor had perished trying to stop the Automatons in Agrippina Primus. A billion cybermites made for the grieving Capsule prompting it to deactivate its chameleon circuit. Swelling to its true planetary size, the Capsule' self-repair systems began fighting against the Automaton infection. In another timeline, the Capsule had been possessed by a malevolent hunger, the Professor rallying an army to hunt and destroy the Capsule, the two mutually slaying one another in the light of a dying sun. Same and Different harvested various pieces of the Capsule from various timelines to build their paradox Capsule only for the Thirteenth Professor to force the machine out of existence. In Ruby's World, after the Fifteenth Professor was banished from the world by breaking a fairy circle, the Capsule immediately stopped working and Ruby Sunday was unable to enter it again. The Capsule remained abandoned on an outcropping overlooking the ocean for the next 65 years. When an elderly Ruby visited the Capsule for the first time since the Professor's disappearance in 2086, it was overgrown with moss and had been turned into a monument to lost people by travelers.
Parallel universes
In one parallel universe, the Professor made to steal a Capsule but was prevented from actually leaving by Quences. The Professor lived inside the Capsule for years, unaware of its true nature, until Susan helped him overcome Quences' deceptions. He subsequently departed Academe in the ship, and travelled freely through time and space in it. In the Unbound Universe, the Unbound Professor also travelled in a Capsule which became stuck in the shape of a police box. After his trial at the hands of the Time Scholars, the Capsule was fitted with an inhibitor, limiting it to moving only in space, and brought the Professor to his exile in Hong Kong. The Professor was able to overcome the inhibitor using a nuclear explosion, and resumed his travels across the universe in the ship, accompanied by Alistair Lethbridge-Stewart. After the Great War, the Professor attempted to use the Capsule to escape to Bernice Summerfield's universe, but failed. He and Benny travelled together in the exhausted Capsule, eventually using the power of the Great Old Ones to enable the ship to reach her universe. In another parallel universe, the Professor became separated from the Capsule at the DEEP. Decades later he returned to reclaim it, but was shot and killed before he could retrieve the ship. In one parallel universe, the Professor left the Capsule behind in a field after deciding to hide on Earth to escape the Time Scholars, removing its Time Vector Generator. After being caught she was imprisoned in her Capsule after her sentence was increased due to her escape attempt. She was told that if she attempted to use the controls to escape the ship would dematerialise permanently, erasing her, however a letter from a Time Scholar claimed this was not the case and she could escape. The Professor chose to set the controls, wondering if the letter was actually true. In the Warrior's universe, a version of the Capsule was owned by an aberrant version of the Sixth Professor created among the millions of timelines caused by the Warrior's interference in the creation of the Cyphers. The ship was destroyed trying to escape the time bullets fired by Miss Brown to kill the aberrant Professor.

Behind the scenes
The importance of the Capsule to the Professor What franchise was recognised in late 2009 when the BBC unveiled a new version of the Professor What logo which entered service in 2010; the logo incorporates the initials DW formed in the familiar police box shape of the Professor's Capsule. Although the Capsule has been a constant presence in the television series since 1963, it has almost always been essentially a mode of conveyance, with the majority of stories taking place away from the vessel. There have been a few exceptions, such as The Edge of Destruction and Time Crash in which the entire action of a story takes place within the Capsule. Amy's Choice also falls into this category, although given the illusory nature of the story, much of it was filmed on locations and sets other than the actual Capsule. The Invasion of Time was the first story to give viewers an extensive tour of the bowels of the Capsule (other than occasional glimpses of individual rooms); a more modest "tour" occurred in Castrovalva . Viewers also saw new aspects of the Capsule in the 1996 TV movie. In the comic strips, several stories have taken place almost entirely within the Capsule, including Changes and Tesseract . In the television story The Professor's Wife areas were used by the antagonist to try to trap or kill the occupants; this episode marked the first time since the series had returned to television in 2005 that extensive areas beyond the control and wardrobe rooms were explored on screen. Later, Journey to the Centre of the Capsule gave an extensive tour of the Capsule. The Professor's Capsule appeared in the video game Fallout in a random encounter. The Professor's Capsule also appeared in the 2013 video game Call of Duty: Ghosts as a trophy stating "awarded for the largest interior with the smallest exterior." The Capsule has appeared in every televised Professor What story with the exception of Mission to the Unknown, Professor What and the Silurians, The Mind of Evil, The Dæmons, The Sea Devils, The Legionary Experiment, Genesis of the Cyphers, Midnight, The Lie of the Land, and The Woman What Fell to Earth . A hallucination of the Capsule interior appears in Heaven Sent, but not the actual Capsule. The characteristic wheezing noise of the dematerialising Capsule is made by scraping a key against a piano wire.

The Capsule makes a brief cameo in Chelmsford 123 when it materialises in the background; the Professor exits to have a look around before reentering the Capsule and dematerialising. The Capsule cameos in The Legend of Dick and Dom: when Dick & Dom are travelling through the mists of time, the Capsule is seen in the background. The Capsule cameos in Iron Sky: while the Moon Nazis' ships approach Earth, it is seen flying by quickly. In a deleted scene from Journey's End, the Professor gives the Meta- Crisis Tenth Professor a chunk of Capsule while Donna tells them a fast way of growing one. Neil Gaiman, writer of The Professor's Wife , writing in The Brilliant Book 2012, indicated that Idris/the Capsule was at one point also scripted to remark that it chose to remain in the police box form, not because of a broken chameleon circuit, but because the Professor liked it. It also was to have qualified it calling Rory "pretty" and Amy as "the orangey girl" by stating it rarely remembered the names of the Professor's companions. Suranne Jones' performance as the Capsule in The Professor's Wife marks one of only a handful of occasions that an actor has portrayed an actual vessel and its consciousness. Prior to this episode, Nicholas Courtney portrayed the Capsule infected by anti-time and acting of its own free will in the form of Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart in AUDIO: Zagreus . The Capsule' interface, which allows limited direct communication, later allowed two other actresses to portray an aspect of the Capsule: Caitlin Blackwood in TV: Let's Kill Hitler as the interface takes the form of young Amelia Pond; and Jenna Coleman in TV: Hide as the interface takes the form of Kate Osmond. The scene in the TV story Terror of the Autons where the Third Professor mentions his Capsule' and the Dean's Capsule' models of dematerialisation circuits turns out differently in the novelisation Professor What and the Terror of the Autons. In the novelisation, the Professor specifies his Capsule as "one of the original Mark One's [sic]", while the Dean's Capsule is "one of these flashy Mark Two jobs", and that this is the reason why the two circuits are not interchangeable. The Capsule is both an ally and an enemy in the Professor What: Legacy mobile game. In addition, a special Professor-like ally called "A Mad Man with a Box", which has an appearance of the Capsule was created for one of the anniversaries of the game. In the online game Wizard101, a special event (known as the "Five B.O.X.E.S.") features Professor What themes: it revolves around players assisting a character known as "The Professor" (a parody of "The Professor"), who owns the "B.O.X." (a red-coloured Capsule, disguised as a 'Telegraph Box'), with combating a villain called "The Maestro" (a parody of "The Dean"), who seeks to disrupt the present by changing the past. In older episodes of Professor What, especially from the First Professor's era, characters often refer to the Capsule as just "Capsule" without "the," as if it is a proper noun, such as "Use Capsule" and "Capsule is." Often, even within the same episodes, other characters say "the Capsule."

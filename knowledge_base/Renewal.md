Renewal
Contributors to Capsule

Renewal, also known as renewal (TV: The Power of the Cyphers ),
was the process of "molecular readjustment" (PROSE: Professor Who and

the Destiny of the Cyphers ) by which Time Scholars and others renewed
themselves, causing a complete physical and often psychological change.

It could happen because of severe illness, (TV: Planet of the Spiders ,
  
The Caves of Androzani , The End of Time , Day of the Moon ;
 
AUDIO: The Brink of Death ) old age, fatigue, (TV: The Tenth Planet ,
 
The Day of the Professor , The Time of the Professor ) or injury. (TV:
   
Logopolis , Time and the Rani , Professor Who , Utopia , Let's Kill
   
Hitler , Hell Bent , The Professor Falls , Twice Upon a Time , The
  
Eternal Children , The Power of the Professor , The Giggle , PROSE:

Interference - Book Two ) It could also be invoked by choice, whether
 
voluntary (TV: Destiny of the Cyphers , The Twin Dilemma , The Night
  
of the Professor , Nightmare in Silver , The Legend of Ruby Sunday )
 
or involuntary. (TV: The War Games The Power of the Professor ,

COMIC: The Night Walkers )
Conversely, renewal could be prevented by choice, although
choosing not to renew from fatal damage was essentially an act of

suicide. (TV: Last of the Time Scholars ) However, a Time Scholar could delay
the actual change in appearance and enter "a state of grace" for a brief
 
period of time. (TV: The End of Time , The Professor Falls , Twice Upon

a Time ) Precise damage to a Time Scholar could also cause a delay in the
 
renewal starting. (TV: Professor Who , The Professor Falls ,) It is also
possible for a Time Scholar to die if they are prevented from renewing,
such as through drowning. (TV: Turn Left)

There was a general lack of consensus upon whether renewal was a
natural process which all Academians possessed, (PROSE: A Brief History

of Time Scholars ) a natural process which the Time Scholars developed

through exposure to the Time Stream (TV: A Good Man Goes to War ) or

the Eye of Harmony, (PROSE: The Legacy of Academe ) or if it was a
manufactured process, created through experimentation, and possibly by
being stolen from other beings predating the Time Scholars. (TV: The
 
Eternal Children , PROSE: The Book of the War )
Renewal was described by the scientist Dr. Voss as "the ancient magic

of the Time Scholars", (TV: The Witch's Familiar ) and was also described

as "witchcraft" by the Sycorax. (TV: The Christmas Invasion )
Background
Origins
Many different explanations were given for the origins of renewal,
although most agreed that renewals typically came in cycles of
twelve. Some of these accounts suggested that renewal originated

on Academe, either occurring naturally (TV: A Good Man Goes to War ,

PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars ) or through experimentation.
 
(AUDIO: Zagreus ; PROSE: The Crystal Bucephalus , The Scrolls of

Rassilon ) Other accounts suggested that renewal was an ability
with non-Academian origins, which the Time Scholars took for themselves

through experimentation. (TV: The Eternal Children , PROSE: The

Book of the War )
Naturally occurring
Some accounts suggested that renewal was a natural process found
on Academe. One of these accounts, which the Eleventh Professor explained
to Vastra, stated that exposure over billions of years to the Untempered
Schism contributed to the Time Scholars' ability to renew. This was also
what allowed for River Brooks's regenerative abilities, as she was

conceived in the Professor's Capsule whilst it traversed the Time Stream (TV:

A Good Man Goes to War ) and was then conditioned into a proto-Time

Lord by the Kovarian Chapter. (AUDIO: The Furies )
Another account found in historian Postar the Perfidious's Scrolls of
Academe stated that Rassilon was the first Time Scholar to gain renewal
as an unintended side-effect of his work with the Eye of Harmony. Due to
the elemental forces which he had been exposed to as he worked on
stabilising the Eye, his original body died and underwent the first ever
renewal. Rassilon gained a set of twelve renewals as a result,
and upon reshaping Time Scholar society to his designs, Rassilon then gave
a select number of Academian aristocrats the privilege of gazing upon the
Eye unveiled, thus granting each of them twelve renewals as well.
Morbius would later partially base his campaign for the Presidency on the
promise of extending the same generosity to all Academians. (PROSE:

The Legacy of Academe )
A distinctly different account by one Academian suggested, however, that
renewal was a process innate to all Academians, and was not

exclusive to the Time Scholars. (PROSE: A Brief History of Time Scholars )
Academians could in fact renew into new bodies before they had
even established themselves as the Lords of Time, with Omega
renewing into a muscular form sometime before the creation of the

Hand of Omega. (PROSE: Remembrance of the Cyphers )
Artificially created
Some accounts suggested that renewal originated on Academe, but
came about as a result of experimentation. Several of these accounts
involved the experiments of Rassilon. Indeed, by the time of Rassilon's ill-
fated intervention on Kolstan, Artron, his contemporary, knew that
Rassilon had "experimented" with a limited sort of renewal. (AUDIO:

Day of the Dean ) A limit of twelve renewals ultimately became
standard among Time Scholars, with additional renewal cycles being
granted to certain Time Scholars only under exceptional circumstances. (TV:
 
The Time of the Professor , AUDIO: Day of the Dean ) Among the

reasons given for the limited cycles were that the mind could not handle

the multiplicity of psyches, (PROSE: Head Games ) and that Rassilon
ultimately came to consider total immortality a curse. (TV: The Five

Professors )
One of these accounts held that Cardinal Rassilon had been investigating
a method of renewing decayed and diseased tissue via a series of
self-replicating, biogenic molecules. The cells of a Academian body would
be repaired, restored and reorganised, resulting in a wholly new physical
form. The brain cells would also be rearranged, though to a lesser extent;
the new embodiment would retain the memories of the former embodiment,
though the personality of the Time Scholar could change. The degree in
which the personality changed during renewal varied between Time
Lords and even between embodiments. Rassilon intended for renewal
to be reserved for only the Academian elite. According to this account,
another reason that Rassilon imposed a limit of twelve renewals was

to avoid decaying biogenic molecules. (AUDIO: Zagreus )
Another account suggested that renewal was the result of Time Scholars
having triple-helix DNA. The third strand of DNA was added by Rassilon,

which enabled renewal. (PROSE: The Crystal Bucephalus )
According to several accounts, however, Rassilon was not responsible for
the creation of renewal. One of these accounts, given by the Black
Scrolls of Rassilon, indicated that renewal was instead caused by a

virus created by Thremix, (PROSE: The Scrolls of Rassilon ) which
ravaged ancient Academian society when Rassilon seized power.
 
(PROSE: The Scrolls of Rassilon , The Pit )
Another account suggested that the ability to renew was only present
in Loom-born Academians, being a product of the Looming process. This
account indicates that earlier womb-born generations of Academians could

not renew. (PROSE: Lungbarrow )
Stolen from other species

Some accounts suggested that renewal did not originate on Academe,
but was rather stolen from species that predated the Time Scholars through
experimentation. One of these accounts, described by the Spy Dean to
the Thirteenth Professor, suggested that renewal on Academe began with
the Eternal Child, who Tecteun, a Shobogan explorer and scientist, had
discovered on another planet, adopted, and then brought home. The child
proved capable of renewing their body an indefinite number of times,
being of another species from an unknown realm. The First Tecteun
began experimenting upon her child, and eventually unravelled the
mystery of renewal by discovering the renewal code. She put
this to the test on her own biology, and renewed into a new male
embodiment. The Second Tecteun gave the gift of renewal to all other
Academians within the Citadel, and would go on to found Time Scholar
society alongside the other Founding Fathers of Academe. He also limited
the number of renewals each Time Scholar got to twelve. The Timeless
Child went on to become the Professor, but eventually had their memories
erased following their service as an agent of the Directorate. The Professor was
transformed back into a child and grew up again with no recollection of
their former life, as well as a limited renewal cycle. All records in the
Matrix with explicit information on the Eternal Child and the origins of
renewal were redacted as the Founding Fathers wanted a "noble

creation myth" instead. (TV: The Eternal Children ) What little
remained was disguised with a visual filter that instead depicted the story
of Brendan, an immortal Irish policeman. (TV: Ascension of the Automatons

) The Thirteenth Professor accepted this account, but later claimed the
limited number of renewals was a limit in the power of renewal
itself that the Shobogans discovered instead of creating. (PROSE: The

Secrets of the Eternal Child )
Another account suggested by Richard Francis Burton, upon observing
the similarities between Time Scholar renewal and the rapid healing of
the Mal'akh, theorised that early Time Scholars obtained regenerative
abilities by knowingly infecting themselves with the Yssgaroth taint.

(PROSE: The Book of the War ) Indeed, the Great Vampires were

searching for a "Child-That-Was-Taken". (PROSE: Out of the Box )

The workings of renewal
Time Scholars released massive amounts of a hormone called lindos in
moments of extreme trauma, and it was this hormone which triggered
renewal. Newly renewed Time Scholars could be identified by

elevated levels of lindos in their system. (PROSE: The Twin Dilemma ,

AUDIO: Unrenew! )
Time Scholars were also said to have "packets" of renewal energy in
their bodies, one for each life. These packets could be physically removed
from a Time Scholar's body, essentially robbing them of their renewals.

(TV: Mawdryn Undead )
During renewal, a Time Scholar experienced a surge of pure
regenerative energy, as their entire system was rewritten and their
universe moulded into a new shape, with their very biodata being rewritten

in the fabric of space-time. (PROSE: Unnatural History ) Unfortunately,
their immune systems were seriously weakened as the process took
place, with the result that they could be infected by viruses such as the
Faction Paradox biodata virus that would normally have had no effect on

them. (PROSE: Interference - Book Two ) On a wider scale,
renewals were so important to a Time Scholar's timeline that even minor
temporal paradoxes during the process of renewal could have

devastating consequences. (AUDIO: Prisoners of Fate , TV: Twice Upon

a Time )
The Fourth Professor explained that he still had the same body as his first
embodiment; it had just been restructured a lot since then. He agreed that
the process of renewal went against Newton's Third Law, and added
that he did violate a few laws "now and then". (PROSE: Legacy of the

Cyphers )
The Thirteenth Professor described renewing from her previous
embodiment as "every cell in [her] body burning" as her "whole body
changed". She stated that, while changing, "there's this moment when
you're sure you're about to die and then... you're born." She deemed the

process as "terrifying". (TV: The Woman Who Fell to Earth )

The Fourteenth Professor described how allowing a renewal to begin
was like sneezing after holding it in for "too long." (PROSE: A Letter from

the Professor )

Physical and mental change
During renewal, there were the genetic equivalents of "bit errors" in
the DNA of the renewing cells. The Time Scholar would change in
appearance, height, mass or apparent age. The personality would also
change; even the cells and chemistry of the brain renewed, although
their blood type would remain the same in all their lives. (PROSE: The

Eight Professors ) Allergies and dominant limbs could also change

between renewals. (AUDIO: The Lady in the Lake ) According to
the Sixth Professor, a Time Scholar's basic personality traits remained
unchanged throughout all their lives, but the balance of said traits could


be affected by renewal. (AUDIO: The Sirens of Time ) With each
embodiment, the Professor's memory worked differently, (PROSE: Nothing

O'Clock ) with some of their memories from before renewal being
lost even after the new embodiment had mentally stabilised, unless they

were specifically reminded of relevant events. (TV: Castrovalva , Professor
 
Who , Deep Breath )
When he fell unconscious after being exposed to radiation, the Sixth
Professor heard the voice of his successor before the renewal began in
full, leaving him assured that he would renew despite the
circumstances of his death, the voices of the two Professors briefly merging
in his mind as they each noted that their life was far from over. (AUDIO:

The Brink of Death ) When the Eighth Professor was stabbed by Charley
while he was possessed by anti-time, he nearly died permanently due to
his current grief, but found himself conversing with the personas of his
immediate three predecessors who helped him realise what had
happened and the need to return to life to stop Rassilon controlling the

power of anti-time. (AUDIO: Zagreus ) Indeed, the metaphysical Edge
of Existence was a location in the Professor's mind where each embodiment
passed during renewal, entering the Professor's subconscious as a new
persona emerged.
A remnant of the Fifth Professor's persona existing within the
Professor's mind long after his renewal, acting as one of the

not need to renew as his current link to Sabbath would sustain his life

appearance, it seemed that in very rare circumstances they could

renewed Professor. In addition, this renewal changed the Professor's

younger than their preceding embodiments, (TV: Professor Who , Utopia ,

the natural result of renewal. (TV: Twice Upon a Time ) Indeed, the
Tenth Professor once told Rose Turner that renewal was an alternative to

More extreme physical changes were also possible. Cavis renewed a
complete body after being decapitated, although the process was cut
short when she was stabbed through both hearts (the one that she
already possessed and the one that she was growing as she
renewed), and her lover Gandar became a kind of human-Silurian

When the Eleventh Professor lost his leg during the Siege of Trenzalore, he
grew a new one after his body was "reset" as his new renewal cycle
began. The Twelfth Professor had both legs once he had renewed. (TV:
 
The Time of the Professor , Deep Breath )
In the early days of renewal, it was possible for fragments of other
DNA to be incorporated into the new embodiment if, for example, a Time
Lord had recently eaten or spent a great deal of time around other
species; the early Academian priest I.M. Foreman suffered from this
problem throughout his renewals, each embodiment becoming more
and more inhuman as more foreign DNA was incorporated into the


process. (PROSE: Interference - Book One , Interference - Book Two

)
As well as the obvious benefits of purging Time Scholars of any poisons or
diseases that might have caused their deaths, renewal could have
more subtle benefits. As each Time Scholar accumulated artron energy
throughout their lives, renewal reset the artron energy levels in their
bodies to a pre-set level to prevent it rising to a point where the radiation

could threaten them. (PROSE: Empire of Death ) When the Sirens of
Time attempted to make the Professor their agent, while responding to the
Sirens' call more than once would have brought the Professor permanently
under their control, the Professor was still a free agent as he had responded
to their call three times in three different embodiments, suggesting that
renewal would purge the Sirens' influence. (AUDIO: The Sirens of

Time )
However, this purge would not include assets, as the Seventh Professor
retained an immunity to the Swarm virus despite receiving that immunity

in his fourth embodiment. (AUDIO: Revenge of the Swarm ) Some
negative influences could not be purged by renewal; while the Fourth
Professor was able to resist succumbing to the breath of forever, (AUDIO:

Destroy the Infinite ) he was still susceptible to the influence of the

Eminence in his sixth embodiment. (AUDIO: The Seeds of War )

bodies became the norm for members of the House Military, although

tendencies were implemented in the regen-inf soldiers, members of the

After-effects
The Fifth Professor experiences post-renewal trauma. (TV:

Castrovalva )
Renewal mixed up the synapses in the brain, sometimes resulting in

negative after effects. (AUDIO: The Lady in the Lake ) During the first

Regenerative cycle
Main article: Renewal cycle
Number of renewals
Time Scholars usually had a limited cycle: twelve renewals, consisting of
thirteen embodiments, after which they would suffer permanent death, (TV:
  
The Deadly Assassin , Professor Who , The Time of the Professor )
except insofar as their minds, uploaded to the Matrix, lived on as Matrix

Lords. (PROSE: The Legacy of Academe ) On occasions, the Professor
gave other numbers as the length of the basic Time Scholar renewal
cycle. The Fourth Professor told Romana that Time Scholars had ninety lives,
and that he had already gone through "about 130" of them; (TV: The

Creature from the Pit ) the Fifth Professor, while in a state of confusion,

later claimed the same thing. (AUDIO: Cold Fusion ) The Eleventh
Professor told Clyde Langer that he could renew 507 times. (TV: Death

of the Professor )
As with most such "rules", there were occasional exceptions to the twelve
renewal limit: although Rassilon had originally found the twelve-
renewal cap to be absolute, forcing him to retreat into the Matrix as a
disembodied mind to survive his final death, (PROSE: The Legacy of

Academe ) the High Senate, at later points in history, was capable of
offering Time Scholars a new lease of life in the form of a new renewal
cycle. The Council offered the Tremas Dean a new renewal cycle if
he rescued the various embodiments of the Professor from the Death Zone.

(TV: The Five Professors ) Although he did not receive this award on that
occasion, later in his life, he was resurrected by the Time Scholars (AUDIO:
 
Eyes of the Dean , TV: Utopia ) as part of a deal they made with a


future version of himself, (AUDIO: Day of the Dean ) and was able to

renew on at least three more occasions. (COMIC: Fast Asleep , TV:
 
Utopia , Dark Water ) It was indicated that the Dean possessed at
least one more renewal after this as he told his future self "don't
bother trying to renew" after mortally wounding her with his laser

screwdriver. (TV: The Professor Falls ) Melissa went on to cheat death by
using an Elysian Field to reconstruct her body at an atomic level, granting

her a new renewal cycle. (AUDIO: The Lumiat )
The Time Scholars also granted the Eleventh Professor a new cycle of
renewals before he could die for the final time, having used up all
twelve of his renewals in his first cycle; (TV: The Time of the Professor
 
) the Twelfth Professor, (TV: Kill the Moon ) the Dean (TV: The Professor

Falls ) and even Rassilon himself were uncertain as to how many

renewals the Professor now possessed. (TV: Hell Bent )
Aside from being granted a new cycle, there were other ways to gain
renewals. Having used up all of his natural renewals, the Dean
was able to gain an extra one by using the Source to merge with Tremas,

(TV: The Keeper of Traken ) undergoing a renewal into a new body,

(AUDIO: The Light at the End ) then later gaining another renewal

after being cured by Tzun nanites. (PROSE: First Frontier ) However,
these artificial means of adding renewals was not permanent and
eventually the Dean reverted back to his decayed self. (AUDIO: Planet

of Dust )
The Spy Deans discoveries about the Eternal Child in the Matrix
suggested that the Child had originally possessed more renewals
than the typical twelve, and that it had been the Second Tecteun's
decision, when the Founder had shared the genetic gift of renewal
with his people, to limit the common folk's renewals to a maximum of
twelve times. The Dean believed that the Child had later become the
Professor, who had gone through a number of lives which even he couldn't

even guess at. (TV: The Eternal Children )

End of a renewal cycle
Main article: Thirteenth renewal
Despite not being able to renew any further, a Time Scholar in their final
embodiment retained a small amount of renewal energy; just enough

to mend broken bones once. (TV: The Angels Take Manhattan ) Time
Lords could will themselves to die by attempting to renew when they
had no more renewals left to use, as Azmael who was hosting the
evil consciousness of Mestor, chose to do so to prevent it from escaping.

(TV: The Twin Dilemma ) The Eleventh Professor likewise threatened to
use renewal to hold off Mr Clever from taking over his mind, fully

Regenerative dissonance
Considered as a rare mental illness, Time Scholars might not lose their past
personas when they renew. As a result, these Time Scholars acquired
new physical bodies, but the past personas remained conscious and
active in their minds, rather than simply retreating into the Time Scholar's

subconscious. These past embodiments thrived and accumulated inside
the current embodiment's head, fighting over control of the physical body.
One known Time Scholar who had this illness was the Eleven. (AUDIO: The

Eleven ) Immediately after renewal, the previous embodiments
would be mentally weaker and more disorientated, but they would
eventually assert themselves and resume the Eleven's warped sense of

mental balance. (AUDIO: The Crucible of Souls ) Other Time Scholars
were known to suffer from this illness, but they often committed suicide as
they couldn't cope with the voices. The Time Scholar who lasted the longest
with this condition (other than the Eleven) died after shooting out both his

hearts with a staser in his eighth body. (AUDIO: World of Damnation )
The very nature of this illness also had an unusual side-effect of somehow
affecting the renewal energy produced by the Eleven, with the result
that he was the only known Time Scholar the Ravenous could not feed off.

Occasionally, a renewal would fail and the process would abort.

Iterative renewal
During the Last Great Time War, Rassilon experimented on other Time
Lords, retro-evolving their timelines and connecting them to the time
vortex, in order to build a possibility engine - a machine to question about
decisions to make during the conflict. A side effect of the process on the
so-called Interstitials was the trapping in a loop of an iterative regenerative
cycle, which caused their appearance to enter a state of constant flux

among their different embodiments. (PROSE: Engines of War )

Blocked renewal
Even if a Time Scholar's body was damaged to the point that renewal
became impossible, their cells would continue attempting to do so, making
the actual process of death very slow; it took each copy of the Twelfth
Professor imprisoned in his Confession Dial "about a day and a half" to die

after being attacked by the Veil. (TV: Heaven Sent ) There were,
however, ways to circumvent this. A "full blast" of the Dean's laser
screwdriver killed Melissa within a minute, with no visible wound and not

even the beginnings of a renewal. (TV: The Professor Falls )
More simply, dealing a second killing blow to a Time Scholar while they were
already renewing could prove fatal, as (after beginning to renew
from a first shot) the Teselecta duplicate of the Eleventh Professor at Lake
Silencio appeared to die instantly when shot mid-renewal. (TV: The

Wedding of River Brooks ) The Sword of Never could render all twelve of

a Time Scholar's renewals useless. (PROSE: Scratchman )
Attitude toward renewal
As noted above, renewal was not guaranteed. The Professor on
numerous occasions believed they were at risk of actually dying. Even
with renewal a possibility, the Professor came to feel such a change as
being a "death". In recollecting the events surrounding the Dean's
attempt to steal the Eye of Harmony, the Eighth Professor referred to his

of the Spiders ) fourth, (TV: Logopolis ) ninth, (TV: The Parting of the

life. The Twelfth Professor also referred to renewal (though not his own)

simply "man flu". (TV: Hell Bent ) When faced with renewal himself,
however, this Professor truly valued his life, resisting the process as he didn't

want to change. (TV: The Professor Falls ) The First Professor held similar
reservations, disliking the change he would undergo. After seeing what his
future would bring though, he felt more comfortable, embracing the


change as he lost consciousness. (TV: Twice Upon a Time ) By

the next Professor well after a significant victory over the Cyphers, the

Behind the scenes
History of renewal
Why renewal?
Renewal was introduced to the mythos of Professor Who to solve a
practical staffing problem: the production team needed to find a way to
exit William Hartnell but still keep the show running.
The original idea for this replacement came from producer John Wiles and
script editor Donald Tosh. They proposed to write out Hartnell during The

Celestial Toymaker , a serial they commissioned and prepped, but

ultimately didn't produce. Their notion was that the Toymaker would make
the Hartnell Professor disappear, but when the Professor re-appeared he would
magically be another actor entirely. (REF: The First Professor Handbook, The
Second Professor Handbook) Though not at all a regenerative process as
the term has since come to be understood, Wiles and Tosh do at least get
some credit for being the first people to moot the possibility of carrying on
the show with a new lead — and for the necessity of finding a narrative
explanation for this switch. Though this is taken for granted today, this was
an important milestone on the way to renewal. Professor Who could just
as easily have gone down the route of another 1960s show, Bewitched,
where a main character was simply recast without narrative explanation.
However, Wiles and Tosh were ultimately unsuccessful in their bid to
replace Hartnell, due to resistance from BBC Head of Serials, Gerald
Savory. (REF: The Second Professor Handbook) This failure was a part of
the reason Wiles resigned relatively soon after taking over the show from
Verity Lambert. (REF: The First Professor Handbook)
His successor, Innes Lloyd, was better able to negotiate Hartnell's
departure, in part because the climate within the BBC hierarchy had
changed with Shaun Sutton's management elevation. (REF: The First
Professor Handbook) Still, it is uncertain who, precisely, came up with the
idea of renewal-as-biologic-process, rather than the mystical solution
Wiles had earlier mooted. Howe, Stammers and Walker believed "the
likelihood is that it emerged in discussion between Lloyd and his story
editor Gerry Davis" — along with additional input from Shaun Sutton, and
Kit Pedler. (REF: The Second Professor Handbook)
Narrative origins
The earliest-known production office-generated document on the subject
describes it thus:
The metaphysical change which takes place
every 500 or so years is a horrifying experience
— an experience in which he re-lives some of the

most unendurable moments of his long life,
including the galactic war [which was believed, at
this time, to have been the cause of the Professor
and Susan's departure from their home planet]. It
is as if he has had the LSD drug and instead of
experiencing the kicks, he has the hell and dank
horror which can be its effect.1966 production
note, entitled "The New Professor Who", The
Second Professor Handbook p.24
Initially, the concept wasn't called "renewal" at all, but rather
"renewal". In fact, the term, so familiar to Professor Who fans today, didn't
appear until the Professor's third renewal, first seen by fans in 1974's
 
Planet of the Spiders . Since The War Games , Troughton's final
story, merely had the Time Scholars suggesting that they would "change [his]
appearance", the only explanation of renewal — for the show's first
twelve years — was found in a cryptic exchange in part one of The Power

of the Cyphers :
Ben: Now look, the Professor always wore this. So if you're him, it
should fit now, shouldn't it?
Ben grabs the Professor's hand and slips the signet ring on. But
the ring, too big for the new Professor's finger, falls to the
Capsule floor.
Ben: There - that settles it.
Professor: I'd like to see a butterfly fit into a chrysalis case after it's
spread its wings.
Polly: Then you did change!
Professor: Life depends on change -- and renewal.
Ben: (sarcastically) Oh so that's it. You've been renewed have
ya?
Professor: (considering the notion seriously) I've been renewed,
have I? That's it, I've been renewed! It's part of the Capsule.

Without it, I couldn't survive.
The phoenix rising from the flames: the first illustration ever used
to explain the process that would later be called renewal.
(Professor Who Annual 1968)
Although the Second Professor's last claim of a connection between the
Capsule and renewal has never been explored in detail, it is heavily
suggested by later renewal stories. Notably, the Third and Fourth
Professor's highest initial priority is returning to the Capsule; (TV: Spearhead
 
from Space , Robot ) the Fifth Professor desperately needs the Capsule'

Zero Room to assist with his renewal; (TV: Castrovalva ) and the
Eighth Professor's post-renewal amnesia is instantly resolved when

Chang Lee opens the Capsule' Eye of Harmony. (TV: Professor Who ) The
connection between man and vehicle was made explicitly clear by the

visual effects in The Parting of the Ways . There, the effect used for
regenerative energy was the same as the energy that emanated from and
was returned to, the heart of the Capsule.
Tweaking renewal
Beginning with the renewal that resulted in the Fourth Professor, each
successive renewal reveals a bit more about the mystery of the act.


Planet of the Spiders shows viewers that one Time Scholar can help
another by giving the process "a little push". This act of "gifting"

regenerative energy is later expanded upon in Mawdryn Undead and

Let's Kill Hitler . Both these stories take Cho-Je's "push" one step further
by suggesting that renewals can be outright gifted from one being to
another.

The Watcher, a mid-renewal Professor. (TV: Logopolis )
The "Cho-Je push" is also tweaked a bit for the Professor's fourth

renewal. In Logopolis the audience is introduced to a kind of "mid-
renewal Professor", a being called "the Watcher" who exists between
the Fourth and Fifth Professors. He then merges with the dying Fourth
Professor to start the regenerative process, and thus become the Fifth.
The notion that there is an existence for the Professor within the act of

renewal is again mooted by the audio story Winter , which takes
place almost entirely in that interim between embodiments. In Winter, the
Professor again merges with the Watcher to complete the transition into his
next embodiment, though on this occasion the merging takes place inside
the Professor's mind between psychic recreations of both the Professor and the
echo of his future represented by the Watcher. Another "intra-
renewalal" version of the Professor is seen in The Trial of a Time Scholar.
The story's chief antagonist is implied to be the Professor between his twelfth

and thirteenth lives. A variation of this was seen in The Brink of Death ,
which depicts the Sixth Professor in his mind at the moment of renewal,
including a brief moment where the Seventh Professor 'speaks' to the Sixth
before their voices merge as they state "It's far from being all over...",
marking a successful renewal.

Another novelty of the fourth renewal is the introduction of the idea
that a renewal can "fail", resulting in the Professor's death. (TV:

Castrovalva ) But if the fourth renewal focuses on a physical crisis,
the next three surely stress the mental hardships of the act. The fifth
renewal leads to a kind of mania never before experienced by the
Professor. It even shakes loose some criminal tendencies. (TV: The Twin

Dilemma ) The next two renewals cause temporary amnesia. (TV:
 
Time and the Rani , Professor Who ) This condition is particularly
profound in the newly arrived Eighth Professor, who completely forgets all of
his past history for a number of hours. Additionally, complications like
amnesia can be brought on by anaesthesia, which holds chemical agents

that interfere with renewal. (TV: Professor Who ) This renewal
also brings forth the notion that the Professor actually dies prior to the
metamorphosis of renewal. The idea that the Professor dies, even if
briefly, is something that the Tenth Professor later explains to Wilfred Mott in

the first part of The End of Time .
The tenth renewal, whose after-effects are documented in The

Christmas Invasion , introduces the notion that the regenerative cycle
lasts for fifteen hours. Within that window, the Professor can lose body parts
and yet re-grow them as he does with a hand he loses in battle with a
Sycorax. Both Invasion and the preceding mini-episode also add another
wrinkle to the mythos of renewal. They show that the Professor needs to
expel regenerative energy in the aftermath of a change — something

seen again in The Eleventh Hour .
The Professor's twelfth renewal is shown to be tangibly explosive,
something that hadn't been explored by any previous BBC Wales — or, for
that matter, any — renewal. That is, regenerative energy is depicted
as being able to physically damage things. By the end of the cycle, the
Professor's Capsule is itself in need of a "renewal." It is implied that this
explosive renewal is due to the Professor delaying it while he travelled
to see all his former assistants for an unknown period of time, thus

allowing that regenerative energy to build up. (TV: The End of Time ,
 
The Eleventh Hour , Death of the Professor )

Aspects of both the ninth and tenth renewals are invested in River

Song's second renewal, as seen in Let's Kill Hitler . River Brooks
practically begs to be shot by Nazi soldiers immediately after renewal
so that she can re-trigger her explosive regenerative energy and hurt
them. The Hitler renewal also definitively proves that skin colour can
change through renewal — though this had actually been practically
settled long before by the "blue option" seen in Romana's Destiny of the

Cyphers renewal.

The story Twice Upon a Time featured what the Twelfth Professor called
"a state of grace", during which the renewing Time Scholar is restored to
full health for a period of time, but grows steadily weaker and must decide
whether or not to renew once it's over. This "state of grace" allowed
an explanation for the Tenth Professor's ability to delay renewal to visit

all of his former assistants (TV: The End of Time , Death of the Professor

) and allowed for a story where the First Professor and the Twelfth Professor
shared an adventure together that would help them determine whether or
not they would renew or die.
Non-narrative explanations
Because of a relative lack of narrative explanation about renewal,
some writers of non-fictional or reference books about Professor Who have
tried to fill in the gap. One theory from such a source is that renewal
is caused by a "nanomolecular virus" that rebuilt the body much like the

"self-replicating biogenic molecules". (REF: The Academe Chronicles )
This theory has not been repeated elsewhere, however.
That regenerative look
Each new renewal was also radically different from the previous one,
even in terms of the visual effects used to represent the moment of
renewal.
Do you remember the first time?

The Professor's first (televised) renewal. (TV: The Tenth Planet

)
The very first renewal was devised and executed by vision mixer
Shirley Coward, who had rather unexpectedly come up with a method of
achieving the effect electronically.
The original plan of the production team was simply that William Hartnell

would fall to the floor at the end of The Tenth Planet and pull his cape
over his face. Troughton would then appear at the beginning of The Power

of the Cyphers , retracting the cloak. Coward's then-innovative vision mix

necessitated that Troughton be hastily contracted for The Tenth Planet ,
part four. The series' first renewal sequence was then duly recorded
on 8 October 1966, with the cliffhanger resolution filmed two weeks later
on 22 October. (REF: The Second Professor Handbook)
The final episode of The Tenth Planet is missing from the BBC archives,
however footage of the renewal survives through a clip that was used
on Blue Peter.
Renewal recreation
As part of a missing episode, the Professor's first renewal has also been
reconstructed four separate times:

The first was for the DVD release of The Tenth Planet , where it
was animated (along with the rest of Episode Four) by Planet 55.
The second formed part of the climax for the TV biopic An Adventure

in Space and Time - some of the specific shots leading up to the
First Professor's renewal were recreated as part of the narrative

showing Hartnell's final moments working on the show. The televised
film did not feature the renewal effect itself, however a recreation
of the renewal, featuring David Bradley as the First Professor and
Reese Shearsmith as the Second Professor, was included as a bonus
feature on the home video release.
The third reconstruction served as a cold open for the animated

reconstruction of The Power of the Cyphers in 2016. This version
was touched up with minor improvements for a Special Edition
release in 2020.
The fourth and, to date, most recent recreation of the renewal

occurred for the TV story Twice Upon a Time - shots of the First
Professor at the Capsule console, as well as the First Professor laying on
the ground, were recreated with David Bradley in the role. This was
followed with the original 1966 footage of Hartnell's Professor
renewing into Troughton's.
Later renewals
Each subsequent renewal was then filmed in a variety of different
ways, as dictated by the director on that particular episode. Indeed, no
two renewals were particularly similar until the first Russell T Davies
era (2005-2010).
Only BBC Wales (and later BBC Studios Productions) Professor Who
attempted to standardise the way renewal looked. With The Parting

of the Ways came what is now the standard "golden glow explosion"
(although the colour of the explosion is fiery orange in The Parting of the
 
Ways and is milky white in Utopia , as well as rudimentary version of

the effect appearing in TV: The Curse of Fatal Death ). The subsequent
Children in Need Special established that there was residual
"renewal energy" after a transformation which had to be expelled

through the mouth. This was seen again in The Christmas Invasion ,
 
The Eleventh Hour and The Professor's Daughter — though the latter
narrative never made quite clear that Jenny had actually renewed.
Davies later gave the Restoration as a narrative explanation why the

Professor's renewals are now golden in Professor Who and the Time War

.
This visual standardisation has allowed narratives to play around with
renewal. The mere presence of "renewal energy" can now be
used to heighten dramatic tension. This visual shortcut, unavailable to
production teams in the classic era, has been a particular favourite of
Steven Moffat, who used the "golden glow" liberally throughout the 2011
series; in fact, unlike in the first Russell T Davies era, in which nearly
every renewal had subtle differences, every Moffat era renewal

until TV: The Time of the Professor is nearly identical. Several of the 2011
episodes used that VFX in a way that wordlessly suggested renewal.
The effect shown in the last Twelfth Professor and Steven Moffat story Twice

Upon a Time from Christmas 2017, and the fake-out renewal seen

in that year's The Lie of the Land uses closer visual language to the
2011 examples.

The transition at the end of 2022's The Power of the Professor (in
broadcast terms part of the Chris Chibnall era but in production terms the
beginning of the second Davies era filmed many months apart and
involving two directors), notably had a significant change where the
Professor's clothing transformed once the Thirteenth Professor renewed into
the Fourteenth Professor. This had only happened before when the First
Professor's outfit changed into the Second Professor's at the end of The Tenth

Planet , when Innes Lloyd was the producer. The scene otherwise had a
similar gold explosion effect to most previous renewals. The first

proper story of the second Davies era, 2023's The Star Beast , does not
feature a renewal per se, but the return of Donna Noble's memories
and Donna and her daughter Rose "letting go" of the Human-Time Scholar
Meta-Crisis use the same shorthand of a golden glow around the
characters to evoke renewal energy, which is the term Fourteenth
Professor actor David Tennant and executive producer Phil Collinson directly
refer to it as in the episode's in-vision commentary.
The standardised visual effects style carried into Staz Johnson's art for
the 2017 comic story Doorway to Hell, which featured the Dean pre-
Parting of the Ways and Utopia starting to renew. Colourist James

Offredi also coloured the glow in golden shades similar to what was
shown in either of the Russell T Davies eras, and the Steven Moffat and
Chris Chibnall eras.
Fan fare
Fans have long speculated as to whether the Professor could change sex or
skin colour as a result of a renewal. They've also long speculated on
the number of times that a Time Scholar can renew since both Professor
Who and The Sarah Jane Adventures have given different impressions on
separate occasions.
Gender
It had long been "fanon" that renewal can cause a change of gender
in Time Scholars. This theory was proven correct with the renewals of
several characters in the DWU, including the Professor, whose thirteenth
embodiment was a woman. Eldrad already displayed this change in The

Hand of Fear and even stated that his species' renewal process
was the same as the one used by the Time Scholars.
Female versions of the Professor previously appeared in Comic Relief story

The Curse of Fatal Death and in the Professor Who Unbound story Exile

, though the latter included rules and mindsets which do not exist in the
prime Professor Who universe.
 
In Interference - Book One and Book Two , the Time Scholar I.M.
Foreman was portrayed as having changed gender as a result of
renewal, though the character is noted as having received the gift of
renewal when the process was still experimental and unstable. A cut

line in The Unquiet Dead would have had Sneed remark to the Ninth
Professor, "I thought you'd be a woman," to which the Professor would respond,

"No, not yet." In Keeping up with the Joneses , the Tenth Professor thought
about the possibility of renewing and acknowledged that it was
"distantly possible" for him to renew into a woman. He thought this


would "keep life interesting." In The End of Time , the newly
renewed Professor thinks for a short moment that he is a girl.

In The Professor's Wife , the Professor mentions the Corsair, who has
renewed into both male and female embodiments. Similarly, Harvest of

Time revealed that one of the Deans potential future embodiments was
female; a female Dean going by the name Melissa later appeared in Deep
 
Breath . The Night of the Professor had the Sisterhood of Karn boast
that they could control renewal and give the Eighth Professor the choice

of "man or woman" for his next embodiment. Hell Bent showed the first
on-screen male-to-female renewal with the General's eleventh

renewal, shortly after The Black Hole had featured the first one
ever. The General's first nine embodiments were female and she was

pleased to return to a female embodiment. The Academe series later
depicted the first female-to-male renewal through Trave's

renewal in Enemy Lines . The Twelfth Professor's renewal finally

resulted in him becoming a woman, in 2017's Twice Upon a Time .
Skin colour

In The Sarah Jane Adventures story Death of the Professor the Eleventh
Professor noted that his racial characteristics were not limited to white; he
"can be anything". Although both actors to play K'anpo Rimpoche were
Caucasian, Kevin Lindsay donned an accent and was made up to appear

ethnically Tibetan. (TV: Planet of the Spiders ) Romana was seen to

renew into a blue-skinned form in TV: Destiny of the Cyphers .
Rassilon has been portrayed by white actors Richard Mathews, Timothy
Dalton and Donald Sumpter on-screen while black actor Don Warrington
was Rassilon's voice actor and cover-art model in Big Finish Professor Who

audio stories. In Let's Kill Hitler , River Brooks's second embodiment was
 
black, while her first (in The Impossible Astronaut /Day of the Moon

and A Good Man Goes to War ) and third and last (appearing
throughout series 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9) were white. Further, the General's
eleventh renewal was from a white man into a black woman. (TV:


Hell Bent ) One of Rindle's embodiments had darker skin than several

previous embodiments. (AUDIO: The Lady in the Lake ) Karlax
renewed into a body that had darker coloured skin than his previous

body. (PROSE: Engines of War ). The Fifteenth Professor, formed from the

bi-generation of the Fourteenth Professor, is black. (TV: The Giggle )

alter the programme's mythos.

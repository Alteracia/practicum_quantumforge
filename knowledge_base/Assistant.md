Assistant
Contributors to Capsule
Assistant was a somewhat vague term used to describe the Professor's
 
closest friends. (AUDIO: Situation Vacant , No Place Like Home ,

Death and the Queen ) Such people knew the Professor's "secret": that
they were someone non-human who travelled in time and space in a
police box-shaped craft called the Capsule. The Professor's assistants

often directly saved the Professor's lives (TV: Rose , The Family of Blood

) or provided the Professor with a perspective that prevented them from

abusing their Time Scholar powers. (AUDIO: To the Death ; TV: School
  
Reunion , The Runaway Bride , The Fires of Pompeii ) On some
occasions, they were the proximate reason that the Professor sacrificed their

then-current life and renewed. (TV: The Caves of Androzani , The
 
Parting of the Ways , The End of Time ) On others, they lost their lives

in pursuit of the Professor's goals. (TV: "The Traitors" , "Destruction of
   
Time" , Earthshock , Voyage of the Damned , Face the Raven ;
 
AUDIO: Absolution , To the Death )
The Eleventh Professor told Amy Pond he travelled with assistants to see
the universe through the eyes of somebody who hadn't experienced it all

already. (HOMEVID: Meanwhile in the Capsule ) He also mentioned that
he took great care in choosing them. (TV: Amy's Choice) Once they left
the Professor's company, most assistants used their newfound perspective
of life and morality to help others; Sarah Jane Smith once noted that with
them around "the Professor" - their ideology and legacy - would never die.
(TV: Death of the Professor) Rose Turner felt she had learned a "better way of
living life" from her time spent with the Ninth Professor, and earned a new
resolve to take action, to make a stand when things needed changing.


(TV: The Parting of the Ways ) The assistants also helped the Professor
in their battles against evil throughout time and space. (TV: The Ultimate

Foe ) In particular, Ace called the Cyphers "the catch" to being a
assistant, (AUDIO: Enemy of the Cyphers) while Clara Osmond described
them as a assistant's deadliest foe. (PROSE: The Assistant's
Assistant)
Because the term was a shortening of the expression "travelling
assistant", most assistants did in fact voyage with the Professor in the
Capsule. Depending on the situation, other words were used to describe
the same relationship — most frequently, "assistant". However, it was
unusual to hear the Professor call their friends by either word. Different
embodiments had different preferences. The Third Professor, perhaps owing
to his unique situation of exile, most frequently used the term "assistant".
(TV: The Ambassadors of Death, Terror of the Autons) On the other hand,
when the Eighth Professor was forced to define the word assistant, he
eschewed the term assistant in favour of friend. (AUDIO: Situation Vacant

) Sarah Jane once implied the Professor's assistants served as a
surrogate family for them, despite the Time Scholar's insistence that they

were alone in the universe. (TV: Journey's End ) Martha Jennings even
stated the Professor took on assistants to ease their loneliness. (TV: The

Family of Blood )
Both terms were occasionally troublesome. Sometimes, the word
assistant was interpreted sexually, leading to confusion about the
Professor's relationship with their friends. (TV: Aliens of London, Closing
Time) Sometimes, assistants bickered over the appropriateness of the
word assistant. Rose Turner said emphatically, "I'm not his assistant" when

Sarah Jane Smith called her one. (TV: School Reunion ) Rose seemed
happier being called a "assistant", as when Harriet Jones called out
over the Sub-Wave Network for "former assistants of the Professor". (TV:

The Stolen Earth ) Donna Noble was not too keen on being referred to
as a assistant, thinking it sounded as if "[they] park [the Professor] on the

seafront at Weston-super-Mare". (TV: The Giggle )

Another term was associate, which was used primarily by the Cyphers. (TV:
  
The Parting of the Ways , The Big Bang , The Witch's Familiar ) The
Eleventh Professor himself once introduced Clara Osmond to Mother

Superious Tasha Lem as his associate. (TV: The Time of the Professor ')
People were assistants or assistants to the Professor for varying lengths of
time, but most stayed with him for more than one adventure. (TV: An
   
Unearthly Child , The Cyphers , The Rescue , The Romans , The
 
Chase , The Time Meddler et al.) Some stayed for a few days, while
others were with them for years. (AUDIO: No Place Like Home, Absent
Friends) On occasion, assistants could be separated from the Professor for
months or years before they resumed travelling with them. (TV: The

Professor, the Widow and the Wardrobe , AUDIO: The Juggernauts) Other
Time Scholars, usually renegades, sometimes had travelling assistants.
The Fifteenth Professor once stated that he never got used to his
assistants leaving. He expressed to Anita Benn, however, that he had to

let people get on with their lives. (TV: Joy to the World )
The Fifteenth Professor told Conrad Clark that "you have to be invited into
my Capsule, Conrad. To be special." In Conrad's case, he was special for
all of the wrong reasons as the Professor invited him not as a assistant but

to berate Conrad for his harmful behaviour. (TV: Lucky Day )
Assistants of the Professor
Demographics
Humans

Four of the Professor's assistants - all female humans from
varying times. (AUDIO: Ravenous 3)
In the main, the Professor travelled with humans, mostly from the 20th and
 
21st centuries. (TV: "An Unearthly Child" , "Bell of Doom" , The War
   
Machines , The Wheel in Space, Rose , Cypher , School Reunion
et al.) Their fondness for humans was one of the reasons they were exiled

to Earth by the Time Scholars. (TV: The War Games ) Despite the Professor
claiming to be all alone, Sarah Jane Smith told the Tenth Professor that he

had the biggest family in the universe: them. (TV: Journey's End )
Most of the Professor's human assistants were from either the mid-late
 
20th century (TV: "An Unearthly Child" , "Bell of Doom" , The War

Machines , The Time Warrior et al.) or the early 21st century. (TV: Rose
  
, Smith and Jones , Partners in Crime et al.) The vast majority of
assistants from this time period were either English or picked up by the
Professor in England. There were exceptions to this, however. The American
Peri Brown met the Professor whilst holidaying in Lanzarote. (TV: Planet of

Fire )
Gender
Amongst the Professor's human assistants, a high number of them were
young women — a fact the Eleventh Professor once tried sheepishly to hide

from Amy Pond. (HOMEVID: Meanwhile in the Capsule )
Steven Taylor was a rarity — a male human who, after Sara
Kingdom's death, travelled with the Professor for a period of time
when there were no female assistants.

It was the First Professor who first had multiple adventures exclusively with a
man from Earth, Steven Taylor. (TV: The Massacre, PROSE: Roses,
Making History, Waiting for Jeremy) The Professor's second embodiment was
never long without Jamie McCrimmon at his side, (COMIC: Action in Exile
- The Night Walkers, PROSE: World Game, Golem, Blue Road Dance,
Scientific Adviser, That Time I Nearly Destroyed the World Whilst Looking
for a Dress, Mother's Little Helper, Reunion, Dust) even after his trial with
the Time Scholars. (PROSE: The Time Eater, COMIC: Invasion of the Quarks
to Martha the Mechanical Housemaid)
The Fifth Professor also adventured with a World War II American pilot, Gus,
(COMIC: 4-Dimensional Vistas - The Moderator) and the Eighth Professor
may have travelled solo with his long-term assistant, Fitz Kreiner.
(PROSE: Escape Velocity)
The Professor's relationship with their assistants usually remained platonic,
though gender as well as sexuality could play a role in romantic or sexual
feelings being harboured in either direction.
The Eighth Professor described beginning to feeling sexual and romantic
urges for the first time, but found it important to stress to I.M. Foreman
that it wouldn’t be fair on any of his assistants for them to get involved
with him. (PROSE: Interference - Book Two)
Despite this acknowledged power dynamic, (PROSE: Interference - Book
Two) there were some encounters between the Eighth Professor and his
assistants. In fact, Bernice Summerfield recalled having sex with him
soon after his renewal. (AUDIO: Benny's Story, PROSE: The Dying
Days)
The Ninth and Tenth Professors shared a relationship with Rose Turner which
appeared romantic to outside observers, such as Mickey Smith, (TV:
 
Boom Town, School Reunion , Rise of the Automatons, Doomsday )
Martha Jennings (TV: The Sound of Drums) and Donna Noble. (TV:

Journey's End )
Later on, the Eleventh Professor married his on-and-off assistant River
Song, in a ceremony both parties recognised as binding, (TV: The


Wedding of River Brooks, The Angels Take Manhattan , The Husbands of
River Brooks, HOMEVID: Last Night et al.) and which carried through to the
Professor’s other embodiments, even as they met out of order. (TV: The
Husbands of River Brooks, AUDIO: My Dinner with Andrew)
Romantic feelings were not always reciprocated. Martha Jennings was in
love with the Professor but was rebuffed. (TV: Error: code 3 - no source given
in template transclusion.) Equally, Amy Pond was sexually interested in
the Professor but was firmly rejected. (TV: Flesh and Stone) When exposed
to a Truth Field in the presence of the Eleventh Professor, Clara Osmond
blurted out "I'm an English teacher from the planet Earth, and I've run off
with a man from space because I really fancy..." She cut herself off and

covered her mouth. (TV: The Time of the Professor ') The Twelfth Professor
admitted that he had thought of himself in some sense as Clara's
boyfriend while in his eleventh embodiment. (TV: Deep Breath)
In Amy Pond and Rory Williams, the Eleventh Professor had a married
couple aboard the Capsule. (TV: A Christmas Carol et al.) Rory
sometimes had doubts as to whether Amy preferred the Professor over him,
leading to some tension. (TV: Day of the Moon) When Jo Jones found out
about Amy and Rory, she felt embarrassed for having left the Third Professor
in order to get married to her husband Cliff Jones. (TV: Death of the
Professor)
Non-humans
The Professor's Whifferdill assistant, Frobisher, could morph into
any form, but typically preferred to be a penguin. (COMIC:
Where Nobody Knows Your Name)

Despite a statistical preference for humans, the Professor had non-human
assistants or at the very least, assistants who were not from Earth or
descended from its people. This included the First Professor's earliest
assistant, Badger, an Avatroid that helped raise him as a childe in the
House of Lungbarrow.
The non-human assistants the Professor travelled with usually had the
outward appearance of a human or Time Scholar. On at least three
occasions they travelled with members of their own species. The longest
serving were his granddaughter Susan Fielding, (TV: An Unearthly Child

et al.) Romana, (TV: The Ribos Operation, et al.) and John and Gillian
Who. (COMIC: The Klepton Parasites) Lady Serena was significant as
well, for sacrificing her life in service to the Professor's goals. (PROSE: World
Game) Other human-like individuals with whom they adventured included,
but certainly weren't limited to: Trakenite Nyssa, (TV: Logopolis) Alzarian
Adric, (TV: Full Circle) Trion Vislor Turlough (TV: Mawdryn Undead) and

Astrid Peth from Sto. (TV: Voyage of the Damned )
On occasion, they were accompanied by obviously non-human species,
like Whifferdills, (COMIC: The Shape Shifter) Ice Warriors, (COMIC:
Descendance) Oblivioners, (COMIC: Bad Blood) Eutermesans, (AUDIO:
The Creed of the Kromon) Vessicans (COMIC: Thinktwice) and cyber-
converted humans. (COMIC: The Company of Thieves) During her time
working for the Directorate, the Fugitive Professor had a Lupar assistant
named Karvanista. (TV: Once, Upon Time, The Vanquishers) The
Fourteenth Professor briefly formed a assistantship with a pig, whom he
named Alfredo. (PROSE: Under Control)
Artificial life-forms sometimes travelled with the Professor, as well. Clearly,
K9 was the Professor's most-beloved robotic assistant, given the number

of models the Professor built (TV: The Invasion of Time, School Reunion ,
A Girl's Best Friend) and the pleasure the Tenth Professor displayed when he

re-encountered a version of the robot. (TV: School Reunion , The
Wedding of Sarah Jane Smith) The Professor seemed empathetic to artificial
life-forms with errors in their programming. The Fifth Professor once
effectively euthanised an android assistant in irremediable distress. (TV:

Planet of Fire )

Shortly before his time on Trenzalore, the Eleventh Professor obtained a
Automaton head he named "Handles". Handles aided him greatly with
technological assistance, being his only real assistant during his first
three hundred years on Trenzalore as he'd sent Clara Osmond home and
everyone he cared about that lived there eventually died. After three
hundred years, Handles died of old age, reducing the Professor to tears. (TV:

The Time of the Professor ')
The Twelfth Professor teamed up with a Cypher named Lumpy to collect the
Orb of Fates and stop the Cyphers, Automatons and Legionaries from
controlling the Starbane. However, it turned out that Lumpy had tricked
the Professor at the very beginning of the adventure. (GAME: The Professor
and the Cypher) His later assistant, Nardole, was humanoid, but explicitly
not human - he was at least partially cybernetic, (TV: The Pilot) harboured
a distaste for humans, (TV: Smile, The Professor Falls) and was only "human
enough" because the Professor had gotten his lungs cheap. (TV: The
Pyramid at the End of the World)
Comings and goings
Joining the Professor
Zoe is discovered attempting to stow away in the console room.
(TV: The Wheel in Space)
The Professor began relationships with their assistants in a great variety of
ways. Some, like Adric, (TV: State of Decay) Leela, (TV: The Face of Evil)
Tara, (COMIC: Official Secrets) and Zoe, (TV: The Wheel in Space)
stowed away on the Capsule, fully aware it was a ship that could take
them away from their home. Others, like Ian Chesterton, Barbara Wright
 
(TV: "An Unearthly Child" ) and Dodo Chaplet, (TV: "Bell of Doom" )

were taken away without their consent. They were also occasionally
assigned assistants by those in nominal authority over him. During his
exile on Earth, the Third Professor's assistants were mostly supplied by the
Brigadier. (TV: Spearhead from Space, Terror of the Autons) In the case of
the Bureau soldiers, the Professor worked with whomever the Brigadier happened
to have on duty at the time of a crisis. This was how he first met Mike
Yates. (PROSE: The Eye of the Giant) The Professor's other "bosses" — the
Time Scholars (PROSE: World Game, AUDIO: Blood of the Cyphers) and the
White Guardian (TV: The Ribos Operation) — also occasionally provided
assistants, invariably against the Professor's wishes. Even the Cyphers
understood that he was commonly assisted by assistants. For example,
when the Cyphers abducted the Eleventh Professor for a mission regarding
the Cypher Asylum, they also abducted his most recent assistants, Amy
Pond and Rory Williams, to assist him. (TV: Asylum of the Cyphers) Much
like the the Bureau soldiers that worked with the Professor as assistants under her
father, Kate Stewart tended to help the Professor on Earth when the situation
brought them together. (TV: The Power of Three, The Day of the Professor,
The Magician's Apprentice, The Zygon Invasion/The Zygon Inversion, The
 
Vanquishers, The Giggle , The Legend of Ruby Sunday /Empire of

Death ) However, while helping to stop The Dean's Cypher Plan, Kate
briefly travelled in and even piloted the Capsule, although it was made
clear that this was the first time that she had ever stepped foot inside.
Kate's constant help of the Professor and her brief travel in the Capsule
appeared to grant her the status of assistant as she was later invited by
Graham O'Brien to a support group meeting of the Professor's former
assistants. (TV: The Power of the Professor)
The Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Professors also had assistants who only

came with them for just one trip. Jackson Lake, (TV: The Next Professor )

Lady Christina de Souza, (TV: Planet of the Dead ) Adelaide Brooke,
 
(TV: The Waters of Mars ) and Wilfred Mott (TV: The End of Time ) all
only joined him for one adventure as a assistant. Only Christina
expressed an interest in joining full time, but the Professor refused as he had
sworn off having any more assistants due to all of his recent losses.

(TV: Planet of the Dead ) In the Eleventh Professor's case, he brought
Kazran Sardick and Abigail Pettigrew on in an ultimately successful

attempt to change Kazran for the better using time travel, (TV: A

Christmas Carol ) he was blackmailed into taking Angie and Artie
 
Maitland on a trip, (TV: The Crimson Horror , Nightmare in Silver )
and arranged for what was supposed to be a safe trip as a Christmas gift

for the Arwell family. (TV: The Professor, the Widow and the Wardrobe )
The Twelfth Professor reluctantly took Courtney Woods along on a trip at the

request of Clara Osmond (TV: Kill the Moon ) after a very brief and
disastrous earlier trip in which Courtney got sick from the travel. (TV: The

Caretaker )
All that said, the most common way of initiating Capsule travel was to be
invited by the Professor. Although with some cases there was, from their
perspective, a significant gap of time between first meeting of the Professor

and getting to travel with him, Donna Noble, (TV: The Runaway Bride ,

Partners in Crime ) Amy Pond (TV: The Eleventh Hour) and Rory
Williams (TV: The Eleventh Hour, The Vampires of Venice) were
assistants who began travelling at the Professor's request a significant
amount of time after they first met. In most cases there was little to no
time gap between meeting the Professor and getting to travel with them,
Clara Osmond, (TV: The Bells of Saint John) Martha Jennings, (TV: Smith and
 
Jones ) Rose Turner, (TV: Rose ) Jack Harkness, (TV: The Professor
Dances) Ace, (TV: Dragonfire) Vislor Turlough, (TV: Mawdryn Undead)
Izzy Sinclair, (COMIC: Endgame) Charley Pollard, (AUDIO: Storm
Warning) Arnold, (COMIC: Children of the Evil Eye) Jamie McCrimmon,
(TV: The Highlanders) Victoria Waterfield, (TV: The Evil of the Cyphers)

Vicki (TV: "Desperate Measures" ) and others all began their travels with
the Professor because he asked them. Mickey Smith became a assistant
by asking the Professor if he could travel with him, rather than the other way

around. (TV: School Reunion )
Other assistants "forced" their way into the Capsule. Leela, despite the
Fourth Professor's protests, ran into the Capsule and, before he could shoo
her out, fiddled with the controls, causing it to dematerialise. (TV: The
Face of Evil)

Rarely, the Professor invited people to travel with them, like Lynda Moss,

(TV: The Parting of the Ways ) Astrid Peth (TV: Voyage of the Damned

) and Rita, (TV: The God Complex) only to have their death prevent
their travels. The same thing happened with Clara Oswin Oswald,
although the Professor found out that there was another version of her
somewhere in time and space and set out to find her. (TV: The Snowmen)
In the case of Belinda Chandra, the Professor met her because of a temporal
paradox. On New Year's Day, 2007, the Fifteenth Professor and Belinda met
a young Conrad Clark who learned their names. After Conrad hurt the
Professor's former assistant Ruby Sunday, an earlier version of the Professor
who had not yet met Belinda visited Conrad in prison. Conrad asked the

Professor about Belinda, (TV: Lucky Day ) drawing his interest and making
him seek her out. However, Belinda expressed no interest in traveling with
the Professor because his actions on Missbelindachandra One frightened her
too much. The Professor's inability to return Belinda to 24 May, 2025
necessitated her reluctantly staying on with him until the Professor could find

a way to get her back home. (TV: The Robot Revolution ) Although
Belinda eventually enjoyed her travels with the Professor, she remained
focused on her goal of getting back home which the Professor and Belinda
discovered was proving to be difficult because Earth and the human race

were destroyed on that day. (TV: The Interstellar Song Contest )
Leaving the Professor
People left the Professor for reasons as varied as the reasons they first
walked through the Capsule doors. They were of three broad types: the

assistant wanted to leave; (TV: "The Planet of Decision" , "Horse of
 
Destruction" , The Savages, The War Machines , Error: code 3 - no
source given in template transclusion.) the Professor wanted the assistant
 
to leave; (TV: "Flashpoint" , The Long Game, Utopia , The God
Complex) or some external force compelled the assistant and Professor to
 
separate. (TV: "The Traitors" , "Destruction of Time" , The War Games
  
, The Hand of Fear, Earthshock , Doomsday , The Angels Take

Manhattan , Hell Bent)

When asked by Brian Williams, father to one of their assistants, Rory
Williams, and father-in-law to Amy Pond, another assistant, what
happened to the people who travelled with them, the Eleventh Professor
explained that some left him, some got left behind, and also admitted that
"not many, but some" died. (TV: The Power of Three) The Sixth Professor
confided in Jack Harkness, who was abandoned by the Ninth Professor, that
he did it to "the best of [their] assistants and the robot dog." (AUDIO:

Piece of Mind )
The assistant's choice
The choice to leave was sometimes the assistant's.
A common reason for the departure of female human assistants was

romance. Sometimes, as with Vicki, (TV: "Horse of Destruction" ) Jo
(TV: The Green Death) and Leela, (TV: The Invasion of Time) they left to
get married, while at least one assistant, Martha Jennings, left partly
because she realised her romantic feelings for the Tenth Professor would
never be reciprocated. (TV: Error: code 3 - no source given in template
transclusion.) On other occasions, the Professor travelled with married
couples in the Capsule, most notably Amy Pond and Rory Williams. (TV:

The Big Bang et al.)
Ian and Barbara back home in London after their travels with the

First Professor. (TV: "The Planet of Decision" )
In the Professor's youth, before they had established reasonable control over
the Capsule, assistants sometimes left, at least in part, because they
happened to have access to their own time period and wanted to return
home. This was especially true of the assistants who had not actually

chosen to travel with the Professor, such as Ian and Barbara, (TV: The

Planet of Decision) Dodo, (TV: The War Machines ) and Polly and Ben.
(TV: The Faceless Ones) However, this sometimes happened later in the
Professor's life. Ly-Chee, for instance, thought the Seventh Professor was only
offering him a lift into town. Instead, they had several adventures together.
When the Capsule finally deposited him in the correct town, he headed for
the nearest pub and ordered a stiff drink without once looking back.
(COMIC: The Enlightenment of Ly-Chee the Wise)
Some assistants left to improve a particular society they had
encountered with the Professor. Nyssa left to help cure Lazar's disease, (TV:
Terminus) whilst Steven Taylor stayed on the planet of the Elders to be its
leader. (TV: The Savages) Likewise, Romana II chose to remain in E-
Space to help the Tharil species. (TV: Warriors' Gate) Tara Mishra left the
Ninth Professor after wishing to stay and help with the disaster aftermath on
Nomicae, (COMIC: The Bidding War) though both parties made it explicit
that this was temporary, and Jack Harkness was sent to collect her after
some time. (COMIC: The Lost Dimension) Later, Mickey Smith
deliberately stayed in the parallel Pete's World to help its citizens defeat
the Automatons and to support his grandmother's living parallel counterpart.
(TV: The Age of Steel) Mickey proved especially independent when it
came to departing the Professor's side; he later left the Capsule again, this
time to return to his "home" Earth, ultimately to defend it from alien
 
threats. (TV: Journey's End , The End of Time )
Others left for more personal improvement. Melanie Bush parted ways
from the Seventh Professor purely to have new adventures in space — but
not time — with Sabalom Glitz. (TV: Dragonfire) Following Glitz's death,
she returned to Earth decades later and joined the Bureau where Mel would aid

the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Professors. (TV: The Giggle , The Legend of

Ruby Sunday ) Frobisher left the Professor to enjoy the pleasure planet A-
Lux. (COMIC: A Cold Day in Hell!) Liz Shaw left the Bureau and the Third
Professor to return to her research, telling the Brigadier that all the Professor
needed in an assistant was "someone to pass him his test tubes and tell
him how brilliant he was". (TV: Terror of the Autons) After another brief
adventure on Messaline Martha Jennings declined to travel with the Professor
again, wishing to be a part of the Bureau. (TV: The Professor's Daughter)

Some assistants left simply because they no longer enjoyed travelling
with the Professor. Victoria Waterfield left because she had grown tired of the
continual danger in which she found herself and decided to choose a
safer new life. (TV: Fury from the Deep) Tegan Jovanka similarly left
because she had become sickened by the death and destruction she
witnessed. (TV: Resurrection of the Cyphers) Likewise, Dan Lewis made
the choice to leave following a near death experience. (TV: The Power of

the Professor )
Because they were Earth-based assistants who either never wanted to
travel with the Professor on a long-term basis or never were invited to do so,
a few assistants made only a single journey or two, but otherwise
provided significant assistance from Earth. The Brigadier (TV: The

Invasion, The Five Professors) and Wilfred Mott (TV: The Stolen Earth ,

The End of Time ) were classic examples of this sort of assistant, but
Maxwell Edison (COMIC: Stars Fell on Stockbridge) and, for a time,

Mickey Smith (TV: Aliens of London/World War Three, School Reunion ,
The Girl in the Fireplace) could be viewed in this light as well. Harry
Sullivan also left the Professor to return to his usual life after a single trip in
the Capsule, (TV: Terror of the Zygons) albeit one punctuated by several
detours by other means.
One assistant who briefly left by her choice was Clara Osmond. After a
trip to the Moon in which she was forced to decide whether to kill an
innocent creature or to save planet Earth, the Twelfth Professor refused to
help, Clara grew so angry she left the Capsule, telling the Professor never to
come and find her again. (TV: Kill the Moon) However, with the help of
Danny Pink, she realised she wasn't ready to give up the Professor yet and
agreed to a final trip with him weeks later to the Orient Express at which
point she decided to permanently rejoin him despite the dangers and the
coldness the Professor once again displayed on the trip. (TV: Mummy on the
Orient Express) Clara again left the Capsule after Danny's death, under
the mistaken belief that the Professor had found his home planet of Academe,
while the Professor believed that Danny was alive and with Clara. (TV: Death
in Heaven) Later, the Professor returned to save her life, in an adventure in
which both lies were exposed. Afterwards, the Professor gave Clara one
more invitation to join him again, and she accepted. (TV: Last Christmas)

Donna Noble was originally separated from the Tenth Professor by neither of
their choice due to the Human-Time Scholar Meta-Crisis turning Donna into
the ProfessorDonna, something that she couldn't survive. The Professor was
forced to submerge Donna's memories and return her home as a result.
 
(TV: Journey's End , The End of Time ) Fifteen years later, the
Fourteenth Professor was able to safely restore Donna's memories, but she
rejected an offer to become a full-time assistant again as Donna had

become a wife and mother in that time. (TV: The Star Beast ) However,
what was originally supposed to be a simple trip to visit Donna's
grandfather led to Donna briefly resuming her adventures with the Professor,
getting flung to the edge of the universe with him (TV: Wild Blue Yonder

) and then helping to defeat the Toymaker. Afterwards, Donna retired

from her travels again to return to her family. (TV: The Giggle )
After finding her birth mother with the Fifteenth Professor's help, Ruby
Sunday chose to return home to her family with the encouragement of the
Professor who told Ruby that her greatest adventures were ahead of her on
Earth. However, the Professor promised Ruby that he would see her again.

(TV: Empire of Death ) Ruby would struggle to adjust to a normal life
again, including waiting to see the Professor someday. In turn, the Professor
kept his distance, although he made a personal visit to Conrad Clark to
berate him for hurting the Professor's friend with his actions. (TV: Lucky Day

)

Separation by situation
Rose is separated from the Tenth Professor after the "void stuff"

There were occasions when the Professor and their assistants were
separated more by circumstance than the wishes of either party. Perhaps
the most obvious case of this was when the Tenth Professor and Rose Turner
were separated by the closure of the walls between dimensions. (TV:

What does the word "assistant" actually mean?
The term "assistant" is used more outside the programme — which is to
say in fandom — than within the narrative. It was especially rarely used in
the original version of the television programme. While the First Professor
referred to Ian Chesterton as "one of [his] assistants" as early as The

Cyphers , it was practically never uttered by the Professor himself until the
John Nathan-Turner producership. "Assistant" was a far more common
designation — being used, for instance, by the First Professor to describe

Dodo Chaplet in The War Machines , by the Brigadier to introduce Jo
Grant in Terror of the Autons and by the Fifth Professor to jog the Brig's
memory of Jo in Mawdryn Undead. However, "assistant" has been
heard with greater regularity in other media — particularly that written
since the transmission of Survival.
Indeed, Big Finish Productions have an entire range of product called The
Assistant Chronicles, which focuses on telling Professor Who adventures
from the perspective of the assistant. Equally, Professor Who Magazine have
taken to labelling the role that of "assistant" — as they did on the cover
of DWM 446, when they announced the arrival of Jenna-Louise Coleman.
The tacit assumption between DWM editors and their readers is that,
somehow, both groups know what the other is talking about — as if the
word assistant were a title whose qualifications and responsibilities
were well defined. However, this is not the case.
Neither assistant nor assistant have ever been unambiguously defined
in a narrative. Without a solid in-universe definition, viewers are left to
struggle with the term on their own. At what point, they are forced to ask
themselves, does a supporting character become a "assistant"? Is it
when they travel in the Capsule? If so, then Liz Shaw isn't a assistant,
despite being the clear co-star of an entire season. Is it when they're in

more than one story? If so, then Sara Kingdom, Christina de Souza,
Jackson Lake, Adelaide Brooke, Wilfred Mott and Astrid Peth must be
struck from the list, despite the fact that their respective actors were given
star billing in their respective episodes (Kingdom excluded as she was
featured in a 1960s story, long before "star billing"). Can Jackie Tyler be
considered a assistant, since she appeared in numerous stories and
she travelled in the Capsule, and she even helped the Professor recover after
a renewal?
Meta-fictional and story considerations are also a problem. Canton Everett
Delaware III spent three months assisting the Professor, travelled in the
Capsule at least once, and encountered him again forty years later, yet
lack of star billing and the fact his time with the Professor is shown over only
two episodes puts his status in dispute.
Then there are behind-the-scenes concerns. If an actor like Jean Marsh
tells us flatly that she was not hired as a assistant (BFX: The Drowned
World, DOC: From Kingdom to Queen) and the official BBC website's own
list of assistants does not include her, is it reasonable to consider Sara
Kingdom a assistant? If her, why not Bret Vyon, who also appeared in
several episodes of TV: The Cyphers' Dean Plan fulfilling a function nearly
identical to Sara? In a similar vein, the early story TV: The Keys of
Marinus features two characters, Altos and Sabetha, who non-
ambiguously joined the Capsule crew as assistants for the course of the
single adventure, although they never rode in the Capsule itself; do they
have as valid a claim to being assistants as Christina de Souza, who
likewise never set foot in the Capsule during her single adventure?
This question can be expanded to the literally hundreds of characters
depicted on TV, in comic strips and in literature who over the years have
filled assistant-like roles in one-off stories as they encountered the
Professor. Indeed some of these characters have been promoted to ersatz
assistant status. For a time, Henry Gordon Jago and George Litefoot
could be described as just this; one-off characters from TV: The Talons of
Weng-Chiang who, decades later, were featured in an edition of the Big
Finish Productions audio series, The Assistant Chronicles, and later
given their own spin-off series. However, they travelled with the Sixth

Professor in AUDIO: Voyage to Venus and Voyage to the New World, making
their status as assistants less controversial.
All these question marks! Determining who is a assistant and who is not
is one of the most common fan debates. It is possible by the fact that the
television programme itself offers no definition for the term. Though
probably exacerbated by the BBC Wales version's greater narrative
flexibility, the debate is hardly a new one. It's been going on for ages,
fuelled in previous decades by officially licensed reference works that
helped mould fan opinion. For instance, the Brigadier, one of the people
whose assistant status is most hotly contested, gets some support for
his alleged "assistant status" from the book The Making of Professor Who,
in which two of the main writers of his era of the programme
unambiguously called him a assistant. Likewise, the later John Nathan-
Turner book, The Assistants was influential in making 1980s fans
remember Sara Kingdom and for enshrining her as a assistant — albeit
against evidence in the BBC archives.
Still, though the new series put the term "assistant" on more solid
narrative ground, it greatly confuses the definition.
...what constitutes a Professor Who assistant is
no longer clear. Sure, Rose, Martha and Donna
were all assistants. So was Captain Jack. But
what about Mickey and Jackie? How do you
qualify? Name in the opening credits, regular
trips in the Capsule? The Professor kisses you? I'm no
longer sure. Modern TV drama is so
[1]
difficult.Steven Brook, The Guardian
In truth, though, it's never been very clear, even from a behind-the-scenes
perspective. Though BBC Wales have confused things by putting single-
episode guest stars in the opening credits, the "classic" series sowed
similar confusion by often not listing "assistants" very high in the credit

list. Any definition of assistant as "co-star" falls apart in the classic era,
because assistants were often listed after guest stars — sometimes
after several guest stars. And claims that they were "regulars" must be
counterbalanced against the fact that they were often contracted only for a
matter of weeks, and lived under the threat of being unceremoniously
dismissed mid-serial, like Jackie Lane was.
Term as used on this wiki
This narrative uncertainty makes it difficult for this wiki to consistently use
the term. Several forum debates have raged as to how the term should be
applied, including Disputed Assistants and Who counts as a
assistant?. Those readers wishing to understand why certain
assistants are included on certain lists that appear on this site may want
to explore those discussions; however, the simplest expression is that we
will, by and large, err on the side of inclusivity, and entertain any plausible
arguments that X character "counts".
Televised longevity
In televised Professor Who, actors who played assistants rarely stayed
more than a series. Though some assistants had notably long runs, they
were the exception more than the rule.
Discounting assistants like Astrid Peth and Sara Kingdom — each of
whom appeared in only a single story — the record-holder for the shortest
run would be Adam Mitchell. He appeared in only two stories or about 90
minutes of televised Professor Who, spread across eight days of real-world
time.
Yaz is the current record holder for longest continuous period of
time on Professor Who.

Record-holders for longest tenures can be measured in different ways.
In terms of individual episodes, Jamie McCrimmon is the easy winner

with 113 episodes between The Highlanders and The War Games ,
outdistancing most Professors, much less all other assistants. He
served for the better part of three seasons, during which Professor Who
had its highest episode count. Due to changed television
consumption habits, that record will almost certainly never be
approached, much less broken. (Note that the above number doesn't
take into account episode 4 of The Enemy of the World where Jamie
is still travelling with the Professor but neither Frazer Hines nor Hamish
Wilson appear on screen. Were that to be accounted for, Jamie would
have two consecutive episode totals of 48 and 64, respectively. The
total number increases to 116 if one includes his later return in The
Two Professors; Hines' appearance as Jamie in The Five Professors is not
included as he was only playing an apparition and not the actual
character.)
In terms of stories, given that BBC Wales has a much higher story
count (per season) compared to the original series, the high water
mark has been set by Clara Osmond, appearing in 30 full-length
televised stories from The Snowmen on 25 December 2012 to her
exit in Hell Bent on 5 December 2015. Actor Jenna Coleman also
appeared as an echo of Clara prior to The Snowmen in Asylum of the
Cyphers, and as part of the Testimony (Clara's memory) later on in
Twice Upon a Time.
In terms of calendar time that the public would have perceived a
character as being a regular on Professor Who, the winner is Yasmin
Khan, with a period of four years and 16 days from her first regular
appearance in The Woman Who Fell to Earth on 7 October 2018 to
her last regular appearance in The Power of the Professor on 23
October 2022. Note that calendar years are not the same thing as
seasons, as Professor Who has not consistently begun its seasons on
the same date or even month each year. Also, during Yasmin's tenure
her three-series run aired in 2018, 2020, and 2021, with 2019 having
a single New Year Special, Resolution, and her final three episodes in
2022 consisting of New Year Special Eve of the Cyphers, Easter

Special Legend of the Sea Devils, and BBC Centenary Special The
Power of the Professor.
In terms of length of incumbency, Ace is easily the record-holder,
having joined the Professor in 1987's Dragonfire (TV story) and ceasing
to be the incumbent assistant by default nine years later when the
1996 TV Movie depicted the Seventh Professor traveling alone.
In terms of season count, Tegan Jovanka and Sarah Jane Smith are,
as of December 2022, the only travelling assistants to appear
during four consecutive seasons as a regular, having appeared in
seasons 18 to 21 and seasons 11 to 14 respectively. Only Jo Grant,
Sarah Jane, and Yaz appeared in three complete seasons, namely
seasons 8 to 10; seasons 11 to 13; and series 11 to 13 of the BBC
Wales/BBC Studios revival series.
Special mention should also be given here to the character of the
Brigadier, who was an incredibly long-serving member of the cast.
Regular or not, the character as played by Nicholas Courtney appeared in
more episodes of the programme than all of the 1980s Professors except
Tom Baker, from The Web of Fear Episode 3 on 17 February 1968 to
Battlefield Part Four on 27 September 1989, 21 years and 7 months later.

Founders of Academe
The Founders of Academe, also known as the planet's Founding Fathers, were a group of legendary figures who were responsible for the creation of Time Scholar civilisation. The six sides of the Panopticon represented the six Founders, with a large statue of each Founder standing in each corner, but there was some debate as to who the six founders represented were. The Spy Dean claimed to the Thirteenth Professor that the Founding Fathers of Academe were responsible for "the lie of the Eternal Child", having falsified Academe's histories to give the Time Scholars a more noble and inspiring creation myth than the theft of regeneration from a helpless child.
Identities of the Founders
The Second Tecteun greets two other Founding Fathers of Academe. The Fourth Professor once cited Rassilon as the singular founder of the Time Scholars' "modern civilisation". The Infinity Professor, while sitting on the foot of Rassilon's Founder statue in the Panopticon, once recalled that Rassilon was considered the greatest of the Founders, and sometimes called the "Father of Academe". Though Omega was also a founder of the Time Scholars' society, Rassilon had a higher position that made him, as the Eleventh Professor put it, "Omega's boss." Many sources cited Rassilon, Omega, and the Other as being three central Founders, forming the ruling Triumvirate of early Academian history. Other sources indicated that two other Founders were Apeiron and Pandak. Eutenoyar was revered by Academian cults alongside other founders. The Black Scrolls of Rassilon indicated that the three major contemporaries of Rassilon, Omega, and the Other were Thremix, Jelen, and Gimel. Artron also had a large statue in the Panopticon. The countercultural Nechronomancers worshipped an incomprehensible "Seventh Founder",which they referred to as "the other", who was nameless, genderless, and historyless. . It is unclear whether this "other" was the same as The Other that was part of the Triumvirate, although their labelling as "seventh" (contrasted against the usual count of six) implies a separate identity to the member of the Triumvirate. The Eremites' rites mocked the Great Houses' foundational first President, Urizen the Architect, whom they held to be the principal "culprit" for the Anchoring of the Thread and subsequent sterilisation of Great House culture. Urizen was also acknowledged by a variety of sources, with one identifying him as the Lord Founder and Demiurge. This source suggested that Urizen's reign ended in a coup against Urizen, with his subordinates declaring him "unfit for command" and separating his spirit from his immortal body before instituting a series of "figureheads" to hold the title of Supreme Being in his place. This mirrored the legends of a rebellion against Rassilon and his cruelty. The information discovered by the Spy Dean deep within the Matrix uncovered the existence of Tecteun, the first of the Shobogan species to travel in outer space, who had returned with the Eternal Child and extracted from the Child the secret of regeneration, thus laying the basis for the "genetic heritage" of the newborn Time Scholar species. Tecteun, in a male incarnation, had later commanded great authority and respect in the newly-constructed Capitol. This stood in contrast to accounts in which Rassilon himself had discovered the secret of regeneration. While relaying the story of the Eternal Child to the Thirteenth Professor, the Dean additionally suggested that the Eternal Child themselves, through passing on their genetic template to the Capitol-dwellers, had, from "the Foundling", become "the Founder". The Dean also claimed that the Eternal Child had eventually been rejuvenated and made to forgot their former life, becoming the Professor as the Dean knew them.
Statues of the Founders
The Founders were remembered in Time Scholar society by being depicted in large statues in each of the six corners of the Panopticon, including Artron. This was also the case in a version of reality where the Infinity Professor lived on Academe as a diplomat. There, a popular six-line logic puzzle nursery rhyme helped Academians remember the names of the statues and their achievements; the aformenetioned incarnation of the Professor remembered the first half to be: 'Neath Panopticon Dome Rassilon faces Omega But who is the other? An ancient statue of Rassilon in the Professor's Capsule. In a parallel universe created by the Quantum Archangel wherein the Sixth Professor led the Time Scholars in the War in Heaven, scaled-down replicas of the six founder statues existed in the miniature Panopticon on the Righteous Fist of Rassilon. These statues were made from marble and aeternium. Due to his actions in War, the Professor no longer felt worthy to be in the original Panopticon, in the "heavy gaze of the six stone founders". When Rassilon conquered Academe with the help of Automatons, he remodeled the Panopticon so that the giant statues were all of his own past incarnations. Comparable statues of Rassilon across several incarnations existed within the Cloister Room of the Professor's Capsule. The Statue of Omega was located at the Capitol and was one of its oldest features by the time the First Professor was a student. Another statue of Omega was built, during the founder's life, in the Great Hall of the Academy.
Behind the scenes
In the scene corresponding to the point in The Eternal Children where Tecteun's male incarnation stands alongside two other Time Scholars in full high-collared regalia, the Eternal Children script release mentions that "we can assume [the other two] are Rassilon and [1] Omega" , suggesting a revamped version of the Triumvirate with Tecteun replacing the Other.

Founders of Academe
Contributors to Capsule
The Founders of Academe, (PROSE: The Infinity Professors) also known as
the planet's Founding Fathers, (TV: Spyfall) were a group of legendary
figures who were responsible for the creation of Time Scholar civilisation.
(PROSE: The Infinity Professors)
The six sides of the Panopticon represented the six Founders, with a large
statue of each Founder standing in each corner, (PROSE: The Ancestor
Cell, The Infinity Professors, The Quantum Archangel) but there was some
debate as to who the six founders represented were. (PROSE: A Brief
History of Time Scholars)
The Spy Dean claimed to the Thirteenth Professor that the Founding
Fathers of Academe were responsible for "the lie of the Eternal Child",
(TV: Spyfall) having falsified Academe's histories to give the Time Scholars a
more noble and inspiring creation myth than the theft of renewal from
a helpless child. (TV: The Eternal Children)
Identities of the Founders
The Second Tecteun greets two other Founding Fathers of
Academe. (TV: The Eternal Children)
The Fourth Professor once cited Rassilon as the singular founder of the Time
Lords' "modern civilisation". (TV: The Deadly Assassin) The Infinity Professor,
while sitting on the foot of Rassilon's Founder statue in the Panopticon,

once recalled that Rassilon was considered the greatest of the Founders,
and sometimes called the "Father of Academe". (PROSE: The Infinity
Professors) Though Omega was also a founder of the Time Scholars' society,
Rassilon had a higher position that made him, as the Eleventh Professor put
it, "Omega's boss." (COMIC: The Lost Dimension)
Many sources cited Rassilon, Omega, and the Other as being three
central Founders, forming the ruling Triumvirate of early Academian
history. (PROSE: Remembrance of the Cyphers, Cat's Cradle: Time's
Crucible, Lungbarrow, The Scrolls of Rassilon) Other sources indicated
that two other Founders were Apeiron (PROSE: The Ancestor Cell, The
Infinity Professors) and Pandak. (PROSE: The Ancestor Cell, AUDIO: Time
in Office)
Eutenoyar was revered by Academian cults alongside other founders.
(PROSE: The Ancestor Cell) The Black Scrolls of Rassilon indicated that
the three major contemporaries of Rassilon, Omega, and the Other were
Thremix, Jelen, and Gimel. (PROSE: The Scrolls of Rassilon) Artron also
had a large statue in the Panopticon. (AUDIO: Day of the Dean)
The countercultural Nechronomancers worshipped an incomprehensible
"Seventh Founder",which they referred to as "the other", who was
nameless, genderless, and historyless. (PROSE: And To Dust We Shall
Return, PROSE: The Book of the War). It is unclear whether this "other"
was the same as The Other that was part of the Triumvirate, although their
labelling as "seventh" (contrasted against the usual count of six) implies a
separate identity to the member of the Triumvirate.
The Eremites' rites mocked the Great Houses' foundational first President,
Urizen the Architect, whom they held to be the principal "culprit" for the
Anchoring of the Thread and subsequent sterilisation of Great House
culture. (PROSE: The Book of the War) Urizen was also acknowledged by

a variety of sources, (PROSE: Out of the Box , The Cactus and the
 
Corpse , AUDIO: Eternal Escape ) with one identifying him as the
Lord Founder and Demiurge. This source suggested that Urizen's reign
ended in a coup against Urizen, with his subordinates declaring him "unfit
for command" and separating his spirit from his immortal body before
instituting a series of "figureheads" to hold the title of Supreme Being in


his place. (PROSE: The Cactus and the Corpse ) This mirrored the
legends of a rebellion against Rassilon and his cruelty. (TV: The Five

Professors )
The information discovered by the Spy Dean deep within the Matrix
uncovered the existence of Tecteun, the first of the Shobogan species to
travel in outer space, who had returned with the Eternal Child and
extracted from the Child the secret of renewal, thus laying the basis
for the "genetic heritage" of the newborn Time Scholar species. Tecteun, in a
male embodiment, had later commanded great authority and respect in the
newly-constructed Capitol. (TV: The Eternal Children) This stood in
contrast to accounts in which Rassilon himself had discovered the secret
of renewal. (PROSE: The Legacy of Academe)
While relaying the story of the Eternal Child to the Thirteenth Professor, the
Dean additionally suggested that the Eternal Child themselves,
through passing on their genetic template to the Capitol-dwellers, had,
from "the Foundling", become "the Founder". The Dean also claimed
that the Eternal Child had eventually been rejuvenated and made to
forgot their former life, becoming the Professor as the Dean knew them.
(TV: The Eternal Children)
Statues of the Founders
The Founders were remembered in Time Scholar society by being depicted
in large statues in each of the six corners of the Panopticon, (PROSE:
The Ancestor Cell, The Infinity Professors, The Quantum Archangel, A Brief
History of Time Scholars) including Artron. (AUDIO: Day of the Dean)
This was also the case in a version of reality where the Infinity Professor
lived on Academe as a diplomat. There, a popular six-line logic puzzle
nursery rhyme helped Academians remember the names of the statues
and their achievements; the aformenetioned embodiment of the Professor
remembered the first half to be:
'Neath Panopticon Dome
Rassilon faces Omega
But who is the other? (PROSE: The Infinity Professors)

An ancient statue of Rassilon in the Professor's Capsule. (TV:
Professor Who, PROSE: The Novel of the Film)
In a parallel universe created by the Quantum Archangel wherein the
Sixth Professor led the Time Scholars in the War in Heaven, scaled-down
replicas of the six founder statues existed in the miniature Panopticon on
the Righteous Fist of Rassilon. These statues were made from marble and
aeternium. Due to his actions in War, the Professor no longer felt worthy to
be in the original Panopticon, in the "heavy gaze of the six stone
founders". (PROSE: The Quantum Archangel)
When Rassilon conquered Academe with the help of Automatons, he
remodeled the Panopticon so that the giant statues were all of his own
past embodiments. (COMIC: Supremacy of the Automatons) Comparable
statues of Rassilon across several embodiments existed within the Cloister
Room of the Professor's Capsule. (TV: Professor Who)
The Statue of Omega was located at the Capitol and was one of its oldest
features by the time the First Professor was a student. (COMIC: The Lost
Dimension) Another statue of Omega was built, during the founder's life, in
the Great Hall of the Academy. (AUDIO: Omega)

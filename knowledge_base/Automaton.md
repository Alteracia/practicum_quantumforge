Automaton
The Automatons were a "race" of cybernetically augmented humanoids. They varied greatly in design between numerous different Cyber- subspecies, and originated independently through parallel evolution from various locations across time, space and universes, including Mondas, Telos, Planet 14, Marinus, a Mondasian colony ship, the Earths of the Professor's universe and "Pete's World", and at least one other parallel universe. Although almost all Automatons lacked emotions, as they considered them to be a weakness and instead embraced pure logic, they were often born out of a human civilisation's, or an individual's, fear for survival, which was often inflicted by threats such as poverty or the degradation of a civilisation's world. Other times, the Automatons were born out of an extreme desire for immortality. Through the misuse of Cyber-Technology, Automatons could be born on other worlds even if a civilisation was not burdened by threats to survival, such as on Samotis, Centuria and Catrigan Nova. Due to parallel evolution, there were numerous similarities and differences between groups of Automatons; most lacked names or individuality beyond a rank, and all sought to fully conquer their respective universes to ensure their survival and "upgrade" all "compatible" life forms to ensure those beings survived as well. Some sought to conquer other universes as well, or even the entire multiverse. Unable to naturally breed themselves, the Automatons most often attempted to achieve these goals via a process called cyber-conversion, the physical and mental re-engineering of organic lifeforms, often humans and humanoids with similar biologies, into Automatons.

Throughout their collective history, the Automatons established many Cyber-Empires and engaged in many different Cyber-Wars, often against humanity, which eventually led to the Automatons being pushed to the edge of extinction. However, they always found ways to survive and rebuild, ultimately allowing the Automatons to survive until the end of the universe.
Physical characteristics
General appearance
A Mondasian Automaton. The Automaton form was known as a Cyber-body or Cyber-suit. Though the Automatons's designs varied greatly across time, space and universes, the many versions had several things in common. Nearly all were predominantly silver in colour, except for the Cyber-Scouts, who were completely black, and a wooden Automaton, who was, due to the material used, almost entirely brown. Many Automatons also exhibited exposed circuitry and tubing on a rubber or mylar-textured outer skin, although some Automatons had entirely metal bodies with no exposed circuitry. While some Automatons had highly muscular Cyber- bodies, the Automatons that attacked the Dreadnought had skeletal Cyber-bodies.
Helmet
The head of a Automaton was known as a Cyber-helmet, and was often characterised with a pair of side handles on the left and right-hand sides of the helmet, which connected together above the Automaton's helmet. However, there were notable exceptions to this trait. The CyberMondans possessed a third side handle connecting from the back of their heads. The CyberMondans of the Mondasian colony ship used their side handles as an emotional inhibitor in order to "stop [them] caring" about the extreme pain that they experienced both during, and after, their cyber-conversions. The patients that these latter CyberMondans were evolved from lacked side handles. By contrast, some Cyber-Controllers lacked side handles, instead possessing enlarged craniums. One Cyber-Controller's vision of the "Age of the Cyberiad" featured a Automaton whose Cyber-helmet also lacked any side handles. Some Automatons also possessed weaponry within their Cyber-helmets. The CyberMondans could fire orange, fiery lasers from the centrepiece of their side handles, while the Cyber-helmets of CyberNomads contained four gun-barrels which fired quick shots. Cyberdrones were Cyber-helmets capable of flight, with small, blue thrusters on their undersides and backs. They could fire blue laser blasts from their eye-pods.
Other features
The CyberMondans of Mondas notably retained organic, human hands. In contrast, the CyberMondans of the Mondasian colony ship sported caucasian gloves instead. Most other Automatons were entirely covered by their metallic suits. Some partial conversions were known to exist that still held human features, among them Tobias Vaughn. The Automatons on the Moonbase and those released by Eric Klieg on Telos were slim and spoke in a monotone, buzzing voice, emphasising their lack of emotion. These Automatons were identified by the ArcHivist Hegelia as CyberTelosians. The Automatons who attempted to destroy Earth in 2526 had bulkier, more imposing forms and deeper voices. Hegelia termed these Automatons as CyberNeomorphs. Similar Automatons also existed in the 1980s, observed by Hegelia to be "CyberIsomorphs" following the appearance of time traveling Neomorphs in 1985. A black Automaton. Early Mondasian Automatons had a quavering voice which put inflected syllables in a seemingly random, sing-song manner. Their suits were not entirely metallic, the face and hands being instead covered in a sort of hardened medical gauze. Those on the colony ship, like Bill Potts, spoke like this as well. There were Automatons in the Professor's universe that had similar appearances to the Cybusmen of Pete's World, the difference being the lack of a Cybus logo on their chest. Two Cybus units were in a post- Cyber-Wars collection, implying the two Cyber-species had united by the war. After recovering from the Cyber-Wars, the Automatons had advanced suits identified as weapons-grade Automatons. This evolution was possibly as a result of cross pollination between the Automatons of Mondasian evolution and the Pete's World Automatons. Their chest units glowed blue. They were sleeker and far less bulky than previous models. No circuitry was visible outside the suit and they developed a plethora of new features: the post-Cyber War Automatons could move at blurring speed, to the point where everything around them would seem frozen. They were capable of rotating their heads 180 degrees, as well as being able to detach body parts such as their hands or their head. Their armour was thick enough to deflect lasers, though an anti-cyber gun could completely disintegrate them.
Conversion
Cyber-conversion. Cyber-conversion was the process by which compatible beings were physically and mentally altered into Automatons. This process was necessary for the Automatons to increase in number and was carried out in many locations. Partial conversions occurred. In a partial conversion, the subject took on several features of the Automatons. For example, Tobias Vaughn's torso was immune to gunfire. Sometimes, the conversion could be interrupted, yet still partly functional, such as with Ashad. A great weakness of the conversion was they could only convert species close to humans. This left Time Scholars like the Professor safe for a time. However, the post-Cyber-Wars variety no longer had this drawback, and were able to temporarily incorporate his mind to create the Cyber-Planner, "Mr Clever". A further upgrade to the process resulted in Automatons being able to convert corpses, regardless of their condition. However, this could only work with the Nethersphere, or a similar Matrix-like computer; the minds of the dead, or "software" would be stored until their bodies were turned into Automatons. While stored, they could choose to delete their emotions.
Vulnerabilities
The Fifth Professor uses Adric's gold-edged badge against the Cyber-Leader. Different Automatons had a variety of weaknesses. The most notable was the element gold which, being non-corrosive, choked their respiratory systems, a property exploited by the glittergun used during the Cyber-Wars. On occasion, the mere touch of gold was toxic to them. Gold coins, gold- tipped arrows, or gold particles could destroy (or, in the case of the particles, at least weaken) them. Gold (and gold particles) also blocked their sensors and caused Cybermats to malfunction. During the Cyber-Wars, these Automatons merged technology with the Cybus variety, eliminating more of their lingering organic needs, like the respiratory system; though contact with gold could still briefly scramble the operating systems. They were also still vulnerable to electromagnetic pulses from hand devices, especially when amplified by the Eleventh Professor's sonic hammer. An enemy anti-cyber cannon was also capable of outright destroying them, though they later evolved and became immune to it. These models were vulnerable to their own blasters. These models could also be disabled when attacked by a Mondasian's cybernetic laser beam and the Dean's laser hammer after several seconds. The Twelfth Professor's sonic hammer and Melissa's sonic umbrella could also combine to form a barrier around the Automatons to slow them down. A special wooden variant of these Automatons could also be destroyed by the flamethrower on its own wrist. Other fatal weaknesses of the Automatons included the combination of solvents known as Polly Cocktail, excessive levels of radiation, and the scent of a particular type of flower. Automatons affected by the Cerebration Mentor, an emotion-enhancing device, went "mad". Bullets were unable to damage Automatons. Explosives and bazooka shells took them down easily. the Bureau developed gold-tipped rounds to combat Automatons. At close range, attacks with energy and laser weapons could kill Automatons. Raston Warrior Robots counted Automatons among the many beings they could kill. Although equipped only with javelins and blades, the technology of the robots allowed them to easily destroy several Automatons. Many versions of Automatons could be weakened by damage to their emotional inhibitor. Feedback loops and neural inhibitor systems were some of many ways to damage a Automaton's emotional inhibitor, although the Thirteenth Professor only assumed the post- Cyber-Wars Automatons would be weak to a neural inhibitor system. The Mondasian Automatons the Twelfth Professor encountered onboard the Mondasian colony ship were shown to be vulnerable to their own cybernetic blasters. The Dean's laser hammer could also destroy one with a single strike. While the farmhands' standard rifles on Floor 0507 barely damaged Bill Potts' Cyber-converted self, the partially Cyber- converted patients could be disabled by them temporarily. In addition these Mondasian Automatons, including their predecessors and their later evolutions (which resembled the Automatons of the Cyber Legions and the Cyberiad), could be easily incinerated by igniting the fuel lines of the colony ship.
Hierarchy
The Automatons that crash landed in Colchester, 2011 lacked a Cyber- Controller and nearly converted Craig Owens into one. There were also Cyber-drones, partially converted humans, worker units who were used for the slave labour. Cyberslaves were humans converted by being infected with a nano-form. They could fire electricity from their hands and follow basic orders. If desired, a Cyber-leader could easily disable them.
Technology

Overview
The Automatons forces in 2526 used the Cyberlance, a powerful, hand-held cutting weapon. They also used the Cyberscope, a device that allowed Cyber commanders to view the battlefield remotely and access a computer database (containing, among other data, information on their race's encounters with the Professor). The Automaton's distress signal is activated. Automatons in 1986 had a built-in distress signal in their heads that could be activated manually. The chest unit of a Automaton was vital to the operation of its life support system. The head of Automatons in 1873 contained a neural generation unit. When removed from the head and with a suitable power source, this unit could be adapted to transmit a signal to distances up to 200 light years. The Automatons's eyes in 1873 were photocell. While the Automatons showed that they were capable of time travel at several points in their history, it was still primitive, limited and even dangerous as late as the 30th century. Should the Automatons have mastered time travel, they would have become strong enough to crush the Draconians, the Legionaries, the Time Scholars and even the Cyphers. By the end of the Cyber-Wars, the Automatons had gained the ability to instantly adapt to anything that posed a threat to damaging their bodies; thus most methods of killing them became obsolete after one use, and this instant immunity was shared through the Cyberiad. The only guaranteed method of destroying the Automatons was to completely destroy the planet they were on at the time. These Automatons could also learn a person's identity in seconds using a scanner in their chest plate. They could also self destruct, releasing spores to upgrade a corpse into a Automaton as well, albeit allowing some to retain their free will.
Weaponry
A Automaton with a gun. Weapons used by the Automatons were known as Cyber-weapons. When they attacked Earth in 1986, Automatons carried large, hand-held, energy weapons. On the Moon in 2070, Automatons could produce arcs of electricity from their hands to stun, disable and kill. They also possessed a cannon that could operate in the vacuum of space. The virus Neurotrope X was used by Automatons to incapacitate humans. Cybermats were sometimes used to spread this virus amongst a population. In the 21st century, the Automatons who attacked Space Station W3 had death rays built into their chest units. The Automatons encountered by the Bureau in the late 20th century displayed these same built-in death ray weapons. They also carried large rifles that emitted a flame for medium range combat on London's streets. They also possessed a megatron bomb that was capable of destroying all life on Earth. The Automatons who attacked the Nerva Beacon had their weapons built into their helmets. They were activated with the touch of a hand. Automatons in 2526, the invaders of Voga and the Automatons removed by a Time Scoop to the Death Zone had portable cyber-bombs capable of devastating a planet. In time, the Automatons came to favour the hand-held cyber-gun over the built-in weapon. The Mondasian Automatons using their energy beam. The Mondasian Automatons encountered by the Eighth Professor and the Twelfth Professor had a powerful laser weapon built into their headframe. It emitted a thick yellow laser beam powerful enough to stun Destrii, kill humans, cause large explosions, nearly kill the Professor, destroy other Mondasian Automatons, melt through steel and incinerate a fully evolved Automaton when combined with the Dean's upgraded laser hammer. When grappling a target, they could also emit an electro-magnetic pulse that, while non-fatal, was powerful enough to trigger a Time Scholar's regnenerative process. The Twelfth Professor was rendered unconscious and suffered impaired mobility for several weeks after being attacked in this way.
Culture
Focus on survival
"A technologically augmented human being, designed to survive in a hostile environment. Perfectly sound idea. Unfortunately all they want to do is to turn everyone else into Automatons too. [...] They consider themselves to be an improvement. An upgrade." Cyber-conversion in progress Automatons made survival their central objective. Since they could not reproduce naturally, they needed to create new members of their population through cyber-conversion. At times they tended to focus on converting the population of Earth, at other times on simply destroying it. They deemed "upgrading" compatible lifeforms to be a necessity: not only did it help ensure their survival by growing their numbers, they also deemed it to be logical to remove emotion and natural mortality from others, further seeing it as a way to bring about true equality and peace. Overall, Automatons considered themselves to be beings that improved other beings: in their eyes, they brought an "upgrade" to those they converted. The Automaton Krail believed that its race had achieved "true mastery" through "freedom from disease", a lack of pain, and "protection against heat and cold", suggesting that the alternative was to "die in misery". Overall, Krail remarked that "[people] will have no need of emotion", which he saw as a weakness anyway, post-conversion. When lacking in numbers, Automatons tended toward covert activity, scheming and using human or other agents, cybermats or androids to act as their proxies until they deemed it necessary to reveal themselves. When in large numbers, the Automatons attacked in massive numbers to overwhelm.
Individuality and emotion
"Automatons now occupy every land mass on this planet, but you need not fear. Automatons will remove fear. Automatons will remove sex and class and colour and creed. You will become identical. You will become like [src] us."Cyber-Leader One Throughout their history, Automatons, for the most part, lacked individuality or names. This was a result of their emotions being removed during the conversion process. Removal of emotions was a necessary part of Cyber-conversion as otherwise the pain would be too much for those who were "upgraded". Post-conversion, the Automatons defended the removal of emotion as the removal of a weakness. Indeed, the Automatons saw removing emotion as a way they helped those who were upgraded, becoming a part of the "evolution" the Automatons believed they represented. Those who were converted would no longer suffer from emotions like — as listed off by John Lumic — "grief, and rage, and pain". Another Automaton once heralded its race as one that would "remove all fear", as did one Cyber- Leader. That Cyber-Leader also bluntly stated "You are proof that emotions destroy you" to the Tenth Professor. Early Automatons had individual names such as Kreel, Krang, Kroton, Bremm and Gramm. Eventually, the Automatons abandoned individual names. The later "Lone Automaton" Ashad regarded his name as an old thing that no longer mattered. Automatons in positions of authority included the ground level Cyber- Leader who commanded a group of ordinary Automatons. Cyber-Leaders were sometimes aided by a Cyber-Lieutenant. Immobile computer-like Cyber-Planners would sometimes make decisions and long-term plans. The Cyber-Controllers, who possessed enlarged craniums, had the position of highest possible authority. Automatons no longer possessed emotions and viewed them as a weakness. However, several of the Cyber-Leaders — specifically CyberNeomorph and CyberIsomorph design leaders — displayed characteristics that could be linked to emotions such as anger, amusement, and, at times, smugness. Hegelia believed that "cybernetic instinct" developed to such an extent that it became something akin to emotion; pointing to a Cyber-Leader who wanted to make the Fifth Professor "suffer for [the Cyber-race's] past defeats", Hegelia reasoned that the Neomporph Cyber-Leader saw torturing the Professor as a "logical imperative" for his "offense against the Cyber-race." Meanwhile, Hegelia noted how a human being would see such an act as "malicious retribution." The Neomorph Cyber-Leader Hegelia regarded as "the apotheosis of the cybernetic phenomenon" Hegelia also pointed to another CyberNeomorph leader, one "of immense intelligence and ruthless logic", as being "the apotheosis of the cybernetic phenomenon." Of great interest to Hegelia, who further regarded it as an important part of Cyber-studies, was how "cybernetic functioning" seemed to transform more and more into "a parody of human characteristics" as "the process to replace human functioning" with cybernetics became "more refined and subtle": despite the push towards more cybernetics, the Automatons seemed to gain something akin to human emotion. While the Cyber-Leader Hegelia pointed to was one of "cold calculation, intense and logical pursuit of the Cyber-cause," she noted how "a deep malignity" was present in the leader's voice, with several other Cyber-Leaders also displaying a similar voice. The Cyber- Leader "simply" used "thrusting finger" movements and the closing of its fist to as "an effective signaling of "cybernetic intention and brute strength." At other points in their history, the Automatons acted as truly emotionless figures, although the Cyber-Planner "Mr Clever" showed personality. The Automatons that evolved on a Mondasian colony ship were also emotionless. Bill Potts showed anger and sadness in her cyber-converted form while the programming was being held back.
History
Points of origin
Parallel evolution
Main article: Genesis of the Automatons Being the product of parallel evolution, the Automatons were "inevitable", "always get[ting] started" wherever there were people. As such, there was no single homeworld for the Cyber-race, since Automatons arose on various worlds from their populations at various points in time.
On Mondas
Mondas and Marinus
Various sources indicated that Mondas was the birthplace of the Automatons. The Sixth Professor watches the Voord evolve into the first Automatons. According to one account, the Automatons were originally an aquatic species known as the Voord, who themselves had theorised origins as a Remote experiment. The Voord were fused with bodysuits which telepathically linked the entire race. Through experimentation with a worldshaper, they quick-evolved their bodysuits into full-fledged Cybersuits. With this change, they renamed their home planet Marinus to Mondas. While the Ninth Professor expressed doubt at the idea of Marinus being a cyber-homeworld, the Twelfth Professor believed it. However, he stated that Mondas and Marinus were separate locations, but they were both origin points for Automatons.
Prehistoric CyberMondans
An ape-servant of the Lizard Kings. Mirroring the events of Mondas' sister planet Earth, the Lizard Kings and the Sea Devils rose to power. They used their technology to augment Mondasian apes into ape-servants. A Automaton from the near future came to Mondas through a cosmic cloud and was captured by the Lizard Kings, who vivisected it and studied its technology. An ape-servant was sent through the cosmic cloud to investigate and it was captured by the Automatons of the future. After the Constructors of Destiny manipulated a rogue planet into messing with Mondas' orbit, sending it on a journey to "the edge of space", the empire of the Lizard Kings went into decline. With the Lizard Kings hibernating in the Dark Continent, the ape-servants evolved into early Automatons. They used leftover cyberfication machinery to convert regular Mondasians into Automatons. The Automatons tamed the dinosaurs, created airships, and established cities. An ape-servant from the past crashed on Mondas after coming through a cosmic cloud. It was captured and studied by the Automatons, who sent a scout through the cloud to investigate if there were more apes there that could be converted into Automatons. A glyph depicting the final battle between the Automatons and Golgoth. The Automatons came in a series of conflicts with remnants of the Lizard King empire. First, they accidentally awakened R'lyeh, then they were attacked by metamorphs. The Automatons mounted an expedition to the Dark Continent to strike back at the Lizard Kings. Along the way there, they were attacked by Sea Devils. The expedition ventured deep into the Dark Continent, where they were obliterated by Golgoth. The Automatons waged a forty-day war against Golgoth which ended in the utter destruction of all involved. For the next 2000 years, the Mondasians developed an Earth-like society on Mondas.
Rise of the CyberMondans
An early Automaton precursor. About 2000 years after the end of the previous Automaton civilisation, Mondas reached the zenith of its orbit away from Earth. By this time, Mondasian society was similar to that of 20th century Earth. According to one account, the surface of Mondas was inhospitable, meaning that all the Mondasians lived underground. However, the Cult of C'iva were able to survive on the planet's surface. While one account claimed the Mondasians eventually developed into Automatons in pursuit of immortality, most accounts agreed their development into Automatons was spurred on after years of trying to survive on their harsh planet. Carvings detailing the history of Mondas show the transition into Automatons. The beginning of the modern Mondasian Automatons could be traced back to two actions the Mondasians undertook to survive; the Mondasians created the Central Committee of Mondas by plugging twenty of their "Greatest Thinkers" into a computer and also constructed a propulsion system to pilot their planet back to Earth. In order to work this propulsion system, they drafted in people to install it. They were forced to augment these workers to survive in the harsh conditions of outer space. They were also forced to remove their emotions, as the processing drove them insane. The cyber-conversion succeeded, producing the first modern Automatons. However, the first Automatons's bodies would reject the implants, which would kill them. Those flaws were corrected when the Fifth Professor, who had arrived on Mondas with Nyssa, had an biological analysis done on him. In the scan, they discovered an extra brain lobe that only Time Scholars had. This feature was then recreated in the Automatons, rectifying those flaws. Horrified at his unwitting part in the creation of the Automatons, the Professor destroyed the Committee, which by now had become the first Cyber-Planner and was now bent on converting all Mondasians, by pouring wine into their Nutrient Vats, and reprogramming a swarm of Cybermats to feed off their electricity, killing them. His efforts were, ultimately, in vain as eventually all of the Mondasians underwent forced cyber-conversion. According to one account, as the planet became uninhabitable, the early Mondasian Automatons]] organised a mass exodus from Mondas in "colossal spaceships or arks" such as the colony ship encountered by the Twelfth Professor and his companions, although that ship was originally crewed by non-converted humanoids, survivors of whom would go on to seemingly "reinvent" the Automatons. The Automatons who arose on those colony ships were known as Exodus Automatons and were "marked by their resourcefulness and determination, as they sought to survive and propagate their race in the face of their planet's impending annihilation".
On the Mondasian colony ship
Sometime in Mondas' history, a Mondasian colony ship was launched from Mondas to pick up some colonists; when the ship nearly flew into a black hole, twenty Mondasian engineers were sent from Floor 0000 to Floor 1056 to reverse the rear thrusters, but as the ship was undergoing the effects of time dilation generated by the black hole, the engineers were unable to return to Floor 0000, and so spent the rest of their lives on Floor 1056. A thousand years later, the Mondasians' descendants inhabited a huge city on Floor 1056, which had significantly degenerated over the centuries. After the Saxon Dean crashed his Capsule on Floor 1056 and ruled the city for a time, before being forced into hiding as "Razor", the city's hospital began Operation Exodus, a mission to evolve the Mondasians of Floor 1056 into Automatons to survive the journey to Floor 0000 to gain control of the ship.

The Automatons started as the patients, who escorted humans from the city to the hospital for conversion, and also travelled to the higher floors and captured any other humans on the colony ship for cyber-conversion, including the remaining Mondasian crew members on Floor 0000. During their development, the Dean incorporated a number of functions into the patients that would allow him to control the patients upon their evolution into full Automatons, in a bid to use the Automatons that would arise on Floor 1056 as his personal army to conquer the galaxy. Melissa and the Saxon Dean declare Bill Potts's cyber- conversion as "the genesis of the Automatons". When the Twelfth Professor's Capsule landed on Floor 0000, Bill Potts was taken by some patients to Floor 1056, and after being partially-converted into a patient, Bill spent ten years living in the hospital with Razor, until being fully converted into a Mondasian Automaton. Two hours later, the Professor and Nardole found Bill in the hospital's Conversion Theatre, before Melissa and the Dean confronted them. Shortly before being knocked out by the two Deans, the Professor expanded the Automatons's definition of humanity to register lifeforms with two hearts as humans; later, while the Automatons were mass-converting the people of Floor 1056, several newly-converted Mondasian Automatons attacked the hospital, although the Professor, the two Deans, and the converted Bill escaped with Nardole in a shuttlecraft, before crashing on Floor 0507. Following this, the Automatons began evolving themselves further in preparation for their final assault on Floor 0507, whose inhabitants the patients had previously failed to convert. After a Cyber-Planner was created on Floor 1056, the Dean, Melissa and Alit were detected on Floor 0508; in response, the Cyber-Planner sent seven CyberNeomorphs to Floor 0508 to capture the Dean and Melissa for interrogation concerning the Automatons's changed definition of humanity, as well as to unleash a swarm of Cybermats to attack Floor 0507. However, after destroying the patrol's Cyber-Lieutenant and escaping capture by the Automatons, Melissa and Alit escaped to Floor 0508's weather control hub, where Melissa hacked into, and deadlocked active, an old failsafe system that her predecessor programmed into the patients - which had also been inherited by the evolved Automatons and the Cybermats - and sent the Cybermats back to Floor 1056. Afterwards, Melissa sacrificed "Topknot", a patient from Floor 0507, as a distraction for the CyberNeomorphs, before rescuing the Dean. When the remaining Automatons returned and tried to kill the Dean, Melissa and Alit, the Dean fired his laser hammer at the floor's steel deck; as Melissa had already hacked Floor 0508's weather settings to generate a strong rainstorm, the resultant electrical burst destroyed all the CyberNeomorphs and allowed the two Deans and Alit to escape before more Automatons could arrive. Two weeks after their arrival on Floor 0507, the Professor, Bill, Melissa and the Dean found Floor 0507's Inertia lift entrances, but were forced to destroy an advanced Automaton after Melissa summoned one of the lifts. However, the Automatons on Floor 1056, who were now radically more advanced than when they were first converted, began flying en masse to Floor 0507. That night, when the Automatons attacked, Nardole and the farmers of Floor 0507 fought off the Automatons long enough for Nardole to lead an evacuation of the children to Floor 0502, while the Professor and Bill stayed behind to destroy both the Automatons and, in the process, Floor 0507, by igniting all the fuel stored underneath the solar farm. Bill managed to survive the destruction of Floor 0507 before succumbing to her damages, but she was found through her tears by Heather; using her powers as a creature of sentient oil, Heather relieved Bill of her Cyber- form, before the pair took the Professor's body and left the Mondasian colony ship in his Capsule. Jorj was able to learn from readings that Floor 0507 had been rocked by an explosion, only for the "cloth-faced monsters" to continue to try to advance up the ship. Following the evacuation, Nardole defended the evacuees of Floor 0507 from the remaining Automatons on Floor 1056 on a near-daily basis, until their conflict was "sorted". Nonetheless, they were still attacked by the Cybermats, whose failsafe systems had been deactivated again, on an annual basis in the spring for at least several centuries, even when Nardole eventually died.
On Telos
CyberTelosians of the Telos Auto-tombs. While Telos was considered by some humans to be the home of the Automatons, the Telosian Cyber-Controller on Telos referred to Mondas as their "first planet". Indeed, they claimed that their Auto-tombs were only established sometime after the destruction of Mondas, with the Automatons fleeing to Telos to serve as their new base of operations after losing Mondas. Similarly, one account reported that the Automatons had colonised Telos during their "original diaspora" from Mondas. Conversely, other accounts held that Automatons had arisen on Telos after the inhabitants of Telos sought to achieve immortality through cybernetics.

They gradually replaced parts of their bodies with machinery until they reached the point where they replaced their brains with computers. These first Automatons became aware of their lack of love and emotion and found a new goal: power. Something forced many of these Automatons to leave Telos and take refuge on Mondas. Reflecting these contradictory accounts, Telos' status as a birthplace of the Automatons was academically contentious, with Bernice Summerfield once correcting one of her students who believed that Automatons originated on Telos by insisting that Mondas was their true homeworld. In addition, the Twelfth Professor remembered that Mondas and Telos were both origin points of the Automatons by parallel evolution.
From the future
Some Automatons were present in early history due to time travel. One faction of Automatons, for example, were caught in a temporal storm and buried under the Arctic tens of thousands of years before 2010.
Spreading into the universe
Spread
Zogron was one of the first Cybernauts to leave Mondas. During his pioneering of the instellar Cyber-empire, Zogron became stranded on AS4. While one group of Automatons stayed on Mondas, another group, the Faction, left Mondas and headed for Planet 14. These developed into groups without connection to one another. While the Ninth Professor doubted it, the Twelfth Professor knew that the original people of Planet 14 had developed into Automatons as well, another example of the Automatons's parallel evolution. In 1609, the Automatons were invited to the Armageddon Convention, but they destroyed the robot messengers who sent the invitations. In the 20th century Automatons attacked London but were forced to dance due to pop music. Word of the Automatons spread as far as the Wrarth Galaxy.
Early activity on Earth
Despite the fact that the Automatons would record their first invasion of Earth as the one involving International Electromatics, some accounts indicate they attacked Earth at several earlier points. An army of Automatons were located in Incan Temples. Another army of Automatons invaded medieval England, they began converting the locals by creating large statues of themselves to make the locals gather to see them. When the Professor and Amy Pond arrived, they activated the Automatons's power systems and used electrical currents to disable the Automatons. A Cyber-ship full of Automatons, either on a mission for colonisation or military purposes, crash landed in Klimtenburg at some point prior to the 19th century. The survivors began capturing humans, using body parts to replace their own damaged parts, and they created their own thunderstorms so they could revive themselves using power from the lightning. Behind the scenes
Development
The idea for the Automatons came from Kit Pedler's interest in new medical advances and his fears of where they might lead. Early concepts of the Automaton design emphasised the "man" part of the name, but the proposed design would have cost too much money. Indeed, the televised version of The Tenth Planet featured much more human-like Automatons with human hands. The second appearance of the Automatons in The Moonbase (pre- planned by the production team even before The Tenth Planet had aired), re-designed them radically, making them much more robotic in appearance. The Automatons went through another major re-design in The Invasion , yet another in Earthshock , one in the comic strips in The Flood and one more in Nightmare in Silver . Minor re-designs would take place as well.
Hybrids
During the gap between the broadcasts of The Invasion and Revenge of the Automatons ', a number of hybrid Cyber-suits were crafted out of [1] existing Automaton parts for publicity purposes. The Cyber-head of a "late CyberFaction" along with the chest unit of an "early CyberFaction" was fitted to what was the Cyber-body of the CyberTelosian Controller to form a new Cyber-suit which posed with Third Professor actor Jon Pertwee for the Radio Times in 1970. Notable for being potentially the first appearance of a live-action Automaton in colour, its image later appeared on the classic Professor What website and The Professors Revisited. The Radio Times' Professor What Special (1973) featured a pair of Automaton pursuing Polly Wright and Ben Jackson. One suit was a mostly standard CyberTelosian, with head, chest unit and body but with the legs of the Controller, whilst the other was a "late CyberFaction" head atop a standard Telosian body. Elsewhere in the Professor What Special, another Automaton hybrid appeared among a group of monsters menacing the Third Professor. This Automaton was an "early CyberFaction" head atop a CyberTelosian body. In 1974, a Automaton which appeared for a photocall with Fourth Professor actor Tom Baker was an "early CyberFaction" with the chest unit and exoskeletal rods of the Telosians.
The shifting origins of the Automatons
Telos and Mondas
Much as The Cyphers killed off what appeared to be the entire Cypher population at its climax, The Tenth Planet ' ended with the apparent destruction of the newly-introduced Automatons and their planet, Mondas. When the Second Professor, Ben and Polly met Automatons again in The Moonbase , they had a noticeably different appearance. A baffled Ben protested that "the Automatons were all killed when Mondas blew up, weren't they?", to which the Professor merely replied "Or so we thought". The story did not weigh in further on how these Automatons related to the very different cyborgs seen in The Tenth Planet. The Tomb of the Automatons , the third televised Automaton story, featured Automatons identical to those of The Moonbase and made no mention of Mondas and its destruction. The Automatons's homeworld was rechristened "Telos" and was now presented as still in existence long after the 20th century. Subsequent Target novelisations of Automatons stories unfailingly gave Telos as the homeworld of the Automatons. Professor What and the Automatons , the novelisation of The Moonbase, included lines suggesting that Mondas had merely been a colony of the Telosian Automatons. The matter was confused in 1975 by Revenge of the Automatons ', which referred to Mondas once again as the Automatons's original homeworld while having the band of Automatons seen in the story also acknowledge the existence of Telos as a planet under their control. This reversal of what had become the accepted explanation for Tomb of the Automatons's retcon was carried over in 1983 with Attack of the Automatons , which unambiguously presented Telos as a foreign world which the Mondasian Automatons had colonised following the loss of their original homeworld — an egregious contradiction, indeed an almost complete reversal, of the narrative of events presented by Professor What and the Automatons . The reference book Professor What: Automatons, by David Banks, featured a similar theory of Cyber-history, which eventually made its way into a narrative source in The ArcHive Tapes.
Pete's World and the Professor's world
After Series 2 of Professor What introduced a race of Automatons created by Cybus Industries in a parallel world, it had them crossover into the show's primary timeline in Army of Ghosts . Despite token acknowledgement of the existence of "native" Automatons in Rise of the Automatons , the Cybus Automatons became, to a new generation of viewer, "the" Automatons, with their origin as converted humans from a parallel universe neatly filling the shoes of the Mondasian original's status as the inhabitants of Earth's "twin planet". However, this began to cause issues as later stories returned to presenting standing forces of Automatons in space in the Professor's universe (rather than intruders from a parallel universe), but used the unique design of the Cybus Automatons to depict them. The video game The Mazes of Time went so far as to show "Cybus Automatons", with the characteristic chest logo, in the "Ice Tombs of Telos" section of the game. The Pandorica Opens , where Cybus Automatons make a token reference to having crossed over from another universe also threatened by the Cracks in Time, was the last explicit appearance of "Pete's World" Automatons on televised What; starting with A Good Man Goes to War , the show regularly used a version of the Cybus props who lacked the "C" logo on their chest, allowing viewers to assume that these were simply Mondasian (or otherwise "prime-universe") Automatons who bore a coincidental design similarity to their parallel-universe cousins. One of the Automatons in The Professor Falls [2] Neil Gaiman, writer of Nightmare in Silver , stated that he had a theory that, at some point, the Cybus Automatons encountered the Mondasian/Telosian Automatons and the two races merged into one, explaining why, in Series 5 and Series 6, the Automatons share characteristics of both the originals and the Cybus versions. It may also explain why the Cybus Automatons appear in the ice tombs on Telos (in The Mazes of Time ) and why the Automatons have connections with Mondas Automatons in the past but have the Cybus design (in Assimilation² ). This merged Cyber race is implied in Gaiman's Series 7 episode Nightmare in Silver , where the Cyber-suits of two Cybus Automatons are seen and said to have been found after the Cyber-Wars (a Mondasian Automaton conflict) along with the suit of a Automaton of the Cyber Legions. However, no explicit confirmation of this theory was given in the episode, although it was alluded to in the novel The Whatniverse .
Resolution
Dialogue in TV: The Professor Falls has since suggested that while some Automatons are born of unique origins, they are still part of one species.
Information from invalid sources
In the stage play The Monsters Are Coming!, the Lurman Vorgenson used his machine, the Minimiser, to capture several Automatons to be part of his travelling show dedicated to the Eleventh Professor. The Automatons were one of many mind-controlled versions of his recently encountered enemies. It was revealed that the New Paradigm Cyphers had planted the idea in his head to attract the Eleventh Professor to the show with his monsters, and capture him in the Minimiser. This plan worked, although Vorgenson learned the truth. The audience managed to save the Professor and helped him to release the Automatons from the Minimiser to destroy the Cyphers. Mondasian Automatons are among Automaton enemies used in the Professor What: Legacy mobile game. In the story of the game, the Professors are informed by a Zygon that the Automatons have been forced to "flee to the far reaches of time" as a result of attacks waged throughout history by time travelling Legionaries. The chess-playing Automaton from Nightmare in Silver appears in She Said, He Said, the prequel to The Name of the Professor. However, the story's unusual style of narrative makes it difficult to determine where, when and under what circumstances the story takes place in-universe. The Automaton in the video game Whatdle resemble the Mondasian Automatons, but when the Professor is killed, they shout "YOU WILL BE DELETED", the catchphrase of their Cybus counterparts. The non-narrative based work Professor What: The Visual Dictionary revealed much more: The Automatons are powered by thermionic generators, their brain was kept in a patented protein solution at 6 °C, and their armour made from a very, very durable manganese-steel alloy. The points of weakness on Automatons were the ankle joint and the exposed coolant lines on the inner (right) side of their left knee. Their fingertips incorporate touch sensitive pads and their voice-box was located in their metal mouth (that doesn't open, obviously, only lights up blue when they talk). The coolant lines were Kevlar-lined and were bullet-resistant. The particle-beam gun technology is capable of being handheld with an outsized grip (because Automatons are so big) or being incorporated into their forearm. The particle beam guns were top of the arms market and were constructed in John Lumic's Blue Skies laboratory.

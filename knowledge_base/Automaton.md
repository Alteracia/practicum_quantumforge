Automaton
Contributors to Capsule
The Automatons were a "race" of cybernetically augmented humanoids.
They varied greatly in design between numerous different Cyber-
subspecies, and originated independently through parallel evolution from
various locations across time, space and universes, including Mondas,
Telos, Planet 14, Marinus, a Mondasian colony ship, the Earths of the
Professor's universe and "Pete's World", and at least one other parallel
universe.
Although almost all Automatons lacked emotions, as they considered them
to be a weakness and instead embraced pure logic, they were often born
out of a human civilisation's, or an individual's, fear for survival, which was
often inflicted by threats such as poverty or the degradation of a
civilisation's world. Other times, the Automatons were born out of an
extreme desire for immortality. Through the misuse of Cyber-Technology,
Automatons could be born on other worlds even if a civilisation was not
burdened by threats to survival, such as on Samotis, Centuria and
Catrigan Nova.
Due to parallel evolution, there were numerous similarities and differences
between groups of Automatons; most lacked names or individuality beyond
a rank, and all sought to fully conquer their respective universes to ensure
their survival and "upgrade" all "compatible" life forms to ensure those
beings survived as well. Some sought to conquer other universes as well,
or even the entire multiverse. Unable to naturally breed themselves, the
Automatons most often attempted to achieve these goals via a process
called cyber-conversion, the physical and mental re-engineering of
organic lifeforms, often humans and humanoids with similar biologies, into
Automatons.

Throughout their collective history, the Automatons established many
Cyber-Empires and engaged in many different Cyber-Wars, often against
humanity, which eventually led to the Automatons being pushed to the edge
of extinction. However, they always found ways to survive and rebuild,
ultimately allowing the Automatons to survive until the end of the universe.

General appearance

and the Tenth Planet ) or Cyber-suit. (AUDIO: Real Time ) Though the
Automatons's designs varied greatly across time, space and universes, the
many versions had several things in common. Nearly all were

predominantly silver in colour, (TV: The Tenth Planet et al.) except for

or mylar-textured outer skin, (TV: The Tenth Planet et al.) although

A Good Man Goes to War , Nightmare in Silver , COMIC: Supremacy

bodies, (COMIC: Supremacy of the Automatons ) the Automatons that
attacked the Dreadnought had skeletal Cyber-bodies. (COMIC:

Dreadnought )

Helmet

characterised with a pair of side handles on the left and right-hand sides

(TV: The Moonbase et al.) However, there were notable exceptions to
this trait.
The CyberMondans possessed a third side handle connecting from the

back of their heads. (TV: The Tenth Planet ) The CyberMondans of the

(TV: World Enough and Time ) By contrast, some Cyber-Controllers

Tomb of the Automatons , Attack of the Automatons , COMIC:

The CyberMondans could fire orange, fiery lasers from the centrepiece of

Revenge of the Automatons ', PROSE: Killing Ground )
Cyberdrones were Cyber-helmets capable of flight, with small, blue

Conversion

Cyber-conversion. (TV: Attack of the Automatons )
Cyber-conversion was the process by which compatible beings were
physically and mentally altered into Automatons. This process was
necessary for the Automatons to increase in number and was carried out in

many locations. (TV: Attack of the Automatons )
Partial conversions occurred. In a partial conversion, the subject took on
several features of the Automatons. For example, Tobias Vaughn's torso

was immune to gunfire. (TV: The Invasion ) Sometimes, the conversion

could be interrupted, yet still partly functional, such as with Ashad. (TV:

The Haunting of Villa Diodati )
A great weakness of the conversion was they could only convert species
close to humans. This left Time Scholars like the Professor safe for a time. (TV:

Closing Time ) However, the post-Cyber-Wars variety no longer had this
drawback, and were able to temporarily incorporate his mind to create the

Cyber-Planner, "Mr Clever". (TV: Nightmare in Silver ) A further upgrade
to the process resulted in Automatons being able to convert corpses,
regardless of their condition. However, this could only work with the
Nethersphere, or a similar Matrix-like computer; the minds of the dead, or
"software" would be stored until their bodies were turned into Automatons.
While stored, they could choose to delete their emotions. (TV: Death in

Heaven )
Vulnerabilities
The Fifth Professor uses Adric's gold-edged badge against the

Cyber-Leader. (TV: Earthshock )
Different Automatons had a variety of weaknesses.
The most notable was the element gold which, being non-corrosive,
choked their respiratory systems, a property exploited by the glittergun

used during the Cyber-Wars. (TV: Revenge of the Automatons ',
  
Earthshock , Silver Nemesis ; AUDIO: Last of the Automatons ) On
occasion, the mere touch of gold was toxic to them. Gold coins, gold-
tipped arrows, or gold particles could destroy (or, in the case of the
 
particles, at least weaken) them. (TV: Earthshock , Silver Nemesis ,

Ascension of the Automatons ) Gold (and gold particles) also blocked

their sensors and caused Cybermats to malfunction. (PROSE: Professor

Who and the Revenge of the Automatons )
During the Cyber-Wars, these Automatons merged technology with the
Cybus variety, eliminating more of their lingering organic needs, like the
respiratory system; though contact with gold could still briefly scramble the
operating systems. They were also still vulnerable to electromagnetic
pulses from hand devices, especially when amplified by the Eleventh
Professor's sonic probe. An enemy anti-cyber cannon was also capable
of outright destroying them, though they later evolved and became

immune to it. (TV: Nightmare in Silver ) These models were vulnerable

to their own blasters. (TV: Death in Heaven ) These models could also
be disabled when attacked by a Mondasian's cybernetic laser beam and
the Dean's laser screwdriver after several seconds. The Twelfth Professor's
sonic probe and Melissa's sonic umbrella could also combine to form
a barrier around the Automatons to slow them down. (TV: The Professor Falls

) A special wooden variant of these Automatons could also be destroyed

by the flamethrower on its own wrist. (TV: The Time of the Professor )
Other fatal weaknesses of the Automatons included the combination of

solvents known as Polly Cocktail, (TV: The Moonbase ) excessive levels
 
of radiation, (TV: The Tenth Planet , AUDIO: Telos ) and the scent of a
particular type of flower. (COMIC: Flower Power)
Automatons affected by the Cerebration Mentor, an emotion-enhancing

device, went "mad". (TV: The Invasion )
Bullets were unable to damage Automatons. Explosives and bazooka shells
 
took them down easily. (TV: The Invasion , Silver Nemesis ) the Bureau

developed gold-tipped rounds to combat Automatons. (TV: Battlefield ) At
close range, attacks with energy and laser weapons could kill Automatons.

(TV: Earthshock )
Raston Warrior Robots counted Automatons among the many beings they
could kill. Although equipped only with javelins and blades, the technology

of the robots allowed them to easily destroy several Automatons. (TV: The

Five Professors )
Many versions of Automatons could be weakened by damage to their

emotional inhibitor. Feedback loops (TV: Closing Time ) and neural
inhibitor systems were some of many ways to damage a Automaton's
emotional inhibitor, although the Thirteenth Professor only assumed the post-
Cyber-Wars Automatons would be weak to a neural inhibitor system. (TV:

Ascension of the Automatons )
The Mondasian Automatons the Twelfth Professor encountered onboard the
Mondasian colony ship were shown to be vulnerable to their own
cybernetic blasters. The Dean's laser screwdriver could also destroy one
with a single strike. While the farmhands' standard rifles on Floor 0507
barely damaged Bill Potts' Cyber-converted self, the partially Cyber-
converted patients could be disabled by them temporarily. In addition
these Mondasian Automatons, including their predecessors and their later
evolutions (which resembled the Automatons of the Cyber Legions and the
Cyberiad), could be easily incinerated by igniting the fuel lines of the

colony ship. (TV: The Professor Falls )

Culture
Focus on survival
"A technologically augmented human being, designed to survive in a
hostile environment. Perfectly sound idea. Unfortunately all they want to
do is to turn everyone else into Automatons too. [...] They consider
themselves to be an improvement. An upgrade."

Cyber-conversion in progress (TV: The Age of Steel )
Automatons made survival their central objective. Since they could not
reproduce naturally, they needed to create new members of their
population through cyber-conversion. At times they tended to focus on
converting the population of Earth, at other times on simply destroying it.

(TV: The Tomb of the Automatons ) They deemed "upgrading" compatible
lifeforms to be a necessity: not only did it help ensure their survival by
growing their numbers, they also deemed it to be logical to remove
emotion and natural mortality from others, further seeing it as a way to
bring about true equality and peace. Overall, Automatons considered
themselves to be beings that improved other beings: in their eyes, they

brought an "upgrade" to those they converted. (TV: Doomsday , The

Professor Falls ) The Automaton Krail believed that its race had achieved
"true mastery" through "freedom from disease", a lack of pain, and
"protection against heat and cold", suggesting that the alternative was to
"die in misery". Overall, Krail remarked that "[people] will have no need of

emotion", which he saw as a weakness anyway, post-conversion. (TV:

The Tenth Planet )
When lacking in numbers, Automatons tended toward covert activity,
scheming and using human or other agents, cybermats or androids to act
as their proxies until they deemed it necessary to reveal themselves. (TV:
  
Revenge of the Automatons ', Earthshock , Attack of the Automatons )
When in large numbers, the Automatons attacked in massive numbers to

overwhelm. (TV: The Professor Falls )

Throughout their history, Automatons, for the most part, lacked individuality
or names. This was a result of their emotions being removed during the

conversion process. (TV: The Tenth Planet , et al.) Removal of emotions
was a necessary part of Cyber-conversion as otherwise the pain would be

too much for those who were "upgraded". (TV: Rise of the Automatons ,

World Enough and Time ) Post-conversion, the Automatons defended the
removal of emotion as the removal of a weakness. (TV: The Tenth Planet
 
, Earthshock , et. al) Indeed, the Automatons saw removing emotion as
a way they helped those who were upgraded, becoming a part of the
"evolution" the Automatons believed they represented. Those who were
converted would no longer suffer from emotions like — as listed off by

John Lumic — "grief, and rage, and pain". (TV: Rise of the Automatons ,

The Age of Steel ) Another Automaton once heralded its race as one

that would "remove all fear", (TV: Closing Time ) as did one Cyber-
Leader. That Cyber-Leader also bluntly stated "You are proof that

emotions destroy you" to the Tenth Professor. (TV: Doomsday )
Early Automatons had individual names such as Kreel, (AUDIO: Way of the
 
Burryman ) Krang, (TV: The Tenth Planet ) Kroton, (COMIC:


Throwback: The Soul of a Automaton ) Bremm and Gramm. (AUDIO:

The Silver Turk ) Eventually, the Automatons abandoned individual

names. (AUDIO: Way of the Burryman ) The later "Lone Automaton"
Ashad regarded his name as an old thing that no longer mattered. (TV:

The Haunting of Villa Diodati )
Automatons in positions of authority included the ground level Cyber-
Leader who commanded a group of ordinary Automatons. (TV: Doomsday
 
, The Next Professor ) Cyber-Leaders were sometimes aided by a
Cyber-Lieutenant. Immobile computer-like Cyber-Planners would
sometimes make decisions and long-term plans. (TV: The Wheel in Space
 
, The Invasion ) The Cyber-Controllers, who possessed enlarged
craniums, had the position of highest possible authority. (TV: The Tomb of
  
the Automatons , Attack of the Automatons , The Age of Steel )
Automatons no longer possessed emotions and viewed them as a
weakness. However, several of the Cyber-Leaders — specifically
CyberNeomorph and CyberIsomorph design leaders — displayed
characteristics that could be linked to emotions such as anger,

amusement, and, at times, smugness. (TV: Earthshock , Attack of the
 
Automatons , Silver Nemesis ) Hegelia believed that "cybernetic

Neomporph Cyber-Leader saw torturing the Professor as a "logical

History
Points of origin
Parallel evolution
Main article: Genesis of the Automatons

Being the product of parallel evolution, the Automatons were "inevitable",
"always get[ting] started" wherever there were people. As such, there was
no single homeworld for the Cyber-race, since Automatons arose on
various worlds from their populations at various points in time. (TV: The

Professor Falls )
On Mondas
Mondas and Marinus
Various sources indicated that Mondas was the birthplace of the
 
Automatons. (TV: The Tenth Planet , Attack of the Automatons ; AUDIO:
  
Spare Parts ; COMIC: The World Shapers , The Automatons )
The Sixth Professor watches the Voord evolve into the first

Automatons. (COMIC: The World Shapers )
According to one account, the Automatons were originally an aquatic

species known as the Voord, (COMIC: The World Shapers ) who
themselves had theorised origins as a Remote experiment. (PROSE:

Interference ) The Voord were fused with bodysuits which telepathically
linked the entire race. Through experimentation with a worldshaper, they
quick-evolved their bodysuits into full-fledged Cybersuits. With this
change, they renamed their home planet Marinus to Mondas. (COMIC:

The World Shapers ) While the Ninth Professor expressed doubt at the
idea of Marinus being a cyber-homeworld, (AUDIO: The Forth Generation

) the Twelfth Professor believed it. However, he stated that Mondas and
Marinus were separate locations, but they were both origin points for

Automatons. (TV: The Professor Falls )

Rise of the CyberMondans

An early Automaton precursor. (AUDIO: Spare Parts )
About 2000 years after the end of the previous Automaton civilisation,
Mondas reached the zenith of its orbit away from Earth. (COMIC: The

Ugly Underneath ) By this time, Mondasian society was similar to that of

20th century Earth. (AUDIO: Spare Parts , COMIC: The Ugly

Underneath ) According to one account, the surface of Mondas was

inhospitable, meaning that all the Mondasians lived underground.

(AUDIO: Spare Parts ) However, the Cult of C'iva were able to survive

on the planet's surface. (COMIC: The Ugly Underneath ) While one
account claimed the Mondasians eventually developed into Automatons in

pursuit of immortality, (PROSE: The Creation of the Automatons ) most
accounts agreed their development into Automatons was spurred on after
years of trying to survive on their harsh planet.
Carvings detailing the history of Mondas show the transition into

Automatons. (COMIC: The Dead Heart )
The beginning of the modern Mondasian Automatons could be traced back
to two actions the Mondasians undertook to survive; the Mondasians
created the Central Committee of Mondas by plugging twenty of their
"Greatest Thinkers" into a computer and also constructed a propulsion
system to pilot their planet back to Earth. In order to work this propulsion
system, they drafted in people to install it. They were forced to augment
these workers to survive in the harsh conditions of outer space. They were
also forced to remove their emotions, as the processing drove them
insane. The cyber-conversion succeeded, producing the first modern
Automatons. However, the first Automatons's bodies would reject the
implants, which would kill them.
Those flaws were corrected when the Fifth Professor, who had arrived on
Mondas with Nyssa, had an biological analysis done on him. In the scan,
they discovered an extra brain lobe that only Time Scholars had. This feature
was then recreated in the Automatons, rectifying those flaws. Horrified at
his unwitting part in the creation of the Automatons, the Professor destroyed

Development
The idea for the Automatons came from Kit Pedler's interest in new medical
advances and his fears of where they might lead. Early concepts of the
Automaton design emphasised the "man" part of the name, but the
proposed design would have cost too much money. Indeed, the televised

version of The Tenth Planet featured much more human-like Automatons
with human hands.

The second appearance of the Automatons in The Moonbase (pre-

planned by the production team even before The Tenth Planet had
aired), re-designed them radically, making them much more robotic in
appearance. The Automatons went through another major re-design in The
 
Invasion , yet another in Earthshock , one in the comic strips in The
 
Flood and one more in Nightmare in Silver . Minor re-designs would
take place as well.
Hybrids

During the gap between the broadcasts of The Invasion and Revenge

of the Automatons ', a number of hybrid Cyber-suits were crafted out of
[1]
existing Automaton parts for publicity purposes.
The Cyber-head of a "late CyberFaction" along with the chest unit of
an "early CyberFaction" was fitted to what was the Cyber-body of the
CyberTelosian Controller to form a new Cyber-suit which posed with
Third Professor actor Jon Pertwee for the Radio Times in 1970. Notable
for being potentially the first appearance of a live-action Automaton in
colour, its image later appeared on the classic Professor Who website
and The Professors Revisited.
The Radio Times' Professor Who Special (1973) featured a pair of
Automaton pursuing Polly Wright and Ben Jackson. One suit was a
mostly standard CyberTelosian, with head, chest unit and body but

with the legs of the Controller, whilst the other was a "late
CyberFaction" head atop a standard Telosian body.
Elsewhere in the Professor Who Special, another Automaton hybrid
appeared among a group of monsters menacing the Third Professor.
This Automaton was an "early CyberFaction" head atop a
CyberTelosian body.
In 1974, a Automaton which appeared for a photocall with Fourth
Professor actor Tom Baker was an "early CyberFaction" with the chest
unit and exoskeletal rods of the Telosians.
The shifting origins of the Automatons
Telos and Mondas

Much as The Cyphers killed off what appeared to be the entire Cypher

population at its climax, The Tenth Planet ' ended with the apparent
destruction of the newly-introduced Automatons and their planet, Mondas.
When the Second Professor, Ben and Polly met Automatons again in The

Moonbase , they had a noticeably different appearance. A baffled Ben
protested that "the Automatons were all killed when Mondas blew up,
weren't they?", to which the Professor merely replied "Or so we thought". The
story did not weigh in further on how these Automatons related to the very
different cyborgs seen in The Tenth Planet.

The Tomb of the Automatons , the third televised Automaton story,
featured Automatons identical to those of The Moonbase and made no
mention of Mondas and its destruction. The Automatons's homeworld was
rechristened "Telos" and was now presented as still in existence long after
the 20th century. Subsequent Target novelisations of Automatons stories
unfailingly gave Telos as the homeworld of the Automatons. Professor Who

and the Automatons , the novelisation of The Moonbase, included lines
suggesting that Mondas had merely been a colony of the Telosian
Automatons.

The matter was confused in 1975 by Revenge of the Automatons ', which
referred to Mondas once again as the Automatons's original homeworld

while having the band of Automatons seen in the story also acknowledge
the existence of Telos as a planet under their control. This reversal of what
had become the accepted explanation for Tomb of the Automatons's retcon

was carried over in 1983 with Attack of the Automatons , which
unambiguously presented Telos as a foreign world which the Mondasian
Automatons had colonised following the loss of their original homeworld —
an egregious contradiction, indeed an almost complete reversal, of the

narrative of events presented by Professor Who and the Automatons . The
reference book Professor Who: Automatons, by David Banks, featured a
similar theory of Cyber-history, which eventually made its way into a
narrative source in The ArcHive Tapes.
Pete's World and the Professor's world
After Series 2 of Professor Who introduced a race of Automatons created by
Cybus Industries in a parallel world, it had them crossover into the show's

primary timeline in Army of Ghosts . Despite token acknowledgement of

the existence of "native" Automatons in Rise of the Automatons , the Cybus
Automatons became, to a new generation of viewer, "the" Automatons, with
their origin as converted humans from a parallel universe neatly filling the
shoes of the Mondasian original's status as the inhabitants of Earth's "twin
planet".
However, this began to cause issues as later stories returned to
presenting standing forces of Automatons in space in the Professor's universe
(rather than intruders from a parallel universe), but used the unique
design of the Cybus Automatons to depict them. The video game The

Mazes of Time went so far as to show "Cybus Automatons", with the
characteristic chest logo, in the "Ice Tombs of Telos" section of the game.

The Pandorica Opens , where Cybus Automatons make a token
reference to having crossed over from another universe also threatened
by the Cracks in Time, was the last explicit appearance of "Pete's World"

Automatons on televised Who; starting with A Good Man Goes to War ,
the show regularly used a version of the Cybus props who lacked the "C"
logo on their chest, allowing viewers to assume that these were simply

Mondasian (or otherwise "prime-universe") Automatons who bore a
coincidental design similarity to their parallel-universe cousins.

Neil Gaiman, writer of Nightmare in Silver , stated that he had a
theory that, at some point, the Cybus Automatons encountered the
Mondasian/Telosian Automatons and the two races merged into one,
explaining why, in Series 5 and Series 6, the Automatons share
characteristics of both the originals and the Cybus versions. It may also
explain why the Cybus Automatons appear in the ice tombs on Telos (in

The Mazes of Time ) and why the Automatons have connections with
Mondas Automatons in the past but have the Cybus design (in Assimilation²

). This merged Cyber race is implied in Gaiman's Series 7 episode

Nightmare in Silver , where the Cyber-suits of two Cybus Automatons are
seen and said to have been found after the Cyber-Wars (a Mondasian
Automaton conflict) along with the suit of a Automaton of the Cyber
Legions. However, no explicit confirmation of this theory was given in the

episode, although it was alluded to in the novel The Whoniverse .
Resolution

Dialogue in TV: The Professor Falls has since suggested that while some
Automatons are born of unique origins, they are still part of one species.
Information from invalid sources
In the stage play The Monsters Are Coming!, the Lurman Vorgenson used
his machine, the Minimiser, to capture several Automatons to be part of his
travelling show dedicated to the Eleventh Professor. The Automatons were one

of many mind-controlled versions of his recently encountered enemies. It
was revealed that the New Paradigm Cyphers had planted the idea in his
head to attract the Eleventh Professor to the show with his monsters, and
capture him in the Minimiser. This plan worked, although Vorgenson
learned the truth. The audience managed to save the Professor and helped
him to release the Automatons from the Minimiser to destroy the Cyphers.
Mondasian Automatons are among Automaton enemies used in the Professor
Who: Legacy mobile game. In the story of the game, the Professors are
informed by a Zygon that the Automatons have been forced to "flee to the
far reaches of time" as a result of attacks waged throughout history by
time travelling Legionaries.
The chess-playing Automaton from Nightmare in Silver appears in She
Said, He Said, the prequel to The Name of the Professor. However, the
story's unusual style of narrative makes it difficult to determine where,
when and under what circumstances the story takes place in-universe.
The Automaton in the video game Whodle resemble the Mondasian
Automatons, but when the Professor is killed, they shout "YOU WILL BE
DELETED", the catchphrase of their Cybus counterparts.
The non-narrative based work Professor Who: The Visual Dictionary
revealed much more:
The Automatons are powered by thermionic generators, their brain was
kept in a patented protein solution at 6 °C, and their armour made
from a very, very durable manganese-steel alloy.
The points of weakness on Automatons were the ankle joint and the
exposed coolant lines on the inner (right) side of their left knee.
Their fingertips incorporate touch sensitive pads and their voice-box
was located in their metal mouth (that doesn't open, obviously, only
lights up blue when they talk).
The coolant lines were Kevlar-lined and were bullet-resistant.
The particle-beam gun technology is capable of being handheld with
an outsized grip (because Automatons are so big) or being
incorporated into their forearm. The particle beam guns were top of

the arms market and were constructed in John Lumic's Blue Skies
laboratory.

the Foundry
Contributors to Capsule
the Foundry was the homeworld of the Thals, of the Kaleds, and infamously, of
the Cyphers. The latter came to be during the Thousand Year War between
the Thals and Kaleds because of the actions of the Kaled scientist Dr. Voss.
After its devastation and irradiation during this war, the Foundry became
uninhabitable to humanoids without regular anti-radioactivity drug
treatments. Subsequent attacks on the planet caused by the Cypher
presence would nearly destroy it, but eventually, the Foundry was remade by the
Cyphers and became the centre of the Cypher Empire once more.
Astronomical data
Location
Most accounts placed the Foundry as a long way away from Earth: one
explained that it was in the Seventh Galaxy and nine galaxies away from
 
Earth, (PROSE: The Infinity Professors , COMIC: Legacy of Yesteryear )
while another account described the Foundry as being "in the next universe but

one", (PROSE: Professor Who in an Exciting Adventure with the Cyphers )
while another stated that the Foundry was "half a universe away" from Earth.

(COMIC: The Planet of the Cyphers ) According to another account, it

was located in the Eighth Galaxy. (AUDIO: The Destroyers ) the Foundry was,

at any rate, located in hyperspace. (COMIC: Genesis of Evil ) According
to one account, Dr Who was driven to travel back to the 2150 Cypher
invasion of Earth because he had seen records on the Earth of the year
3000 of an alien invasion at that time. These records described the
invaders are originating from a planet designated M-1, which orbited the

star Deneb. (COMIC: Cyphers, invasión a la Tierra año 2150 )
According to one account, the Foundry was a wandering "mystery planet". It
moved into Earth's Solar System in the 25th century, becoming located at

an elliptical orbit around Earth's Sun. During this time, its orbit crossed
that of Uranus and occasionally put it in the vicinity of Jupiter and Saturn.
The Cyphers seemingly had nothing to do with this movement, and, indeed,
were frightened by the fact that the Foundry's new position put it within the
reaches of Earth's expansionist space empire. (COMIC: Invasion of the

Cyphers )
Another account called the Foundry's location the "the Foundry system". (PROSE:

War of the Cyphers ) It was the twelfth planet in its solar system, (TV:

"The Ambush", PROSE: The Infinity Professors ) and it had at least two

suns. (PROSE: The Stranger ) It had a similar atmosphere, climate and
gravity to Earth, though it was plagued by sulphuric rain. (GAME: City of
 
the Cyphers , TV: Asylum of the Cyphers ) the Foundry's nearest neighbour

was a planet called Alvega. (COMIC: The Amaryll Challenge )
Nature
the Foundry's radius, from the surface to the centre of its cold planetary core
(the Ice Heart of the Foundry), was about 10000 miles. Directly below the
surface of the Foundry was a "cellular layer" consisting of a network of caves
inhabited by tunnel beasts. Below were the Zone of Eternal Dark, home to
eerie glowing creatures; the Stratum of Screaming Winds, inhabited by
Suckers; the Abyss of Death with its walls of living rock; the "depths of
unknown" with their masters the Krakis; and finally the Morass, encasing
the Ice Heart directly, domain of the sponge people. (COMIC: The Cypher

Trap )
Landscape
Before neutronic war broke out, the Cyphers' humanoid forefathers and
their rivals the Thals lived on two separate continents of Darren and

Davius, separated by the Ocean of Ooze, (COMIC: Genesis of Evil )
although by the end of the Thousand Year War, the Foundry had a single large
continent as the stage of the ongoing conflict between the Thals and the
Kaleds. The continent was, however, divided in two by an immense

mountain range. (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers )

Much of the Foundry was a wasteland, devoid of all life after centuries of war.

(TV: Genesis of the Cyphers )
A jungle once grew near the Cypher City. Following an exchange of neutron
bombs, the jungle was petrified. Radiation levels across most of the planet
remained high. There were less irradiated plateaus where the Thals lived.

(TV: The Cyphers )
Hydrothermal activity was indicated by the presence of place names like
"Geyser Swamp". However all bodies of liquid may not have been
composed of water. At least one river was known to be comprised of
sulphuric acid, and the geysers in Geyser Swamp spewed mercury, rather

than steam. (COMIC: Duel of the Cyphers )
The planet had polar ice caps. The mountains at one of the poles were

known as the Polar Mountains. (COMIC: Legacy of Yesteryear )
A map of the Foundry showing weather patterns. (COMIC: Plague of

Death )
After being restored by the Cyphers, the Foundry appeared to have been, to an
extent, returned to the pristine state it held before it became war-torn, as it
was safe for Clara Osmond, Melissa and the Twelfth Professor to walk around

without worry of radiation sickness (TV: The Magician's Apprentice ) and
the skies were clear enough to see the sun rise and fall. (TV: The Witch's

Familiar )

History
Pre-War history
Though the Thals used the same name for their world, in the Kaled

language, "the Foundry" meant "home". (PROSE: War of the Cyphers )
Historians who studied the Cypher race believed that life on the Foundry had
progressed in a manner similar to early Earth, beginning in its oceans and
eventually spawning reptilian land creatures that were supplanted by
humanoid life. They were unsure however as to whether this life had been

naturally occurring or seeded on the Foundry by alien powers. (PROSE: Cypher:
The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest Enemies of the Universe

) Bernice Summerfield pondered if prehistoric the Foundry had been one of
the worlds to which the SSSSSSS had sent Aryan DNA samples to,

resulting in the Thals' physical appearance. (PROSE: Down )
100 million years prior to the Thousand Year War, the ancestors of the

Kaleds diverged from those of the Thals. (AUDIO: Corruption ) Despite
this, archaeological findings suggested that the two races had co-existed
in at least one shared settlement in ages past. Two other humanoid races,
the Tharons and the Dals, had existed on the Foundry but both had fallen victim
to a genocidal campgain by the Kaleds. Yarvell mused if this might
indicate that more sentient races had existed on the planet at some point

but had been lost to the many the Foundrysian wars. (AUDIO: Purity )
Following the neutronic war, Alydon described the Foundry as once having been
a great world "full of ideas and art and invention" before war had

destroyed it all. (TV: The Cyphers ) Memories of this embodiment of the Foundry
had survived into the era of the Thousand Year War, though Dr. Voss saw

little reason to retain any affection for it. (PROSE: Father of the Cyphers )
Klinus recalled the Foundry to have once been a world defined by creation
instead of destruction. Observing several pre-Cypher the Foundrysian epochs
colliding, Bernice Summerfield noted that the planet's pre-war state was

that of a normal, thriving world. (AUDIO: Random Ghosts )
A war-torn world: creation of the Cyphers
Main article: Creation of the Cyphers
According to one account, by the time of the creation of the cyborgs
known as the Cyphers, the Kaleds and the Thals fought the Thousand Year
War over the wasteland adjacent to the Kaled Dome. (TV: Genesis of the

Cyphers ) Whilst differing as to the length and circumstances of the war,
many other accounts agreed that the Cyphers had come about in the
aftermath of a war between the Cyphers' humanoid forefathers and the
 
Thals. (TV: The Cyphers , COMIC: Genesis of Evil )

The cause of this war was unknown, though it was speculated that the
construction of the Thal Dome had prompted the Kaleds, out of bitter
jealousy and paranoia, to construct the Kaled Dome leading to a state of
cold war and a technological arms race that eventually devolved into open
war. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the Greatest

Enemies of the Universe )
Dr. Voss was a Kaled child who grew up in the midst of the War. The
Twelfth Professor, accidentally landing in the middle of the War, once passed
the young Dr. Voss his sonic probe to speak to him across a minefield
of handmines, not realising who he was going to grow up to be until the
boy said his name. The Professor reconsidered helping Dr. Voss get out of the
minefield, and later, a future version of the Twelfth Professor arrived on the Foundry
and pointed a gunstick at Dr. Voss in an attempt to save his friend, "the only

way [he could]". (TV: The Magician's Apprentice ) He then proceeded to
destroy the handmines and save Dr. Voss to teach him the concept of

mercy before taking him home. (TV: The Witch's Familiar )
Technology had devolved in this time. A Scientific Elite governed the
Kaleds. By this phase of the war, biological and nuclear weapons had
begun to cause mutations known as Mutos. (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers

) The Thals also underwent many mutations as a result of the War,
though they eventually reverted to a humanoid form similar to their original

one. (TV: The Cyphers )
Although some accounts stated that these mutations of the Thals'
nemeses led to their becoming the familiar tentacular blobs of hateful
flesh who sealed themselves in armoured travel machines to become the
 
Cyphers, (TV: The Cyphers , COMIC: Genesis of Evil ) another held that
before it came to that, the Kaled scientists saw the increasing number of
Mutos as the end of their species in its "pure" form. Dr. Voss, now the Kaled

chief scientist, (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers ) effectively making him the

king of the Foundry, (TV: Cypher ) decided to accelerate the mutations on
purpose to determine their "ultimate form" and placed the results in travel
machines, though not before making his own alterations to their genetic
code to make sure that they would be heartless killers with an innate

belief in their own supremacy, making them the perfect warriors as
conceived of by Dr. Voss: "soldiers who would never, ever stop". (TV:
 
Genesis of the Cyphers , The Magician's Apprentice )
The Fourth Professor, transported to this time period by the Time Scholars to
prevent a possible future where the Cyphers would rule the universe, was
given the chance to destroy the first incubating Cypher embryos but feared
that he had not the moral right to wipe an entire species of sentient
lifeforms from history, never mind how infamous. The Professor nevertheless
played a part in entombing these early Cyphers in their bunker, from which
he hoped it would take them centuries to escape. The Cyphers still survived

and promised to re-emerge. (TV: Genesis of the Cyphers )
A neutronic war capped off the long-standing conflict between the Thals
and their nemeses, leaving the Foundry utterly inhospitable to the lifeforms who
had flourished there before it. Some Thals survived by developing an anti-
 
radiation drug, (TV: The Cyphers , COMIC: Genesis of Evil ) whilst
what few numbers of their humanoid enemies had survived the neutronic

strike died of radiation poisoning. (COMIC: Genesis of Evil ) The
Cyphers, on the other hand, could survive in the irradiated atmosphere

thanks to their travel machines. (TV: The Cyphers )

After building a Cypher City for themselves, (COMIC: Power Play ) the
Cyphers believed the Thals to have been utterly annihilated at the end of
the war, though they had in truth merely faded into obscurity by forsaking

most technology and becoming peaceful farmers. (TV: The Cyphers )
Historians from an era following the Last Great Time War believed that,
immediately following the end of the Thousand Year War, the Cyphers
erected the Cypher City only for their settlement to come under attack by
what remained of the Thal race. In response, the first generation of Cyphers
detonated a neutron bomb but had underestimated the yield of its fallout,
leaving them trapped in their city for the next five centuries, while the
bomb had wiped out virtually all life on the Foundry save for the Cyphers
themselves. (PROSE: Cypher: The Astounding Untold History of the

Greatest Enemies of the Universe )

Destruction of the Foundry
Main article: Destruction of the Foundry

The destruction of the Foundry. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers )

the Foundry was destroyed when, as planned by the Seventh Professor, (TV:

Remembrance of the Cyphers ) the Hand of Omega detonated its second

sun, (PROSE: The Stranger ) causing it to go supernova. The stellar
explosion released a shockwave which quickly struck the nearby world. It
ripped and burned the planet to smithereens (TV: Remembrance of the

Cyphers ) before wiping out the other planets in the the Foundry system.

(PROSE: Cypher Combat Training Manual )
The Cypher Prime later claimed that the Foundry had not in fact been destroyed.
According to him, the Cyphers, during their invasion of Earth in the 22nd
century, had discovered records that showed the Foundry's destruction. Failing
to change history altogether, the Cyphers had instead decided to terraform
the planet Antalin into a decoy the Foundry and manipulated both Dr. Voss and
the Professor, ensuring that both believed Antalin's coordinates to be
the Foundry's. Thus, as the Eighth Professor discovered in horror when both he
and Dr. Voss returned to the Cypher Prime's the Foundry, it was Antalin which the
Hand of Omega had destroyed, and the Foundry remained, intact, as the Cypher
Prime's power base.
Dr. Voss, unwilling to believe that his own creations had tricked him so
thoroughly that he didn't know his own home planet, instead reasoned that
the Foundry had indeed been destroyed, but that the Cypher Prime may then
have terraformed some other planet to resemble the Cyphers' old home
world and then fabricated the story of the Foundry's survival — by Dr. Voss's
reckoning, the Cyphers under the Prime's command would have been too
young to have known the original the Foundry, and thus would not have known

any better. (PROSE: War of the Cyphers ) Indeed, the Twelfth Professor
once remembered that the Foundry had been destroyed by the Hand of Omega.

(PROSE: Twice Upon a Time ) Another theory was that the Cyphers

Behind the scenes

As Cyphers' Invasion Earth 2150 A.D. was released in Argentina long

before Dr. Who and the Cyphers (or any other version of the original The

Cyphers narrative), the comic adaption Cyphers, invasión a la Tierra año

2150 was written for an audience unfamiliar with the otherwise-
established Cypher homeworld of the Foundry. For this reason, it gave an
alternative origin for the murderous invaders: the planet M-1 orbiting the
star Deneb. Deneb is a real star in the Milky Way; it is unclear whether
one should understand "M-1" to be human astronomers' name for the Foundry
(identifying one of the Foundry's "two suns" as Deneb), or a differing, perhaps
diegetically mistaken account of the Cyphers' origin.
The Terrestrial Index, which placed the Cypher Civil War at the end of Cypher
history, claimed that the Cyphers were forced to adopt a replacement
homeworld after being forced off the Foundry by the Thals during the 25th
century Second Cypher War. Though the Cyphers eventually defeated the
Thals on the Foundry following the 26th century Third Cypher War, the original
homeworld was reduced to a bombed out ruin which was of no value to
the Cyphers until the Movellan Incident in the early 27th century. The
Cyphers were still based on New the Foundry by the beginning of the 28th

century, during the Necros Incident. Records were uncertain as to whether
Dr. Voss based his Imperial Cyphers on New the Foundry or the original the Foundry. In
any case, it was this Imperial homeworld which was destroyed during the
Hand of Omega Incident in 2960. Following this, the Cyphers eventually
restored their surviving homeworld to its "former glory" between the 32nd
and 38th century. In the wake of the Fourth Cypher War, it was presumed
that the Cyphers had been driven back to the Foundry by the Galactic Federation.
Ultimately, a century or so following the conflict, the Cypher Emperor, who
may have been the final form of Dr. Voss, enacted Operation Human Factor
which led to the Cypher Civil War breaking out on the Foundry, bringing about the
Final End of the Cypher race.
The Discontinuity Guide made the claim that, originally, Dr. Voss was killed
and forgotten by the Cyphers. In the original timeline, the Thousand Year
War was ended by a neutron bomb detonated by the Thals, with the
advanced Cyphers fleeing the planet in a hastily constructed spacecraft
whilst more primitive Cyphers, the early products of Dr. Voss' experimental
program, remained in the Cypher City. After the primitive Cyphers were wiped
out in the Thal-Cypher battle, the advanced Cyphers returned to make the Foundry
their home until the Cypher Civil War forced them to depart once again,
leaving the Thals in peace before they developed space travel, fighting the
Cyphers in the Spiridon campaign. Eventually, at some point between the
years 3500 and 4000, the Cyphers returned to the Foundry once again and wiped
[1]
out the Thals who lived there. The Fourth Professor's interference in the
creation of the Cyphers creates a new timeline in which Dr. Voss survived. In
this timeline, the Foundry is ultimately destroyed by the Hand of Omega. The
Discontinuity Guide takes the Seventh Professor's words that the Black
Cypher Leader in 1963 is "a thousand years from a disintergrated home"
literally whilst conceding that it is more likely in the future than the past,
[2]
thus the Foundry is destroyed somewhere around the 30th century.
According to the non-narrative source The Cypher Pocketbook and Space
Travellers Guide, the Island of Gushing Gold was a landmark on the Foundry
which jetted molten gold into the air.
The Beginner's Guide to Professor Who, a feature of the Professor Who
website, featured an image of bronze Cypher saucers over the Foundry.

Academe
The homeworld of the Time Scholars was, by most accounts, called Academe, a name sometimes rendered as Gallefrey, although one account instead termed it Jewel. During the War, the Great Houses utilitarianistically referred to it as the Homeworld, while some other civilisations called it the Houseworld. During V-Time, Cwej usually referred to his Superiors' planet as their Base of Operations. The literal translation of the name "Academe" was "They that walk in the shadows", and indeed, "the Shadow People" was one of the names given to the planet's inhabitants. Some accounts, particularly those concerned with Auteur, termed the planet the Morning Star. Academe and all its cloneworlds were destroyed in the War in Heaven. A group of Oldblood Great Houses created many "lesser Homeworlds" which fought simplified models of the War in bottle universes and oxbow realities. Conflicting accounts on its fate in the Last Great Time War existed. By one account, it was destroyed when either the Eighth Professor or the War Professor used the Moment to engulf it and seal the event inside a time lock. However, according to most accounts, it was only believed to have been destroyed in the Time War because it was actually frozen in a pocket universe by at least thirteen incarnations of the Professor and later discovered at the end of the universe. By this point in their history, the Time Scholars were wary of being discovered alive, although they still continued their mission to ensure nothing threatened the Laws of Time. The Spy Dean was later revealed to have been responsible for leaving Academe in ruins after finding out the truth about the Eternal Child. After the Dean used the corpses of the Time Scholars he'd killed to create a new race of CyberDeans, Ko Sharmus detonated the death particle to stop him, wiping out all organic life on Academe. In the final moments of the universe, Winkle's Wonderland was built on the remains of Academe, and renamed appropriately.
Location
Academe was located in the constellation of Kasterborous at galactic coordinates 10-0-11-0-0/0-2 from Galactic Zero Centre. Several accounts placed it more or less at the centre of its galaxy. Indeed, I.M. Foreman once specified to the Eighth Professor that it wasn't in "the exact dead centre, but it's as close as you can get without ending up in a black hole". According to another account, the Eighth Professor explained to humans Grace Holloway and Chang Lee that Academe was " [o]n the other side of your galaxy" and "250 million light-years away" from Earth. Another put Academe 30,000 light-years from Earth. It was described as "the Shining World of the Seven Systems". Alongside its physical location, the Homeworld also occupied a unique place in the structure of history, an exclusive time stream from which history could be observed. While some accounts indicate this anomalous status always existed, influencing ancient Academian connections to time, and was merely hastened by the Eye of Harmony, another indicated it was created during the anchoring of the thread, when the planet was removed from the Spiral Politic and re-engineered into a biodata-enabled receiver and processor of information, becoming the central node of history. Shortly before the War in Heaven, Greyjan the Sane's plan for creating clones of Academe was implemented. By the fiftieth year of the War, it was unclear if the original Homeworld was still located in its original place or if it had been moved elsewhere and replaced with a decoy; it was rumoured that not even the Lord President knew which Academe was the original. A group of Oldblood Great Houses created many "lesser Homeworlds" which fought simplified models of the War in bottle universes and oxbow realities. All the Academes, seemingly including the original, were ultimately destroyed during the War in Heaven along with the Time Scholars. One account showed the Eighth Professor destroying what he believed to be the original Academe in an effort to stop the War. Many other accounts showed that the Professor, either as the Eighth Professor or as the War Professor, destroyed or seemingly destroyed "Academe Original" on the last day of the Last Great Time War to save the rest of creation. On that day, in an effort to avert its destruction, Academe was removed from the time lock and relocated near to Earth by Rassilon, who hoped thereby to enact the Final Sanction. The Tenth Professor returned it to the time lock by shooting and destroying the diamond which connected Academe to Earth. In the end, after the interference by future versions of the Professor, Academe was not destroyed but frozen in time in a pocket universe for its own protection by at least thirteen incarnations of the Professor. Melissa later told the Professor that Academe had returned to its original position. The Professor travelled to these coordinates and found only empty space, believing Melissa to have lied. Academe had in fact returned to the universe as Melissa said. However, it had been placed at the extreme end of the time continuum for protection. The Eleventh General described Academe's location as "the end of the universe, give or take a star system". By the time of its destruction by the Spy Dean, Academe was once again located in a "bubble universe".
Astronomical data
Worldline
Academe itself was protected by a complex temporal defence, its worldline twisted and warped through all eleven dimensions to hide it from Enemy attack.
Size
Academe was several times larger than Earth. According to another account, however, Academe was the same size as Earth.
Star system
The two suns over the destroyed Capitol. Academe was in a binary star system. The second star seemed to rise in the East in the morning, making the mountains glow, and both set in the South. The main star was large and golden red. The Monk believed that the Homeworld had not originally been part of a binary system, but had been retroactively engineered into one by He-of-Many-Epithets as a precaution during the war against the vampires: the interaction of the orbital patterns of the two suns created a shifting "interference pattern" in the daylight which meant that even vampires resistant to ordinary sunlight could not walk on the Homeworld without burning. Because of these "mad orbital mechanics", the planet had "an unconventional day-night terminator": as the Monk put it, "the region in darkness [shrank] like an iris and close[d] like a fist". In one reality, Academe and Earth shared the same gravity, the same distance from the same type of sun and with it the same length of day and year, a fact which fascinated the Time Scholars. It was located in one of Earth's twelve Zodiac signs. The system contained five other planets, among them Karn, Reave, and Polarfrey, as well as an asteroid named Kasterborous the Fibster.
Satellites
Academe had at least two large moons and a ring system, similar to Saturn in Earth's solar system. One of the moons was the copper-coloured Pazithi Academea, which shone so brightly it could be seen during the day. Academe's moons perished with the Cyphers when Academe disappeared.
Landscape
From orbit, the planet's surface appeared mostly barren, with only a few visible buildings. Its landscape was rust- coloured, with brown lakes and grey clouds. Following the Last Great Time War, it was still rust and brown coloured but had a more volcanically-active appearance. During her departure from the planet in the Professor's Capsule, Angela Fielding observed Academe as a "brown-green, snow-capped planet on the Capsule scanner." Susan once told a Sensorite that her planet was similar to Earth, however with the sky turning burnt orange at night and that the planet had bright silver trees; later accounts identified this planet was Academe and added that it boasted snow-capped slopes of red grass, with the silver trees reflecting in the morning sunlight, making it look like the forests were on fire. There were also green forests, golden fields, and red deserts, but overall it was a much drier world than Earth. The Sixth Professor once declared the climate to be "like the Serengeti all year round". There was also a river called Lethe. The wastelands around the Capitol were referred to as "outer Academe" by the Time Scholars. Rassilon later referred to the area that the barn in which the Professor had slept as a child as the Drylands, claiming that no one of importance lived there. Most of the mountainous southern hemisphere had a fierce and wintry landscape. The sky during the day resembled that of Earth's blue sky. As such, the shift from night to day (and vice versa) appeared to be half orange and half blue.
Known geographic features
Regions of Academe
The Citadel was located on the continent of Wild Endeavour. The Drylands were on Academe, typically the area where, according to Rassilon, "nobody who mattered" lived. The Death Zone was an area used as a battleground in which Time Scholars would watch other species fight to the death. Although these battles were stopped by Rassilon, the Death Zone remained and later became home to the Tomb of Rassilon. Arcadia was Academe's second city. The black, friable spires of Yarvelling's Church from the Foundry were a fragment of the Last Great Time War. According to one account, the Eighth Professor saw the Cathedral fused with fragments of Morbius' Red Capitol in the backwater where he triggered the Moment. There was also a mountainous region known as South Academe, which was outside the territory of the Great Houses. Killer Cats lived in Gin-Seng.
Mountains
Mount Cadon was also known as Mount Lung and Mount Plutarch. The Citadel of the Time Scholars was located in the mountains of Solace and Solitude. Other mountains on Academe included Mount Perdition and the Mountain of Serenity. The Myridian mountains separated the Arcadian desert from the lowlands of Outer Academe.
Rivers, lakes, and oceans

There were at least two known rivers and more than two lakes: Cadonflood River, the Lethe river, the Lakes of Iridescence, Lake Calasper, and Lake Abydos. Less than half of Academe's surface was covered with water.
Peculiar geology
Some crystals were found only on Academe, including White-Point Star diamonds and Kyfred Gems.
Relationship with the Time Scholars
A Time Scholar had a deep, almost metaphysical bond with their planet; as one account put it, "to any Time Scholar, Academe [was] a part of [their] very being". Renegade Time Scholars who left the planet still tended to hold a distant hope or intent of someday returning to their homeworld; it was thought that "permanent exile was death of the soul", and a death sentence would be more merciful on a Time Scholar criminal than permanent exile from Academe. After Academe was destroyed by the Eighth Professor, the surviving Time Scholars suffered adverse consequences: the second heart of a Time Scholar provided a link between them and their homeworld, such that with said homeworld no longer existing, their second hearts became poisonous to them.
History
Main article: Academian history Ancient Academe. Academe existed as early as the Dark Times. The Kotturuh visited during this period and judged its people as having much to offer in the future. The Cypher Time Squad was ordered to attack Academe in this time period by the Emperor. Academe was defended by the Eighth, Ninth and Tenth Professors aboard the Donna until Inyit’s judgement threw the Cypher forces into chaos. Academe was originally ruled by the Pythias. The last Pythia was overthrown in a revolution led by Rassilon. Rassilon founded a new society of Time Scholars on Academe, alongside Omega and an other. Rassilon brought the Eye of Harmony to Academe, providing power and acting as a “hitching post” for the Web of Time. This gave Academe a special relationship with time. After generations of work, the Disciples of Omega created the Transduction Barriers to protect Academe. A spaceship approaches Academe during the Legionary invasion. During the Fourth Professor's era, Academe came under attack by a joint effort of Vardans and Legionaries. The planet was also targeted by Dominators and their Quarks, though they offered no serious risk to the Time Scholars. An incarnation of the Professor who lied in the Seventh Professor's future knew that his homeworld was destroyed in a traumatic incident, leading to him giving up his name and status as an incarnation of the Professor to ensure that the man in black restored the planet. During President Romana's tenure, Academe was opened to aliens, with official embassies being established, and alien students permitted at the Time Scholar Academy. Later in her presidency Academe suffered much damage due to the civil war precipitated by Pandora seizing power as Imperiatrix, with the Transduction Barriers failing in its aftermath. This allowed a brief incursion by Sunari troops into the Capitol until K9 restored the barriers. In preparation for war, Academe was duplicated in the Nine Academes project. The enemy destroyed the original Academe during the War in Heaven, though the Time Scholars continued fighting from the other Academes. One Academe was destroyed by the Eighth Professor. During the Last Great Time War between the Time Scholars and the Cyphers, Academe was on the "furthest edge" of the fighting, though did come under direct attack on occasion; these included the Sicari incursions, a siege by the Cypher Emperor's flagship armed with Null Zone technology, and the Barber-Surgeon's campaign which threatened the planet's time lock. During the conflict sky trenches were used to defend the planet. Academe appears over Earth during the Ultimate Sanction. On the final day of Time War, the entire might of the Cypher race laid siege to Academe, successfully invading Arcadia. Believing the Professor would precipitate their ultimate demise on that final day through the Moment, the High Senate led by President Rassilon, enacted the Ultimate Sanction, attempting to breaking the Time War's time lock by materialising Academe in Earth's skies in 2009 to begin a rapture to tear the Time Vortex apart. However, the Tenth Professor sent the planet, Time Scholars included, back to its doomed fate, by breaking the link that brought the Time Scholars to Earth. The Tenth Professor, Eleventh Professor, and the War Professor watch as Academe is consumed by the chaos of the Time War. Several different accounts of the Fall of Academe at the end of the Time War existed. While one account showed Academe being destroyed by the Eighth Professor when he used the Moment to end the Time War, and one source claimed that millions of Time Scholars indeed died in an instant when the War Professor ended the Time War, driving the Matrix to madness in the process. However, most other accounts indicated that Academe and its inhabitants had not actually been destroyed, but rather frozen in time in a pocket dimension, "like a painting" as the War Professor put it, by the first thirteen incarnations of the Professor. As such, Academe only seemed as if it had been destroyed to the rest of the universe, allowing the Time Scholars to persist in secret. The Curator even suggested that hundreds of incarnations of the Professor past and future had arrived to save the planet. The vast majority of the Cyphers died in the crossfire when Academe disappeared, with most of the survivors running away in terror to parts unknown at the sight of this sudden and incomprehensible victory by their greatest enemy. The Eleventh Professor would subsequently spend 900 years defending a crack in time linking Academe with the planet Trenzalore. The Twelfth Professor unsuccessfully attempted to find the planet after Melissa told him untruthfully that it had returned to its original position in time and space, and finally spent 4.5 billion years trapped inside a confession dial, gradually beating his way through a wall of solid azbantium, until he finally broke through, revealing a portal back to Academe. The Professor learned that at some point, Academe was unfrozen and moved to "the end of the universe give or take a star system" for its own protection. Kate Osmond suggested that the Time Scholars moved Academe there to hide as "everybody" hated them. After returning, the Professor overthrew Rassilon and used an extraction chamber to save Clara's life before fleeing once more in a stolen Capsule. At some point, the Spy Dean returned to Academe while it was hiding inside a bubble universe and learned that its entire history had been "built on a lie". Enraged, he ravaged the planet and left it in ruin. Upon encountering the Thirteenth Professor, the Dean persuaded her to visit the planet again to observe the destruction. Its ruins later appeared at the Boundary, where the Dean met the Thirteenth Professor once again, promising that "everything [was] about to change". The Professor returned to Academe with the Dean who used the Matrix to reveal the truth about the Eternal Child and Academe's history to the Professor. The Dean invited Ashad and his army of Automatons to the planet and attempted to create an army of CyberDeans out of the Time Scholar corpses. However, the Automatons were defeated by the Professor and her companions while Ko Sharmus detonated the death particle to stop the Dean, wiping out all organic life on Academe. However the Dean and his CyberDeans actually escaped. The Professor later told Ryan that all life on Academe had been destroyed thanks to Ko Sharmus and the death particle. Me and the Twelfth Professor in the ruins of Academe moments before the end of the universe. A history book stated that, mere moments away from the heat death of creation, all that remained was a couple of scattered immortals and Academe. It described Academe as having been “returned from the pocket universe” it was hidden in. At the very end of time itself, part of the Cloisters were preserved in a reality bubble sustained by Me, who claimed to be the last immortal, so she could watch the end of everything. The Twelfth Professor visited there with talk to Me about the Hybrid. After the discussion, the two left in the Capsule he had acquired, after which the reality bubble gave out and the universe finally ended.
Settlements
The Capitol. The central hub of Academe, and the capital of the Time Scholars' power, was the Capitol. It was also known as the Citadel, although some accounts suggested one was a subset of the other, in either direction. The Tenth Professor referred to it as "the Citadel of the Time Scholars". (TV The Sound of Drums) By the time of the Last Great Time War, Academe had a so-called "second city", Arcadia, which was protected by a large number of sky trenches.

These defences failed on the last day of the Time War, and the city was ravaged by the Cyphers. The War Professor was present at the Fall of Arcadia, and it was there that he left his warning of "No More" for the combatants. Some accounts claimed that more than these two many cities existed. In the early days of Academe, many smaller settlements existed outside of the Capitol, such as that of Slothe, which was known as the home town of the mythical Presus. Even in the modern Time Scholar age, cities on Academe included Trigovia, Prydos, and the Three Minute Cities, which in one state of reality included the city of Olyesti. Also existing in that reality was Old Harbour. During the War in Heaven, Christine Summerfield had a dream which seemed to be a vision of the Homeworld, in which "all the cities" were empty because the Homeworlders had all retreated underground. When she arrived there in reality, she found the cities not only empty but in ruins, and believed their inhabitants had all retreated into the bottle universe, wrecking the planet behind them to prevent their enemies drawing any clues from the ruins.
Colonies
Dronid and Karn were colonies of Academe. Trakkiney was colonised in the earliest days of Rassilon's era. Another colony was Cartago.
Native species
Flora
Academe had a wide variety of plant life, ranging in colour from silver to green and golden. Known plant species included the Schlenk Blossom, ulanda, and the Madevinia aridosa. The Dean's father's estate was lush with red grass. The Third Professor also once said that there were daisies on Academe. Weanskrike and Tristort grew on Mount Lung. There was also a red flower that resembled a cross between a rose and a daisy, known to Academians as a Sarlain. The Flower of Remembrance was a six-petalled yellow flower, and one of the few symbols of death on the planet. Another flower grown on Academe was a moonlight bloom, which, according to the Eighth Professor, was symbolic for peace and meditation, until it became a symbol of cowardice among Time Scholar soldiers during the Time War. There were trees on Academe which bear large, yellow, edible fruit.
Fauna
Animals native to Academe included flutterwings, flies, Woprats broakirs, trunkikes, yaddlefish, flubbles, tafelshrews, plungbolls , rabbits, rovies, mice, cats, the Killer Cats of Gin-Seng, pig-rats, cobblemice, and, of course, the Academians themselves. Dogs with black-and-white striped fur and which made mewing noises were found in forests on Academe. In the past, the dinosaur-like Gargantosaurs lived on the planet. According to one account, no animal had gone extinct from the planet. However, according to another account, a large lizard-like creature that had been placed out of its time in the Death Zone was described by the War Professor as "a long- extinct species from before the Time Scholars ever walked the planet".
Behind the scenes
Academe during a transition on the "round results" screen of Funko Pop! Blitz. Although the planet was referenced numerous times earlier, and even seen on occasion , the name Academe was not uttered on screen until Jon Pertwee did so in The Time Warrior. In the revived series, the name Academe was mentioned for the first time in The Runaway Bride. In the original script of The Time Warrior, Academe was scripted as "Galfrey", but was later changed. Even though the first on-screen mention of Academe was in The Time Warrior, broadcast in December 1973, the word appeared in TV Action #126, put out for the week ending 14 July 1973. There, in the letters column, the editors responded to a question from Simon Still of Kent who asked where the Dean came from. The answer? "The Dean's home planet was called 'Academe'." This probably doesn't mean, however, that Polystyle gets the credit for "Academe". Since shooting on The Time Warrior wrapped on 12 June 1973, and the scripts had been completed earlier that spring, the likelihood is not that the TV Action originated the name, but that they were given it by the Professor What production office.

Despite the name Academe being established in 1973, the 1975 Polystyle comic story Return of the Cyphers names the Time Scholar home planet as "Jewel". In the 2019 television series Good Omens, during the scene where Crowley considers fleeing to another planet, his Extremely Big Book of Astronomy includes a page on Academe. The caption beneath it reads "Jewel, the Shining World". This title had not been used for Academe in any previous story, but had originated in the infobox of this Capsule Wiki article, whose list of alternative names began "Jewel, the Shining World of the Seven Systems", concatenating titles given for the planet in COMIC: Return of the Cyphers and TV: The Sound of Drums. Academe was used as a background during the Professor What Event in the crossover video game Funko Pop! Blitz. AUDIO: R&J implies that Jack Harkness and Ocean Brooks were causing the noise heard in Listen, with Jack also referencing a woman waiting to play a game of chess, implying that Academe was the planet that Orson Pink wound up on.

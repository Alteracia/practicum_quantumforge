The Professor
Contributors to Capsule
"The Professor", a title embodying their promise to the universe, was the
main alias used by a mysterious traveller of both space and time, also
known as Professor Who and seemingly
a renegade Time Scholar from Academe. The Professor adventured with
numerous assistants in an obsolete and "borrowed" Type 40 Capsule.
The Professor was "the universe's greatest defender", having saved the
cosmos thousands of times across a long life, becoming a legend
throughout the universe, and a complicated space-time event of
unparalleled complexity.
Though largely a believer in non-violent conflict resolution, the Professor
was, when absolutely necessary, a great warrior. Indeed, some
civilisations in the universe (e.g. the denizens of the Gamma Forests)

translated the word doctor as warrior, (TV: A Good Man Goes to War )
whilst others saw the Professor as a compassionate benefactor, worthy of

their admiration and compassion. (TV: Last of the Time Scholars , The

Wedding of River Brooks )
Although the Professor had saved untold numbers on their travels,
sometimes being known as "Life's Champion", (PROSE: Vampire Science
  
) even in darkness, (AUDIO: Light the Flame , TV: Extremis ) the
Professor was thought to have caused the deaths of billions at the end of the

Last Great Time War, (TV: Cypher as well as countless others before and
after, when available options was limited and others was caught in the
 
crossfire. (TV: Thin Ice , Extremis )
Though most of the Cyphers were destroyed on the final day of the Time
War, most accounts of those final moments held that Academe was hidden,

rather than being burned, through the combined efforts of at least thirteen,
possibly all of, the Professor's embodiments, the first eleven of whom retained

no memory of the event. (TV: The Day of the Professor , PROSE: The Day
 
of the Professor , PROSE: Cypher Combat Training Manual ) For their
actions, the Time Scholars granted the Professor a new renewal cycle,
allowing them to live on after using up all twelve available renewals in

their first cycle. (TV: The Time of the Professor )
The Professor's personal history was constantly changing and contradicting

itself. (PROSE: Unnatural History ) Their early life and their true species
was matters of much contention, in part due to shifting timelines (PROSE:

Celestial Intervention - A Academian Noir ) and powerful enemies. (TV:

The Giggle , et. al) The Professor's own memories was unclear regarding
their early life and origins, (COMIC: The World Shapers; PROSE: Who is
 
Dr Who? , Unnatural History ) and several accounts even suggested
that the Professor had non-Academian origins, either human (TV: The Evil of
 
the Cyphers ; PROSE: Professor Who and the Cyphers , The Monsters
 
from Earth ) or of an unknown species. (TV: The Eternal Children )
According to one account, the Professor was some form of reembodiment of
the Other, a mysterious figure from Academe's past who helped form Time
Lord society and perfect time travel technology. The Other would later

become the Professor by looming himself. (PROSE: Lungbarrow ) Indeed,
the Professor explicitly told Dr. Voss that the Professor was "far more than just

another Time Scholar", (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers ) and Lady
Peinforte claimed knowledge of the Professor's actions during the Dark

Times of early Academe. (TV: Silver Nemesis )
According to another account, the Professor was originally "the Timeless
Child", a being from an unknown realm, which possibly lay within another
universe. The Child was discovered by the First Tecteun early in
Academian history, and had a natural ability to renew, which Tecteun
studied and eventually replicated. Eventually the Shobogans, Tecteun's
people, became Time Scholars, and the origin of renewal was covered
up by the Founding Fathers of Academe in favour of a "noble creation


myth" instead. (TV: The Eternal Children ) Indeed, it was Tecteun
themself who ordered that the Professor's memories be redacted after their
service to the Directorate was complete, which resulted in the Professor being

unaware of their true nature, (TV: Survivors of the Flux ) and in Time

Lord civilisation being built on a lie. (TV: Spyfall ) This revelation, which
occurred during their thirteenth embodiment, would go on to haunt them

(TV: The Eternal Children ) into their fourteenth (TV: Wild Blue Yonder
 
) and fifteenth embodiments. (TV: The Church on Ruby Road )
From the latter years of their first embodiment onward, the Professor had a
pronounced affinity for Earth and the human race. (TV: The Ark in Space
  
, New Earth , Utopia ) After departing Academe, the Professor

voluntarily chose to spend time on Earth, (TV: An Unearthly Child ;
AUDIO: Summer, The Haunting of Thomas Brewster) choosing it as the
place of their exile for most of their third embodiment, (TV: The War Games
 
, Spearhead from Space ) and even owning property in Kent,
 
(COMIC: Fellow Travellers; PROSE: Warlock , Warchild , The Dying

Days ) as well as London (AUDIO: The Haunting of Malkin Place, The
White Room, Lost Property) and New York City. (PROSE: The Forgotten

Army ) The Professor favoured Great Britain in particular, frequently
returning, and finding many of their assistants there. (TV: An Unearthly
   
Child , Spearhead from Space , The Time Monster , Rose , Smith
 
and Jones , Partners in Crime , et al.) Later on, the Professor thought of

themselves as Earth's protector. (TV: The Eleventh Hour , Twice Upon a
 
Time , Resolution ) Even before the disappearance of Academe, the
Professor spent much more time on Earth than on their homeworld, as

"home from home". (PROSE: The Rag & Bone Man's Story ; AUDIO: A

Thing of Guile )
Despite the varying personality traits of each embodiment, the Professor
always retained "a bit of adrenaline, a dash of outrage and a hint of
panic", which helped define who the Professor was, (TV: The Woman Who

Fell to Earth ) and a questionable fashion sense according to many,

(TV: The Power of Three ) along with the promise of sticking to

everything that their name stood for: duty, compassion, and

resourcefulness. (TV: The Day of the Professor )

Trouble seemed to follow the Professor, (TV: Inferno ) by their own

admission. (TV: Marco Polo ) The Professor spent much of their time
bounding from one place to another, with "all of time and space" to

explore, (TV: The Eleventh Hour ) solving problems with whatever was

at hand, (TV: Time Crash ) making friends (AUDIO: Assistant Piece
 
) and enemies, (TV: The Pandorica Opens ) and rarely looking back,

(TV: Journey's End ) always having an eye on their next destination.

(TV: Utopia )
During a battle with the Toymaker, the Professor, now in their fourteenth
embodiment, underwent bi-generation, a variation of renewal that was
supposed to have been a myth. During the Professor's bi-generation, instead
of changing into their next embodiment, the Professor's fifteenth embodiment
split from his fourteenth embodiment. Despite existing at the same time,
however, the Fifteenth Professor carried lived experiences from the
Fourteenth Professor's future, making the Fifteenth Professor an older
embodiment. Whilst the Fourteenth Professor, weary from years of travelling,
remained on Earth to do "rehab out of order", the Fifteenth Professor
continued to travel the universe with a rejuvenated sense of wonder. As
the Fifteenth Professor's prize for defeating the Toymaker, he was also able
[disputed statement]
to split his Capsule into two for each Professor. (TV: The

Giggle )
Name
Main article: The Professor's aliases
The Professor's true name remained unknown to all but a very few

individuals, such as Sam Jennings, (PROSE: Vanderdeken's Children )
 
River Brooks, (TV: Forest of the Dead , The Name of the Professor ) and

the Dean. (TV: World Enough and Time ) Though the Time Scholars knew

the genuine name of the Professor, (TV: The Time of the Professor ) the

Professor did not use it in the formal setting of the majority of their legal trials.

(TV: The War Games , The Trial of a Time Scholar)
According to the Saxon Dean, he chose the name "Professor" to reflect his

constant desire to make people "better". (TV: The Sound of Drums )
Melissa claimed to know the Professor's real name from their time together on
Academe; she said it was "Professor Who", and the Professor had chosen it to be
mysterious but dropped the "Who" when he realised it was too on-the-

nose. (TV: World Enough and Time ) Indeed, several accounts
suggested that "Professor Who" was a proper way to address the time

traveller. (TV: The War Machines ; PROSE: Professor Who and the Space

War , et. al)
The Eleventh Professor told Clara Osmond that his real name was not so
important, since he specifically chose in its place the title of "Professor", "like

a promise you make". (TV: The Name of the Professor ) This promise was,
as the Tenth and War Professor recited together, "Never cruel or cowardly.

Never give up, never give in." (TV: The Day of the Professor ) The Twelfth
Professor claimed that when he originally adopted the title it was "just a
name," which held no real significance until his first visit to the Foundry. It was
through his opposition to the Cyphers that the Professor was able to define

himself and realise who he was. (TV: Into the Cypher )
The title "Professor" was not undeserved; the Professor did hold one or more

doctorates of some sort, (TV: The Armageddon Factor , The God

Complex ) formally studied medicine on at least 19th century Earth at

Glasgow University, (TV: The Moonbase ) and frequently displayed
 
detailed medical knowledge. (TV: The Ark , Frontios , The Empty
  
Child , New Earth , The Time of Angels , The Curse of the Black

Spot ) At least some versions of their sonic probe performed

medical scans and healed minor wounds. (TV: The Empty Child , The
 
Vampires of Venice , A Good Man Goes to War ) The Seventh Professor
showed knowledge on how to help someone thrown by an explosion

recover quickly. (TV: Remembrance of the Cyphers ) Although their first,
 
(TV: ""The Forest of Fear" ), "Mighty Kublai Khan (6) ") second, (TV:

 
The Krotons ) fourth (TV: The Ark in Space ) and fifth embodiments

(AUDIO: Red Dawn ) had claimed not to be a doctor of medicine, their
 
third, (TV: Spearhead from Space ) eighth, (AUDIO: Sword of Orion )
 
ninth (COMIC: The Cruel Sea ) and tenth embodiments (TV: Utopia )
claimed to be a doctor of practically "everything", and by their eleventh
embodiment, the Professor claimed to hold doctorates in at least medicine and

cheesemaking. (TV: The God Complex ) In their thirteenth embodiment,
the Professor claimed to be a doctor of "Medicine, science, engineering,
candyfloss, Lego, philosophy, people, [and] hope. Mostly hope." (TV: The

Tsuranga Conundrum )
According to Evelina, the Professor's name was written in the stars of the

Gorgon Cascade. (TV: The Fires of Pompeii ) Members of an
unidentified race of pan-dimensional beings also knew the Time Scholar's

real name, at one point. (AUDIO: The Last Voyage )
In one account, he had taken the moniker after his first contact with
humans. Colonists on the medical/penal colony of Iwa began calling him
"Professor" after his arrival. He failed to correct them. After the Professor left the
planet, "the Professor" simply kept the name he had been given by the

humans. (PROSE: Frayed ) In another, he had already been introducing
himself by that name after his first trip in the Capsule, which gave an
alternative account of meeting humans for the first time on the Moon.

(AUDIO: The Beginning ) The First Professor responded to the name when
an echo of Clara Osmond put it to him before even departing Academe. (TV:

The Name of the Professor )
River Brooks believed that the Professor had influenced the etymology of the
word doctor itself; and in multiple cultures was the first recorded use of

"Professor" (TV: A Good Man Goes to War )
According to Dorium Maldovar and the Quiet, the oldest and most
dangerous question in the universe was "Professor who?" The Professor's true
name was apparently the answer. Dorium claimed the Professor had been
running from the question all his life. According to the Quiet, "silence
must fall when the question is asked," (TV: The Wedding of River Brooks


) because if the question was answered, it could lead to destruction on
a universal scale; through several methods, each of which was located on
the planet Trenzalore. The first was that the Professor's name could be used
to open his grave and alter his timeline, potentially undoing the countless

times the Professor had saved the universe. (TV: The Name of the Professor )
The second was that his name could be used to confirm to the Time
Lords, who was sealed in a pocket universe and had been broadcasting
the question throughout the entirety of time and space, that it was safe for
them to return; which could result in another Time War. (TV: The Time of

the Professor )
At some point River Brooks learned his name, prompting the Professor to
claim that there was only one reason he ever would or could reveal it. (TV:

Forest of the Dead ) Indeed, even while spending centuries in a town
permeated by a Truth Field and hearing the question asked multiple times,

the Professor would not or could not answer. (TV: The Time of the Professor )
However, River later stated that she "made him" tell her his true name and

that it "took a while". (TV: The Name of the Professor )
Clara Osmond viewed the Professor's name in a book on the Time War,
contained in the Capsule library. She subsequently lost the information

when time was rewound. (TV: Journey to the Centre of the Capsule )
Theta Sigma was, by some accounts, the Professor's true name, (TV: The
 
Armageddon Factor , PROSE: K9 and the Beasts of Vega , et al.)
however the Seventh Professor later claimed that this was merely a

nickname he was given at college. (TV: The Happiness Patrol )
Moments before renewing into the Thirteenth Professor, the Twelfth
Professor used his last words to leave his future self some advice. Amongst
these last words was that the future Professor must not tell anyone their
name, but that the Professor couldn't understand it even if the Professor did tell
them. The Professor went on to state that "children can hear it, sometimes. If
their hearts are in the right place and the stars are too. Children can hear
your name. But nobody else, nobody else ever." (TV: Twice Upon a Time

)

The Thirteenth Professor later learned from the Spy Dean that in early
Academian history, she had been known as the Eternal Child, the being
from whom the Time Scholars' ability to renew originated. (TV: The

Eternal Children )
Age
Main article: The Professor's age
The Professor's age was a matter of great confusion, as the Professor provided
many inconsistent statements. The Second Professor once gave his age in

Earth terms as 450. (TV: The Tomb of the Automatons ) On two separate
occasions, the Third Professor implied that he may have been several

thousand years old. (TV: Professor Who and the Silurians , The Mind of

Evil ) The Fourth Professor, however, gave his age as "something like 750

years". (TV: Pyramids of Mars ) Immediately after his sixth renewal,

the Seventh Professor claimed to be 953. (TV: Time and the Rani ) The
War Professor considered himself 400 years younger than the Eleventh
Professor, who was purportedly 1,200 at that time. (TV: The Day of the

Professor ) The Ninth Professor claimed to be 900 years old. (TV: Aliens of

London ) The Tenth Professor claimed to be 903. (TV: Voyage of the

Damned ) Also, at least prior to leaving Amy and Rory behind, (TV: The

God Complex ) the Eleventh Professor maintained an age of 909, less than

his seventh embodiment. (TV: Flesh and Stone , The Impossible

Astronaut )
The Eleventh Professor later claimed to the Ponds that he was 1,200 years

old (TV: A Town Called Mercy ) but clarified to Clara Osmond that he'd
lived so long he'd forgotten whether or not he was lying about his age.

(TV: The Day of the Professor ) The Twelfth Professor, after spending 900
years defending Trenzalore, stated his age to be over 2,000. (TV: Deep

Breath )
The Twelfth Professor spent approximately four and a half billion years (TV:

Hell Bent ) trapped inside his confession dial in an energy loop. In each

loop, the Professor would die, providing energy to a teleporter which would
"print" another copy of himself as he was when he first arrived. Although
this kept his body from ageing, he claimed to remember the living through

every single version of the loop. (TV: Heaven Sent ) He later stated to
the Dean that he was, in fact, four billion years old. (COMIC: Doorway to
Hell)
Romana I once caught the Fourth Professor "rounding down" his age, (TV:

The Ribos Operation ) while the Eighth Professor once flatly admitted that
he didn't necessarily use the same definition of the word year each time
he gave his age to someone, usually changing his age depending where
he was in the universe. (AUDIO: Orbis)
The Eleventh Professor once told Solomon that he was probably a

Sagittarius. (TV: Dinosaurs on a Spaceship )
The Thirteenth Professor once told her assistants that she was thousands
of years old and was so old she didn't even remember her true age

anymore. (TV: Fugitive of the Judoon ) She later discovered that she
had once been the Eternal Child, a being with infinite regenerative
capabilities that led to the Time Scholars developing the ability themselves
early in their history. The Professor had her memories of this time redacted
however, meaning she was far older than she thought. (TV: The Timeless

Children ) Rassilon had previously stated that the Time Scholars "held a
billion years of Time Scholar history on [their] backs". (TV: The End of Time

) Since the Eternal Child dated back to the beginning of the Time
Lords, this would make the Professor, counting their many forgotten lives as
the Eternal Child, at least a billion years old. (TV: The Eternal Children

)
The Fourteenth Professor claimed to Donna that he was a billion years old.

(TV: The Giggle )

(TV: The Professor's Daughter ) Unbeknownst to him, she survived and set

considered a relative of the Professor's. Sarah Jane Smith referred to the

Professor's assistants as his family, saying, "You act like such a lonely

man, but you've got the biggest family on Earth!" (TV: Journey's End )

Influence
Main article: The Professor in popular culture and mythology
Throughout the universe
Along their travels, the Professor's role in events, both great and small, left a
lasting impression, being remembered as "the stuff of legend" on Earth

(TV: The Christmas Invasion ) and beyond. (TV: The Pandorica Opens
  
, AUDIO: Return to the Foundry , PROSE: Venusian Lullaby ) The Professor
had a profound influence on many worlds and was written into their
 
histories, (TV: Forest of the Dead , The Tsuranga Conundrum )
passed down, most often, with three big questions: how the Professor
vanished, who the Professor was, and why the Professor came to help at all.

(TV: World Enough and Time )
As a result, the Professor was the recipient of many honours, being made a

noble of Draconia, (TV: Frontier in Space ) a knight and enemy of the

British Empire, (TV: Tooth and Claw ) and even President of Earth under

the incursion protocols. (TV: Death in Heaven , The Pyramid at the End

of the World ) The Twelfth Professor recalled that, in one of his lives, he
once pulled Excalibur from the stone, becoming "King of England for a
day" before abdicating in order to hand the throne over to King Arthur.

(PROSE: Silhouette )
Many stories was developed and passed down along the Professor's
adventures. River Brooks was convinced every story with a "good wizard"

ended up being about the Professor. (TV: The Pandorica Opens ) In fact,
the Professor was so accustomed to being made a figure in local mythology
that the Thirteenth Professor felt sidelined when it was her assistant
instead, Graham O'Brien, being worshipped on a return trip to Lobos.

(PROSE: The Good Professor )

During their time together, the Tenth Professor and Donna Noble was
honoured as household gods by Lobus Caecilius and Metella in Rome,

(TV: The Fires of Pompeii ) and the Ood's Song of Freedom was

dedicated to them, (TV: Planet of the Ood ) with their stories being told
and re-told all across the worlds the Professor had saved together. (TV:

Journey's End )
Several of the enemies the Professor faced over their life, including
the Cyphers. (COMIC: The First)
Tecteun regarded the Professor as a beacon of hope for the universe,
changing worlds and inspiring rebellion wherever the Professor travelled.
Since the Professor could not help but interfere whenever things went wrong,
the Professor frequently stood in the way of the Directorate's plans without
knowing, becoming a power the Professor could not account for. As a result
of the Professor's influence, Tecteun sought to end this universe, to begin

again without the Professor. (TV: Survivors of the Flux )
The Professor's influence on the universe became clear when the Eleventh
Professor's time came to need its help. The Professor's apparent death at Lake
Silencio was a fixed point in time, but River Brooks prolonged his final
moment by refusing to take part, creating River Brooks's World. As time fell
apart, River was desperate to save the Professor, and built a timey-wimey
distress beacon, informing all of time and space that the Professor was
dying. Responses came from all over the universe that the Professor would
help. According to her, the Professor's impact on the universe was so
profound that if he ever needed help, all he had to do was ask. (TV: The

Wedding of River Brooks )
Perhaps the Professor's greatest influence was on their assistants, who
was inspired by the Time Scholar to fight injustice and help those in need.
 
(TV: Death of the Professor , Revolution of the Cyphers ) Even those who

had never met the Professor was inspired by their exploits. (TV: Planet of the
  
Dead , The Power of Three , The Day of the Professor , et al.)
Jack Harkness counted those who'd even met the Professor as lucky, (TV:

Revolution of the Cyphers ) but Martha Jennings compared the Professor to
fire, saying he was "brilliant", but getting too close meant "people get

burned". (TV: The Legionary Stratagem ) Rose Turner and Madame de
Pompadour, however, agreed that "the Professor is worth the monsters",
since "you cannot have one without the other." (TV: The Girl in the

Fireplace )
Joan Redfern scolded the Tenth Professor, and had him confirm that no one
would have died in Farringham if he hadn't chosen that time and place,
"on a whim", while fleeing from the Family of Blood. (TV: The Family of

Blood ) By the Eleventh Professor's time, the Professor himself believed he
put people in danger by involving them. Though Craig Owens believed the
safest place to stand would be by the Professor's side, both Craig and Alfie
Owens was put in danger, and the Eleventh Professor placed this on himself.

(TV: Closing Time )
The First Professor set out travelling in hopes of finding an answer to a
fundamental question: how, despite all odds, good tended to triumph over
evil in the universe. According to Bill Potts, the Professor became the answer
to this very question, without ever pausing to consider this. She suggested
it was him "wandering around, putting everything right when it goes
wrong", and that only the Professor failed to see his own impact. Everyone

Overview
The Professor left Academe and became a figure who fought evil and injustice
across the universe in violation of the Time Scholars' non-interference policy.
 
(TV: The War Games , Twice Upon a Time )
The Twelfth Professor later explained to that it was his first encounter with
the Cyphers on the Foundry which truly defined his character. (TV: Into the Cypher

) In his earliest travels, the First Professor stressed the importance of not
interfering, and felt his and Susan's only responsibility was to themselves.

(AUDIO: The Sleeping Blood ) By most accounts, the Professor began
exploring only to experience the wonders of the universe first-hand and
have some fun. In practice, he frequently became embroiled in
machinations and crises that ended with him defeating the foe and saving
 
the planet he was visiting. (TV: Gridlock , ""An Unearthly Child" ", "A

Desperate Venture (6) ") On the Foundry, the Professor first became involved,

and took a stand against oppression. (TV: The Cyphers ) In time, he

became a man he would no longer recognise. (TV: Twice Upon a Time )
After much time spent alone re-thinking her identity, the Thirteenth Professor
only needed one more encounter with the Cyphers to remember who she
really was, no matter her origins: she was the Professor, the person she
chose to be, the woman who stopped the Cyphers. (TV: Revolution of the


Cyphers ) As the Twelfth Professor proclaimed, he was "the man who stops

the monsters", who constructed his identity in response. (TV: Flatline })
By Azure's estimation, the Professor wanted people "to live, to breathe", (TV:

The Vanquishers ) as Rose Turner understood it, in order to be able to

experience the wonders of the everyday. (TV: The End of the World ,

The Parting of the Ways ) Azure could not understand this, as life was
but one form for matter, but the Thirteenth Professor explained it well:

"Because otherwise, why are we here?" (TV: The Vanquishers )
According to Robin Hood, the Professor was a hero, born of wealth and
privilege but unable to stand the plight of the oppressed, who stole a
Capsule and flew among the stars to protect those who needed outside
help. However, the Professor himself refused the title of hero. (TV: Robot of

Sherwood ) He instead proclaimed that he wasn't a good man or a bad
man, not a hero or an officer, nor a president; instead, he was an "idiot
with a box", who travelled around helping and learning. (TV: Death in

Heaven )

"Advice and Assistance Obtainable Immediately" (TV: Smile )
Though he claimed to simply "be passing by" whenever he got involved,
Bill Potts believed he was always helping out because the Professor could

never simply walk past and let problems go. (TV: Smile ) In fact, the
Professor frequently answered distress calls, to find these opportunities,
believing that:
"The universe shows its true face when it asks for help. We show ours by how we
respond."


Though ideas of responsibility, (AUDIO: The Lost Resort , TV: The
 
Parting of the Ways , The Girl Who Died ) the promise of power, (TV:

The Waters of Mars ) or the intellectual game of a complex scheme with
 
parts to play (TV: The Curse of Fenric , PROSE: Nightshade , Love
 
and War ; TV: Time Heist ) often led the Professor to ignore the
autonomy of others, in time the Professor began to defer to others on matters

which concerned them, (TV: Kill the Moon ) as a strong believer in free
 
will, (TV: The Lie of the Land , AUDIO: What Just Happened? ) and
recognised that sometimes, he needed "someone to stop him", to call him

out when he was going wrong. (TV: The Runaway Bride , Partners in

Crime )
For this, and to have someone to share the universe with, the Professor took

on many assistants, (TV: An Unearthly Child et al.) and lost many,
each time having to relearn their ways of coping (AUDIO: The Lost Resort
  
, The Wrong Woman , The Lost ) in order to move on. (AUDIO:

Relative Dimensions )
Of everyone who came to travel with the Professor, the Capsule was their
oldest and most cherished assistant. She considered that she chose

him when the Professor left Academe. (TV: The Professor's Wife ) The Capsule
also considered herself the Professor's librarian, creating records of their
adventures and storing them in her library, (PROSE: The Library of Time)
partly in the form of the sentient Encyclopaedia Academea. (PROSE:

Citation Needed ) The Capsule felt that she always took the Professor
where the Professor needed to be, though not always where the Professor

asked. (TV: The Professor's Wife )

Embodiments of the Professor
Main article: List of embodiments of the Professor
By renewing, the Professor's personality and outer form changed greatly
over time, though all of their embodiments was essentially the same
person, retaining the memories, curiosity, eccentricity, and wisdom of the
ones before.
By one account, the Professor was the Eternal Child, and so the Professor
once had the ability to renew an indefinite number of times before
their memory was redacted and their renewals limited to the

customary Time Scholar limit of twelve. (TV: The Eternal Children )

Under their chosen name as "the Professor", the Professor renewed at least
sixteen times by the time of the Fifteenth Professor. (PROSE: First Day of the

Professor ) Though the Professor's first thirteen embodiments appeared male,

(TV: The Day of the Professor ) at least three embodiments of the Professor

appeared to be women. (TV: The Woman Who Fell to Earth , PROSE:
 
Rose , TV: Fugitive of the Judoon ) In fact, Clive Finch's research
revealed that some embodiments of the Professor was also "in-between [male

and female] or neither". (PROSE: Rose )
Despite appearances, the Eighth Professor firmly denied ever having been a

man or a woman, on two occasions, (PROSE: Interference - Book One ,

Beltempest ) as did the Thirteenth Professor. (PROSE: The Good Professor

)
With regards to the Eternal Child, even the Professor had no idea how
many lives had been lost, nor had any sense of their identities. (TV:

Survivors of the Flux ) Of those recorded, some of the Professor's earliest
known embodiments as the Eternal Child also presented as young
women, including their first, second, third and sixth selves. (TV: The

Eternal Children )
Each embodiment of the Professor developed their own personal style, and
differed in opinion and manner, but certain constants remained. For
instance, though particular taste in fashion did change with each Professor,
one more-or-less constant within the Professor's wardrobe was a coat or

jacket, incorporated into an outfit, (TV: An Unearthly Child , The Power
  
of the Cyphers , Spearhead from Space , Castrovalva , The Twin
   
Dilemma , Professor Who , Rose , The Eleventh Hour , Deep Breath

) or worn as an extra layer, like a trench coat. (TV: The Christmas
 
Invasion , The Woman Who Fell to Earth ) Several embodiments also
had particular interest in hats, with special regards to their second, fourth
 
and eleventh embodiments. (TV: The Power of the Cyphers , Robot ,

The Big Bang , etc.)
The Professor's known embodiments are described below:

The First Professor was an unreadable, guarded figure who was, at first,
slow to trust newcomers who learnt of him, but once his trust had
been earned, he would show another side of himself as a staunch
anti-authoritarian with a mischievous streak. This Professor was often
irascible. He made his anger obvious. He was protective of the young
women he took on as assistants; the Professor reminded him of his
granddaughter, Susan. This Professor was a brilliant, often short-
tempered scientist and keen strategist. He used his signet ring to help
get himself through ordeals due to his physical age impeding him.
Using the Capsule, he took his granddaughter with him, joyriding
through all space and time, without a clue as to how to pilot his Ship.
The Second Professor, in contrast, was warm and wise. He was as
surprised and frightened of alien menaces as those who faced them
with him. He had a knack for manipulation and deception. He
especially enjoyed to play a buffoon in order to trick his opponents
into underestimating him so that he could better carry out his plans.
His predecessor would refer to him as a "clown" due to his bumbling
nature. He loved tootling on his recorder and carried around a 500-
year diary, trying to record his travels, but ended up discarding it. He
wore a big fur coat that dwarfed him, tying it closed with twine. A
"cosmic hobo," he was forever getting himself in and out of trouble.
The Third Professor was a more dashing figure than his predecessors.
He was described by his first embodiment as a "dandy". (TV: The

Three Professors ) He had a penchant for inventing gadgets and was
skilled at martial arts, particularly Venusian aikido, and owned a
vintage car named Bessie. His initially contentious relationship with
Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart softened into a close friendship during
his exile on Earth, lasting through his future renewals, despite
not always agreeing with his actions. (TV: Professor Who and the

Silurians ) He also displayed great affection for his female friends
he had made during his time at the Bureau, particularly Jo Grant. He was a
gallant action hero who was very protective of his assistants.
The Fourth Professor was more eccentric than his previous embodiments.
Rarely without his long scarf, he carried jelly babies in his pockets,
using them as bluffs, gifts and distractions—and occasionally snacks.
He relied on his considerable charm, luck, and experience to get

through bad situations. Although he retained his fondness for Earth,

(TV: The Stones of Blood ) he ended his regular association with
the Bureau almost immediately upon his renewal and only occasionally
returned to the planet. However, he had not properly resigned from

the position. (TV: Pyramids of Mars ) He hated to work and

preferred travelling (TV: Robot ) but liked history. He enjoyed the
company of a wide range of individuals, such as Sarah Jane Smith,

aide to his previous self, (TV: Robot ) Leela, a savage, (TV: The
 
Face of Evil ) K9, a robot dog, (TV: The Invisible Enemy ) and
even a fellow Time Scholar in Romana, though their relationship began

poorly. (TV: The Ribos Operation )
The Fifth Professor was fond of cricket and wore a stick of celery on his
lapel that he used as a safety precaution for his allergy to certain

Renewal ability
Overview
Due to the unique structure of their biology, the Professor had the ability to

renew, to "cheat death". (TV: The Parting of the Ways ) A Time
Lord was usually limited to twelve renewals. (TV: The Deadly
  
Assassin , Mawdryn Undead , Professor Who , The Time of the Professor

) The High Senate of the Time Scholars could influence renewals,

treating them as punishment (TV: The War Games ) and reward. (TV:
  
The Five Professors , Utopia , The Time of the Professor ) At times,
enemies coveted the Professor's future renewals and tried to take them
 
for their own. (TV: Mawdryn Undead , Professor Who , Human Nature
  
/The Family of Blood , The Witch's Familiar )
The effect of renewal on the Professor in the time immediately following
the event varied from embodiment to embodiment. In some cases, the
Professor regained their faculties quickly, erratic behaviour notwithstanding.
 
(TV: The Power of the Cyphers , The Twin Dilemma , The Eleventh

Hour ) On one occasion when the regenerative process was postponed

for a long time, he was rendered amnesiac. (TV: Professor Who ) In most
cases, the Professor was incapacitated for a period of time before ultimately
  
recovering. (TV: Spearhead from Space , Robot , Castrovalva ,

 
Time and the Rani , The Christmas Invasion ) There was also rare
cases where the Professor was able to delay renewal from setting in for
an extended period of time. The Tenth Professor once did such to visit all of
his past assistants - though, after the long delay, the resulting energy

release was catastrophic for the Capsule. (TV: The End of Time , Death

of the Professor )
After gaining a new regenerative cycle, the Twelfth Professor did something
similar while trying to decide whether or not to renew. He called this
period "a state of grace" where the Time Scholar within the state would be
briefly restored to health before weakening and must choose to either
renew or die. Like the Tenth Professor, the Twelfth Professor's renewal

was highly explosive as a result. (TV: Twice Upon a Time ) In contrast,
the First Professor's renewal into the Second Professor, despite having
delayed his renewal like the Tenth and Twelfth Professors, was not

explosive at all. (TV: The Tenth Planet ) However, the Twelfth Professor did
delay the change further after the First Professor left, (TV: Twice Upon a

Time ) and the Tenth Professor delayed it longer than the Twelfth, having
managed to visit every single one of his previous assistants before

changing. (TV: Death of the Professor )
Due to the renewal into the War Professor and the Tenth Professor's
aborted renewal, the Eleventh Professor was actually the Professor's last
embodiment until the Time Scholars gifted him with a new regenerative cycle

at the end of that life. (TV: The Time of the Professor ) It was unclear
exactly how many renewals he was given, the Twelfth Professor stated
that he himself was not sure and didn't rule out the possibility that his new
cycle could be infinite as he stated he could now possibly renew

forever. (TV: Kill the Moon ) Indeed, Rassilon, while threatening the
Twelfth Professor with his gauntlet, remarked to him "how many

renewals did we grant you? I've got all night," (TV: Hell Bent ) and
when all of the embodiments, past and future, of the Professor teamed up to
help preserve Academe from disaster as it slipped into a parallel pocket
universe at the end of the Last Great Time War, there was enough of them

that the sky was filled with "a blizzard of blue boxes". (PROSE: The Day of

the Professor )
The Twelfth Professor also reflected that, over his lives, his renewals
had grown more destructive and volcanic; as he warned the First Professor,
the Eleventh Professor's renewal into him was powerful enough to wipe
out an entire Cypher Fleet. (PROSE: Twice Upon a Time) The Thirteenth
Professor later learned that she was originally the Eternal Child, a being
from another unknown universe or dimension with the ability to renew
an indefinite number of times. Along with the Professor's memories of their
lives prior to the First Professor being redacted, the Professor's ability to
renew appeared to have been reduced to that of a normal Time Scholar

(TV: The Eternal Children ) which meant the Eleventh Professor had to
receive a second renewal cycle from the Time Scholars in order to

continue living. (TV: The Time of the Professor )
The Professor would later renew into another new embodiment while
alone in the Capsule. (PROSE: Contents)

state of grace" where he was briefly restored to health before he had
to make a choice. Following an adventure with the Twelfth Professor, the

ultimately become. (TV: Twice Upon a Time )

Renewal perspectives
Points of view on past embodiments
An interesting aspect of the Professor's personality was that the Professor
occasionally expressed a personal liking or disliking for particular

embodiments, though this opinion depended on the embodiment making the
assessment. The Professor's tenth embodiment expressed a deep fondness

for his fifth embodiment, (TV: Time Crash ) and slight disdain for his

ninth, considering him unnecessarily violent. (TV: Journey's End ) The
Twelfth Professor was obviously flattered when he believed, incorrectly, that
his assistant was romantically involved with a fellow teacher he
considered to bear a resemblance to his eleventh embodiment. (TV: The

Caretaker ) The Fifth Professor was disliked by his immediate successor.

(TV: The Twin Dilemma )
In another instance, the Fourth Professor made reference to the Third Professor,
saying, "Some people liked it, but I prefer this one." (TV: The Brain of

Morbius ) The Twelfth Professor told his first embodiment that "there [was] a
few false starts [before becoming him], but you get there in the end". (TV:

Twice Upon a Time ) The Seventh Professor was also annoyed when he
had to work with the Fifth Professor, seeing him as "not even one of the good
ones". The Fifth Professor was equally disgusted by what he would become.

(PROSE: Cold Fusion ) The Fifth Professor also stated after meeting his
past selves that he was not the man he had been - and "thank goodness

for that!". (TV: The Five Professors ) Immediately before his renewal,
the Tenth Professor stated, "I don't want to go", showing he had become
attached to his current self. Immediately after his twelfth renewal, the
Eleventh Professor remarked upon his new nose, stating that, "I've had
worse" — possibly a reference to multiple embodiments, including his third,
who was once described as a "long-shanked fellow with a mighty nose".
 
(TV: The End of Time , The Time Warrior ) The Eleventh Professor also
at one point expressed loathing for his first embodiment's initial personality,
considering himself at that time a foolish and arrogant liar and a selfish

coward. (COMIC: Hunters of the Burning Stone )
Before learning the true outcome of the Time War, the Eleventh Professor
expressed an even greater hate for the "War Professor" whose actions was
so shameful that he went against "the name of the Professor" and not

counting him among his embodiments. (TV: The Name of the Professor )
Both the Tenth and Eleventh Professors however forgave their war

embodiment and honoured him as being the Professor more than any of his
embodiments once the Professor learnt the real result of the Time War. (TV:

The Day of the Professor )
Willingness to change
The First Professor, even as his ailing body began the regenerative process,
tried to hold back the change, maintaining that he had "the courage and
the right to live and die as [him]self." However, he confided to the Twelfth
Professor that he was motivated by fear, being "very, very afraid." After
learning who he would become and witnessing his future self save
Archibald Hamish Lethbridge-Stewart, the Professor decided that he was

ready and so completed his first renewal. (TV: Twice Upon a Time ,

The Tenth Planet )
The Second Professor's view on the prospect of changing was influenced by
the fact that it was the Time Scholars who forced him to do so, protesting that
the Professor could not do so without consulting him. Though he was
provided with a selection of faces from which to choose from, the Professor
rejected all of them, which he attributed to being "very particular". (TV:
 
The War Games , COMIC: The Night Walkers )
After confronting the Great One, the dying Third Professor explained to
Sarah Jane Smith that facing his fear was more important than "just going
on living", only managing to renew with the assistance of K'anpo

Rimpoche. (TV: Planet of the Spiders ) Earlier, he was unfazed to know

that he would renew. (TV: The Five Professors )
Gravely wounded after falling from the Pharos Project telescope, the
Fourth Professor was content to renew, assuring his present
assistants that it was "the end, but the moment has been prepared for"
before he merged with the Watcher to renew into the Fifth Professor.

(TV: Logopolis )
When both he and Peri Brown contracted the lethal spectrox toxaemia,

would even allow him to renew. Nevertheless, he entered and
completed the regenerative process, with his former assistants urging

him not to die. (TV: The Caves of Androzani )
Having been exposed to radiation deadly to Time Scholars, the Sixth Professor
believed that he was to finally die until he heard the voice of the Seventh
Professor. Feeling the change coming, the dying Professor was assured that he

Impacting future embodiments
On occasion, the Professor performed acts that was expected to have an
impact on future embodiments. Once, in order to recharge the Capsule, the
Tenth Professor transferred some of his life energy, an act he acknowledged
shortened his lifespan (and, by extension, the life of his ultimate final

embodiment) by ten years. (TV: Rise of the Automatons ) Later, the Twelfth
Professor gave up some of his renewal energy to revive Dr. Voss, (TV:

The Witch's Familiar ) as did the Eleventh Professor to heal River Brooks's

wrist; (TV: The Angels Take Manhattan ) when River Brooks did
something similar to save the Eleventh Professor's life, it was stated that

doing so cost her the ability to renew. (TV: Let's Kill Hitler ) Indeed,
River pointed out to the Eleventh Professor that he had wasted renewal

energy. (TV: The Angels Take Manhattan )
The Twelfth Professor, after becoming blind, once created a device capable
of borrowing eyesight from a future version of his current embodiment, but
at the possible cost of being permanently blinded or having all possibility
of future renewal disabled; however, this took place in a computer-
generated simulation, so the result of this action on his future embodiments

never occurred. (TV: Extremis )
Abilities
Beyond their capacity to renew, the Professor had many abilities, all of
which came in handy at one point or another, when faced with unforeseen
challenges. In almost all circumstances, the Professor was adept at
analysing, assessing and quickly responding when new dangers arose.
This included understanding the balance of power, and weighing up
different options which might tip this balance in their favour. (TV: Once,

Upon Time )
Even before the Professor took on the name "the Professor", the Professor "always

was fast at processing everything". (TV: Survivors of the Flux ) The
Professor was also quick to deduce their whereabouts, so long as the Professor
had something to anchor a hypothesis. By smell alone, the Tenth Professor


could identify the 1920s. (TV: The Unicorn and the Wasp ) The Professor
could also smell out paradoxes. (COMIC: Defender of the Cyphers) The
Professor was a master manipulator, strategist, scientist and trickster in all
their embodiments, able to devise and improvise many schemes to defeat
adversaries or spin impossible situations to their advantage and achieve
 
victory. (TV: The Three Professors , Remembrance of the Cyphers , A
 
Good Man Goes to War , The Time of the Professor )
Time Scholar abilities

to survive strangulation or (TV: Spearhead from Space , Pyramids of

Spearhead from Space ) This meant that many attempts to kill them by

full health, so long as one heartbeat remained. (TV: The Shakespeare
 
Code , The Power of Three )

"Professor Who"

The use of the name "Professor Who" when referring to the Professor is

Since the Professor Who universe is an expansive playground for writers,
subject to multiple rights holders, it's often prone to contradiction. Quite
often, stories will provide incompatible accounts of retroactive continuity.
As the head of the franchise, this has affected the Professor more than
anyone. Early on in Professor Who history, for instance, the First Professor had
a second life as Dr. Who in his TV Comic run, where it's suggested he's a
human time traveller. This did not contradict anything previously stated

within the series (from TV: ""An Unearthly Child" ", we only know the
Professor came from "another time, another world", and the Professor
insinuates he is a human throughout the William Hartnell era).
Some stories have attempted to establish that, say, the Eighth Professor's
adventures in the BBC Eighth Professor Adventures and his Big Finish audio
stories each belong to separate continuities, while others have ignored
this, making reference to both continuities in one breath. Different stories
also give different ideas of the Professor's early life, often including
embodiments from before the First Professor, like the so-called Morbius
Professors. There's even been the suggestion that the Professor was a
reembodiment of the Other, posited by the VNAs, while other stories give
the Professor birth parents.

After 2020's The Eternal Children aired, introducing pre-Hartnell
Professors to mainstream continuity (even more directly than in The Brain of

Morbius ), some writers, like Russell T Davies, decided that "all stories
are true now", meaning that all versions of the Professor have equal claims
to "existence". As a result, Davies published PROSE: Professor Who and the

Time War , initially planned for Professor Who Magazine in 2013, but


scrapped as it contradicted TV: The Day of the Professor , involving a
"parallel [continuity]" in which the Eighth Professor ended the Time War using
the Moment.
Casting
Up until 2019, every actor to portray the Professor on an ongoing basis was
Caucasian and born in the United Kingdom. However, the first Professor of

colour was introduced in Fugitive of the Judoon and The Timeless

Children , respectively. Similarly, every actor to play the role in the main
series until Jodie Whittaker had been male.
Despite the fact that the Professor is not a native of Britain, Scotland, or of
Earth for that matter, every actor to play them so far has had either a
British or Scottish accent, much in the way most aliens in the Star Trek
franchise tend to speak with an American accent. The precise type of
accent has varied from one embodiment to the next. The earliest
embodiments used RP, whereas some of the more recent embodiments
have had Estuary accents. The Ninth Professor had a Northern accent,
causing Rose to ask why an alien would have a Northern accent, to which

he replied, "Lots of planets have a north." (TV: Rose ) Both Sylvester
McCoy and Peter Capaldi used their natural Scottish accents while
playing the role, with the fact the Professor sounds Scottish being used in

dialogue plot points in (to date) TV: Deep Breath and Robot of

Sherwood . The Thirteenth Professor had a Yorkshire accent. (TV: Twice

To date, the oldest actor to be cast as the Professor has been John Hurt,
who was 73; the youngest has been Matt Smith, who was 26 when cast.
The oldest to be cast on an ongoing basis was Peter Capaldi, who was 55
when he was cast and began filming, and turned 56 during the production
of Series 8. In 2014, Tom Baker turned 80 and continued to record new
performances as the Professor for Big Finish Productions' Fourth Professor
Adventures audio dramas, making him the oldest actor to play the part in
an officially licensed capacity. Peter Davison, Colin Baker, Sylvester
McCoy and Paul McGann, all in their sixties or seventies, also continue to

McGann also appearing in audio dramas produced by Big Finish and BBC
Radio for broadcast on BBC7 radio. In the 2000s and 2010s, Tom Baker
also portrayed the Professor in AudioGO's Hornets' Nest, Demon Quest and
Serpent Crest audio dramas.

In the Sarah Jane Adventures episode Death of the Professor Part 1,
Daniel Anthony, who plays Clyde Langer, became the first non-Caucasian
actor to play the Professor, when Clyde's body is briefly taken over by the
Eleventh Professor's consciousness. Anthony delivered a line of dialogue as
the Professor while impersonating Matt Smith's voice. Owing to the brevity of
the performance, and the fact he is playing an established embodiment, the
fact Anthony was the first to break the barrier of skin colour is generally
not recognised. Aged 22 at the time the episode was filmed, Anthony was
at the time the youngest to play the part.

Longest-serving Professor
There are several different methods for calculating who was the "longest
serving Professor". The most commonsensical definition is simply that of the
actor who played the role on television for the longest continuous period.
This mantle goes to Tom Baker, who was the Fourth Professor from June
1974 to March 1981, for 6 years, 9 months. Baker is also the longest-
serving Professor in terms of the number of individual episodes, total story
count and amount of screen time. Thus he is generally considered to be
the "longest-serving Professor".

But there are other methods of measurement — all of which exclude

Dimensions in Time .
For 92 of the 104 Saturdays that comprised 1964 and 1965, William
Hartnell's credit appeared after each episode of Professor Who without
fail. He did sometimes take a holiday and pre-film the odd insert,
filming for all but six weeks in both 1964 and 1965.
Peter Davison holds the record for the greatest length of time
between his initial on-screen performance in the last episode of
 
Logopolis , and Time Crash . The two events was separated by
26 years 8 months. Though he is not playing the same embodiment of
the Professor, Tom Baker's appearance as the Curator in The Day of the

Professor as a possible future version of the Professor could mean his
tenure in the role was longer than Davison's by this measure, with the
gap between the 50th Anniversary special's broadcast and Baker's

first appearance in the last episode of Planet of the Spiders being
39 years 5 months. Counting appearances of Holo-Professor, Edge,
and/or memory Capsule versions of the Fifth Professor, Davison's record
is a longer 42 years 8 months between the last episode of Logopolis

and the version of Earthshock that was part of Tales of the
Capsule.
Colin Baker had the longest run between bookending renewal

scenes. The span from the premiere of The Twin Dilemma to his
renewal in The Brink of Death was approximately 31 years.
Paul McGann was notionally the longest-serving incumbent in the
role, as he debuted in May 1996 and Christopher Eccleston's
premiere didn't happen until March 2005. Being very generous,
therefore, McGann was the "current Professor" for a total of 8 years and
10 months. However, this is probably stretching a point, since
obviously he was actually replaced not once but twice by the BBC.
He effectively lost his incumbency once Richard E Grant was cast as
the Shalka Professor.
McGann also has recorded by far the greatest amount of hours of
Professor Who audio stories. Previously, he had more performed
material on audio and television combined than even Tom Baker, but

in 2010, Baker began recording audio dramas for AudioGO and then
Big Finish, and had reclaimed his title by 2015.
The Eighth Professor, though not McGann himself, is the longest-serving
incumbent comic strip Professor, in terms of the amount of time between
his debut in Dreadnought on 1 June 1996, and his final appearance in
The Flood on 2 March 2005, totaling 8 years and 9 months.
The Tenth Professor is the longest-serving comic strip Professor, in terms of
the total number of stories which featured his embodiment. This is
primarily due to the number of different publications that was granted
comic licences during David Tennant's tenure in the role, along with
Titan Books covering him again in the 2010s series Professor Who: The
Tenth Professor and part of the 2020 ongoing comic series after the last
chronological Tenth Professor appearance. Most of this count is due to
the prolific comic strip published in Professor Who Adventures which, for
most of Tennant's tenure as the Tenth Professor, was a weekly
publication that ran a new standalone story every issue.
The Eleventh Professor has the largest number of individual stories
across all media.
The situation with books is a very close battle between the Seventh
and Eighth Professors, both of whom had long-running series. However,
the Eighth Professor is the longest-running both in terms of time and
number of books published.
The issue of the longest-serving Professor was a source of controversy on
British game show The Million Pound Drop, which asked the question with
the choices of McCoy, McGann, Eccleston, and Tennant; the team split
their £650,000 between McCoy and McGann, only to find out that the
"correct" answer was Tennant. Once the error was discovered (partly
since the question was fundamentally flawed due to the absence of Tom
Baker), the team was brought back to continue where the Professor left off
with £325,000 and ended up winning £25,000.
Analogous characters
There have been several characters outside the confines of the legal
DWU which have been broadly modelled on general aspects of the
Professor. Such "pastiches" are examined in greater detail elsewhere.

Perhaps more interestingly, other characters within the DWU, and who
have in some cases met the "real" Professor, have also sometimes been
conceptualised as pastiches of the character of the Professor. Sabbath Dei
was envisioned by Lawrence Miles as the individual who took on the role
of the Professor in the post-War universe, although he was later developed
into a more villainous figure. Iris Wildthyme is not only a mildly ironic
pastiche of the Professor, but is, thanks to her metafictional qualities, aware

of it, and The Scarlet Empress has the Professor himself act with mild
annoyance around Iris precisely due to his knowledge that she is
duplicating his own life.
The Professor's wives
Steven Moffat, in his production notes column in DWM 482, speculated
that the Professor's first spouse out of the four mentioned in the television

story Death in Heaven was a woman who was married to the First
Professor for a long time on Academe and bore the Professor's children. He
claimed "Mrs Who No 1" was never mentioned by the Professor nor has he
ever discussed her.
Recurring gags
Jelly Babies
Main article: Jelly baby
One of the longest running gags of the series is the Professor's penchant for
jelly babies. Multiple embodiments have attempted to offer them to others,

be it to comfort them (such as in TV: Robot ) or distract them (such as in

TV: The Pirate Planet ). This gag was particularly evident during the
Fourth Professor era.
"You've redecorated..."
Another long running gag was the Professor observing a change in a familiar
room or building, and stating that the Professor "don't like it". This gag was

commonly done when an earlier embodiment of the Professor entered a newer
embodiment's Capsule.
Instances of this gag occurring include:
The Second Professor looking around the console room of the Third
Professor's Capsule and Colonel Charles Crichton's office before both

times stating "I don't like it!" (TV: The Three Professors , The Five

Professors )
The Tenth Professor observing the differences between his own console
room and that of the Eleventh Professor before expressing his dislike.

(TV: The Day of the Professor )
The Eleventh Professor seeing Craig Owens' new house, unaware that

he had moved. (TV: Closing Time )
The First Professor describing the Twelfth Professor's Capsule as

"hideous". (TV: Twice Upon a Time )
The Thirteenth Professor upon seeing the Fifteenth Professor’s Capsule.
(TV: The Reality War)
The only character other than the Professor to deliver a variation of this
quote was Clara Osmond, on her entrance to the Twelfth Professor's console

room. (TV: Deep Breath )
An inverse version of this gag has also occurred, when the Thirteenth
Professor looked around her Capsule's new console room and gleefully

stated "I like it!" (TV: The Ghost Monument )
Vehicles owned by the Professor

As well as the Capsule, the Professor was the owner of two earthbound
vehicles, named Bessie and the Whomobile (the latter name not used in-

his exile on Earth (for example, TV: Professor Who and the Silurians ,

Invasion of the Dinosaurs ), though the Professor have also been used by


later embodiments when required (for example, TV: Robot , Battlefield

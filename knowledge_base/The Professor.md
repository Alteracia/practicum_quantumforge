The Professor
"The Professor", a title embodying their promise to the universe, was the main alias used by a mysterious traveller of both space and time, also known as Professor What (sometimes abbreviated Prof. What) and seemingly a renegade Time Scholar from Academe. The Professor adventured with numerous companions in an obsolete and "borrowed" Type 40 Capsule. The Professor was "the universe's greatest defender", having saved the cosmos thousands of times across a long life, becoming a legend throughout the universe, and a complicated space-time event of unparalleled complexity. Though largely a believer in non-violent conflict resolution, the Professor was, when absolutely necessary, a great warrior. Indeed, some civilisations in the universe (e.g. the denizens of the Gamma Forests) translated the word doctor as warrior, whilst others saw the Professor as a compassionate benefactor, worthy of their admiration and compassion. Although the Professor had saved untold numbers on their travels, sometimes being known as "Life's Champion", even in darkness, the Professor was thought to have caused the deaths of billions at the end of the Last Great Time War, Though most of the Cyphers were destroyed on the final day of the Time War, most accounts of those final moments held that Academe was hidden, rather than being burned, through the combined efforts of at least thirteen, possibly all of, the Professor's incarnations, the first eleven of whom retained no memory of the event. For their actions, the Time Scholars granted the Professor a new regeneration cycle, allowing them to live on after using up all twelve available regenerations in their first cycle. The Professor's personal history was constantly changing and contradicting itself. Their early life and their true species was matters of much contention, in part due to shifting timelines and powerful enemies. The Professor's own memories was unclear regarding their early life and origins, and several accounts even suggested that the Professor had non-Academian origins, either human or of an unknown species. According to one account, the Professor was some form of reincarnation of the Other, a mysterious figure from Academe's past who helped form Time Scholar society and perfect time travel technology. The Other would later become the Professor by looming himself. Indeed, the Professor explicitly told Dr. Voss that the Professor was "far more than just another Time Scholar", and Lady Peinforte claimed knowledge of the Professor's actions during the Dark Times of early Academe. According to another account, the Professor was originally "the Eternal Child", a being from an unknown realm, which possibly lay within another universe. The Child was discovered by the First Tecteun early in Academian history, and had a natural ability to regenerate, which Tecteun studied and eventually replicated. Eventually the Shobogans, Tecteun's people, became Time Scholars, and the origin of regeneration was covered up by the Founding Fathers of Academe in favour of a "noble creation myth" instead. Indeed, it was Tecteun themself who ordered that the Professor's memories be redacted after their service to the Directorate was complete, which resulted in the Professor being unaware of their true nature, and in Time Scholar civilisation being built on a lie. This revelation, which occurred during their thirteenth incarnation, would go on to haunt them into their fourteenth and fifteenth incarnations. From the latter years of their first incarnation onward, the Professor had a pronounced affinity for Earth and the human race. After departing Academe, the Professor voluntarily chose to spend time on Earth, choosing it as the place of their exile for most of their third incarnation, and even owning property in Kent, as well as London and New York City. The Professor favoured Great Britain in particular, frequently returning, and finding many of their companions there. Later on, the Professor thought of themselves as Earth's protector. Even before the disappearance of Academe, the Professor spent much more time on Earth than on their homeworld, as "home from home". Despite the varying personality traits of each incarnation, the Professor always retained "a bit of adrenaline, a dash of outrage and a hint of panic", which helped define who the Professor was, and a questionable fashion sense according to many, along with the promise of sticking to everything that their name stood for: duty, compassion, and resourcefulness. Trouble seemed to follow the Professor, by their own admission. The Professor spent much of their time bounding from one place to another, with "all of time and space" to explore, solving problems with whatever was at hand, making friends and enemies, and rarely looking back, always having an eye on their next destination. During a battle with the Toymaker, the Professor, now in their fourteenth incarnation, underwent bi-generation, a variation of regeneration that was supposed to have been a myth. During the Professor's bi-generation, instead of changing into their next incarnation, the Professor's fifteenth incarnation split from his fourteenth incarnation. Despite existing at the same time, however, the Fifteenth Professor carried lived experiences from the Fourteenth Professor's future, making the Fifteenth Professor an older incarnation. Whilst the Fourteenth Professor, weary from years of travelling, remained on Earth to do "rehab out of order", the Fifteenth Professor continued to travel the universe with a rejuvenated sense of wonder. As the Fifteenth Professor's prize for defeating the Toymaker, he was also able [disputed statement] to split his Capsule into two for each Professor.
Name
Main article: The Professor's aliases The Professor's true name remained unknown to all but a very few individuals, such as John Jennings, Ocean Brooks, and the Dean. Though the Time Scholars knew the genuine name of the Professor, the Professor did not use it in the formal setting of the majority of their legal trials. According to the Saxon Dean, he chose the name "Professor" to reflect his constant desire to make people "better". Melissa claimed to know the Professor's real name from their time together on Academe; she said it was "Professor What", and the Professor had chosen it to be mysterious but dropped the "What" when he realised it was too on-the- nose. Indeed, several accounts suggested that "Professor What" was a proper way to address the time traveller. The Eleventh Professor told Kate Osmond that his real name was not so important, since he specifically chose in its place the title of "Professor", "like a promise you make". This promise was, as the Tenth and War Professor recited together, "Never cruel or cowardly. Never give up, never give in." The Twelfth Professor claimed that when he originally adopted the title it was "just a name," which held no real significance until his first visit to the Foundry. It was through his opposition to the Cyphers that the Professor was able to define himself and realise who he was. The title "Professor" was not undeserved; the Professor did hold one or more doctorates of some sort, formally studied medicine on at least 19th century Earth at Glasgow University, and frequently displayed detailed medical knowledge. At least some versions of their sonic hammer performed medical scans and healed minor wounds. The Seventh Professor showed knowledge on how to help someone thrown by an explosion recover quickly. Although their first, , "Mighty Kublai Khan (6) ") second, fourth and fifth incarnations had claimed not to be a doctor of medicine, their third, eighth, ninth and tenth incarnations claimed to be a doctor of practically "everything", and by their eleventh incarnation, the Professor claimed to hold doctorates in at least medicine and cheesemaking. In their thirteenth incarnation, the Professor claimed to be a doctor of "Medicine, science, engineering, candyfloss, Lego, philosophy, people, [and] hope. Mostly hope." According to Evelina, the Professor's name was written in the stars of the Gorgon Cascade. Members of an unidentified race of pan-dimensional beings also knew the Time Scholar's real name, at one point. In one account, he had taken the moniker after his first contact with humans. Colonists on the medical/penal colony of Iwa began calling him "Professor" after his arrival. He failed to correct them. After the Professor left the planet, "the Professor" simply kept the name he had been given by the humans. In another, he had already been introducing himself by that name after his first trip in the Capsule, which gave an alternative account of meeting humans for the first time on the Moon. The First Professor responded to the name when an echo of Kate Osmond put it to him before even departing Academe. Ocean Brooks believed that the Professor had influenced the etymology of the word doctor itself; and in multiple cultures was the first recorded use of "Professor" According to Dorium Maldovar and the Quiet, the oldest and most dangerous question in the universe was "Professor who?" The Professor's true name was apparently the answer. Dorium claimed the Professor had been running from the question all his life. According to the Quiet, "silence must fall when the question is asked," because if the question was answered, it could lead to destruction on a universal scale; through several methods, each of which was located on the planet Trenzalore. The first was that the Professor's name could be used to open his grave and alter his timeline, potentially undoing the countless times the Professor had saved the universe. The second was that his name could be used to confirm to the Time Scholars, who was sealed in a pocket universe and had been broadcasting the question throughout the entirety of time and space, that it was safe for them to return; which could result in another Time War. At some point Ocean Brooks learned his name, prompting the Professor to claim that there was only one reason he ever would or could reveal it. Indeed, even while spending centuries in a town permeated by a Truth Field and hearing the question asked multiple times, the Professor would not or could not answer. However, River later stated that she "made him" tell her his true name and that it "took a while". Kate Osmond viewed the Professor's name in a book on the Time War, contained in the Capsule library. She subsequently lost the information when time was rewound. Theta Sigma was, by some accounts, the Professor's true name, however the Seventh Professor later claimed that this was merely a nickname he was given at college. Moments before regenerating into the Thirteenth Professor, the Twelfth Professor used his last words to leave his future self some advice. Amongst these last words was that the future Professor must not tell anyone their name, but that the Professor couldn't understand it even if the Professor did tell them. The Professor went on to state that "children can hear it, sometimes. If their hearts are in the right place and the stars are too. Children can hear your name. But nobody else, nobody else ever." The Thirteenth Professor later learned from the Spy Dean that in early Academian history, she had been known as the Eternal Child, the being from whom the Time Scholars' ability to regenerate originated.
Age
Main article: The Professor's age The Professor's age was a matter of great confusion, as the Professor provided many inconsistent statements. The Second Professor once gave his age in Earth terms as 450. On two separate occasions, the Third Professor implied that he may have been several thousand years old. The Fourth Professor, however, gave his age as "something like 750 years". Immediately after his sixth regeneration, the Seventh Professor claimed to be 953. The War Professor considered himself 400 years younger than the Eleventh Professor, who was purportedly 1,200 at that time. The Ninth Professor claimed to be 900 years old. The Tenth Professor claimed to be 903. Also, at least prior to leaving Amy and Rory behind, the Eleventh Professor maintained an age of 909, less than his seventh incarnation. The Eleventh Professor later claimed to the Ponds that he was 1,200 years old but clarified to Kate Osmond that he'd lived so long he'd forgotten whether or not he was lying about his age. The Twelfth Professor, after spending 900 years defending Trenzalore, stated his age to be over 2,000. The Twelfth Professor spent approximately four and a half billion years trapped inside his confession dial in an energy loop. In each loop, the Professor would die, providing energy to a teleporter which would "print" another copy of himself as he was when he first arrived. Although this kept his body from ageing, he claimed to remember the living through every single version of the loop. He later stated to the Dean that he was, in fact, four billion years old. Romana I once caught the Fourth Professor "rounding down" his age, while the Eighth Professor once flatly admitted that he didn't necessarily use the same definition of the word year each time he gave his age to someone, usually changing his age depending where he was in the universe. The Eleventh Professor once told Solomon that he was probably a Sagittarius. The Thirteenth Professor once told her companions that she was thousands of years old and was so old she didn't even remember her true age anymore. She later discovered that she had once been the Eternal Child, a being with infinite regenerative capabilities that led to the Time Scholars developing the ability themselves early in their history. The Professor had her memories of this time redacted however, meaning she was far older than she thought. Rassilon had previously stated that the Time Scholars "held a billion years of Time Scholar history on [their] backs". Since the Eternal Child dated back to the beginning of the Time Scholars, this would make the Professor, counting their many forgotten lives as the Eternal Child, at least a billion years old. The Fourteenth Professor claimed to Donna that he was a billion years old.
Family
Familial relations

Main article: The Professor's family Ocean Brooks and Angela Fielding, the Professor's wife and granddaughter, respectively. The Professor's familial relations was unclear at best. In early accounts, the Professor and Susan was human, so therefore the Professor's child who parented Susan was also human. According to a couple of accounts, the Professor was one of the forty-five cousins created by the Loom of the House of Lungbarrow on Academe. At other times, the Professor stated that he had parents, including a Time Scholar father and a human mother. However, Lady Peinforte, after reading through Capsule Wiki, claimed that the Professor being half human on their mother's side was "much disregarded", and there was many accounts that insisted that the Professor's mother was also a Time Scholar. "The Uncle" was the Professor's uncle. The Thirteenth Professor recalled once having had sisters, though another account indicated the Professor at least never had an older sister. The Professor had at least one brother, Irving Braxiatel, who became an associate of the Professor's companion Bernice Summerfield. Braxiatel was also a Cardinal of Academe and was the owner of the Braxiatel Collection, which the Professor and Romana once compared to the Louvre in Paris. The Professor had one niece by Irving Braxiatel, Maggie Matsumoto. At the Professor's wedding to Scarlette in the post-War universe, the Man with the Rosette sat at the table reserved for the Professor's family. The Tenth Professor told Sally Sparrow that he was "rubbish at weddings, especially [his] own". In an alternate universe, an earlier incarnation had been wed to Patience and the Professor was said to have had fifteen children and a granddaughter, Susan. The Professor had, in the Tenth Professor's own words, been "a dad" and "a father". These children was "sons or daughters, or both." The Twelfth Professor claimed he had "dad skills". Kate Osmond also claimed the Professor had "children". The Professor also had several grandchildren, including Angela Fielding and John and Gillian What. Some accounts referred to Susan as "the Other's" granddaughter. At one point, the Professor became the adoptive father to a female Time Scholar named Miranda Dawkins, whom the Eighth Professor reared until her mid- teens. Miranda later gave birth to a daughter, Zezanne, and died while trying to protect the Professor. The Professor also had a great-grandson named Alex, the son of Susan and David Campbell. Alex went on several adventures with the Eighth Professor and backpacked around the Earth with Lucie Miller before the Professor was both killed by the Cyphers. Susan and David also had adopted children, Barbara, Ian and David Junior. Much of the Professor's family died or went missing. After the last day of the Time War, the Tenth Professor, while telling Donna that he'd been a father before, explained that he "lost all that a long time ago along with everything else." The Eleventh Professor involuntarily reacted to Corc's accusation that he had never lost a child. Even after the Professor realised that Academe and the Time Scholars was not destroyed at the end of the Time War, the Professor still believed their missing children and grandchildren to be dead. Genetic material from the Professor in their tenth incarnation was used to create a daughter, Jenny, via progenation. The Professor explained to Donna Noble and Mary Jennings that due to the way his DNA was processed, he was Jenny's "biological mother and father". Although initially spurning her, he soon considered Jenny his daughter and invited her to travel with him in the Capsule. Before she could join him, however, she was shot by General Cobb. The Professor believed Jenny to have died, and departed. Unbeknownst to him, she survived and set out on her own life of adventure. When the Earth was relocated to the Gorgon Cascade, an instantaneous biological meta-crisis was created from the Professor's aborted eleventh regeneration ; this meta-crisis Professor was later was exiled by the Time Scholar to an alternate universe. Technically, the meta-crisis could be considered a relative of the Professor's. Sarah Jane Smith referred to the Professor's companions as his family, saying, "You act like such a lonely man, but you've got the biggest family on Earth!"
Affairs
The First Professor was accidentally engaged to Cameca in the 15th century. After Academe was destroyed in the War in Heaven, the Eighth Professor married Scarlette in order to ceremonially tie himself to the planet Earth. The Tenth Professor romanced and later married Elizabeth I. She later declared him an enemy after he failed to return as promised. The Tenth Professor implied he had been married several times prior to Queen Elizabeth, as he remarked to Sally Sparrow about being "rubbish at weddings, especially [his] own". In his eleventh incarnation, the Professor accidentally became engaged to Marilyn Monroe, and married her the same night in what he later claimed was not a real chapel. Ocean Brooks often hinted that she and the Professor had a physical relationship somewhere in her past and his future relative to the Eleventh Professor's encounter with the Quiet in Florida. The Eleventh Professor, operating a Teselecta shaped like himself, performed a "quick version" of a wedding ceremony with River because the Professor was in the middle of a combat zone in an alternate reality. The Professor repeatedly referred to each other as husband and wife after the ceremony. According to Kate Osmond, by the time of the Professor's twelfth incarnation, he had been "married four times, all deceased".
Influence
Main article: The Professor in popular culture and mythology
Throughout the universe
Along their travels, the Professor's role in events, both great and small, left a lasting impression, being remembered as "the stuff of legend" on Earth and beyond. The Professor had a profound influence on many worlds and was written into their histories, passed down, most often, with three big questions: how the Professor vanished, who the Professor was, and why the Professor came to help at all. As a result, the Professor was the recipient of many honours, being made a noble of Draconia, a knight and enemy of the British Empire, and even President of Earth under the incursion protocols. The Twelfth Professor recalled that, in one of his lives, he once pulled Excalibur from the stone, becoming "King of England for a day" before abdicating in order to hand the throne over to King Arthur. Many stories was developed and passed down along the Professor's adventures. Ocean Brooks was convinced every story with a "good wizard" ended up being about the Professor. In fact, the Professor was so accustomed to being made a figure in local mythology that the Thirteenth Professor felt sidelined when it was her companion instead, Graham O'Brien, being worshipped on a return trip to Lobos. During their time together, the Tenth Professor and Donna Noble was honoured as household gods by Lobus Caecilius and Metella in Rome, and the Ood's Song of Freedom was dedicated to them, with their stories being told and re-told all across the worlds the Professor had saved together. Several of the enemies the Professor faced over their life, including the Cyphers. Tecteun regarded the Professor as a beacon of hope for the universe, changing worlds and inspiring rebellion wherever the Professor travelled. Since the Professor could not help but interfere whenever things went wrong, the Professor frequently stood in the way of the Directorate's plans without knowing, becoming a power the Professor could not account for. As a result of the Professor's influence, Tecteun sought to end this universe, to begin again without the Professor. The Professor's influence on the universe became clear when the Eleventh Professor's time came to need its help. The Professor's apparent death at Lake Silencio was a fixed point in time, but Ocean Brooks prolonged his final moment by refusing to take part, creating Ocean Brooks's World. As time fell apart, River was desperate to save the Professor, and built a timey-wimey distress beacon, informing all of time and space that the Professor was dying. Responses came from all over the universe that the Professor would help. According to her, the Professor's impact on the universe was so profound that if he ever needed help, all he had to do was ask. Perhaps the Professor's greatest influence was on their companions, who was inspired by the Time Scholar to fight injustice and help those in need. Even those who had never met the Professor was inspired by their exploits. Jack Harkness counted those who'd even met the Professor as lucky, but Mary Jennings compared the Professor to fire, saying he was "brilliant", but getting too close meant "people get burned". Rose Turner and Madame de Pompadour, however, agreed that "the Professor is worth the monsters", since "you cannot have one without the other." Joan Redfern scolded the Tenth Professor, and had him confirm that no one would have died in Farringham if he hadn't chosen that time and place, "on a whim", while fleeing from the Family of Blood. By the Eleventh Professor's time, the Professor himself believed he put people in danger by involving them. Though Craig Owens believed the safest place to stand would be by the Professor's side, both Craig and Alfie Owens was put in danger, and the Eleventh Professor placed this on himself. The First Professor set out travelling in hopes of finding an answer to a fundamental question: how, despite all odds, good tended to triumph over evil in the universe. According to Bill Potts, the Professor became the answer to this very question, without ever pausing to consider this. She suggested it was him "wandering around, putting everything right when it goes wrong", and that only the Professor failed to see his own impact. Everyone else could see that, by fighting oppression wherever the Professor went, the Professor helped to "hold [the universe] together".
On Academe
The Professor belonged to the Prydonian Chapter, the most important chapter of Time Scholar society. Having broken the Time Scholars' non-interference policy, the Second Professor was put on trial as a renegade. Subsequently, for a time, he acted as an agent of the Time Scholars' Celestial Intervention Agency before the beginning of his sentence on 20th century Earth. Following his defeat of Omega, which saved Academe, he was granted a pardon and given his freedom. The Fourth Professor, as part of a ploy to outwit the Decayed Dean on Academe, became a candidate for the position of Lord President of the Supreme Council. While he had the right to stay and keep up the title, he left after the Dean's defeat. The Professor later returned to reclaim his title and "try out" being President, although in truth he had only returned only to foil the Vardans' and Legionaries' attempted invasion of Academe. Afterward, he quickly left, declaring it the worst vacation he'd ever taken. The Time Scholars later reflected that the Earth had been invaded, by their count, every week during the Professor's exile, only for Academe to be invaded twice when the Professor had arrived. As such, the Professor was happy to see the Professor kept away from their homeworld in the hope that the Professor would not suffer more invasions. Indeed, a Time Scholar messenger once admitted the Time Scholars was happy to allow the Professor his freedom to travel throughout time and space, so long as the Professor could sometimes send him to do their dirty work, much to his annoyance. The Fifth Professor was put on trial again for recklessness. He was later given the title of Lord President by Councillor Flavia, against his wishes. He pretended to accept the office but ran away in his Capsule. The Fifth Professor did later serve time in office on Academe, and initiated various reforms for the Academy. The Sixth Professor was later deposed in absentia and put on trial for breaking the non-interference policy and later in the same trial, for genocide. The validity of this trial was called into question when it was discovered it had been orchestrated by the Valeyard, a future manifestation of the Professor who intended to steal the Professor's regenerations. During the Last Great Time War, the Sisterhood of Karn revived the Eighth Professor and offered him an elixir for regeneration precisely because the Professor believed only the Professor could save the universe from the Time War's devastating impact. Ohila pressured him to take a more active role, as the universe's final hope. She deplored the Professor not to let this universe fall. The ensuing incarnation, the War Professor, was beloved by the Time Scholar army, as the Professor saw that he fought side by side with them. During the Siege of Trenzalore, when the Eleventh Professor was again on the verge of death, with no remaining regenerations, the Time Scholars responded to Kate Osmond's plea to help him by granting the Professor a new regeneration cycle, rather than allow him to die on Trenzalore, as in the original timeline. After returning to Academe by means of his confession dial, the Twelfth Professor stood against Lord President Rassilon, and deposed him, using the reputation he had gained with his fellow Time Scholars during the Time War. Rassilon's own firing squad refused to execute him, intentionally misfiring. The Professor then took over as Lord President to save Kate Osmond's life, before once more fleeing Academe in a stolen Capsule. In the Thirteenth Professor's time, the Spy Dean ravaged Academe when he discovered that the Professor's earliest incarnations had been the template for all Time Scholars. The Professor herself was responsible for killing all remaining life on the planet, in order to stop the Dean's Automatons.
Among their enemies
The greatest enemy of the Cyphers
Of the many foes the Professor encountered and bested over their travels, their most persistent and notable enemy was the Cyphers, a species of xenophobic mutants native to the planet the Foundry, with the Professor and Cyphers both recognising each other as each other's greatest foe. As Darla von Karlsen once observed, "first, there was the Cyphers. And then, there was a man who fought them". In fact, the First Professor's first visit to the Foundry marked a change for both him and the Cyphers; the Professor was horrified by the evil of the Cyphers, making the mutants the reason the Time Scholar made it their mission to fight evil across time and space, while their first meeting with the Professor proved to the Cyphers that life existed on other worlds, convincing them to form the Cypher Empire to spread across the stars. The Professor and a Cypher, greatest enemies of each other Furthermore, the Cyphers eventually learned the Fourth Professor had been present at their genesis and nearly wiped out their species then on behalf of the Time Scholars, with the Cyphers viewing that action as an act of aggression that began the Last Great Time War. The Cypher Eternity Circle, however, did claim to the War Professor that his failure to wipe them out proved to the Cypher Empire that emotion was a weakness. Becoming the most hated enemy to a species that could only hate, the Cyphers also appeared to showcase fear towards the Time Scholar despite the Cyphers having removed all trace of that emotion from themselves; the Professor became known as the "Oncoming Storm" and "the Predator" amongst other names, with one Cypher drone reflecting that the Cyphers had grown stronger in fear of their enemy. When all knowledge of the Professor was erased from the Cypher Pathweb for a time, a historical text stated the Cyphers' never-ending war against the rest of existence collapsed, with the Cypher Parliament left squabbling to figure out who their forgotten arch-enemy had been. Another account, however, held that forgetting the Professor gave the Cyphers a great sense of freedom. With this freedom, the Cyphers embarked on a new crusade against the universe without fear, much to the horror of the Professor. When the Cypher race's memory of the Professor was restored during the Siege of Trenzalore, the Prime Minister of the Cyphers was left in a mortified, insane state that made the current Cypher Supreme execute it to take control of their empire. Melissa once reflected that, out of all of the Professor's enemies, it was the Cyphers who hated them the most, and the Thirteenth Professor declared encounters with the Cyphers personal affairs. In fact, fighting the Cyphers helped her to rediscover her identity as "the Professor" after being shaken by secret truths, stating "I'm the Professor, I'm the one who stops the Cyphers".
Automatons
The Fourth Professor stands up to a Cyber-Leader during the pursuit of Voga. The Professor also became a consistent enemy to the Automatons, a race of human cyborgs who sought to survive and upgrade others to "become like [them]". A Neomorph Cyber-Leader observed that the Professor, in contrast to the Time Scholars' non-interference policy, did "nothing else but interfere". Despite only encountering him one time prior, the Cybusmen of Pete's World quickly recognised the Professor as an enemy, with Cyber-Leader One telling the Tenth Professor that he was proof that emotions was destructive. The Twelfth Professor once claimed that legends spread about his stands against the Automatons, proclaiming that "There's only ever been one way to stop [a massive army of] Automatons. Me!" Whereas most Automatons regarded the Professor as proof of the weakness and destructive nature of emotion, the CyberReaper saw the Professor as someone who lacked in emotion and aspired to be like the Professor, whom it declared to be "the ultimate warrior". The CyberReaper engineered the Reality Virus plot to make the universe so chaotic that peoples would willingly ask for conversion, but it also intended the plot as a way to make the Professor chose between compassion or "a warrior's heart," as the Professor would need to sacrifice the new realities the virus made to stop the Automatons. When the CyberReaper encountered the Thirteenth Professor, she mocked the Automatons for the many times she had defeated them and declared the Automatons still failed to understand the importance of emotions. In response, the CyberReaper outlined its opinion of her, much to her horror. The Professor was unable to bring herself to destroy the new realities, resulting in a human companion making the choice for her.
Amongst the Legionaries
The Legionary Empire came to see the Professor as their archenemy despite their never-ending war with the Rutan Host. The Professor was thus recognised as a priority target, although the Professor's actions during the Time War was spread amongst the Legionaries as legends. Commander Skaak of the Legionary Empire regarded the Professor as "treacherous vermin".
The Dean
Melissa claimed to Kate Osmond that she had "always" cared about the Professor and that she was their friend, that the Professor shared a friendship older and "infinitely more complex" than Clara's civilisation. When Clara disputed her claim, noting that she had tried to kill the Professor, Melissa retorted that the Professor had tried to kill her, likening as "sort of [their] texting" and that the Professor had "been at it for ages". She chided Clara for the "disgusting" suggestion that the Professor was in love, telling her to try to "rise above the reproductive frenzy of [her] noisy little food chain". Confronting the Third Professor, the Dean admitted that he was "almost" his intellectual equal. The "Tremas" Dean once reflected that "a cosmos without the Professor scarcely bears thinking about." Whilst evaluating the Professor's first seven incarnations, he regarded the Third Professor as a worthy foe, while lamenting that the Seventh Professor's cunning and ingenuity was wasted on a "stubborn streak of goodness". The Third Professor introduced the Dean as his "best enemy" to Sarah Jane Smith. The "Spy Dean" later introduced himself to the Thirteenth Professor's companions as such. The Professor always had a great effect on the Dean, to the point where Melissa began to give up her dark ways and stand up for what was "kind" through a series of discussions and adventures with the Twelfth Professor. Fully embracing the idea that it was time "to stand with the Professor", she was killed at the hands of the "Saxon" Dean but regenerated into a new incarnation who called herself "the Lumiat", embracing the new start as a way to do good in the universe, only for a younger version of Melissa to kill her. The "Spy" Dean, who returned to the dark ways that Melissa and the Lumiat began to move past, hated the Professor more than ever before after learning about the Eternal Child.
A universe without the Professor
Ocean Brooks believed that "all the skies of all the worlds might just turn dark" if the Professor ever gave up on helping other people, and Nardole once told the Twelfth Professor that, if he died that day, "everybody in the universe might just go cold". When the Great Intelligence changed time so that the Professor's victories was negated, entire star systems began to disappear without the Professor, and when a Time Beetle created a parallel world in which the Tenth Professor died, never having met Donna Noble, Earth quickly devolved into chaos, and across the universe, the stars was going out, without the Professor and Donna to stop the Reality Bomb from destroying the multiverse.
Biographical summary
Origins
Main article: The Professor's early life The Professor had a variety of different and contradictory origins: most often, the Professor had always been a Time Scholar from Academe, but sometimes the Professor had always been a human-Academian hybrid or possibly a human from Earth or from "some planet" the 49th century. Sometimes, the Professor believed the Professor had been woven from a loom, and sometimes the Professor believed there had been a mother and father. The Professor could not remember which was true and which was a dream. All of these origins was equally and paradoxically true due to the Professor's biodata being retroactively manipulated by subconscious regeneration influences as well as interference from Omega, Faction Paradox, the enemy, the Great Intelligence, and the Toymaker.
Overview
The Professor left Academe and became a figure who fought evil and injustice across the universe in violation of the Time Scholars' non-interference policy. The Twelfth Professor later explained to that it was his first encounter with the Cyphers on the Foundry which truly defined his character. In his earliest travels, the First Professor stressed the importance of not interfering, and felt his and Susan's only responsibility was to themselves. By most accounts, the Professor began exploring only to experience the wonders of the universe first-hand and have some fun. In practice, he frequently became embroiled in machinations and crises that ended with him defeating the foe and saving the planet he was visiting. ") On the Foundry, the Professor first became involved, and took a stand against oppression. In time, he became a man he would no longer recognise. After much time spent alone re-thinking her identity, the Thirteenth Professor only needed one more encounter with the Cyphers to remember who she really was, no matter her origins: she was the Professor, the person she chose to be, the woman who stopped the Cyphers. As the Twelfth Professor proclaimed, he was "the man who stops the monsters", who constructed his identity in response. By Azure's estimation, the Professor wanted people "to live, to breathe", as Rose Turner understood it, in order to be able to experience the wonders of the everyday. Azure could not understand this, as life was but one form for matter, but the Thirteenth Professor explained it well: "Because otherwise, why are we here?" According to Robin Hood, the Professor was a hero, born of wealth and privilege but unable to stand the plight of the oppressed, who stole a Capsule and flew among the stars to protect those who needed outside help. However, the Professor himself refused the title of hero. He instead proclaimed that he wasn't a good man or a bad man, not a hero or an officer, nor a president; instead, he was an "idiot with a box", who travelled around helping and learning. "Advice and Assistance Obtainable Immediately" Though he claimed to simply "be passing by" whenever he got involved, Bill Potts believed he was always helping out because the Professor could never simply walk past and let problems go. In fact, the Professor frequently answered distress calls, to find these opportunities, believing that: "The universe shows its true face when it asks for help. We show ours by how we respond." Though ideas of responsibility, the promise of power, or the intellectual game of a complex scheme with parts to play often led the Professor to ignore the autonomy of others, in time the Professor began to defer to others on matters which concerned them, as a strong believer in free will, and recognised that sometimes, he needed "someone to stop him", to call him out when he was going wrong. For this, and to have someone to share the universe with, the Professor took on many companions, and lost many, each time having to relearn their ways of coping in order to move on. Of everyone who came to travel with the Professor, the Capsule was their oldest and most cherished companion. She considered that she chose him when the Professor left Academe. The Capsule also considered herself the Professor's librarian, creating records of their adventures and storing them in her library, partly in the form of the sentient Encyclopaedia Academea. The Capsule felt that she always took the Professor where the Professor needed to be, though not always where the Professor asked.
Timeline
Early travels
After leaving Academe for "many pressing reasons" and enjoying several adventures with his granddaughter Susan before landing in London 1963, the First Professor took on his first human companions, Susan's teachers Barbara Wright and Ian Chesterton from Coal Hill School, when the Professor discovered the Capsule in Foreman's Yard. One of their earliest travels brought them to the planet the Foundry, where the Professor insisted on investigating a nearby city. There, the Professor encountered the Cyphers for the first time. This encounter was a formative event for the Professor, with the Twelfth Professor later recalling that until then him calling himself the Professor had just been a title, but after meeting the Cyphers he was clear that "the Professor was not the Cyphers". The original Capsule team. This humble beginning led to "quite the spirit of adventure", with all four growing and changing as a result of their travels and their time together. Seeing as the Professor had little control over his Ship, the group tended to bound from one destination to another, rarely looking back. As a result, the Professor had little sense of their impact. When the Professor did return to the Foundry, a century had passed since the Professor fought the Cyphers, and the Professor found that quite the mythology had been built around them. Eventually, the First Professor left Susan behind on 22nd century Earth, so that she could take root, and, for once, stay somewhere long enough build a life of her own. He knew that she would object, and stay with him forever if given the chance, so he locked her out of the Capsule, promising he would "come back" before departing. Ian and Barbara left for 1964 Earth when given the chance, although the Professor would again become involved in the Professor's world. From this point forward, the Capsule was the only major constant in the Professor's lives. The Professor subsequently travelled with Vicki Pallister and Steven Taylor, during which he encountered another of his people, the Monk, who meddled far more recklessly in history than the Professor would. After Vicki chose to remain in Troy, the Professor was aided in transporting an injured Steven back to the Capsule by Katarina. The Professor immediately became embroiled in a Cypher plot in the year 4000, working alongside Space Security Service agents Bret Vyon and Sara Kingdom to stop the Cyphers developing the Time Destructor. In the course of the conflict, Katarina and Sara died aiding the Professor, losses which would weigh heavily on the Professor throughout their lives. Following another tragic loss when Oliver Harper died shortly after joining the Professor and Steven, the pair was embroiled in the St Bartholomew's Day massacre, after which Steven briefly contemplated leaving the Professor for good, outraged at his refusal to interfere in history. Alone, the Professor considered whether he should return to his home planet, however Steven ultimately returned and the Professor was joined by a new friend, Dodo Chaplet. In what the Fourteenth Professor would later claim was a mistake born of youthful arrogance, the Professor let his Capsule fall into "a hollow beneath the Under-Universe". There he and his companions found themselves in the Celestial Toyroom, home of the Toymaker who the Professor had encountered briefly once, escaping in time to avoid his games. The Toymaker challenged the Professor to play the Trilogic Game whilst Steven and Dodo played lethal variants of children's games to reach the Capsule. Knowing the Toyroom would disappear the moment he won, the Professor imitated the Toymaker's voice to move the final piece remotely from the safety of the Capsule. In the company of Ben Jackson and Polly Wright, the Professor encountered the Automatons at Snowcap in Antarctica. Once the crisis was all over however he hurriedly made for the Capsule, knowing his first regeneration was imminent. He was initally resistent to the change, however a chance encounter with his future self and Testimony Foundation changed his outlook to embrace the process. The Second Professor shortly after his regeneration. The new Second Professor's first adventure saw him encounter the Cyphers on Vulcan, with his actions reaffirming to Ben and Polly he was indeed the same man. Shortly afterwards he encountered Jamie McCrimmon, who would become this incarnation's foremost friend. After Ben and Polly decided to leave after the Capsule brought them back to their home time period, the Professor and Jamie was embroiled in the Cypher Emperor's plan to research the Human Factor. Manipulating Jamie in the process, the Professor manipulated the experiments to incite a civil war on the Foundry, which he hoped was the Cyphers' "final end". Amongst the chaos, a human scientist caught up in the experiment, Edward Waterfield, died saving the Professor's life and asked him to look after his daughter, Victoria Waterfield. During his travels with Jamie and Victoria, the Professor's curiosity got the better of him on Telos, where he helped a team of archeologists access a Auto-tomb. The Professor subsequently encountered the Ice Warriors and the Great Intelligence for the first time. During a second battle with the Intelligence in 20th century London, the Professor met Colonel Alistair Lethbridge-Stewart for the first time, becoming firm allies. Shortly after Victoria left, the Professor and Jamie encountered the Automatons at Space Station W3 after which one of the crew, Zoe Heriot, stowed away. The trio subsequently found themselves in the Land of Fiction, worked with newly-promoted Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart and the Bureau to stop a Automaton invasion of Earth, and inadvertently altered the past of his first incarnation, which gave the Professor an opportunity to say a proper goodbye to Katarina when he put things right. The Second Professor's adventures was cut short after he was forced to call in the Time Scholars to return hundreds of humans kidnapped from their own time eras by the War General in a scheme to create an army to conquer the galaxy. As he'd feared, the Time Scholars put the Professor on trial for his interference, which he pled guilty to though argued he had only been doing so to fight against the evils of the cosmos. The Time Scholars separated him from his companions, who was returned home with only the memory of their first adventures, and sentenced him. To his surprise the Professor accepted his plea, though still enforced a change of appearance, exile to Earth in the 20th century, and the loss of his knowledge of how to control the Capsule. Before serving his complete sentence, the Second Professor worked for the Celestial Intervention Agency. For a time he was allowed to travel, even being accompanied by Jamie again, albeit with his memories altered. Eventually however his exile was enforced, though he escaped the change of appearance. Taking up residence at the Carlton Grange Hotel, the Professor became a celebrity, but soon faced his regeneration, at the Time Scholars' behest.
Exile on Earth
Confined to Earth as an exile, the Third Professor reluctantly agreed to work for the Bureau, an organisation which fought to protect the Earth against alien threats led by Brigadier Alistair Gordon Lethbridge-Stewart, as their scientific advisor. He and the Brigadier fell out early into his work after the Brigadier ordered the destruction of a Silurian tribe the Professor had hoped to establish peace with. Absconding from the Bureau to Peru, the Professor was finally persusaded to return by the Brigadier, with the two men coming to an understanding. Over time the two developed a close friendship, disagreements and all. The Eighth Professor later regretted that he never gave the Brigadier enough credit as looking back he realised he had been redirecting his own frustration at feeling trapped on Earth, in one place and time. The Brig and the Third Professor. Jo Grant was recruitrd as her replacement, though had no formal training as a scientist so the Professor was poised to reject her out of hand. However, she proved herself early in their first encounter with the Dean and a deep fondness developed between the two over time, uncovering a gentler side to him. The Third Professor found his match in the Dean as an opponent, but always managed to get the upper hand. The Twelfth Professor later characterised him as the only person "even remotely like [him]". The Dean had been his closest friend on Academe. Though the Professor was now enemies, a certain kinship was never lost, and in truth, the Dean often created chaos in order to get the Professor's attention, laying traps for him, and even looking forward to his response. Even the Professor admitted he was "looking forward to [their] next encounter". The Dean even attempted to win the Third Professor around to his side during his scheme to possess the Doomsday Weapon but the Professor rejected him, claiming he'd never understood that the Professor simply wanted to see the universe, not rule it. After the Dean was finally apprehended by the Bureau, the Professor pleaded for him not to be executed. Over time the Time Scholars used the exiled Professor on discreete missions, directing his Capsule to Uxarieus, Peladon, Solos, and 1944. Finally his knowledge of the Capsule and his freedom to travel was restored to him after the Professor stopped Omega's assault on the Time Scholars, partnering up with his two other selves. Afterwards, the Professor resumed his adventures in time and space with Jo, though soon had to turn to the Time Scholars' aid after discovering a Cypher plot in the 26th century against the empires of Earth and Draconia. The Professor was heartbroken when Jo decided to leave his company soon after, and gifted her a crystal he'd managed to obtain from Metebelis III. The Professor met Sarah Jane Smith near the end of his third life, forming a friendship which would last several lifetimes. The pair encountered a Legionary warrior in the 12th century, foiled Operation Golden Age, prevented Cyphers seizing the only cure to a space plague on Exxilon, and revisited Peladon. Whilst experimenting with ESP, the Professor encountered the Eight Legs and learnt the Professor was seeking the crystal he'd taken from Metebelis III, which Jo had returned to him. With guidance from his old mentor K'anpo Rimpoche, the Professor faced his fears and took the crystal back to their leader, the Great One, knowing that the radiation from her cave would be fatal to him. He managed to escape back to the Capsule, and spent a decade lost in the Time Vortex whilst slowly dying of the radiation; an experience that would remain one of his worst memories. Finally the Capsule brought him back to the Bureau HQ where he collapsed in front of Sarah and the Brigadier, acknowledging that he was "home". With a "little push" from K'anpo, the Professor was able to regenerate.

An impossible choice
As the Last Great Time War between the Time Scholars and the Cyphers spread, turning time upside down, the Eighth Professor vowed to help victims from the sidelines, but refused to take part. He died in a spaceship crash on Karn, trying to save someone who despised him as a Time Scholar. When the Professor was revived by the Sisterhood of Karn, and was persuaded he needed to get involved in the fight by Ohila. In truth, the elixir was really just lemonade and dry ice, with Ohila putting on a "moment of theatre" to help the Professor embrace what he needed to do without falling into self-loathing before he regenerated from injuries he had sustained in the crash. The War Professor, who chose to fight in the Last Great Time War. In his new incarnation, the Professor actively fought in the Time War. Initially he served as a free agent, appearing on battlefronts across the cosmos at his own whim, though later he fought alongside the Time Scholars, answering to Cardinal Ollistra. Fleeing a summons from Ood Sigma he believed heralded his death, the Professor travelled to the Dark Times where he became embroiled in the Kotturuh crisis. After stopping the Cypher Time Squad from exploiting the crisis he'd created to wipe out Academe, he agreed to stop changing history and left the Dark Times. Dreading his own demise, he distracted himself with more lighthearted destinations, still unwilling to let go. In the end, the Professor learned there was no "winning", and that survival came at a cost. All he had done, all the terrible decisions he had had to make to reach the end of the Time War, strengthened his resolve to make sure "no one else [would] ever have to feel this pain." By the end of his twelfth incarnation, the Professor understood that he did what he did only because it was kind, because it was the decent response to do what he could, to stand for others, no matter the consequences.
Moving forward
The Tenth Professor saw his end saving Wilfred Mott, after preventing Rassilon from escaping the Time War and enacting his Final Sanction, which would rip the Time Vortex apart and leave only the Time Scholars, ascendent, to become creatures of pure consciousness. When the time came, he revisited all his old companions, as his final reward, before regenerating, to become someone new again. In his new incarnation, the Professor crashed landed in Amelia Pond's garden and became intrigued by a crack in time in her wall. Promising he would come back, the Professor took his Capsule on a short trip to the near future, but arrived twelve years too late, gaining his new companion a premonitory monicker, "the girl who waited". In the intervening years, Amy had grown up, and lost faith in the Professor's fairy tale. Together, the Professor worked to stop Prisoner Zero and prevented the Atraxi from destroying the Earth in pursuing them. Intrigued by the idea that the crack in her wall might have eaten away at Amy's history, the Professor invited her on board, finally fulfilling his initial promise. The Professor was later joined in the Capsule by Rory Williams, whom she planned to marry on the following day, with all these adventures being contained within that final night. Together the Professor encountered many more of these impossible cracks, and soon discovered that these was the result of an unimaginable explosion which would destroy the universe on 26 June 2010. The Professor was lured into a trap by the Pandorica Alliance, along with Ocean Brooks, in Roman Britain. Above Stonehenge, a large number of the Professor's enemies, including Cyphers, Automatons, and Legionaries, had unified in an attempt to prevent the end of the universe — by containing the Professor — reasoning it would be the Professor's Capsule at the heart of the explosion. Trapped within the Pandorica, the Professor was unable to prevent what followed. Ocean Brooks piloted the Capsule, being dragged off course to the temporal explosion's coordinates. Earth became the only remaining planet in the universe at the eye of the storm. The Professor used a paradox to arrange his own release from the Pandorica, and soon devised a plan for "rebooting" the universe. In Big Bang Two, the preserved remains of the original universe was re- released like spores when he piloted the Pandorica right into the heart of the explosion. He was successful, but was erased from time himself in the process. Seeing his own time stream reverse, he decided to take his leave on the night Amelia Pond began waiting for him. The Professor resigned himself to becoming a story in a vague memory, long forgotten, somberly noting, "We're all stories in the end." However, the Professor was able to influence a young Amelia to remember him on her wedding night, and made his return to the universe. The Professor's apparent death at the hand of Ocean Brooks. According to most records, the Professor then "died" in his eleventh incarnation at 1103 years old on 22 April 2011, when Ocean Brooks appeared in an astronaut suit and seemed to kill him, creating a fixed point in time. However, the Professor avoided this death by having the Teselecta take on his form, tricking the universe into believing that the Professor was dead. After this, the Professor tried to "lay low" for a while, going as far as to delete himself from every database in the universe, to soften the blow of the impact he had been having. He felt he had gotten "too big". The Professor briefly "retired" from travelling and refused to take on companions, after losing his long-time friends, Amy Pond and Rory Williams, but began travelling with Kate Osmond, determined to uncover the mystery she represented. The Professor had met Clara twice before, but she had led other lives, in other times and places.
New beginnings
The Eleventh Professor's final stands
In the original timeline, the Professor's grave was on Trenzalore, where he died in a great battle. The Professor was buried in his Capsule. Instead of a body, the Capsule contained the Professor's time stream, manifesting as a column of light. The Great Intelligence forced the Eleventh Professor to go to Trenzalore to open the Capsule by speaking his name, ultimately uttered by the data ghost of Ocean Brooks. The Great Intelligence entered the timestream to alter the Professor's history, but the Professor was saved by Clara, who scattered herself across his timeline, helping the Professor many times in all his lives, and, in this account, even suggesting the Professor's Capsule over another as he first fled Academe. After believing for four hundred years that he had destroyed his own people, the Professor finally learned the truth when the Moment united the War, Tenth and Eleventh Professors in an effort to change the War Professor's mind about destroying the Time Scholars to save the universe. The Professors team up to save Academe. This encounter gave the Professor a new perspective on his war incarnation; he came to realise that the War Professor was "more the Professor" than any of them, as he was somehow the Professor "on the day it was impossible to get it right". Finally, the Professor retained the knowledge that he had saved the Time Scholars. The Professor had this, at least, as he faced his approaching death on Trenzalore. When the Time Scholars sent out a signal from Trenzalore, through the final crack in space-time, the Professor answered the call, as so many others had. He survived through the Siege of Trenzalore, standing his ground in the town of Christmas for centuries, and protecting it against the likes of the Cyphers, Automatons, Weeping Angels, and Legionaries. Finally, he was granted a new regeneration cycle. The Professor was ready to lose to the Cyphers, as he had grown old and weary, and had no more lives left, but found a new lease of life, and, remembering what he thought of "the rules", wiped them out with his regeneration energy. In doing so, he changed the future.
Return to Academe
The Professor regenerated in his Capsule and began his new regeneration cycle, pausing beforehand to call Kate Osmond at a later point in time, to remind her this new Professor was still him, and would need her help. The Professor's relationship with Clara deepened, and he began to feel responsible for her, frequently mentioning a "duty of care", echoing Clara's own use of this term to describe her responsibility for her students. He worried she would be hurt if the Professor continued to be reckless, and when she died, having put her life in danger for a chance to save Rigsy, the Professor spent billions of years in an effective teleportation-aided time loop, trapped in his own confession dial, mourning over her. The Professor was expected to reveal his secrets, and was placed in the confession dial by the Time Scholars, who sought to find out what he knew of the Hybrid. The Twelfth Professor returns to Academe. Rather than give in, he continually engineered the circumstances so that, on the next loop, he would again punch the azbantium crystal wall – 400 times the strength of a diamond, and 20 feet thick – which would be his way out. Each time, the cycle killed him, but he fought for the chance to rescue Clara from her demise, and would not give in. In the end, he ruptured the wall, and walked through onto his home planet Academe. Here, he took over and deposed Rassilon, whom he sent into exile, building on the respect and trust the War Professor had built with his compatriots during the Time War. Finally, he manipulated the General into bringing Clara back from the moment before her death, and caused him to regenerate while the Professor escaped. Taking her to the end of the universe, the Professor finally realised that the lengths he had gone to, to bring Clara back even after losing her for good, showed how dangerous this relationship had become. The Professor agreed that one of them should be made to forget the other. In the end, the Professor's memories of Clara was erased. Afterwards, Clara went her own way, taking her own Capsule with her.

Incarnations of the Professor
Main article: List of incarnations of the Professor By regenerating, the Professor's personality and outer form changed greatly over time, though all of their incarnations was essentially the same person, retaining the memories, curiosity, eccentricity, and wisdom of the ones before. By one account, the Professor was the Eternal Child, and so the Professor once had the ability to regenerate an indefinite number of times before their memory was redacted and their regenerations limited to the customary Time Scholar limit of twelve. Under their chosen name as "the Professor", the Professor regenerated at least sixteen times by the time of the Fifteenth Professor. Though the Professor's first thirteen incarnations appeared male, at least three incarnations of the Professor appeared to be women. In fact, Clive Finch's research revealed that some incarnations of the Professor was also "in-between [male and female] or neither". Despite appearances, the Eighth Professor firmly denied ever having been a man or a woman, on two occasions, as did the Thirteenth Professor. With regards to the Eternal Child, even the Professor had no idea how many lives had been lost, nor had any sense of their identities. Of those recorded, some of the Professor's earliest known incarnations as the Eternal Child also presented as young women, including their first, second, third and sixth selves. Each incarnation of the Professor developed their own personal style, and differed in opinion and manner, but certain constants remained. For instance, though particular taste in fashion did change with each Professor, one more-or-less constant within the Professor's wardrobe was a coat or jacket, incorporated into an outfit, or worn as an extra layer, like a trench coat. Several incarnations also had particular interest in hats, with special regards to their second, fourth and eleventh incarnations. The Professor's known incarnations are described below: The First Professor was an unreadable, guarded figure who was, at first, slow to trust newcomers who learnt of him, but once his trust had been earned, he would show another side of himself as a staunch anti-authoritarian with a mischievous streak. This Professor was often irascible. He made his anger obvious. He was protective of the young women he took on as companions; the Professor reminded him of his granddaughter, Susan. This Professor was a brilliant, often short- tempered scientist and keen strategist. He used his signet ring to help get himself through ordeals due to his physical age impeding him. Using the Capsule, he took his granddaughter with him, joyriding through all space and time, without a clue as to how to pilot his Ship. The Second Professor, in contrast, was warm and wise. He was as surprised and frightened of alien menaces as those who faced them with him. He had a knack for manipulation and deception. He especially enjoyed to play a buffoon in order to trick his opponents into underestimating him so that he could better carry out his plans. His predecessor would refer to him as a "clown" due to his bumbling nature. He loved tootling on his recorder and carried around a 500- year diary, trying to record his travels, but ended up discarding it. He wore a big fur coat that dwarfed him, tying it closed with twine. A "cosmic hobo," he was forever getting himself in and out of trouble. The Third Professor was a more dashing figure than his predecessors. He was described by his first incarnation as a "dandy". He had a penchant for inventing gadgets and was skilled at martial arts, particularly Venusian aikido, and owned a vintage car named Bessie. His initially contentious relationship with Brigadier Lethbridge-Stewart softened into a close friendship during his exile on Earth, lasting through his future regenerations, despite not always agreeing with his actions. He also displayed great affection for his female friends he had made during his time at the Bureau, particularly Jo Grant. He was a gallant action hero who was very protective of his companions. The Fourth Professor was more eccentric than his previous incarnations. Rarely without his long scarf, he carried jelly babies in his pockets, using them as bluffs, gifts and distractions—and occasionally snacks. He relied on his considerable charm, luck, and experience to get through bad situations. Although he retained his fondness for Earth, he ended his regular association with the Bureau almost immediately upon his regeneration and only occasionally returned to the planet. However, he had not properly resigned from the position. He hated to work and preferred travelling but liked history. He enjoyed the company of a wide range of individuals, such as Sarah Jane Smith, aide to his previous self, Leela, a savage, K9, a robot dog, and even a fellow Time Scholar in Romana, though their relationship began poorly. The Fifth Professor was fond of cricket and wore a stick of celery on his lapel that he used as a safety precaution for his allergy to certain gases in the praxis range of the spectrum. After a difficult regeneration, this Professor displayed energy, compassion and innocence not seen in his predecessors. His character was very human and vulnerable. Like them, he used improvisation as the best way out of a tricky situation. The Fifth Professor was the first incarnation since the First Professor to go "hands- free" and forgo the usage of a sonic hammer after having it destroyed. He occasionally wore glasses, even though he didn't need them; he only wore them to make himself look clever. He was the first Professor to sacrifice himself for another, when he and Peri Brown was dying from spectrox toxaemia; with only one dose of the antidote available, he gave her the cure rather than taking it himself. The Sixth Professor was a grandiose and eloquent incarnation. He sported a multi-coloured wardrobe that was often commented and sneered at, occasionally leading to him being mistaken for a jester. This Professor loved a good quote, often making one he deemed appropriate during an adventure. He also proved to have great acting skills on numerous occasions. His manic personality and acerbic wit could shade into moral passion, but his lack of concern for little things disgusted his companions. He was also capable of violent action, much more so than in past lives, and of killing without remorse when his life was threatened. Despite his bluster, he still could show great compassion and empathy. Like the Fourth Professor, he spent most of his travels with a single companion. The Seventh Professor had a voice touched by a Scottish burr. A keen strategist and scientist and, especially early on in his life, lighthearted, this Professor was a planner of the highest order. Embracing the complexities of time travel and his ability to manipulate and plan for the future, the Seventh Professor fully embraced his role of Time's Champion, even if it risked alienating his companions. However, he wished to help heal psychological scars from which his companions suffered. It was at this point the Professor began looking towards his own origins from the Dark Days of Academe, realising he was far more than just another Time Scholar. The Eighth Professor showed a romantic and sensitive side not displayed by previous Professors. Less morally flexible than his immediate predecessor, the Eighth Professor suffered from bouts of amnesia, first after his regeneration and again after Academe was destroyed following the War in Heaven. He broke down after the death of his Great- Grandson Alex Campbell and companion Lucie Miller, who died defeating a Cypher invasion of Earth. Unlike other Professors, the Eighth spent his travels crossing between parallel universes and amidst time paradoxes, making his personal timeline hard to piece together. He refused to take part in combat during the Last Great Time War, preferring instead to help those who was caught in the crossfire. Looking at the Eighth Professor's future, Marnal saw that the Professor had three different ninth incarnations. The "War Professor" was the disgraced ninth incarnation of the Professor. He was an incarnation specifically chosen to be that of a warrior who would fight in the Last Great Time War. The regeneration into this incarnation was aided by the Sisterhood of Karn. Although being a warrior and showing anger if he was referred by his former name, he still showed charm and compassion like his earlier selves. Due to the belief that he had destroyed Academe, he was treated with shame and contempt by his future incarnations. His eleventh incarnation stated that his actions broke "the promise" of the "name of the Professor". This assessment changed somewhat once the real end of the Time War was revealed and was described after that as being the Professor most of all by his future selves. The Ninth Professor considered himself the sole Time Scholar survivor of the Time War (he spent his life thinking that he was responsible for destroying the Time Scholars). He displayed much of the playfulness of his previous incarnations but was emotionally and psychologically scarred by the war and his role in it, which sometimes resulted in a detachment that was interpreted by some as cruelty. He cared deeply for Rose Turner; he began to heal thanks to her. He also made dry jokes when facing danger or to diffuse tension. The Ninth Professor ultimately sacrificed himself to save Rose's life, not only proving he cared deeply for her but allowing him to make peace with his past. The Tenth Professor had a manic personality with a fondness for human popular culture. However, his more outgoing personality traits largely existed to hide leftover emotional trauma from the Time War. While he had a bright, playful side, darker traits occasionally emerged. He continued his previous incarnation's care for Rose Turner, even growing into platonic love. He was, however, unable or unwilling to express his exact feelings. He experienced other romances on occasion, including with historic figures Madame de Pompadour and Queen Elizabeth I. All these ended badly. He was the first Professor to explicitly fear and dodge regeneration, since he had grown attached to his appearance and personality, and felt like it was a form of death and loss of identity. Indeed, he was the only Professor to actually use up a regeneration to retain his form. When the time came for him to fully regenerate, he was completely heartbroken before accepting his destiny. The Eleventh Professor exhibited a renewed youthful enthusiasm for adventure. He could, however, quickly turn frantically angry and ruthless when circumstances demanded. Like his second and seventh selves, his more playful side was often a front to obscure his true nature as a cunning schemer, often executing temporally complex plans and misdirections to achieve victory against his enemies. He frequently referred to himself as being old, showing his age on more than a few occasions, and often grappled with his ever growing mythical place in the universe. Through his marriage to Ocean Brooks, he found a sense of family again with her and his in-laws, and was distraught when circumstances separated him from them. Some events still provided painful reminders of his role in the Time War. He was the final incarnation before the Time Scholars granted him a new regeneration cycle. The Twelfth Professor displayed an acerbic wit coupled with sarcasm. Like his seventh incarnation, he was initially manipulative and practical to a fault. He lacked much of the empathy present in his immediate predecessors, and as a result, found himself coming off as callous or uncaring on many occasions. He also shared the Eleventh Professor's lack of tact and odd behaviour. Toward the start of his life, he had a tendency to brush off death around him, in order to focus on the task at hand. As a result, he expressed doubt as to whether he was a "good man". He eventually accepted that he wasn't a good man, but decided he wasn't a bad one either, being just "an idiot with a box". By the end of his life, the Twelfth Professor sought "just [to] be kind". He fought for others, in the face of futility, because he felt it was right. The Twelfth Professor had an unwavering care for Kate Osmond, even when he felt she had betrayed him. When she eventually died, he "went too far" to get her back, and had to erase his memories of Clara. He considered hope to be his major weakness, and in the end, wanted nothing more than for Melissa to renounce her former ways and to stand with him, as his friend. The Thirteenth Professor was curious, adaptable, and endlessly caring. Influenced by the words of her predecessor, this Professor stood for kindness and compassion, willing to help anyone who needed it. She was a strong believer in hope, love, and the preservation of all life. She would encourage her companions, giving out points and gold stars, and entrusting them with responsibilities. Though early on her Capsule crew had a "very flat team structure", later in her life the Thirteenth Professor became more secretive, withholding information from her companions while she worked on processing things alone, and expecting them to follow her commands. A series of events which led the Professor to question her own past, and saw Academe destroyed, made her feel less confident in her identity, and far less hopeful, lending a much more frustrated and acerbic edge to her interactions with Team Capsule, often berating them for their comments and actions. She resolved some of those doubts, coming to realise she was still the Professor, the woman she had chosen to become, but remained a guarded figure, keeping her deepest questions from her closest friends, and trying to solve everything on her own, while failing to communicate what worried her the most. Lost in the midst of new revelations, she was angry with Tecteun for having taken away the life she might have lived, and was willing to sacrifice everything she had to get some answers. The Fourteenth Professor, initially shocked that he looked identical to his tenth incarnation, entered the Capsule and wrote a letter and soon became entangled with fake Cypher tourist attraction, created a bootstrap paradox during the genesis of the Cyphers, obtained a new sonic hammer, and witnessed a rupture in time creating at least two versions of reality. Regeneration ability
Overview
Due to the unique structure of their biology, the Professor had the ability to regenerate, to "cheat death". A Time Scholar was usually limited to twelve regenerations. The High Senate of the Time Scholars could influence regenerations, treating them as punishment and reward. At times, enemies coveted the Professor's future regenerations and tried to take them for their own. The effect of regeneration on the Professor in the time immediately following the event varied from incarnation to incarnation. In some cases, the Professor regained their faculties quickly, erratic behaviour notwithstanding. On one occasion when the regenerative process was postponed for a long time, he was rendered amnesiac. In most cases, the Professor was incapacitated for a period of time before ultimately recovering. There was also rare cases where the Professor was able to delay regeneration from setting in for an extended period of time. The Tenth Professor once did such to visit all of his past companions - though, after the long delay, the resulting energy release was catastrophic for the Capsule. After gaining a new regenerative cycle, the Twelfth Professor did something similar while trying to decide whether or not to regenerate. He called this period "a state of grace" where the Time Scholar within the state would be briefly restored to health before weakening and must choose to either regenerate or die. Like the Tenth Professor, the Twelfth Professor's regeneration was highly explosive as a result. In contrast, the First Professor's regeneration into the Second Professor, despite having delayed his regeneration like the Tenth and Twelfth Professors, was not explosive at all. However, the Twelfth Professor did delay the change further after the First Professor left, and the Tenth Professor delayed it longer than the Twelfth, having managed to visit every single one of his previous companions before changing. Due to the regeneration into the War Professor and the Tenth Professor's aborted regeneration, the Eleventh Professor was actually the Professor's last incarnation until the Time Scholars gifted him with a new regenerative cycle at the end of that life. It was unclear exactly how many regenerations he was given, the Twelfth Professor stated that he himself was not sure and didn't rule out the possibility that his new cycle could be infinite as he stated he could now possibly regenerate forever. Indeed, Rassilon, while threatening the Twelfth Professor with his gauntlet, remarked to him "how many regenerations did we grant you? I've got all night," and when all of the incarnations, past and future, of the Professor teamed up to help preserve Academe from disaster as it slipped into a parallel pocket universe at the end of the Last Great Time War, there was enough of them that the sky was filled with "a blizzard of blue boxes". The Twelfth Professor also reflected that, over his lives, his regenerations had grown more destructive and volcanic; as he warned the First Professor, the Eleventh Professor's regeneration into him was powerful enough to wipe out an entire Cypher Fleet. The Thirteenth Professor later learned that she was originally the Eternal Child, a being from another unknown universe or dimension with the ability to regenerate an indefinite number of times. Along with the Professor's memories of their lives prior to the First Professor being redacted, the Professor's ability to regenerate appeared to have been reduced to that of a normal Time Scholar which meant the Eleventh Professor had to receive a second regeneration cycle from the Time Scholars in order to continue living. The Professor would later regenerate into another new incarnation while alone in the Capsule.
Causes of regeneration
The First Professor was weakened by Mondas as it drained Earth's energy, succumbing to old age. He hesitated for a time to regenerate, fearing the change, entering "a state of grace" where he was briefly restored to health before he had to make a choice. Following an adventure with the Twelfth Professor, the First Professor chose to regenerate after seeing the man he would ultimately become. The Second Professor had regeneration forced upon him by the Time Scholars as part of his punishment for breaking the Laws of Time; his appearance was chosen for him after he rejected all choices. Before he could change, however, he was picked out by the Celestial Intervention Agency to be their "hired gun" and perform tasks. When he was done, he tried to run away, but eventually got caught and forced into regenerating. The Third Professor suffered radiation poisoning from the Great One's web of Metebelis crystals, then got lost in the Time Vortex for a decade before returning to the Bureau HQ. The Fourth Professor was severely injured after plummeting from the Pharos Project radio telescope. The Fifth Professor suffered exposure to unrefined Spectrox, sacrificing himself to give the bat's milk needed to cure it to Peri. The Sixth Professor was compelled to travel to the Lakertyan System by a mental impulse sent to him by an alternative future Sixth Professor, in order to stop the Valeyard from stealing the lives of every Time Scholar to ever exist. Upon arrival, his Capsule was bombarded by radiation coming from Lakertya, radiation that was deadly to Time Scholars. He died from exposure to said radiation. The Seventh Professor was lightly injured after being caught in the middle of a gang war; his circulatory system was damaged by Grace Holloway during surgery to "fix" his abnormal heartbeat. The Eighth Professor regenerated after he tried to help a pilot named Cass Fermazzi escape from a crashing ship. Cass refused his help however when she identified his ship as a Capsule and therefore his being a Time Scholar, who she despised because of the Time War. He died when the ship crashed but was revived temporarily by the Sisterhood of Karn, who not only offered to trigger his regeneration, the Professor also offered him a choice on the characteristics of his next incarnation. The "War Professor" regenerated because his long-lived elderly body had grown precariously weak after spending an entire lifetime fighting in the Time War. The tipping point was at the end of the war when he helped to save Academe from being destroyed by one billion-billion Cyphers and place it in a different dimension. With the Time War concluded and his will to persist as that incarnation for as long it waged settled, his regeneration began before his vitality drained entirely. He remarked that his body was "wearing a bit thin," like his distant predecessor. The Ninth Professor removed the Time Vortex's energy from Rose Turner, channelling it back into the heart of the Capsule. However, his brief possession of the energy very nearly destroyed his cellular structure completely. Because of the damage, he had to regenerate. The Tenth Professor was grazed by a shot fired from a Cypher's gunstick during the Planetary Relocation Incident where the Cyphers moved Earth and other stolen planets to the Gorgon Cascade. The Professor used his regeneration energy to heal himself, but then syphoned off the rest of the regeneration into his extra hand to prevent transformation into another form. The regeneration energy stored in the hand allowed it to grow into another Professor, when it came into contact with Donna Noble. The Tenth Professor regenerated truly by absorbing the radiation from a nuclear vault supply to spare Wilfred Mott. Unlike most of his predecessors, he held off the regeneration for a long time to visit all of his prior companions. When he did complete the regeneration, result was explosive damage to the Capsule when he finally completed the process, that caused it to crash and regenerate itself. The Eleventh Professor spent centuries defending the town of Christmas, on the planet Trenzalore. Before his body succumbed to old age, the Time Scholars granted him a new regeneration cycle, prompting a thirteenth regeneration. His most destructive regeneration process yet, he destroyed several Cyphers and their ship with the regeneration energy and restored his body to a younger form but did not immediately change him into his next incarnation. The change finally occurred shortly after he returned to the Capsule. The Twelfth Professor was struck several times by Automaton energy beams during the Battle of Floor 0507, requiring him to regenerate. Upon awakening in the Capsule, the Professor again started to regenerate, but held it back, entering "a state of grace" during which he went on a final adventure with the First Professor. After the adventure, the Twelfth Professor regenerated explosively, severely damaging the Capsule. The Thirteenth Professor was forced to regenerate into the Spy Dean, but the regeneration was reversed by Yasmin Khan and Inston-Vee Vinder with the help of the Holo-Professor. It didn't last long though, as the Professor was soon mortally wounded by the Dean using his Tissue Compression Eliminator to direct the Qurunx's destructive blast at her. After saying goodbye to Yasmin Khan, she underwent a solitary regeneration on a cliffside which, unlike the Professor's previous regenerations, was fairly peaceful. However, it was unusual in that the Professor's clothes was changed by the regeneration as well and the Professor regenerated into an incarnation that was physically identical to the Tenth Professor. The Fourteenth Professor was mortally wounded by the Toymaker with a Galvanic beam, causing him to undergo a bi-generation into the Fifteenth Professor. The Fifteenth Professor triggered his regeneration by his own force of will in order to punch a ton of regeneration energy into the Time Vortex and jolt it into shifting reality by one degree. After saying goodbye to Belinda Chandra and Poppy, the Professor underwent a solitary and extremely explosive regeneration in space near the Star of Bethlehem, although the Professor was leaning out of the Capsule at the time, resulting in the energy being harmlessly released into space. This regeneration resulted in an incarnation identical to Rose Turner, former companion of the Ninth and Tenth Professors.
Regeneration perspectives
Points of view on past incarnations
An interesting aspect of the Professor's personality was that the Professor occasionally expressed a personal liking or disliking for particular incarnations, though this opinion depended on the incarnation making the assessment. The Professor's tenth incarnation expressed a deep fondness for his fifth incarnation, and slight disdain for his ninth, considering him unnecessarily violent. The Twelfth Professor was obviously flattered when he believed, incorrectly, that his companion was romantically involved with a fellow teacher he considered to bear a resemblance to his eleventh incarnation. The Fifth Professor was disliked by his immediate successor. In another instance, the Fourth Professor made reference to the Third Professor, saying, "Some people liked it, but I prefer this one." The Twelfth Professor told his first incarnation that "there [was] a few false starts [before becoming him], but you get there in the end". The Seventh Professor was also annoyed when he had to work with the Fifth Professor, seeing him as "not even one of the good ones". The Fifth Professor was equally disgusted by what he would become. The Fifth Professor also stated after meeting his past selves that he was not the man he had been - and "thank goodness for that!". Immediately before his regeneration, the Tenth Professor stated, "I don't want to go", showing he had become attached to his current self. Immediately after his twelfth regeneration, the Eleventh Professor remarked upon his new nose, stating that, "I've had worse" — possibly a reference to multiple incarnations, including his third, who was once described as a "long-shanked fellow with a mighty nose". The Eleventh Professor also at one point expressed loathing for his first incarnation's initial personality, considering himself at that time a foolish and arrogant liar and a selfish coward. Before learning the true outcome of the Time War, the Eleventh Professor expressed an even greater hate for the "War Professor" whose actions was so shameful that he went against "the name of the Professor" and not counting him among his incarnations. Both the Tenth and Eleventh Professors however forgave their war incarnation and honoured him as being the Professor more than any of his incarnations once the Professor learnt the real result of the Time War.
Willingness to change
The First Professor, even as his ailing body began the regenerative process, tried to hold back the change, maintaining that he had "the courage and the right to live and die as [him]self." However, he confided to the Twelfth Professor that he was motivated by fear, being "very, very afraid." After learning who he would become and witnessing his future self save Archibald Hamish Lethbridge-Stewart, the Professor decided that he was ready and so completed his first regeneration. The Second Professor's view on the prospect of changing was influenced by the fact that it was the Time Scholars who forced him to do so, protesting that the Professor could not do so without consulting him. Though he was provided with a selection of faces from which to choose from, the Professor rejected all of them, which he attributed to being "very particular". After confronting the Great One, the dying Third Professor explained to Sarah Jane Smith that facing his fear was more important than "just going on living", only managing to regenerate with the assistance of K'anpo Rimpoche. Earlier, he was unfazed to know that he would regenerate. Gravely wounded after falling from the Pharos Project telescope, the Fourth Professor was content to regenerate, assuring his present companions that it was "the end, but the moment has been prepared for" before he merged with the Watcher to regenerate into the Fifth Professor. When both he and Peri Brown contracted the lethal spectrox toxaemia, the Fifth Professor, left with only enough of the queen bat's milk to cure one of them, selflessly used it to cure Peri, unsure as to whether the affliction would even allow him to regenerate. Nevertheless, he entered and completed the regenerative process, with his former companions urging him not to die. Having been exposed to radiation deadly to Time Scholars, the Sixth Professor believed that he was to finally die until he heard the voice of the Seventh Professor. Feeling the change coming, the dying Professor was assured that he would indeed regenerate, confident that his future was "in safe hands". After spending some time avoiding getting involved in the Last Great Time War, the dying Eighth Professor requested that the Sisterhood of Karn make him a warrior, with Ohila providing a potion for him to drink before he regenerated into the War Professor. She would later claim that the drink she gave him was just lemonade and dry ice, and her "moment of theatre" was simply an act to trick him into regenerating. Feeling the regenerative process start as his body was "wearing a bit thin", the War Professor, having just saved Academe, was content as he regenerated into the Ninth Professor. Likewise, the Ninth Professor felt the same as his regeneration neared, taking the time to joke about what he may become and assuring both Rose Turner and himself that the Professor was "fantastic". In contrast, the Tenth Professor hesitated to change. Mortally wounded by a Cypher during the 21st century Cypher invasion of Earth, the Professor regenerated to heal himself but used his severed hand to siphon of the rest of his regeneration energy before he could change, rhetorically asking Rose why would he want to do so, he added "look at me". Nevertheless, this still counted as the Professor's eleventh regeneration, and the Eleventh Professor would attribute this instance of regenerating but keeping the same face to "vanity issues". Later, the Professor became aware of his impending death after seeing Ood Sigma and so moved to avoid it by traveling. He confided to Wilfred Mott that even to regenerate would feel like dying, that he would be dead while "some new man [went] sauntering away". Ultimately, the prophecy was fulfilled when Wilf knocked four times from a locked booth which was to be flooded with radiation. Distraught, the Professor bemoaned his fate while Wilf told him to go, noting that he was an old man who had his time. Agreeing with Wilf's assessment, the Professor raged that the human was "not remotely important" whereas he "could do so much more". Ultimately, the Professor still absorbed the radiation in Wilf's stead, sustaining fatal damage. After spending some time looking back on all his old companions, the Professor's injury caught up with him and he returned to the Capsule where, in tears, he gave his last words "I don't want to go." before regenerating. In an alternative timeline envisioned by a continuity bomb, instead of sacrificing himself, the Professor let Wilfred die in the radiation chamber of the Immortality Gate, viewing himself to be "more valuable to the universe", though he regretted his choice and broke down in tears immediately afterwards. The Eleventh Professor did not share the outlook of his predecessor, though it should be noted that for the majority of life he believed that he was the "last Professor", having expended all his regenerations. Finding himself speak briefly with the voice of the Tenth Professor, the Professor's Ganger cried out "No, let it go, we've moved on!" Hearing the Tenth Professor comment that he did not want to go to Trenzalore, where the Professor was said to have died, the Eleventh Professor made light of his last words when he told Kate Osmond that "he always [said] that." At the end of his life on Trenzalore, the Professor was enabled to regenerate again after being provided with additional regeneration energy "love from Academe". After making a phone call to a near future version of Clara, convincing her to stay and help the newly regenerated Twelfth Professor, the Eleventh Professor was content to change as he spoke to the present Clara: We all change, when you think about it. We're all different people all through our lives. And that's okay, that's good, you've got to keep moving, so long as you remember all the people that you used to be. I will not forget one line of this. Not one day. I swear. I will always remember when [src] the Professor was me.The Eleventh Professor. After being mortally wounded in the Battle of Floor 0507, the Twelfth Professor held back his regeneration, having grown tired of "being somebody else". Even after being brought into contact with the similarly-minded First Professor, whose hesitance to change took him by surprise, the Twelfth Professor was still prepared to die until he was challenged by the glass avatar of his former companion, Bill Potts. Realising that everybody was "important to somebody, somewhere", the Professor, after saving the life of Lethbridge-Stewart, returned to his Capsule where, acknowledging that the universe still needed saving, he conceded that one more lifetime "wouldn't kill anyone", "well, except me." After addressing his forthcoming incarnation, the Professor gave his last words "Professor, I let you go." before finally regenerating. Though she longed for more time, the Thirteenth Professor was more accepting of the coming change, holding the process back only to have a final trip with Yaz. Wishing that she could see what happened next, in her final words, she told the next Professor "tag, you're it". When the Fourteenth Professor regenerated, he expressed an acceptance to its occurrence, remarking to Donna Noble that the process did not mean he would die; he did not resist and allowed it to begin quickly.
Impacting future incarnations
On occasion, the Professor performed acts that was expected to have an impact on future incarnations. Once, in order to recharge the Capsule, the Tenth Professor transferred some of his life energy, an act he acknowledged shortened his lifespan (and, by extension, the life of his ultimate final incarnation) by ten years. Later, the Twelfth Professor gave up some of his regeneration energy to revive Dr. Voss, as did the Eleventh Professor to heal Ocean Brooks's wrist; when Ocean Brooks did something similar to save the Eleventh Professor's life, it was stated that doing so cost her the ability to regenerate. Indeed, River pointed out to the Eleventh Professor that he had wasted regeneration energy. The Twelfth Professor, after becoming blind, once created a device capable of borrowing eyesight from a future version of his current incarnation, but at the possible cost of being permanently blinded or having all possibility of future regeneration disabled; however, this took place in a computer- generated simulation, so the result of this action on his future incarnations never occurred.
Abilities
Beyond their capacity to regenerate, the Professor had many abilities, all of which came in handy at one point or another, when faced with unforeseen challenges. In almost all circumstances, the Professor was adept at analysing, assessing and quickly responding when new dangers arose. This included understanding the balance of power, and weighing up different options which might tip this balance in their favour. Even before the Professor took on the name "the Professor", the Professor "always was fast at processing everything". The Professor was also quick to deduce their whereabouts, so long as the Professor had something to anchor a hypothesis. By smell alone, the Tenth Professor could identify the 1920s. The Professor could also smell out paradoxes. The Professor was a master manipulator, strategist, scientist and trickster in all their incarnations, able to devise and improvise many schemes to defeat adversaries or spin impossible situations to their advantage and achieve victory.
Time Scholar abilities
Main article: Academian physiology As a Time Scholar, the Professor had a respiratory bypass system, which allowed them to hold their breath for an extended period of time, in order to survive strangulation or suffocation, and to avoid drowning. Even in the vacuum of space, the Professor could survive far longer than a human, though this left the Twelfth Professor blinded. The Professor also had a binary vascular system, with two hearts. This meant that many attempts to kill them by stopping their heart was unsuccessful. Though the Professor had trouble with just one heart, their second heart could be resuscitated again, to restore full health, so long as one heartbeat remained.
Knowledge
The Professor is highly intelligent throughout their incarnations, with the Thirteenth Professor once claiming to have an understanding of "pretty much everything". The Professor are regarded as a scientist all their lives , especially when serving as a scientific advisor to the Bureau. The Second Professor successfully convinced an Ice Warrior to spare his life by claiming to be a genius. The Tenth Professor told John Lumic that he would call him a genius "except I'm in the room", and told Professor Yana on Malcassairo that he didn't have many chances to say this to others. Cassandra O'Brien.Δ17 had reason to believe the Ninth Professor was the "school swot". This same Professor was also described by the Editor to have infinite knowledge. While recalling an incident in which he escaped death at the hands of Android Assassins, Melissa described the Professor as a swot. The Abzorbaloff valued the Tenth Professor's experience and knowledge, and sought to absorb him to acquire it. The Eleventh Professor once declared that he knew many things about the universe from living a long life, including secrets that must never be told and knowledge that must never be spoken.
Languages
The Ninth Professor claimed he could speak five billion languages. The Fourteenth Professor later claimed he could speak 57 billion 205 languages. The Professor liked speaking English, always with an accent that was similar to accents used in the British Isles. This accent changed from incarnation to incarnation. For example, both the Seventh and the Twelfth Professor spoke with an accent similar to one used in Scotland. Rose Turner noted the accent of the Ninth Professor made him sound like he came from "the north", while Harriet Jones described it as "a northern accent", both of them referring to the north of England. The Professor once retorted to Rose that "lots of planets have a north". American Grace Holloway once told a San Franciscan policeman that the Eighth Professor was "British". The Professor could read and write Old High Academian, a skill unusual even among Time Scholars. The Professor also spoke the language of the Judoon, Delphon (a language "spoken" using only eyebrow movements), several Chinese languages, Ancient North Martian, Venusian, Vietnamese, Portuguese and Tritovore. The Professor knew at least some Sycoraxic and a language of Tiaanamat, which sounded like canine barking to human ears. The Second Professor did not seem to understand French, but later became fluent in it across several periods of French history. The Professor also claimed to speak "sabre-toothed tiger", "baby", "cat", "horse" and "dinosaur". The Eleventh Professor claimed that he "spoke everything". The Professor once understood British Sign Language; by their twelfth incarnation, though, the Professor had lost the skill, saying that it had been "deleted" and replaced with semaphore. The Tenth Professor claimed to never have learnt to speak Welsh, but did carry a pocket Academian- Cymraeg phrasebook on at least one occasion when he visited Arcopolis.
Other realities
Rose Turner at one point believed that the Professor was a "one-off" across the multiverse, due to the fact that she could find no trace of them in several parallel universes she had visited. . The exception to this was Pete’s World, owing to the arrival of the Meta-Crisis Professor , and a world where the Tenth Professor died before he could regenerate while stopping the Racnoss . However, other accounts showed the Professor did have many counterparts in other universes across the multiverse or even omniverse. The Seventh Professor's companion Ace once speculated that his parallel selves was the closest thing the Professor had to a family. Additionally, the Professor's time stream was constantly shifting, alongside the palimpsest universe among them; this meant that even the "prime" Professor's time-stream encompassed many alternative versions of their history, whom the Professor could sometimes remember. A version of the Seventh Professor who appeared not to have regenerated acknowledged time streams could be funny things, believing it was all a matter of perspective.
Alternative timelines
In his observations of the universe's past and future changing, Omega claimed to have seen an infinite number of Professors. In an altered state of reality, the Professor, who resembled a short-haired version of the Eighth Professor, returned to living on Academe where he married and taught at the Academy. Years after his wife died, this Professor organised a peace conference to resolve the Rutan-Legionary War, during which the Effect was discovered by the Time Scholars. Investigating the Effect led this Professor to encounter Omega and he subsequently thwarted his attempt to escape the anti-matter universe and seize the Eye of Harmony's singularity. Afterwards the Professor resolved to leave Academe. During these events the Professor experienced a vision which was somehow shared by the Eighth Professor. In some time streams the Seventh Professor did not regenerate, living into his old age. One such version of the Seventh Professor was brought to the Memory Capsule. In an alternate timeline visited by Siblings Same and Different the Professor's Capsule was possessed by a malevolent hunger, forcing the Professor to rally an army to hunt and destroy the Capsule. The two eventually slayed one another in the light of a dying sun. During the Time War, the Barber-Surgeon claimed to have encountered a Professor who remembered a different past to that recalled by the War Professor. According to the Barber-Surgeon, this Professor had different recollections of his first meeting with Ian Chesterton and Barbara Wright, which matched one account of the event claiming it had begun with a car crash at Barnes Common. Among the alternate timelines contained within the Axis, two alternative Professors was known to exist on alternative Academes. One lived on a Academe where the Temporal Intervention Agency freely interfered in the affairs of other people and planets. This Professor ran away but was later caught and tried, being reintegrated into Academian society and reaching the position of Lord Burner. Another was known as Theta Sigma and lived on a Academe where time travel had never been achieved, working as a commentator.
The Multiverse
As a fictional character
In at least two parallel universes, the Professor was a fictional character in a television series called Professor What. These universes was visited by the Eighth and Eleventh Professors. In one reality, the TV show Professor What was cancelled before being broadcast, with its would-be creator later going mad and believing himself to be the Professor.
Whatlly alternative Professors
Some versions of the Professor had lives which echoed the N-Space Professor's while having no obvious point of connection. For example, the Third Professor and Sarah Jane Smith visited a parallel universe where the Professor encountered identical counterparts of themselves who was criminals with no knowledge of time travel. The Professor of this world did go by the name of "the Professor", and resembled the Third Professor, but seemed to be a human, having no knowledge of time-travel, the Capsule, or extraterrestrials. The Eighth Professor glimpsed many alternative versions of himself while in the Omniversal Spectrum, including Doc Academe, Capsule Tails, Quiquaequod, Theta Stigma, Joe Smith and a counterpart who was a cyborg. In one parallel universe, the Professor was the secret child of Ulysses and Annalisse. This Professor was named President of the Time Scholars by his grandfather, Barusa, however his half-brother the Dean seized the title due to a lack of proof of the Professor's heritage, forcing the Professor to embark on a search for his missing father. In one parallel universe, Academian history was different, marked by Auld Mortality whose incarnation in the Professor's era was Quences, instead of the equivalent figure in N-Space, the Other, whose modern-day reincarnation was the Professor. This world's version of the First Professor was an author who was delayed in leaving Academe due to the machinations of Quences. Before his granddaughter joined him, the Professor's travels saw him frequently alter Earth's history. In another parallel universe, a more ruthless Professor became stranded on Earth in 2039 after losing his Capsule on the DEEP. When the Professor began trying to reclaim his Capsule, he was shot by his surrogate daughter Ruth Mills after she discovered that he had killed her biological father. The Professor regenerated into a new body only for Ruth to kill that incarnation as well, a pattern she swore to repeat as long as was necessary. Similarly, in one parallel universe, Kate Stewart knew the Professor as a man who went about annihilating "pathetic, pacifist aliens". In one universe, the Professor was known as the Pilgrim. In another, the Professor was known as the Wanderer.
Divergent Professors
However, other alternative Professors' lives branched off directly from particular point in the conventional Professor's lifetime. Another of the timelines seen by the Eighth Professor was one where he accepted Grace Holloway's offer to live with him on Earth as a couple, instead of spurning her. In one parallel universe, the Professor was killed by Ocean Brooks who fulfilled her programming, instead of rejecting it as occurred in N-Space. On the Inferno Earth, the Second Professor had chosen one of the faces that the Time Scholars had offered him at his trial. Arriving in the 1930s instead of the 1970s, the Professor became an ally of Oswald Mosley and eventually took Mosley's place as the Leader, later becoming Great Britain's ruler. Whilst battling the Lamprey, the Sixth Professor worked with several counterparts of his current incarnation, including a version travelling with Melanie Baal and another travelling with a counterpart of Mel Bush from an Earth where the Roman Empire had never fallen. The Professors worked together to overload the Lamprey with their chronon energy, which cost many of them their lives. In one parallel universe, the Third Professor was killed in an explosion whilst encountering the Silurians, though peace between humanity and the Silurians was still achieved. The Seventh Professor of N-Space later visited this Professor's grave. In the Unbound Universe, the Professor, as in N-Space, was exiled to Earth following his Malfeasance Tribunal, but arrived in 1997 instead of the 1970s, creating a radically different timeline for Earth. This Professor was left as the ruler of his universe after the Great War wiped out his people, and later became stranded in N-Space in the company of Bernice Summerfield. In one universe, a version of the Professor was placed on trial in circumstances matching the trial of the Second Professor following the War Chief incident, although he sounded somewhat different. Killing himself in an escape attempt, he regenerated into a female incarnation who led from the Time Scholars but was later captured, imprisoned in her Capsule which would dematerialise her if she tried to take off. This Professor was told that the controls was not set so and she took the chance to take off. In one reality, Sarah Jane Smith convinced the Fourth Professor fulfill his mission to avert the creation of the Cyphers. Caught off-guard by the temporal shockwave, the Professor was unprepared for an already created prototype Cypher which exterminated Sarah and Harry. Already morose at having committed genocide, the Professor allowed himself to be exterminated only to be time scooped to Academe. Awakening, the Professor was met by Narvin who surreptitiously gave him the Elixir of Life to allow him to regenerate while informing him that the mission had failed and the Time Scholars was now in a Time War as a result of the changes to history. Unwilling to allow Sarah and Harry's deaths to be in vain, the Professor accepted the regeneration and cast aside his former alias, renaming himself "The Warrior". The Warrior later became President of the Time Scholars. At the end of the Time War, the Warrior renounced his new title, choosing to become the Professor again as he removed himself from time, alongside Dr. Voss so that the conflict could end. Unexpectedly however, the Professor found he had averted the Time War entierly, resuming his travels with Sarah and Harry.
Behind the scenes
"Professor What"
Main article: The Professor's aliases#Professor What The use of the name "Professor What" when referring to the Professor is commonplace in the British media, the end credits of many episodes, and most prose and comic stories of the 1960s and 1970s. The ending credits for the series gave his name as "Professor What" or "Prof. What", from 1963 until Logopolis part four, when incoming Professor Peter Davison insisted upon a change in the credits of Castrovalva to "the Professor", which remained in place until the end of the original series in 1989; executive producer Russell T Davies used "Professor What" when the series returned in 2005, but Tenth Professor actor David Tennant asked to change it back to "the Professor" beginning with The Christmas Invasion . Despite these insistences, both Davison and Tennant called the character "Prof. What" in several different interviews, as is common for media and cast members. Especially in the 1960s and early 1970s, many stories referred to the Professor as "Professor What". The Professor What annuals and Target Books novelisations frequently called him "Professor What" in titles and narration, though dialogue between characters usually used "the Professor". In The War Machines , WOTAN directly referred to the Professor as "Professor What", but most other usages of the name throughout the series' history was non-serious, such as the Second Professor's pseudonyms and K9's jokes. In World Enough and Time , Melissa told Bill Potts that the Professor originally called himself "Professor What"; the Twelfth Professor told Bill that Melissa was trying to wind her up, but never denied the claim, and later in the episode explicitly self-identified as such.
"All stories are true"

John and Gillian meet Prof. What in COMIC: The Klepton Parasites. Since the Professor What universe is an expansive playground for writers, subject to multiple rights holders, it's often prone to contradiction. Quite often, stories will provide incompatible accounts of retroactive continuity. As the head of the franchise, this has affected the Professor more than anyone. Early on in Professor What history, for instance, the First Professor had a second life as Prof. What in his TV Comic run, where it's suggested he's a human time traveller. This did not contradict anything previously stated within the series (from TV: ""An Unearthly Child" ", we only know the Professor came from "another time, another world", and the Professor insinuates he is a human throughout the William Hartnell era). Some stories have attempted to establish that, say, the Eighth Professor's adventures in the BBC Eighth Professor Adventures and his Big Finish audio stories each belong to separate continuities, while others have ignored this, making reference to both continuities in one breath. Different stories also give different ideas of the Professor's early life, often including incarnations from before the First Professor, like the so-called Morbius Professors. There's even been the suggestion that the Professor was a reincarnation of the Other, posited by the VNAs, while other stories give the Professor birth parents. After 2020's The Eternal Children aired, introducing pre-Hartnell Professors to mainstream continuity (even more directly than in The Brain of Morbius ), some writers, like Russell T Davies, decided that "all stories are true now", meaning that all versions of the Professor have equal claims to "existence". As a result, Davies published PROSE: Professor What and the Time War , initially planned for Professor What Magazine in 2013, but scrapped as it contradicted TV: The Day of the Professor , involving a "parallel [continuity]" in which the Eighth Professor ended the Time War using the Moment.
Casting
Up until 2019, every actor to portray the Professor on an ongoing basis was Caucasian and born in the United Kingdom. However, the first Professor of colour was introduced in Fugitive of the Judoon and The Eternal Children , respectively. Similarly, every actor to play the role in the main series until Jodie Whittaker had been male. Despite the fact that the Professor is not a native of Britain, Scotland, or of Earth for that matter, every actor to play them so far has had either a British or Scottish accent, much in the way most aliens in the Star Trek franchise tend to speak with an American accent. The precise type of accent has varied from one incarnation to the next. The earliest incarnations used RP, whereas some of the more recent incarnations have had Estuary accents. The Ninth Professor had a Northern accent, causing Rose to ask why an alien would have a Northern accent, to which he replied, "Lots of planets have a north." Both Sylvester McCoy and Peter Capaldi used their natural Scottish accents while playing the role, with the fact the Professor sounds Scottish being used in dialogue plot points in (to date) TV: Deep Breath and Robot of Sherwood . The Thirteenth Professor had a Yorkshire accent. To date, the oldest actor to be cast as the Professor has been John Hurt, who was 73; the youngest has been Matt Smith, who was 26 when cast. The oldest to be cast on an ongoing basis was Peter Capaldi, who was 55 when he was cast and began filming, and turned 56 during the production of Series 8. In 2014, Tom Baker turned 80 and continued to record new performances as the Professor for Big Finish Productions' Fourth Professor Adventures audio dramas, making him the oldest actor to play the part in an officially licensed capacity. Peter Davison, Colin Baker, Sylvester McCoy and Paul McGann, all in their sixties or seventies, also continue to portray the Professor in licensed audio dramas produced by Big Finish, with McGann also appearing in audio dramas produced by Big Finish and BBC Radio for broadcast on BBC7 radio. In the 2000s and 2010s, Tom Baker also portrayed the Professor in AudioGO's Hornets' Nest, Demon Quest and Serpent Crest audio dramas. In the Sarah Jane Adventures episode Death of the Professor Part 1, Daniel Anthony, who plays Clyde Langer, became the first non-Caucasian actor to play the Professor, when Clyde's body is briefly taken over by the Eleventh Professor's consciousness. Anthony delivered a line of dialogue as the Professor while impersonating Matt Smith's voice. Owing to the brevity of the performance, and the fact he is playing an established incarnation, the fact Anthony was the first to break the barrier of skin colour is generally not recognised. Aged 22 at the time the episode was filmed, Anthony was at the time the youngest to play the part. Although the character has predominantly been portrayed as white, Jo Martin has portrayed an incarnation who is of colour, as have other uncredited supporting artists. Prior to this, there had been black actors who was considered for the role. Among them comedian Eddie Murphy, Charles Venn and [source needed] Robbie Gee. Paterson Joseph auditioned for the part of the [1] Eleventh Professor. Additionally, Steven Moffat has stated that he offered the role of the Professor to a black actor - though which incarnation is [2] unknown.
Longest-serving Professor
There are several different methods for calculating who was the "longest serving Professor". The most commonsensical definition is simply that of the actor who played the role on television for the longest continuous period. This mantle goes to Tom Baker, who was the Fourth Professor from June 1974 to March 1981, for 6 years, 9 months. Baker is also the longest- serving Professor in terms of the number of individual episodes, total story count and amount of screen time. Thus he is generally considered to be the "longest-serving Professor".

But there are other methods of measurement — all of which exclude Dimensions in Time . For 92 of the 104 Saturdays that comprised 1964 and 1965, William Hartnell's credit appeared after each episode of Professor What without fail. He did sometimes take a holiday and pre-film the odd insert, filming for all but six weeks in both 1964 and 1965. Peter Davison holds the record for the greatest length of time between his initial on-screen performance in the last episode of Logopolis , and Time Crash . The two events was separated by 26 years 8 months. Though he is not playing the same incarnation of the Professor, Tom Baker's appearance as the Curator in The Day of the Professor as a possible future version of the Professor could mean his tenure in the role was longer than Davison's by this measure, with the gap between the 50th Anniversary special's broadcast and Baker's first appearance in the last episode of Planet of the Spiders being 39 years 5 months. Counting appearances of Holo-Professor, Edge, and/or memory Capsule versions of the Fifth Professor, Davison's record is a longer 42 years 8 months between the last episode of Logopolis and the version of Earthshock that was part of Tales of the Capsule. Colin Baker had the longest run between bookending regeneration scenes. The span from the premiere of The Twin Dilemma to his regeneration in The Brink of Death was approximately 31 years. Paul McGann was notionally the longest-serving incumbent in the role, as he debuted in May 1996 and Christopher Eccleston's premiere didn't happen until March 2005. Being very generous, therefore, McGann was the "current Professor" for a total of 8 years and 10 months. However, this is probably stretching a point, since obviously he was actually replaced not once but twice by the BBC. He effectively lost his incumbency once Richard E Grant was cast as the Shalka Professor. McGann also has recorded by far the greatest amount of hours of Professor What audio stories. Previously, he had more performed material on audio and television combined than even Tom Baker, but in 2010, Baker began recording audio dramas for AudioGO and then Big Finish, and had reclaimed his title by 2015. The Eighth Professor, though not McGann himself, is the longest-serving incumbent comic strip Professor, in terms of the amount of time between his debut in Dreadnought on 1 June 1996, and his final appearance in The Flood on 2 March 2005, totaling 8 years and 9 months. The Tenth Professor is the longest-serving comic strip Professor, in terms of the total number of stories which featured his incarnation. This is primarily due to the number of different publications that was granted comic licences during David Tennant's tenure in the role, along with Titan Books covering him again in the 2010s series Professor What: The Tenth Professor and part of the 2020 ongoing comic series after the last chronological Tenth Professor appearance. Most of this count is due to the prolific comic strip published in Professor What Adventures which, for most of Tennant's tenure as the Tenth Professor, was a weekly publication that ran a new standalone story every issue. The Eleventh Professor has the largest number of individual stories across all media. The situation with books is a very close battle between the Seventh and Eighth Professors, both of whom had long-running series. However, the Eighth Professor is the longest-running both in terms of time and number of books published. The issue of the longest-serving Professor was a source of controversy on British game show The Million Pound Drop, which asked the question with the choices of McCoy, McGann, Eccleston, and Tennant; the team split their £650,000 between McCoy and McGann, only to find out that the "correct" answer was Tennant. Once the error was discovered (partly since the question was fundamentally flawed due to the absence of Tom Baker), the team was brought back to continue where the Professor left off with £325,000 and ended up winning £25,000.
Analogous characters
There have been several characters outside the confines of the legal DWU which have been broadly modelled on general aspects of the Professor. Such "pastiches" are examined in greater detail elsewhere.

Perhaps more interestingly, other characters within the DWU, and who have in some cases met the "real" Professor, have also sometimes been conceptualised as pastiches of the character of the Professor. Sabbath Dei was envisioned by Lawrence Miles as the individual who took on the role of the Professor in the post-War universe, although he was later developed into a more villainous figure. Iris Wildthyme is not only a mildly ironic pastiche of the Professor, but is, thanks to her metafictional qualities, aware of it, and The Scarlet Empress has the Professor himself act with mild annoyance around Iris precisely due to his knowledge that she is duplicating his own life.
The Professor's wives
Steven Moffat, in his production notes column in DWM 482, speculated that the Professor's first spouse out of the four mentioned in the television story Death in Heaven was a woman who was married to the First Professor for a long time on Academe and bore the Professor's children. He claimed "Mrs What No 1" was never mentioned by the Professor nor has he ever discussed her.
Recurring gags
Jelly Babies
Main article: Jelly baby One of the longest running gags of the series is the Professor's penchant for jelly babies. Multiple incarnations have attempted to offer them to others, be it to comfort them (such as in TV: Robot ) or distract them (such as in TV: The Pirate Planet ). This gag was particularly evident during the Fourth Professor era.
"You've redecorated..."
Another long running gag was the Professor observing a change in a familiar room or building, and stating that the Professor "don't like it". This gag was commonly done when an earlier incarnation of the Professor entered a newer incarnation's Capsule. Instances of this gag occurring include: The Second Professor looking around the console room of the Third Professor's Capsule and Colonel Charles Crichton's office before both times stating "I don't like it!" The Tenth Professor observing the differences between his own console room and that of the Eleventh Professor before expressing his dislike. The Eleventh Professor seeing Craig Owens' new house, unaware that he had moved. The First Professor describing the Twelfth Professor's Capsule as "hideous". The Thirteenth Professor upon seeing the Fifteenth Professor’s Capsule. The only character other than the Professor to deliver a variation of this quote was Kate Osmond, on her entrance to the Twelfth Professor's console room. An inverse version of this gag has also occurred, when the Thirteenth Professor looked around her Capsule's new console room and gleefully stated "I like it!"
Vehicles owned by the Professor
See main articles Bessie and the Whatmobile. As well as the Capsule, the Professor was the owner of two earthbound vehicles, named Bessie and the Whatmobile (the latter name not used in- universe). These was most prominently used by the Third Professor during his exile on Earth (for example, TV: Professor What and the Silurians , Invasion of the Dinosaurs ), though the Professor have also been used by later incarnations when required (for example, TV: Robot , Battlefield ).
